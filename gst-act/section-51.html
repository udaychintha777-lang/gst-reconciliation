GST ACT — SECTION 51

Section 51 CGST Act — GST TDS, 1% Deduction, GSTR-7 & TDS Credit

Section 51 explains GST Tax Deducted at Source (TDS). In simple language: certain notified buyers deduct a small part of the GST taxable supply payment and deposit that amount with the Government. The supplier then gets credit of the deducted amount in the electronic cash ledger.

1. Why Section 51 matters

People search for “Section 51 GST”, “GST TDS rate”, “GST TDS threshold”, “GSTR-7”, “who deducts GST TDS?”, “GST TDS credit” and “is GST TDS an ITC?” because GST TDS is easy to confuse with Income-tax TDS.

Section 51 is a GST payment-monitoring mechanism. Certain notified recipients deduct tax from payments made or credited to eligible suppliers and deposit the deducted amount with the Government.

The easiest way to remember it: Buyer deducts GST TDS → Buyer deposits it with Government → Buyer reports it → Supplier gets the amount as a credit in the electronic cash ledger.

2. Section 51 in very simple language

Suppose a Government department has a taxable contract with a supplier for ₹5,00,000 before GST.

If GST TDS applies, the department does not pay the supplier the entire eligible amount. It deducts the prescribed TDS and deposits that amount with the Government.

Step 1 — Supplier bills

The supplier makes a taxable supply under the contract.

Step 2 — Buyer checks TDS

The buyer checks whether it is a notified deductor and whether the contract crosses the threshold.

Step 3 — TDS deducted

The prescribed GST TDS is deducted from the payment/amount credited to the supplier.

Step 4 — Supplier gets credit

After the deductor reports the deduction, the supplier can claim the amount in the electronic cash ledger.

Important: the supplier does not lose the money. The deducted amount is tax already deposited on the supplier's behalf and becomes available as cash-ledger credit when reflected correctly.

3. What is GST TDS?

GST TDS means Tax Deducted at Source under Section 51 of the CGST Act.

It is different from Income-tax TDS. The purpose is to create a reporting trail for payments made by specified Government bodies and other notified deductors to suppliers.

Section 51 permits the Government to mandate deduction by specified categories, at 1% under the CGST mechanism, where the contract value of taxable goods or services exceeds ₹2.5 lakh, subject to the statutory conditions. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Common confusion: “1% GST TDS” does not mean the same thing as a 1% Income-tax TDS. They are two separate laws and two separate compliance systems.

6. Who has to deduct GST TDS?

Not every GST-registered business has to deduct GST TDS.

Section 51 identifies categories such as:

  • a department or establishment of the Central Government or State Government;
  • local authorities;
  • Governmental agencies; and
  • other persons or categories of persons notified by the Government on the recommendation of the GST Council.

The important point is that being registered under GST does not automatically make every person a GST TDS deductor. The person must fall within the statutory/notified category.

7. GST TDS rate

Section 51(1) specifies deduction at 1% from the payment made or credited to the supplier, subject to the statutory conditions.

For an intra-State taxable supply, this is commonly reflected as:

0.5% CGST TDS + 0.5% SGST/UTGST TDS = 1% total GST TDS

For an inter-State situation where GST TDS is applicable under the statutory framework, the corresponding IGST deduction is generally 2% in the operational rules/FAQs. The article therefore distinguishes the 1% Section 51 rate in the CGST Act from the combined tax-head result. citeturn0search4

8. ₹2.5 lakh threshold

The threshold is often misunderstood.

Section 51 uses the words “where the total value of such supply, under a contract, exceeds two lakh and fifty thousand rupees.”

In simple language, the threshold is tested with reference to the contract value of the taxable supply, not simply the amount of one individual invoice.

Important: The GST amount itself is excluded from the value for the deduction calculation. So a contract may cross ₹2.5 lakh because of its taxable value, but GST shown on the invoice is not included in the TDS base.

9. Value on which TDS is deducted

The explanation to Section 51 says the value for TDS is taken excluding CGST, SGST, UTGST, IGST and cess shown in the invoice.

Invoice componentIncluded in GST TDS base?
Taxable valueYes.
CGSTNo.
SGST/UTGSTNo.
IGSTNo.
Cess shown separatelyNo.

This prevents “tax on tax” in the GST TDS calculation.

10. Intra-State vs inter-State supply

The proviso to Section 51(1) is very important: no deduction is made where the location of the supplier and the place of supply are in a State/UT different from the State/UT of registration of the recipient. citeturn0search0

Therefore, a TDS team must check three things rather than looking only at the supplier's State:

  1. recipient's State/UT of registration;
  2. supplier's location; and
  3. place of supply.
Do not decide GST TDS only from the supplier's address. The place-of-supply condition is part of the statutory test.

11. When GST TDS is not deducted

GST TDS should not be treated as a universal deduction on every Government payment.

  • Check whether the recipient is actually a notified deductor.
  • Check whether the supply is taxable.
  • Check the ₹2.5 lakh contract threshold.
  • Exclude GST and cess from the deduction base.
  • Check supplier location and place of supply against the recipient's registration State/UT.
  • Check the specific notified category and applicable rules for the transaction.

Government FAQs also clarify that exempt supplies do not attract GST TDS. citeturn0search4

12. GST TDS registration

A person required to deduct GST TDS has a special registration requirement. Rule 12 provides the electronic application process through FORM GST REG-07.

The registration certificate is issued in FORM GST REG-06 after verification. Where the person is no longer liable to deduct TDS, cancellation can be handled through the prescribed mechanism. citeturn0search2

Easy distinction: A normal GST registration for making taxable supplies and a GST TDS registration for the deductor role are not the same compliance purpose.

13. How deduction works

Eligible contract → Check threshold → Check place of supply → Calculate taxable value → Deduct TDS → Pay supplier → Deposit TDS → File GSTR-7 → Supplier claims cash-ledger credit

The deductor should maintain a transaction-level working because a mistake at the deduction stage can later create reconciliation problems for both the deductor and supplier.

14. Deposit with Government

Section 51(2) requires the deducted amount to be paid to the Government within ten days after the end of the month in which the deduction is made, in the prescribed manner. citeturn0search0

For example, if TDS is deducted during September, the statutory payment deadline under Section 51(2) is 10 October, subject to the applicable rules/portal procedure.

15. GSTR-7

GSTR-7 is the monthly return used by the GST TDS deductor to report deductions.

The deductor should reconcile GSTR-7 with:

  • supplier invoices/payment records;
  • TDS deducted;
  • TDS deposited;
  • GST TDS certificates; and
  • supplier-wise credit reflected in the GST system.

CBIC's implementation guidance explains that the deductor reports the deductions in GSTR-7 and the reporting enables the supplier to receive the corresponding credit. citeturn0search5

16. TDS certificate

Section 51(3) provides for a TDS certificate in the prescribed form and manner.

The certificate gives the supplier evidence of the deduction and helps reconcile the amount appearing in the GST system.

The original certificate provision was changed by Finance Act 2020. The amendment was brought into force from 1 January 2021 through Notification 92/2020-Central Tax. citeturn0search0

17. Supplier's TDS credit

This is the most important point for the supplier.

Suppose a Government customer deducts ₹10,000 GST TDS from your payment. You do not lose that ₹10,000.

After the deduction is reported by the deductor and reflected in the GST system, the supplier can claim the amount as credit in the electronic cash ledger.

Think of it as money already deposited on your behalf. It can then be used for GST payment or handled through the applicable cash-ledger/refund mechanism.

Section 51(5) expressly provides for credit in the electronic cash ledger. citeturn0search0

18. GST TDS is not ITC

GST TDS credit is frequently called “GST credit”, which can create confusion.

GST TDS creditITC
Arises because tax was deducted by the notified deductor.Arises from eligible input tax under Sections 16–18.
Credited to electronic cash ledger.Credited to electronic credit ledger.
It is not input tax credit.It is input tax credit.
Can help pay GST through cash-ledger balance.Can be utilised subject to ITC utilisation rules.

Government FAQs expressly distinguish GST TDS from ITC and state that the amount is credited to the deductee's electronic cash ledger. citeturn0search4

19. Excess or wrong deduction

Section 51(8) links excess or erroneous deduction to the refund framework under Section 54, subject to its proviso.

The important practical point is that a refund to the deductor is not available where the amount deducted has already been credited to the deductee's electronic cash ledger.

Therefore, the deductor and deductee should reconcile a wrong deduction before the credit is wrongly consumed or refunded.

20. Interest for deductor

If a deductor deducts GST TDS but fails to pay the deducted amount to the Government, Section 51(6) makes the deductor liable to interest in accordance with Section 50(1), in addition to the deducted tax. citeturn0search0

In simple language: deducting TDS from the supplier but keeping that money instead of depositing it is a serious default.

21. Default and recovery

Section 51(7) provides the statutory link for determining the amount in default under the applicable demand provisions. This means a TDS default can move beyond a simple reconciliation difference if the deductor does not correct and pay the amount.

For current-period disputes, the applicable demand provision should be checked for the relevant tax period, including the Section 74A framework where applicable.

22. Contracts and amendments

The threshold is linked to the total value of the taxable supply under a contract. Therefore, the finance team should not look at only one payment without first understanding the contract.

23. Construction examples

Construction and infrastructure organisations frequently deal with Government contracts, works contractors, consultants, EPC arrangements and milestone payments. GST TDS controls should therefore be integrated with the contract-payment process.

Example — Government works contract

A Government department awards a taxable works contract to a contractor. Before releasing each RA bill, the TDS desk checks the contract value, supplier GSTIN, place of supply, taxable value and applicable GST TDS rate.

Example — Multiple RA bills

The contract is ₹10 crore and is billed through monthly RA bills. The fact that an individual RA bill is below ₹2.5 lakh does not automatically make the contract fall below the Section 51 threshold. The contract-level test must be considered.

Example — Supplier receives TDS credit

The department deducts GST TDS of ₹50,000. The contractor reconciles the amount with GSTR-7/TDS data and confirms that ₹50,000 is reflected in the electronic cash ledger.

24. Easy numerical examples

Example 1 — ₹5 lakh taxable contract

Taxable contract value = ₹5,00,000. If GST TDS applies, the deduction is calculated on ₹5,00,000, not on the GST amount added to the invoice. At 1% total, the deduction is ₹5,000.

Example 2 — ₹2 lakh contract

Contract taxable value = ₹2,00,000. It does not exceed ₹2.5 lakh, so the Section 51 threshold is not crossed on that contract.

Example 3 — ₹2.5 lakh exactly

The statutory wording says “exceeds” ₹2.5 lakh. Therefore, ₹2.5 lakh exactly is different from a value above ₹2.5 lakh. Always check the applicable rule/notification for the transaction.

Example 4 — GST excluded

Taxable value ₹10,00,000 and GST ₹1,80,000. GST TDS at 1% is ₹10,000, not ₹11,800.

Example 5 — Supplier receives ₹99,000

If the taxable payment base is ₹1,00,000 and GST TDS is ₹1,000, the supplier receives ₹99,000 from the relevant payment amount and the ₹1,000 is deposited as GST TDS.

Example 6 — Inter-State place-of-supply condition

Supplier location and place of supply are in a State different from the recipient's registration State. Section 51's proviso must be examined; the deduction may not apply under the statutory condition.

Example 7 — TDS is not ITC

A supplier receives ₹20,000 TDS credit. It should not be added to the ITC balance. It belongs in the electronic cash ledger.

Example 8 — Wrong TDS

Deductor accidentally deducts twice. The parties should reconcile the duplicate deduction and use the prescribed correction/refund framework.

Example 9 — TDS deducted but not deposited

The deductor deducts ₹30,000 but does not deposit it. Section 51(6) can trigger interest in addition to the tax deducted.

Example 10 — Construction contract

A ₹2 crore Government works contract is divided into monthly RA bills. The TDS team should not treat each RA bill as a fresh independent threshold test without examining the contract-level requirement.

28. GST Council and implementation

GST TDS was designed as a compliance and transaction-reporting mechanism for specified payments, particularly Government-related procurement.

The implementation was initially delayed after GST commenced and was subsequently brought into force from 1 October 2018 through Notification 50/2018-Central Tax. The CBIC implementation circular also described the GSTR-7 reporting and TDS-credit process. citeturn0search5turn0search6

The practical lesson is important: when researching an old transaction, always distinguish the date the provision was enacted from the date the provision became operational.

31. Common mistakes

  • Assuming every GST-registered buyer must deduct GST TDS.
  • Confusing GST TDS with Income-tax TDS.
  • Applying TDS to GST charged in the invoice.
  • Testing only the individual invoice instead of the contract context.
  • Ignoring the supplier location/place-of-supply condition.
  • Calling GST TDS “ITC”.
  • Not reconciling GSTR-7 with supplier GSTIN.
  • Deducting TDS but failing to deposit it within the prescribed time.
  • Failing to issue/complete the prescribed TDS certificate process.
  • Ignoring interest when deducted TDS is not paid to Government.
  • Mixing GST TDS with Income-tax TDS in accounting records.

32. Section 51 GST FAQs

What is Section 51 of the CGST Act?

Section 51 deals with Tax Deducted at Source under GST.

What is GST TDS?

GST TDS is tax deducted by specified/notified recipients from eligible taxable-supply payments and deposited with the Government.

What is the GST TDS rate?

Section 51(1) specifies a 1% deduction under the CGST mechanism. For intra-State supplies this is commonly 0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST/UTGST.

What is the GST TDS threshold?

Section 51(1) uses a contract-value threshold of more than ₹2.5 lakh for the taxable supply under the contract.

Is GST included in the TDS calculation?

No. GST and cess shown in the invoice are excluded from the value used for Section 51 TDS.

Is GST TDS applicable to exempt supplies?

No, the Section 51 mechanism concerns taxable supplies; Government FAQ guidance also confirms no deduction for exempt supplies.

Who has to deduct GST TDS?

Specified Government departments/establishments, local authorities, Governmental agencies and other notified categories, subject to the statutory framework.

Does every Government contractor have to deduct GST TDS?

No. The obligation belongs to the notified deductor, not automatically to every contractor or business.

Is GST TDS the same as Income-tax TDS?

No. GST TDS is under Section 51 of the CGST Act and is reported through the GST system.

Is GST TDS an input tax credit?

No. It is credited to the supplier's electronic cash ledger, not the electronic credit ledger.

Where does GST TDS credit appear?

It is credited to the deductee's electronic cash ledger when the deduction is properly reported and reflected.

What is GSTR-7?

GSTR-7 is the return through which the GST TDS deductor reports the deductions.

When should GST TDS be deposited?

Section 51(2) provides for payment within ten days after the end of the month in which the deduction is made, in the prescribed manner.

What happens if the deductor deducts GST TDS but does not deposit it?

The deductor can become liable for interest under Section 50(1), in addition to the deducted tax.

Can GST TDS be refunded?

Excess or erroneous deduction is dealt with under the Section 54 refund framework, subject to the conditions in Section 51(8).

What if supplier and place of supply are in another State?

The Section 51 proviso must be checked. Where the supplier location and place of supply are in a different State/UT from the recipient's registration State/UT, the section says no deduction shall be made.

What is REG-07?

It is the prescribed registration application form for persons required to deduct TDS or collect TCS under the GST framework.

What is REG-06?

It is the registration certificate issued under the prescribed registration process.

Does the ₹2.5 lakh threshold include GST?

The Section 51 explanation excludes GST and cess from the value used for the deduction. The contract-level threshold and the deduction base should therefore be analysed separately.

Can GST TDS credit pay GST liability?

Yes, once reflected in the electronic cash ledger, it can be used as cash-ledger balance subject to the normal payment rules.

34. Key takeaways

  • Section 51 is the GST TDS provision.
  • Not every GST-registered person is a GST TDS deductor.
  • The Section 51 CGST rate is 1%, subject to the applicable tax-head split.
  • The contract-value threshold is more than ₹2.5 lakh.
  • GST and cess shown in the invoice are excluded from the TDS base.
  • The supplier's location and place of supply must be checked against the recipient's registration State/UT.
  • GST TDS credit goes to the electronic cash ledger, not the ITC ledger.
  • GSTR-7 is the deductor's key reporting return.
  • Deducted tax must be deposited within the statutory time.
  • If TDS is deducted but not deposited, interest can arise under Section 50.
  • For historical transactions, distinguish enactment from the date the TDS provisions became operational.
