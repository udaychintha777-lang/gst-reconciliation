1. What Section 18 does

Section 18 is the GST Act's “special circumstances” ITC provision.

Ordinary ITC under Section 16 normally operates after a person is registered and receiving taxable supplies. Section 18 addresses situations in which the taxpayer's GST status changes and there is already tax embedded in inputs, semi-finished goods, finished goods or capital goods held on the relevant date.

It also deals with the opposite situation: a taxpayer who previously enjoyed ITC later moves into composition, obtains exemption, or cancels registration must give effect to a statutory reversal. Finally, Section 18(3) permits transfer of unutilised ITC when the business itself changes constitution through sale, merger, demerger, amalgamation, lease or transfer with specific provisions for liabilities.

Do not confuse Section 18 with transitional credit under Section 140. Section 18 concerns changes in a registered person's GST status or business constitution under the current GST regime; Section 140 dealt with the transition into GST from earlier indirect-tax systems.

The GST Council's original special-circumstances framework specifically identified fresh registration, voluntary registration, composition-to-regular conversion and exempt-supply-to-taxable conversion as situations in which stock/capital-goods ITC could arise. citeturn0search4turn0search2

3. Section 18(1)(a) — becoming liable to pay tax

This clause applies where a person becomes liable to registration/tax under the normal GST framework and has eligible inputs in stock, or inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods, immediately before the relevant date.

The statutory framework is intended to prevent tax embedded in opening stock from becoming permanently stranded merely because the person crosses the registration threshold and enters the GST system.

Step 1
Identify the date on which liability to pay tax arises.
Step 2
Identify the stock position on the immediately preceding day.
Step 3
Identify eligible invoices and tax amounts.
Step 4
File the prescribed declaration and claim within the statutory procedure.

4. Section 18(1)(b) — voluntary registration

A person who obtains voluntary registration under Section 25(3) can claim eligible ITC on inputs held in stock and inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods immediately before the date of registration, subject to Section 18's conditions and Rule 40.

Example

A business below the compulsory registration threshold voluntarily registers on 1 October. It has eligible raw materials and finished goods in stock on 30 September supported by valid invoices within the prescribed period. The Section 18 mechanism may permit credit of the eligible embedded GST.

Important: Voluntary registration does not create an unrestricted right to all historical purchase ITC. Section 18(2), Section 17 and the prescribed documentary requirements still apply.

5. Section 18(1)(c) — composition to regular taxpayer

When a person ceases to pay tax under Section 10 and becomes liable to pay tax under the normal scheme, Section 18(1)(c) permits ITC on eligible inputs in stock, inputs contained in semi-finished/finished goods and eligible capital goods held immediately before the date from which the normal scheme applies.

This is commercially important because the taxpayer moves from a regime where input tax credit is generally unavailable to the ordinary GST credit chain.

Example: A manufacturer under composition opts out and becomes a regular taxpayer from 1 April. Eligible opening stock and qualifying capital goods on 31 March can be examined for Section 18 credit.

6. Section 18(1)(d) — exempt supply becomes taxable

Where an exempt supply of goods or services becomes taxable, Section 18(1)(d) permits credit on eligible inputs in stock, inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods and eligible capital goods relatable to those supplies immediately before the date the supply becomes taxable.

This prevents a taxpayer from being permanently denied credit on inventory where the tax treatment of the outward supply changes from exempt to taxable.

Key point: This clause is linked to the particular supply becoming taxable. It is not a general reset of every historical ITC amount in the business.

7. Stock and semi-finished/finished goods

For Section 18(1)(a) and (b), the principal stock benefit concerns inputs held in stock and inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods on the relevant date.

For Section 18(1)(c) and (d), the provision additionally permits credit on specified capital goods, subject to the statutory reduction and Rule 40 mechanism.

8. Capital goods under Section 18(1)(c) and (d)

When composition taxpayers become regular taxpayers, or an exempt supply becomes taxable, Section 18 extends the special-circumstances credit to qualifying capital goods.

The credit is not simply the full historical tax component in every case. Section 18(1) itself applies the prescribed reduction and Rule 40 provides the practical computation.

Rule 40 principle: For capital goods, the ITC is reduced by a prescribed percentage for each quarter or part thereof from the invoice date or other prescribed date, rather than treating the entire historical tax amount as immediately available.

The GST Council's 14th meeting material records the original five-percentage-point-per-quarter approach and the declaration mechanism. citeturn0search2

9. Section 18(2) — invoice-age condition

Section 18(2) provides that no credit under Section 18(1) is available in respect of any supply of goods or services or both after the expiry of one year from the date of issue of the invoice.

This is a special-circumstances time limitation and should be checked invoice-by-invoice when preparing a Section 18 claim.

Example: If an eligible stock invoice was issued more than one year before the relevant Section 18 event, the invoice cannot be brought into the special-circumstances credit merely because the goods remain in stock.

The original Council material expressly described the one-year invoice-age condition for Section 18 special circumstances. citeturn0search4

10. Section 18(3) — transfer of unutilised ITC on business reorganisation

Where there is a change in the constitution of a registered person because of sale, merger, demerger, amalgamation, lease or transfer of the business with specific provisions for transfer of liabilities, the unutilised ITC in the electronic credit ledger can be transferred to the resulting business in the prescribed manner.

The purpose is to prevent accumulated eligible credit from being stranded merely because the legal/business structure changes.

But the credit does not move automatically. The prescribed procedure under Rule 41, including the relevant declaration/acceptance process and documentary evidence, must be followed.

The GST Council's 39th meeting specifically examined doubts regarding Section 18(3) and Rule 41 in mergers, demergers, amalgamations and changes in business constitution. citeturn0search7

Rule 41 and Form GST ITC-02 are central to the transfer procedure. The recipient/transferee must accept the transferred credit and account for the corresponding inputs/capital goods as prescribed.

12. Rule 41 and Form GST ITC-02

Rule 41 prescribes the mechanism for transfer of unutilised ITC in business reorganisation. The transferor furnishes the prescribed details; the transferee accepts them electronically; and the credit is then credited to the transferee's electronic credit ledger.

For a demerger, the Rules contain a specific asset-value based allocation mechanism. This is particularly important because a demerger may transfer only one business division rather than the entire legal entity.

Documentation should include: scheme/order of merger or demerger, effective date, GST registrations, asset/liability allocation, ITC ledger, Form ITC-02, acceptance evidence and accounting records.

13. Section 18(4) — moving into composition or exemption

Section 18(4) addresses the reverse situation. Where a registered person who has availed ITC opts to pay tax under Section 10, or where the goods/services supplied by the registered person become wholly exempt, the person must pay an amount by way of reversal relating to:

  • inputs in stock;
  • inputs contained in semi-finished and finished goods in stock; and
  • capital goods or plant and machinery, subject to the prescribed calculation.

The amount attributable to stock is determined proportionately under Rule 44, while capital goods are calculated for the remaining useful life using the prescribed five-year/60-month basis.

Direction of movement matters: Section 18(1) can create credit when the business moves toward taxable/regular status; Section 18(4) reverses credit when it moves toward composition or complete exemption.

14. Section 18(5) — cancellation of registration

On cancellation of registration, the taxpayer is required to pay/reverse the prescribed amount relating to inputs in stock, inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods, and capital goods/plant and machinery on the day immediately preceding cancellation, subject to the statutory mechanism.

Rule 44 provides the practical calculation and requires reporting through the prescribed form. The GST Council's rules material states that amounts arising under Section 18(4) and Section 29(5) are reported in Form GST ITC-03 or GSTR-10 as applicable. citeturn0search24

15. Section 18(6) — disposal of capital goods or plant and machinery

When capital goods or plant and machinery on which ITC has been taken are supplied, the registered person must pay an amount determined under the statutory formula:

Broad comparison: tax on the transaction value under Section 15 or the ITC originally taken reduced by the prescribed percentage points for the period of use, whichever is higher.

The proviso gives a special treatment for refractory bricks, moulds and dies, jigs and fixtures supplied as scrap: tax may be paid on the transaction value under Section 15.

The GST Council's material reproduces this special capital-goods disposal mechanism. citeturn0search10

16. Five-year reduction concept for capital goods

For the Section 18(6) ITC-reduction comparison and the corresponding Rule 44 treatment, capital goods are generally considered over a useful life of five years / 60 months.

For example, if capital goods have been used for 30 months, the remaining useful-life component is calculated using the prescribed monthly reduction mechanism. The exact tax payable must then be compared with the tax on transaction value.

Practical control: Maintain the original invoice date, ITC amount, date of receipt/use, disposal date and remaining useful life in the fixed-asset register so that Section 18 calculations can be automated.

19. Amendment and implementation timeline

1 July 2017: Section 18 became operative with the GST regime.
2017: Rules 40, 41 and 44 established the procedures for claiming, transferring and reversing special-circumstances ITC.
2019: Business-reorganisation questions under Section 18(3)/Rule 41 led to detailed GST Council consideration and clarifications on merger/demerger allocation. citeturn0search7
2024: Rule 40 was aligned with the evolving supplier-reporting framework; GST Council material refers to verification with GSTR-1/GSTR-1A and applicable supplier returns. citeturn0search0
Current principle: Section 18 continues to operate as the special-circumstances bridge between different GST statuses, with the detailed claim/transfer/reversal procedure controlled by Rules 40, 41 and 44.

20. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — fresh registration

A business becomes liable for GST and registers. It has eligible raw materials in stock on the preceding day. Section 18(1)(a) can permit credit subject to the one-year invoice-age condition and all other ITC requirements.

Example 2 — voluntary registration

A small consultancy voluntarily registers. Eligible inputs held immediately before registration can be examined under Section 18(1)(b).

Example 3 — composition to regular

A manufacturer exits composition from 1 April. Eligible stock and qualifying capital goods on 31 March can enter the Section 18(1)(c) calculation.

Example 4 — exempt product becomes taxable

A previously exempt product becomes taxable from a notified date. Eligible stock relatable to that taxable product can qualify under Section 18(1)(d).

Example 5 — old invoice

An eligible stock item has an invoice dated more than one year before the Section 18 event. Section 18(2) prevents credit on that invoice.

Example 6 — merger

Company A merges into Company B with transfer of liabilities. A's unutilised electronic credit ledger balance can be transferred through the prescribed Rule 41/Form ITC-02 process.

Example 7 — demerger

One undertaking is demerged into a new entity. The ITC allocation must follow the prescribed asset-value based mechanism rather than simply transferring the entire electronic credit ledger.

Example 8 — exit from composition

A regular taxpayer moves into composition. Section 18(4) requires reversal/payment relating to eligible stock and capital goods immediately before the change.

Example 9 — cancellation

Registration is cancelled. The taxpayer must calculate the prescribed amount relating to stock and capital goods and report it through the applicable mechanism.

Example 10 — sale of machinery

A machine on which ITC was taken is sold after several years. Section 18(6) requires comparison between tax on transaction value and the reduced ITC amount.

24. Common mistakes

  • Claiming every invoice in opening stock without applying the one-year Section 18(2) limit.
  • Ignoring Section 17 blocked-credit restrictions while preparing a Section 18 claim.
  • Taking full ITC on capital goods under 18(1)(c)/(d) without the prescribed reduction.
  • Transferring ITC in a merger without completing Rule 41/Form ITC-02 procedures.
  • Transferring the entire credit balance in a demerger without applying the prescribed allocation mechanism.
  • Failing to reverse ITC when moving into composition or full exemption.
  • Using only book stock without reconciling physical stock at the relevant date.
  • Ignoring supplier-reporting verification for special-circumstances claims where the Rules require it.
  • Calculating Section 18(6) capital-goods disposal tax using only the original ITC amount and ignoring the transaction-value comparison.
  • Confusing Section 18 special-circumstances ITC with transitional credit under Section 140.

26. Key takeaways

  • Section 18 is the special-circumstances ITC bridge.
  • 18(1) can unlock eligible stock/capital-goods credit when GST status changes toward taxable/regular status.
  • 18(2) imposes a one-year invoice-age condition for Section 18(1) credit.
  • 18(3) allows prescribed transfer of unutilised ITC during qualifying business reorganisations.
  • 18(4)/(5) work in the opposite direction by requiring reversal when a taxpayer enters composition/exemption or cancels registration.
  • 18(6) contains the special capital-goods disposal payment mechanism.
  • Rules 40, 41 and 44 are essential for the practical implementation of Section 18.
  • Section 17 must always be applied alongside Section 18.
  • For mergers/demergers, legal documents, asset allocation and GST forms must all reconcile.
