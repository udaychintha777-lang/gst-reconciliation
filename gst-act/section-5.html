CGST ACT • CHAPTER II — ADMINISTRATION

Section 5 — Powers of Officers

Section 5 is the central administrative provision dealing with the exercise of powers and discharge of duties by Central Tax officers. It also provides for exercise of subordinate officers' functions by superior officers, delegation of the Commissioner's powers and a specific restriction on Appellate Authorities.

Section 5 at a Glance

ProvisionSection 5
ChapterChapter II — Administration
SubjectPowers of Officers

Section 5 should not be read as a blanket grant of every GST power to every Central Tax officer. It operates subject to conditions and limitations, and the particular statutory provision, jurisdiction, assignment and delegation applicable to the action must also be examined.

Core principle: Being a Central Tax officer and having power to exercise a particular statutory function are related but distinct questions.

1. What Does Section 5 Do?

Section 5 establishes the operating framework for Central Tax officers. It answers four important administrative questions:

  • What powers and duties can a Central Tax officer exercise?
  • Can a superior officer exercise powers or duties assigned to a subordinate officer?
  • Can the Commissioner delegate powers to a subordinate officer?
  • Can an Appellate Authority exercise the powers and duties of another officer?

The first three questions are answered by sub-sections (1) to (3). Sub-section (4) creates an express restriction concerning Appellate Authorities.

2. Section 5(1) — Exercise of Powers by a Central Tax Officer

Section 5(1) provides that, subject to the conditions and limitations imposed by the Board, an officer of Central Tax may exercise the powers and discharge the duties conferred or imposed on that officer under the CGST Act.

This means the provision is not an unrestricted power clause. The officer's action must still be traceable to a statutory power or duty, and any conditions or limitations imposed under the legal framework must be respected.

Practical example: If a Central Tax officer is assigned a particular statutory function under the applicable notification, the officer may exercise that function subject to the conditions and limitations governing it. The designation alone is not enough to establish unlimited authority.

3. Section 5(2) — Superior Officer Exercising Subordinate Officer's Powers

Section 5(2) provides that an officer of Central Tax may exercise the powers and discharge the duties conferred or imposed on any other Central Tax officer who is subordinate to him.

The provision facilitates administrative continuity. A higher-ranking officer does not have to remain limited to only those functions that are separately assigned to the higher designation when the Act permits exercise of a subordinate officer's powers.

However, the statutory language is not a substitute for checking the specific provision and applicable conditions. Where a special provision creates a separate authority, appellate structure or exclusive function, that special statutory framework must be respected.

Practical example: A senior Central Tax officer may exercise a function assigned to a subordinate officer, provided the function falls within the statutory framework and there is no contrary restriction.

4. Section 5(3) — Delegation by the Commissioner

Section 5(3) allows the Commissioner, subject to conditions and limitations specified by the Commissioner, to delegate the Commissioner's powers to another officer who is subordinate to the Commissioner.

This is a specific statutory delegation mechanism. It should not be confused with a general assumption that every officer can transfer or delegate powers at will.

Delegation checklist: Identify the original statutory power, the Commissioner who possesses it, the subordinate officer receiving the delegation, the order or notification containing the delegation, its conditions and the period or scope for which it operates.

5. Section 5(4) — Restriction on Appellate Authority

Section 5(4) contains an important safeguard: notwithstanding the other provisions of Section 5, an Appellate Authority cannot exercise the powers and discharge the duties conferred or imposed on another officer of Central Tax.

This preserves the distinction between original administrative/adjudication functions and appellate functions. An appellate authority is not intended to simply step into the shoes of the original officer and exercise that officer's powers under Section 5.

Practical example: If an authority is acting as an Appellate Authority in a GST appeal, Section 5(4) prevents the authority from relying on Section 5 merely to assume the ordinary powers and duties of another Central Tax officer.

6. Section 5 and the Difference Between Power, Jurisdiction and Proper Officer

These concepts are frequently mixed up in GST correspondence. They should be separated.

ConceptMeaning in practical analysis
AppointmentWhether the person is appointed as an officer under the CGST administrative framework.
JurisdictionThe territorial, taxpayer-specific or specialised jurisdiction assigned to the officer/formation.
PowerThe statutory authority to perform a particular act under a particular section.
Proper OfficerThe officer assigned the particular function under the statutory framework, as defined in Section 2(91) and relevant assignment instruments.
DelegationTransfer/authorisation of a power from the Commissioner to a subordinate officer under the permitted framework.

A valid challenge or defence therefore needs to identify the exact statutory source of the officer's power instead of relying only on the officer's designation.

7. Commencement of Section 5

Section 5 was brought into force from 22 June 2017 through Notification No. 01/2017-Central Tax dated 19 June 2017. The commencement notification covered Sections 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 22 to 30, 139, 146 and 164 from 22 June 2017. citeturn1search1

The administrative provisions were therefore operational before the principal GST levy, compliance and procedural provisions commenced from 1 July 2017.

8. Important Notifications Under Section 5

Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax — 19 June 2017

This notification established the initial jurisdiction framework for Central Tax officers. It is important when analysing whether a particular Central Tax formation had jurisdiction over a taxpayer or a specified territorial area.

Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax — 1 July 2017

This notification assigned jurisdiction and powers to officers of various directorates. It illustrates how Section 5 operates through the wider administrative framework rather than granting an identical set of powers to every officer.

Notification No. 79/2018-Central Tax — 31 December 2018

This is a particularly important example of Section 5(1) being used operationally. The notification amended Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax and provided that specified Central Tax officers and officers subordinate to them could exercise powers under Sections 73, 74, 75 and 76 throughout the corresponding territorial jurisdiction in cases assigned by the Board. citeturn1search6

This demonstrates that Section 5(1) can be used as the statutory basis for defining how particular powers are exercised across an assigned jurisdiction.

Other subsequent notifications

The administrative framework under Section 5 has been supplemented and modified through subsequent notifications concerning jurisdiction, proper officers and assignment of functions. For historical analysis, the notification applicable on the relevant date should be checked rather than relying only on the current jurisdiction structure.

9. Amendments to Section 5 — Has the Section Itself Changed?

The current statutory text of Section 5 continues to contain four sub-sections dealing with the same four subjects: officer powers, exercise of subordinate officers' powers, delegation by the Commissioner and the restriction on Appellate Authorities. The point-in-time statutory record also shows the same core text from the commencement date. citeturn1search8turn1search9

Accordingly, the major developments relevant to Section 5 have been through the exercise and assignment of powers under the section, particularly notifications concerning jurisdiction and proper-officer functions, rather than a major rewrite of the operative text of Section 5.

Research caution: A notification issued “in exercise of the powers conferred by section 5” is not automatically an amendment to Section 5. It may instead amend a jurisdiction or assignment notification made under the power contained in Section 5(1).

10. GST Council and Legislative Background

The development of Section 5 can be traced through the early GST Council drafting process. The Council's records show that the Model GST Law contained provisions concerning powers and jurisdiction of officers, and the Law Committee worked on reconciling provisions relating to Sections 4 and 5 during the drafting stage. citeturn0search2turn0search11

The 15th GST Council meeting materials also specifically recorded that Sections 3, 4 and 5 of the CGST Act needed to be notified so that officers could be appointed and the Government could reorganise its field formations before the appointed date. citeturn0search3

This background is useful for understanding why Section 5 was brought into force on 22 June 2017, before the principal GST provisions became operational on 1 July 2017.

Legal distinction: GST Council discussions explain policy and legislative development. The enacted Act, notification, order or circular that has legal force must be identified before relying on a Council discussion as the legal authority for an action.

11. Circulars and Administrative Clarifications

Circular No. 3/3/2017-GST dated 5 July 2017

This circular assigned specified functions as proper officer to designated Central Tax officers. It is important when analysing whether a particular officer was authorised to perform a particular GST function. The circular therefore complements the appointment and power framework under Sections 3 to 5.

Circular No. 2/2/2017-GST dated 4 July 2017

This early circular is part of the initial administrative framework for implementation of the GST law and is relevant when tracing how functions were assigned at the beginning of the GST regime.

Later proper-officer circulars

CBIC has issued later circulars modifying or supplementing proper-officer assignments. Therefore, for a notice or order relating to a particular period, the relevant circular and assignment in force on that date should be examined.

12. Section 5 and Section 167 — Do Not Confuse Them

Section 5 and Section 167 both deal with administrative exercise of powers, but they should not be treated as identical.

ProvisionFocus
Section 5(1)Central Tax officer exercising powers/duties subject to Board conditions and limitations.
Section 5(2)Superior Central Tax officer exercising powers/duties of a subordinate officer.
Section 5(3)Commissioner delegating powers to a subordinate officer.
Section 5(4)Restriction preventing an Appellate Authority from exercising another officer's powers/duties.
Section 167Commissioner's notification-based delegation mechanism for powers exercisable by an authority or officer to another specified authority/officer.

For a particular delegation question, the exact statutory provision relied upon should therefore be identified rather than using Section 5 as a generic reference.

13. Practical Examples

Example 1 — Officer issues an assessment order

A taxpayer receives an order under a demand provision. The first question should not simply be “Is the officer a Central Tax officer?” The team should identify the statutory section, the proper-officer assignment, jurisdiction and any applicable delegation.

Example 2 — Senior officer exercises subordinate officer's power

A Commissioner exercises a function otherwise assigned to a subordinate Central Tax officer. Section 5(2) provides the statutory basis for a superior officer to exercise subordinate officer powers, subject to any special statutory restrictions.

Example 3 — Commissioner delegates a power

A Commissioner delegates a specified power to a subordinate officer. The validity analysis should examine the delegation order, the statutory power originally held by the Commissioner, the identity of the subordinate officer and the conditions imposed by the delegation.

Example 4 — Appellate Authority

An authority hearing an appeal cannot rely on Section 5 to assume the ordinary powers and duties of another Central Tax officer. Section 5(4) expressly preserves the separation.

Example 5 — Cross-jurisdiction demand proceedings

A taxpayer registered in one Commissionerate receives a demand-related proceeding from an officer working in another formation. The team should examine the applicable jurisdiction and the notification under which the officer has the relevant power. Notification No. 79/2018-Central Tax is an example of how the Board assigned specified demand powers across corresponding territorial jurisdictions for cases assigned by the Board. citeturn1search6

Example 6 — Historical 2018 matter

For a dispute relating to a 2018 tax period, the taxpayer should identify the jurisdiction and power-assignment notifications actually in force at that time. The current Commissionerate structure should not automatically be applied retrospectively.

Example 7 — Audit file documentation

If management receives a GST notice, the tax team can strengthen the file by retaining the notice, the officer's designation, jurisdiction details, relevant proper-officer circular, delegation/assignment order and the statutory provision invoked. This creates a clear authority trail for the auditors or legal team.

14. Professional Checklist for Management, Tax Team and Auditors

  1. Identify the officer who has issued or signed the communication/order.
  2. Identify the exact statutory provision under which the action is taken.
  3. Confirm the officer's appointment and Central Tax jurisdiction.
  4. Check the applicable proper-officer/function assignment.
  5. Check whether the power is being exercised directly, through Section 5(2), or through a delegation under Section 5(3) or another applicable provision.
  6. Check conditions and limitations attached to the power.
  7. For historical proceedings, obtain the notification/circular/order applicable on the relevant date.
  8. Check whether the officer is acting as an original authority or an Appellate Authority.
  9. Retain the legal authority trail in the tax/audit file.

15. Statutory Text of Section 5

The provision is reproduced below for ease of reference:

5. Powers of officers.—

(1) Subject to such conditions and limitations as the Board may impose, an officer of
central tax may exercise the powers and discharge the duties conferred or imposed on him
under this Act.

(2) An officer of central tax may exercise the powers and discharge the duties conferred
or imposed under this Act on any other officer of central tax who is subordinate to him.

(3) The Commissioner may, subject to such conditions and limitations as may be specified
in this behalf by him, delegate his powers to any other officer who is subordinate to him.

(4) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section, an Appellate Authority shall not
exercise the powers and discharge the duties conferred or imposed on any other officer
of central tax.

The statutory text should be read with the applicable notifications, orders, circulars, jurisdiction and delegation instruments for the relevant period.

16. Key Takeaways

  • Section 5 is the principal administrative provision governing exercise of Central Tax officer powers.
  • Section 5(1) operates subject to conditions and limitations imposed by the Board.
  • Under Section 5(2), a superior Central Tax officer may exercise powers and duties of a subordinate officer.
  • Section 5(3) permits the Commissioner to delegate powers to a subordinate officer, subject to conditions and limitations.
  • Section 5(4) prevents an Appellate Authority from exercising the powers and duties of another Central Tax officer.
  • Section 5 came into force on 22 June 2017.
  • Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax and subsequent jurisdiction/function notifications are important for practical application.
  • Notification No. 79/2018-Central Tax is a significant example of powers being assigned under Section 5(1).
  • Proper-officer circulars must be distinguished from amendments to the statutory text.
  • For historical proceedings, always establish the officer's authority using the legal instruments applicable on the relevant date.
