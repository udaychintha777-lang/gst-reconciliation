What is GST reconciliation?

GST reconciliation is the process of comparing purchase records in your books with supplier-reported invoice data available in GSTR-2B. It helps you identify records that agree, records that need investigation and invoices appearing on one side but not the other.

Why reconcile Purchase Register with GSTR-2B?

Differences can arise because of invoice number variations, document type differences, tax value differences, supplier filing timing or missing entries in the books. A structured reconciliation makes these exceptions easier to review before GST return and ITC decisions.

GST Reconciliation Guide: Purchase Register vs GSTR-2B

GST reconciliation is more than finding an invoice number in two Excel files. A useful GSTR-2B reconciliation process should show what matched, what partially matched, what is missing from GSTR-2B, what appears in GSTR-2B but not in the Purchase Register, and which records need manual review. This tool is designed around that practical monthly workflow.

Why businesses reconcile GSTR-2B

Purchase Register data comes from the books or ERP, while GSTR-2B provides the GST portal-side view of reported documents. Comparing both helps the accounts and tax team identify invoice omissions, supplier reporting differences, document-number variations, tax-value differences and records that need follow-up.

How the reconciliation works

The engine first looks for a strong GSTIN + document type + normalized document number relationship. It then checks taxable value, GST components and date within the selected tolerance/window. Less certain relationships are kept in Partial Matching or Review Required instead of being silently treated as exact matches.

Built for monthly GST working

Use the result categories to move from a large raw Excel file to a focused exception list. Matched records can be separated from invoices that need supplier follow-up, book verification, duplicate checking or document-level review.

Common GSTR-2B reconciliation differences

Invoice number variation

Spacing, punctuation, case or leading zeros can make two references look different even when they represent the same document. The tool normalizes common formatting differences.

Taxable value difference

The invoice may be identified, but taxable values may differ between the books and portal-side data. Such records are placed in Partial Matching when the relationship is sufficiently strong.

GST component difference

IGST, CGST, SGST or Cess may differ. The report shows the difference so the reviewer can compare the source invoice and accounting entry.

Invoice in books but not in 2B

This can require checking supplier reporting, the relevant GSTR-2B period, document details and the accounting period. The tool does not assume the reason.

Invoice in 2B but not in books

Verify whether the purchase has been recorded, whether it belongs to the business and period under review, and whether the document is a duplicate or other exception.

Duplicate records

Repeated GSTIN + document references can create allocation issues. The reconciliation engine prevents the same GSTR-2B row from being used for multiple Purchase Register rows.

How to Use

Follow these steps every time you perform a reconciliation.

1. Download the Master Excel Template

Use the Download Excel Format button if you want a standard workbook. It contains separate Purchase Register and GSTR 2B sheets with the recommended headings.

2. Let the tool identify document-type differences

Invoice, Credit Note and Debit Note are reconciled separately. If the same reference appears with a different document type, the tool flags the record for review instead of silently treating it as a match.

3. Preserve the source data

Upload your source workbook without manually changing signs or invoice numbers just to satisfy the tool. The reconciliation engine reads the values and reports data-quality issues separately.

4. Upload the Purchase Register

Upload .xlsx, .xls or .csv. If an Excel file contains multiple sheets, the tool identifies the strongest Purchase Register table automatically.

Upload the GSTR-2B workbook/CSV and select the correct sheet. The tool identifies the relevant columns automatically and shows them for confirmation.

7. Run Reconciliation

The tool validates the data and then compares records using GSTIN, document type, invoice number and tax values. Close invoice-number variations can be considered in the second matching pass.

8. Review before relying on the result

Matched records should still be reviewed against the source documents and your accounting/tax records. A reconciliation result is a working tool output and does not itself constitute a GST filing decision.

GST Reconciliation Tool – Frequently Asked Questions

What is a GST Reconciliation Tool?

A GST Reconciliation Tool compares purchase records with GST portal data such as GSTR-2B and identifies matched, unmatched and differing invoice records. It reduces manual invoice-by-invoice comparison and produces a structured reconciliation result for review.

How do I reconcile my Purchase Register with GSTR-2B?

You have two options: upload the original GSTR-2B workbook downloaded from the GST Portal for automatic sheet detection, or download the Excel template and enter data into its Purchase Register and GSTR 2B sheets. Upload the files, check the detected columns, choose a sheet if the workbook contains multiple sheets, run reconciliation and review the matched and exception categories.

What is the difference between Purchase Register and GSTR-2B?

The Purchase Register represents purchase transactions recorded in your books, while GSTR-2B contains the static, auto-drafted input tax credit information made available from supplier filings and other GST data for the relevant period. Reconciliation compares the two datasets to identify differences.

What does “Not in GSTR-2B” mean?

It means a Purchase Register record did not have a corresponding record identified in the uploaded GSTR-2B data. The reason may require investigation, such as supplier filing timing, invoice details or differences between the two datasets.

What does “Not in Purchase Register” mean?

It means a GSTR-2B record did not have a corresponding Purchase Register record identified by the reconciliation process. The item should be reviewed against the books and source documents.

What is a partial match in GST reconciliation?

A partial match indicates that a related invoice has been identified but one or more compared details differ or require review. The exact result should be checked against the source documents before any accounting or ITC decision.

Can I download the GST reconciliation report in Excel?

Yes. After reconciliation, the tool provides an Excel export containing the reconciliation categories and invoice-level comparison information, including Purchase Register and GSTR-2B values.

Is this GST reconciliation tool free?

This reconciliation tool is provided as a free browser-based utility. You can use the available upload, reconciliation and Excel export functions without changing the existing reconciliation workflow.

Can I use Excel for GSTR-2B reconciliation?

Yes. The tool is designed around workbook-based reconciliation and provides Excel template and export functionality. Always use the required input structure and keep the original source files unchanged.

Does the tool replace professional GST review?

No. Reconciliation software helps identify data relationships and exceptions. Taxability, eligibility of ITC, reversals, blocked credits and filing decisions should be independently reviewed under the applicable GST provisions and source documents.

What is the difference between Matched and Partial Matching?

Matched means the reconciliation rules found a strong relationship and the compared values are within the configured tolerance. Partial Matching means a related invoice was identified, but one or more fields such as invoice reference, taxable value, GST amount or date require review.

Why is an invoice shown as Not in 2B?

It means the Purchase Register record did not receive a sufficiently reliable corresponding record from the uploaded GSTR-2B data. Check the supplier GSTIN, invoice number, document type, period and supplier reporting before concluding why it is missing.

Why is an invoice shown as Not in Purchase Register?

It means the uploaded GSTR-2B record did not receive a corresponding Purchase Register record. Review the books, purchase accounting, period and duplicate possibilities.

Can I upload the same Excel template in both upload boxes?

Yes. The downloadable template contains separate Purchase Register and GSTR 2B sheets. You may upload the same workbook in both upload boxes; when more than one sheet is present, the tool lets you select which sheet should be used for that side of the reconciliation.

What happens if I replace an uploaded file?

The previous data for that file is cleared so that the new upload does not accidentally remain in the next reconciliation.

Does the tool read B2B, B2BA, CDNR and CDNRA from GSTR-2B?

For a GST Portal workbook, the tool reads all transaction/detail sheets automatically, including B2B/B2BA, debit and credit notes, ECO, ISD, import tables and reversal/rejected tables. Summary sheets are not double-counted as transactions.

Can I correct the detected column mapping?

Yes. After a file is read, the Field Mapping section shows the detected columns. You can change a field to another worksheet column before running reconciliation.

Is the Excel report only a list of matched invoices?

No. The professional report includes an Executive Summary, All Reconciliation, Matched, Partial Matching, Not in 2B, Not in Purchase Register, Review Required, Duplicate, Vendor Summary, Exceptions, Data Quality and Match Logic sheets when those categories contain data.

Does a Partial Matching result mean ITC must be rejected?

No. Partial Matching is a reconciliation classification, not a legal conclusion on ITC eligibility. The underlying invoice, tax treatment, applicable GST provisions and other records should be reviewed separately.
