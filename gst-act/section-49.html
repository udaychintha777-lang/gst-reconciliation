GST ACT — SECTION 49

Section 49 CGST Act — GST Payment, Cash Ledger, Credit Ledger & ITC Utilisation

Section 49 explains the basic machinery for paying GST. In simple words: where does your money go, how does your Input Tax Credit get used, what can be paid through the cash ledger, what can be paid through the credit ledger, and how do IGST, CGST and SGST/UTGST credits get utilised?

1. Why Section 49 matters

If you search “Section 49 CGST Act”, “how to pay GST”, “GST cash ledger”, “electronic credit ledger”, “how to utilise ITC”, “can ITC pay interest”, “can ITC pay reverse charge”, or “IGST credit utilisation order”, you are essentially asking how GST payment works after the tax liability is calculated.

Section 49 is the payment section. It connects the taxpayer's liability with the money deposited in the Electronic Cash Ledger and the eligible ITC appearing in the Electronic Credit Ledger.

The easiest way to remember Section 49:
Tax liability is created → money/ITC is available in the appropriate ledger → the taxpayer uses the permitted ledger → the liability is discharged.

2. Section 49 in very simple language

Imagine your GST Portal has three digital pockets:

💰 Cash Ledger

This is like money you have deposited into your GST account. You put money into it through a challan. It can be used for tax, interest, penalty, late fee and other permitted amounts.

🧾 Credit Ledger

This is like a GST credit balance earned from eligible purchases. It is commonly called ITC. It can generally be used to pay output tax, subject to the utilisation rules.

📋 Liability Register

This is the bill showing what you owe. When you pay or set off a liability correctly, the outstanding amount comes down.

⚠️ They are not interchangeable

Money in the cash ledger is not the same thing as ITC in the credit ledger. ITC also cannot normally be used to pay interest, late fee or penalty.

The GST Portal itself explains that the cash ledger records deposits and payments, while ITC is maintained separately in the electronic credit ledger. citeturn0search1turn0search2

4. Section 49 statutory structure

Section 49 is long because it covers much more than simply “pay GST”. It deals with depositing money, using the cash ledger, using ITC, the order of ITC utilisation, the date of deposit, transfer of cash balances and restrictions on using credit.

PartEasy meaning
49(1)How amounts are deposited into the cash ledger.
49(2)Money in the cash ledger is used through the prescribed process to pay liability.
49(3)Money in the cash ledger can be used for specified liabilities.
49(4)ITC in the credit ledger can be used for output tax, subject to the law.
49(5)Sets the basic heads and restrictions for ITC utilisation.
49(6)Remaining balances can be used/refunded subject to the Act.
49(7)Interest may arise where an amount deposited is insufficient for the liability.
49(8)Tax collected/paid is subject to the statutory rules.
49(9)Passing-on-of-tax principle for the purposes of the Act.
49(10)Permits transfer of certain cash-ledger balances in the prescribed manner.
49(11)Transferred amounts are treated as deposited in the relevant ledger.
49(12)Government can prescribe a maximum proportion of output tax that a class of taxpayers may discharge through the credit ledger.

The current statutory text also contains the 49(10) cash-ledger transfer mechanism and 49(12) enabling power. citeturn0search4

5. Depositing money into GST

If you need to pay GST in cash, you normally create a FORM GST PMT-06 challan. After the bank successfully credits the Government account and the transaction is communicated to the GST system, the amount is reflected in the electronic cash ledger.

Rule 87 provides the cash-ledger mechanism and PMT-06 challan procedure. The rule permits prescribed electronic banking/payment modes and certain other modes subject to conditions. citeturn0search0turn0search5

Bank payment → Government account → CIN/payment confirmation → Electronic Cash Ledger → Set-off against liability

6. Electronic Cash Ledger

The cash ledger is best understood as a prepaid GST wallet. You deposit money first and then use that balance to discharge the relevant liability.

It is maintained under FORM GST PMT-05. The balance is maintained under major heads such as IGST, CGST, SGST/UTGST and cess, with minor heads such as tax, interest, penalty, fee and others. citeturn0search0turn0search1

Very important: Putting ₹1 lakh into the cash ledger does not by itself mean ₹1 lakh of tax has been paid. The amount must be appropriately debited against a liability through the return/payment process.

7. Electronic Credit Ledger

The electronic credit ledger contains eligible ITC. It is maintained in FORM GST PMT-02.

For example, if you have eligible CGST ITC of ₹50,000, that ₹50,000 is not cash sitting in your bank account. It is a tax credit that can be used only in the manner permitted by the GST law.

The GST Portal explains that ITC can be utilised only against permitted tax liabilities and cannot be used for interest, fee or penalty. citeturn0search2

8. Electronic Liability Register

The electronic liability register records the taxpayer's outstanding GST liabilities. Think of it as the GST Portal's “amount you owe” register.

When tax is declared in the return, when interest or late fee becomes payable, or when a demand is created, the relevant liability is reflected in the prescribed electronic records. Payment/adjustment reduces the relevant outstanding amount.

9. What ITC can actually pay

This is where many people get confused.

ITC is not a general-purpose GST wallet. It is mainly used to pay eligible output tax, subject to the utilisation order.

LiabilityCan ITC generally be used?
Output IGSTYes, subject to utilisation rules.
Output CGSTYes, subject to utilisation rules.
Output SGST/UTGSTYes, subject to utilisation rules.
Reverse-charge taxNo. It is paid through cash/appropriate permitted balance, not ITC.
InterestNo. Cash ledger is required.
Late feeNo. Cash ledger is required.
PenaltyNo. Cash ledger is required.

The GST Portal's GSTR-3B guidance specifically states that reverse-charge liabilities and late fee/interest are paid through the cash ledger rather than ITC. citeturn0search3

10. Order of ITC utilisation

GST does not allow a taxpayer to use every credit against every tax head in any order. The utilisation rules exist to control the flow of IGST, CGST and SGST/UTGST credit.

Simple rule to remember: Use available IGST credit first. After IGST credit is exhausted as required, CGST/SGST/UTGST credits can be used according to the prescribed order.

Section 49(5), Section 49A, Section 49B and Rule 88A have to be read together. The GST Portal describes the current utilisation pattern accordingly. citeturn0search2turn2search1

11. IGST credit

IGST credit is the most flexible of the three main GST credits.

  • First use IGST credit against IGST liability.
  • After the required IGST utilisation, remaining IGST credit can be used for CGST and SGST/UTGST in the permitted manner.

Rule 88A permits the remaining IGST credit to be used towards CGST and SGST/UTGST liabilities in any order and proportion, subject to the statutory framework.

12. CGST credit

CGST ITC cannot be used to pay SGST/UTGST.

After the required IGST credit utilisation, CGST ITC can be used for:

  1. CGST liability first; and
  2. remaining CGST credit can be used towards IGST liability.

This is why CGST credit cannot simply be transferred to SGST.

13. SGST/UTGST credit

SGST/UTGST ITC cannot be used to pay CGST.

After the required IGST credit utilisation, SGST/UTGST credit is used for its own tax head first and may then be used for IGST in the prescribed circumstances.

Easy memory rule: CGST and SGST/UTGST cannot directly pay each other. IGST is the bridge between the heads.

14. Reverse charge

Suppose you receive a service on which GST is payable by you under reverse charge. You may have plenty of ITC available, but that does not mean you can use that ITC to pay the reverse-charge tax.

The reverse-charge tax must be paid through the appropriate cash payment mechanism. Once the reverse-charge tax is paid and other conditions are satisfied, the resulting ITC may become available separately according to the law.

15. Interest, late fee & penalty

Section 49 works with several other provisions because not every GST amount can be paid from ITC.

16. Why cash is needed for interest and fee

Imagine you have ₹2 lakh of ITC and ₹20,000 of interest. You cannot simply tell the GST Portal, “Use ₹20,000 from my ITC.” The credit ledger is restricted to permitted tax utilisation. Interest, late fee and penalty generally need payment through the cash ledger.

This distinction is particularly important when preparing GSTR-3B because a taxpayer may have enough ITC to cover output tax but still need to deposit additional cash for interest and late fee. citeturn0search3

17. TDS/TCS credits

Amounts credited through GST TDS/TCS mechanisms are reflected in the electronic cash ledger framework. They are cash-type credits rather than ITC.

The taxpayer should reconcile such credits with the relevant statements and the cash ledger before filing returns.

18. Transfer between cash ledgers

Section 49(10), as amended, permits a registered person to transfer certain amounts available in the electronic cash ledger to another major head or, subject to conditions, to the cash ledger of a distinct person.

This is useful where money was deposited under the wrong major head or where permitted movement is required between registrations. The transfer is treated as a refund from the original cash ledger for statutory purposes and is subject to prescribed conditions. citeturn0search4

Important: Cash-ledger transfer is not the same as ITC transfer. ITC follows separate rules and cannot be moved simply because the taxpayer wants to change the tax head.

19. Cash ledger refund

If excess money remains in the electronic cash ledger, the taxpayer may generally leave it there for future liabilities or seek refund subject to the applicable procedure.

The GST Portal confirms that excess cash deposited inadvertently can remain in the ledger and may be used later, or a refund can be claimed through the prescribed mechanism. citeturn0search1

20. Date of payment

Section 49 contains an important rule about when a deposit is treated as made. The Act provides that the date of credit to the Government account in the authorised bank is deemed to be the date of deposit in the electronic cash ledger.

This becomes relevant when a taxpayer pays close to a statutory due date and there is a delay in bank confirmation. Keep the challan, CIN and bank transaction evidence.

21. Output-tax cash/credit restrictions

Section 49(12) gives the Government power, on the recommendation of the GST Council and subject to prescribed conditions, to specify a maximum proportion of output tax that a registered person or class of persons may discharge through the electronic credit ledger.

This means the law can require a certain minimum portion to be paid in cash for specified taxpayers or situations. The provision is an enabling power and should not be confused with a universal rule that applies to every taxpayer at all times. citeturn0search4

22. Section 49A

Section 49A was introduced to ensure that IGST credit is used first before CGST, SGST or UTGST credit is used towards the relevant tax liabilities.

In simple terms, if you have IGST credit available, you cannot freely skip it and immediately use CGST/SGST credit where the law requires the IGST credit to be exhausted first.

Section 49A has operated from 1 February 2019 and works together with Rule 88A. citeturn0search4turn2search0

23. Section 49B

Section 49B gives the Government the power to prescribe the detailed order and manner in which IGST, CGST, SGST and UTGST ITC is utilised.

So, think of the provisions this way:

Section 49
Main payment and ledger framework.
Section 49A
IGST credit must be utilised first in the relevant cases.
Section 49B
Government's enabling power to prescribe the utilisation order.
Rule 88A
Operationalises the remaining IGST-credit utilisation mechanism.

Section 49B is currently in force and expressly gives the Government this prescribing power. citeturn2search1

24. Rule 88A

Rule 88A is the practical rule taxpayers use when applying the IGST-credit utilisation framework. After IGST liability is addressed, remaining IGST credit can be used towards CGST and SGST/UTGST liabilities in the permitted order/proportion.

This is why a practical ITC set-off calculation should never look only at “total ITC”. It must look at IGST ITC, CGST ITC and SGST/UTGST ITC separately.

25. Circular 98/17/2019-GST

Circular No. 98/17/2019-GST dated 23 April 2019 is an important reference for the utilisation order after the introduction of Section 49A and Rule 88A.

In practical terms, it clarifies the order in which IGST, CGST and SGST/UTGST credit should be used and prevents direct CGST-to-SGST or SGST-to-CGST cross-utilisation. citeturn2search0turn2search25

26. Easy practical examples

Example 1 — Output CGST and SGST

You have output CGST ₹1,00,000 and output SGST ₹1,00,000. You have eligible CGST ITC ₹70,000 and SGST ITC ₹60,000. CGST ITC can pay CGST; SGST ITC can pay SGST. The remaining liability is paid in cash, subject to the full utilisation rules.

Example 2 — IGST credit

You have IGST ITC ₹1,50,000 and output IGST ₹50,000, CGST ₹50,000 and SGST ₹50,000. First deal with IGST liability. The remaining IGST credit can then be used against CGST and SGST/UTGST in the permitted manner.

Example 3 — CGST cannot pay SGST

You have ₹1 lakh CGST ITC but ₹1 lakh SGST liability. You cannot simply transfer CGST ITC to SGST. The remaining SGST liability needs SGST/IGST credit or cash as legally permitted.

Example 4 — Interest

You have ₹2 lakh ITC but ₹15,000 interest. The ₹15,000 interest cannot normally be paid from ITC; cash payment is required.

Example 5 — Late fee

A taxpayer has no output tax but has ₹2,000 late fee. Having ITC does not eliminate the need for cash payment of the late fee.

Example 6 — Reverse charge

A company has ₹10 lakh ITC but ₹1 lakh RCM liability. The RCM tax must be paid through the appropriate cash mechanism; the ITC cannot be used directly to discharge the RCM tax.

Example 7 — Excess cash deposit

A taxpayer deposits ₹5 lakh but only ₹3 lakh is required. The remaining ₹2 lakh can remain in the cash ledger for future liabilities or may be refundable subject to the applicable procedure.

Example 8 — Wrong cash head

Money is deposited under the wrong major/minor head. The taxpayer should examine the cash-ledger transfer/refund mechanism instead of assuming the balance can be freely used under another head.

Example 9 — Construction company

A contractor has CGST ITC, SGST ITC, IGST ITC and large RCM liabilities. The tax team prepares a head-wise utilisation sheet before filing rather than using total ITC as one number.

Example 10 — Multiple GSTINs

A company has excess cash in one GSTIN and a liability in another. Section 49(10) permits specified cash-ledger transfers to distinct persons subject to conditions; it is not an automatic transfer of ITC.

27. Construction and infrastructure examples

Section 49 is especially important for contractors because their GST payment may involve large ITC balances, RCM on specified procurements, subcontractor invoices, project-wise GSTINs and significant interest/late-fee exposure.

28. Accounting and reconciliation

A finance team should reconcile three things every month:

Books
GST payable, ITC receivable and payments recorded in accounting software.
GST Portal
Cash ledger, credit ledger and liability register.
Returns
GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and other applicable returns.
Bank
Challans, CINs and actual bank payments.

A strong reconciliation should explain every difference. For example, a cash ledger balance is not necessarily a book liability payment until it has been appropriately utilised against a liability.

34. Common mistakes

  • Treating cash and ITC as the same thing.
  • Using ITC to pay interest or late fee.
  • Using ITC to pay reverse-charge tax.
  • Using CGST ITC to pay SGST/UTGST.
  • Using SGST/UTGST ITC to pay CGST.
  • Ignoring the requirement to use IGST credit first where applicable.
  • Looking only at total ITC instead of tax-head-wise ITC.
  • Assuming a cash-ledger deposit itself equals payment of a liability.
  • Mixing cash balances of different GSTINs without checking the Section 49(10) mechanism.
  • Ignoring interest/late fee because sufficient ITC is available.
  • Not reconciling PMT-05, PMT-02 and PMT-01 with the books.

35. Section 49 GST FAQs

What is Section 49 of the CGST Act?

Section 49 explains how GST, interest, penalty, fee and other amounts are paid and how the electronic cash and credit ledgers are used.

What is an electronic cash ledger?

It is the GST Portal record of money deposited by or for the taxpayer for paying GST and other permitted amounts.

What is an electronic credit ledger?

It is the GST Portal record of eligible input tax credit available to the registered person.

Can ITC be used to pay GST interest?

No. Interest is generally paid through the electronic cash ledger.

Can ITC be used to pay GST late fee?

No. Late fee is paid through the cash ledger.

Can ITC be used to pay penalty?

No. Penalty is paid through the cash ledger.

Can ITC be used to pay reverse charge?

No. Reverse-charge tax is not paid by utilising the electronic credit ledger; the tax must be paid through the appropriate cash mechanism.

Can CGST credit be used for SGST?

No. CGST ITC cannot be used to pay SGST/UTGST liability.

Can SGST credit be used for CGST?

No. SGST/UTGST ITC cannot be used to pay CGST liability.

Which ITC should be used first?

IGST credit has priority under Section 49A and the related utilisation rules. The remaining utilisation follows Section 49, Section 49B and Rule 88A.

Can IGST credit pay CGST and SGST?

Yes, after the required IGST utilisation, remaining IGST credit can be used for CGST and SGST/UTGST in the permitted manner.

What is PMT-05?

PMT-05 is the electronic cash ledger.

What is PMT-02?

PMT-02 is the electronic credit ledger.

What is PMT-06?

PMT-06 is the challan used to deposit money into the GST payment system.

Is money deposited in the cash ledger automatically treated as tax paid?

No. The balance must be appropriately debited against the relevant liability.

Can excess cash remain in the cash ledger?

Yes. It can generally be retained for future liabilities or may be claimed as refund subject to the applicable provisions.

Can cash be transferred between GSTINs?

Section 49(10) permits specified cash-ledger transfers to a distinct person subject to prescribed conditions. This is different from transfer of ITC.

What is Section 49A?

Section 49A requires the available IGST credit to be utilised first in the relevant cases before CGST/SGST/UTGST credit is used.

What is Section 49B?

Section 49B empowers the Government to prescribe the order and manner of ITC utilisation.

What is Rule 88A?

Rule 88A provides the operational order for using remaining IGST credit towards CGST and SGST/UTGST liabilities.

37. Key takeaways

  • Section 49 is the main GST payment and ledger provision.
  • Cash Ledger = actual money deposited; Credit Ledger = eligible ITC.
  • ITC is primarily used for permitted output-tax liabilities.
  • Interest, late fee and penalty generally require cash payment.
  • Reverse-charge tax must be paid through the appropriate cash mechanism and cannot be discharged directly from ITC.
  • CGST cannot be used to pay SGST/UTGST, and SGST/UTGST cannot be used to pay CGST.
  • IGST credit has priority under Section 49A.
  • Section 49B and Rule 88A provide the detailed ITC utilisation mechanism.
  • Never calculate GST set-off using only “total ITC”; always work tax-head-wise.
  • For large businesses, monthly reconciliation of books, returns, cash ledger, credit ledger and liability register is essential.
