GST E-Invoicing for RCM from 1 January 2027: Complete Practical Guide

A major compliance change is scheduled from 1 January 2027. E-invoicing is being extended to specified recipient-generated RCM documents for domestic supplies from unregistered persons and for import of services. This guide explains what changes, who should prepare and how to operationalise the process.

Quick answer: what changes from 1 January 2027?

The e-invoicing framework is being extended to cover two additional transaction categories: (1) supplies received from an unregistered person where the recipient is liable to pay GST under reverse charge, and (2) import of services.

Domestic RCM
Recipient-generated self-invoice for qualifying supplies from an unregistered supplier.
Import of services
Recipient-side invoice/document for qualifying imported services liable to RCM.
IRN reporting
Applicable covered documents must be reported through an authorised Invoice Registration Portal, subject to the existing e-invoice framework.
Important: This is an extension of e-invoice reporting to additional document categories. It does not, by itself, create a new RCM levy, change the applicable GST rate, or make every purchase under RCM automatically subject to e-invoicing. Applicability must be tested against the amended notification and the existing threshold/exemptions.

2. Transactions covered from 1 January 2027

A. Domestic supplies received from unregistered persons under RCM

Where a registered recipient receives goods or services (or both) from an unregistered supplier and the recipient is liable to pay GST under reverse charge, the recipient is required to issue the prescribed self-invoice under the applicable GST provisions. From 1 January 2027, the covered self-invoice is brought within the e-invoicing framework for taxpayers to whom the e-invoice mandate applies.

Example – domestic unregistered supplierA company receives a taxable service from an unregistered individual and the transaction is specifically covered by a reverse-charge entry. The company determines the RCM liability, issues the required self-invoice and, where it is within the e-invoice mandate, reports the document to an authorised IRP under the RCMDOM category.

B. Import of services

Import of services is expressly added as a separate category. Where a recipient in India receives a service from a supplier located outside India and the transaction qualifies as an import of service on which the Indian recipient is liable to pay GST under RCM, the recipient-side document is brought into the e-invoice reporting framework for covered taxpayers.

Example – overseas consultancyAn Indian company receives management consultancy from a foreign service provider. If the transaction qualifies as an import of service and GST is payable by the Indian recipient under RCM, the company should assess the invoice/document reporting requirement under RCMIMP from the effective date.
Do not treat every payment to an unregistered person or every overseas remittance as RCM. First establish whether the supply is taxable, whether the supplier/recipient conditions are met, whether it is an import of service where relevant, and whether a specific RCM notification places the tax liability on the recipient.

3. New supply-type codes: RCMDOM and RCMIMP

Notification 05/2026 updates FORM GST INV-01 from e-invoice schema Version 1.1 to Version 1.2 and adds two supply-type codes:

CodeMeaning in the notified schemaTypical transaction
RCMDOMDomestic supply from unregistered person under RCMRecipient-generated self-invoice for a qualifying domestic RCM supply from an unregistered supplier.
RCMIMPImport of servicesRecipient-side invoice/document for a qualifying import of service liable to RCM.

These codes identify the type of supply in the e-invoice schema. They are not separate tax rates, new GST registrations or independent categories of RCM liability.

4. Who needs to comply?

The amendment expands the types of documents/transactions that fall within the e-invoice framework. It should not be read as removing the existing applicability conditions under Notification 13/2020–Central Tax, as amended.

Check the existing e-invoice applicability testThe GSTN implementation advisory describes the existing e-invoice eligibility threshold as aggregate annual turnover (AATO) of ₹5 crore and above. Apply the turnover test under the current Notification 13/2020–Central Tax, as amended, and verify whether the entity falls within any notified class of exemption. Do not treat the new RCM categories as a separate turnover threshold or assume that every registered recipient is covered.

For businesses already covered by e-invoicing, the new RCM document categories require process and system readiness. Businesses below the threshold or within a notified exemption should not assume that the amendment alone makes them liable; they should verify the current notification and any later clarifications.

Practical point: Turnover/applicability should be reviewed GSTIN-wise using the legal test, rather than assuming that the turnover of a group, project or unrelated entity automatically decides the position of every registration.

5. Practical compliance process

A robust recipient-side workflow should be built into the purchase and accounts process:

Identify inward supply→Determine RCM→Prepare recipient document→Validate e-invoice applicability→Report to IRP→Record IRN/QR details
  1. Capture vendor and transaction details: Record supplier name, address, registration status, GSTIN status where applicable, invoice/reference number, date, description, taxable value, place of supply and supporting contract/order.
  2. Determine whether RCM applies: Identify the exact statutory entry/notification and confirm the recipient is liable. Do not classify a transaction as RCM only because the supplier is unregistered or located outside India.
  3. Prepare the recipient-side document: Generate the self-invoice or other prescribed document in accordance with the relevant CGST provisions and applicable rules. Maintain a clear document numbering series and cross-reference the supplier's supporting document.
  4. Check e-invoice coverage: Test the recipient GSTIN against the turnover threshold and exemptions under the e-invoice notification as amended.
  5. Use the correct document/supply classification: Implement the new RCMDOM or RCMIMP category in the accounting/ERP integration in accordance with the final IRP schema and technical specifications.
  6. Obtain and retain IRN and signed QR code: Where e-invoicing applies, report the document to an authorised IRP and retain the returned IRN, signed QR code and acknowledgement with the accounting record.
  7. Discharge RCM tax and report returns: Account for and pay the applicable RCM liability in the prescribed manner. Report the liability in the appropriate GST return tables and evaluate ITC separately under the eligibility conditions.
Important on timing and document sequence: The exact document date, reporting window, cancellation/amendment handling and payload validations must be configured using the applicable CGST Rules, the final IRP release notes and technical documentation. Do not hard-code a reporting timeline based only on a secondary article or an early sandbox build.

7. What does not change?

  • RCM liability is not newly created by these e-invoice notifications. The underlying RCM liability continues to arise from the CGST Act, IGST Act and relevant notifications.
  • Tax rates are not changed by Notification 04/2026. The notification amends the e-invoice coverage wording.
  • ITC is not automatic. Payment of RCM tax and possession of an IRN do not by themselves establish ITC eligibility. The recipient must independently satisfy Section 16 and other applicable conditions, including restrictions under Section 17.
  • Import of goods is not import of services. The new RCMIMP code is specifically described as import of services.
  • Existing e-invoice exemptions continue to require attention. The amendment should be read with the full amended Notification 13/2020, not in isolation.

8. Accounting and GST return impact

A. Suggested accounting flow

For a qualifying domestic RCM service, a typical accounting design may separately capture the expense/asset, supplier payable (where applicable), RCM tax liability and eligible/ineligible ITC. The exact ledger entries depend on the nature of supply, tax rate, place of supply, ITC eligibility and the company's accounting policy.

Accounting componentControl requirement
Expense / assetRecord the underlying purchase/service with the correct cost centre, project, GST classification and supporting supplier document.
RCM tax payableRecognise the output tax liability separately by tax head and return period.
Self-invoice / recipient documentMaintain unique document number, date, supplier details, taxable value, tax rate and linkage to original supporting document.
IRN / QR codeStore the IRN, acknowledgement and signed QR code against the recipient-generated document when e-invoicing applies.
Input tax creditEvaluate eligibility independently; distinguish eligible credit from blocked/ineligible credit and ensure the return reporting is consistent.

B. GST return reporting

The extension of e-invoicing does not replace the recipient's obligation to report RCM liability in the applicable return. The finance team should reconcile the RCM register with the liability reported and tax paid in GSTR-3B, and separately reconcile eligible ITC claimed. Do not assume that the recipient-generated e-invoice automatically populates every relevant GSTR-3B field or grants credit.

A monthly control should reconcile: RCM expense register → self-invoice register → IRN register → RCM liability in GSTR-3B → cash ledger payment → eligible ITC register.

10. Frequently asked questions

1. Is e-invoicing for all RCM transactions mandatory from 1 January 2027?

No. The amendment adds specified categories: domestic supplies received from unregistered persons where the recipient pays tax under RCM, and import of services. The existing e-invoice applicability threshold and exemptions must also be checked.

2. What is RCMDOM?

RCMDOM is the supply-type code described in FORM GST INV-01 for domestic supply from an unregistered person under RCM.

3. What is RCMIMP?

RCMIMP is the supply-type code described in FORM GST INV-01 for import of services.

4. Does the supplier issue the e-invoice in domestic RCM cases?

For the domestic unregistered-supplier scenario, the recipient is responsible for issuing the prescribed self-invoice under the applicable GST provisions. Where the e-invoice mandate applies, the recipient-side document is the document to be reported under the new category. Follow final IRP guidance for the exact payload and workflow.

5. Does every overseas payment require RCMIMP?

No. First determine whether the supply meets the legal definition of import of services and whether GST is payable by the recipient under RCM. An overseas payment alone is not sufficient.

6. Does this amendment change the RCM tax rate?

No. Notification 04/2026 expands the specified e-invoice categories. The applicable rate and liability must be determined under the existing GST law and relevant rate/RCM notifications.

7. Can ITC be claimed merely because an IRN is generated?

No. ITC eligibility must be tested independently under Section 16, Section 17 and other applicable provisions. An IRN is a document-reporting reference, not an ITC approval.

8. Is import of goods covered under RCMIMP?

No. The notified description of RCMIMP is “Import of services”. Import of goods follows the customs and Bill of Entry process.

9. What is the e-invoice schema version from 1 January 2027?

Notification 05/2026 amends FORM GST INV-01 to Schema Version 1.2 and adds RCMDOM and RCMIMP as supply-type codes, effective from 1 January 2027.

10. What should an ERP team do before January 2027?

Review the final IRP technical documentation, implement the new codes and document types, test sandbox scenarios, validate tax and party fields, and ensure IRN/QR data is retained and reconciled.
