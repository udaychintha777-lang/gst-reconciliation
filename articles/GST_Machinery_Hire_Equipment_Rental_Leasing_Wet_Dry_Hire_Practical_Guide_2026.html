This guide explains how to read the contract, identify the actual service and account for it correctly.

Final decision framework

1. Start with one simple question: what exactly has been hired?

When someone says “we hired a JCB”, that sentence does not tell the GST classification. It only tells us that a JCB was involved. GST treatment depends on the rights and responsibilities agreed between the owner and the customer, and on what the supplier actually undertakes to provide.

Consider two arrangements. In the first, the owner drops an excavator at your site. Your company arranges the operator, fuel, maintenance and daily work schedule. In the second, the owner supplies the excavator, operator, fuel and maintenance, and promises to excavate a specified quantity of earth. Both invoices may casually say “JCB hire”, but the commercial substance is different.

Asset accessAre you paying to use a machine for a period of time?
Equipment with operatorIs the supplier making a machine and operator available under your instructions?
Completed activityIs the supplier promising excavation, lifting, compaction or another defined result?
Golden rule: Do not decide the SAC or GST rate from the equipment name alone. Read the scope of work, operator obligations, fuel clause, control of operations, measurement basis, risk allocation and billing method together.

2. Wet hire vs dry hire – explained in everyday language

“Dry hire” and “wet hire” are commercial expressions commonly used in the construction equipment market. They are useful descriptions, but they are not standalone GST rate entries. The contract still needs to be classified under the GST rate notification and service classification.

PointDry hire (typical arrangement)Wet hire (typical arrangement)
MachineOwner supplies equipment only.Owner supplies equipment along with operator/crew.
OperatorHirer arranges and supervises operator.Owner arranges operator; exact control must be read from contract.
FuelOften arranged by hirer.May be included by owner or supplied/reimbursed separately.
MaintenanceMay be hirer's responsibility for routine items, while major repairs remain with owner.Often owner's responsibility, subject to contract.
Payment basisHourly, daily, weekly or monthly equipment rent.Hourly/monthly availability, shift rate or output-based charge.
Typical GST questionIs this leasing/renting without operator under Heading 9973, or a transfer of right to use goods?Is this rental with operator, a transport-vehicle rental, or a separately identifiable construction/service activity?

Example: same excavator, two different contracts

Contract A – dry hire
Excavator supplied for 200 machine-hours in a month. Customer provides diesel, operator and daily instructions. Supplier charges ₹1,80,000 as monthly equipment rent.

Contract B – wet hire
Supplier provides excavator, operator, fuel and routine maintenance. Supplier charges ₹1,250 per operating hour and is responsible for machine availability and operator wages.

Why it matters
Contract A is closer to equipment rental without operator. Contract B needs a closer look at whether it is rental of equipment with operator or a defined excavation/construction service. Do not assume both automatically attract the same entry just because the machine is the same.

Also, the word “wet” does not automatically mean that diesel is included. Some wet-hire contracts include an operator but charge fuel separately. The agreement should state exactly what is included in the hourly or monthly rate.

3. Classification: are you hiring equipment or buying a completed job?

This is usually the most important decision. A supplier may deploy a machine at your site, but that does not always mean the supplier is merely renting the machine. Sometimes the supplier is carrying out an activity using its own machine.

Commercial arrangementClues in the contractClassification issue to examine
Equipment made available for useMonthly rent, minimum hours, equipment availability, customer schedules work and bears output risk.Rental/leasing of machinery; for rental without operator, examine Heading 9973.
Transport vehicle hired with driverVehicle is at customer's disposal for a period; customer determines use, routes/schedule; driver supplied.Heading 9966 rental services of transport vehicles with operators; special rate entries may apply to goods carriage or passenger vehicles.
Excavation measured by cubic metreSupplier promises excavation of a quantity, carries out activity, and bills per cubic metre rather than simply making equipment available.Could be an excavation/construction or other service depending on scope, recipient and contract; do not classify solely as machine hire.
Crane lifting charged per lift/jobSupplier undertakes lifting/placement activity, provides rigging crew and assumes execution responsibility.Examine the actual lifting/handling service and its SAC; not automatically equipment rent.
Complete road work packageSupplier undertakes a defined portion of road construction/repair with materials, labour, machinery and performance responsibility.May be a works contract/construction service under Heading 9954 if statutory conditions are met.

Three questions to ask before choosing the SAC

  1. What is the supplier's promise? To make a machine available, or to deliver a completed result?
  2. Who controls the work? Who chooses when, where and how the machine operates? Who can direct the operator?
  3. How is payment calculated? By time/availability, by quantity excavated, by number of lifts, by kilometres, or by a completed work package?
Do not split a single supply artificially. If equipment, operator, fuel and activity are naturally bundled as one composite supply, examine the principal supply and contract as a whole. Separate invoice lines do not by themselves prove separate supplies.

4. GST rates and SAC framework – what rate should be charged?

There is no one universal “machinery hire GST rate”. The rate depends on the correct service classification and the relevant entry in the applicable rate notification. The current CBIC services rate table distinguishes transport-vehicle rental under Heading 9966 from leasing/rental without operator under Heading 9973.

Broad categoryGST rate approachWhat to verify
Leasing/renting of goods without operator – Heading 9973, entry 17(viia)Rate is linked to the rate applicable to supply of like goods involving transfer of title.Identify the machine as goods, its correct HSN and the applicable goods rate. Do not assume 18% for every item.
Residual leasing/rental without operator – Heading 9973, entry 17(viii)18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST, or 18% IGST) under the current rate table.Confirm that the service does not fall under the more specific entries such as renting of goods.
Rental services of transport vehicles with operators – Heading 9966, residual entry 10(iii)18% in the general residual category.Check whether the vehicle is a passenger vehicle, goods carriage or time-chartered vessel covered by a specific rate entry.
Renting of goods carriage with operator, fuel included – Heading 9966, entry 10(ia)12% under the specific entry in the current rate table.This is a vehicle-specific entry. It should not be applied automatically to excavators, cranes or other non-transport machinery.
Defined excavation/construction/works contract activityRate follows the relevant construction/service entry and its conditions; construction services commonly require a separate Heading 9954 analysis.Check exact scope, recipient, immovable property, contract and any concessional conditions.

How to apply this in practice

Suppose a supplier rents an excavator without an operator. First establish that the transaction is genuinely rental of goods without operator. Then identify the excavator's goods classification and rate. The service entry may link the rental rate to the rate on like goods. If the equipment is supplied with an operator, do not mechanically carry over the dry-hire rate; analyse whether the contract is a rental service with operator or a separate activity.

For a crane, paver, road roller, JCB, generator or batching plant, do not choose a rate merely because another contractor used 18%. The actual equipment classification, exact SAC entry, operator involvement and any specific notification condition must be checked.

Rate-date discipline: The rate table and notifications can be amended. This article is framed on the position reviewed on 23 November 2026. For an invoice relating to an earlier period, use the rate notification effective on the relevant time-of-supply date, not automatically the rate visible today.

6. Diesel, operator wages, repairs, mobilisation and other charges

Many disputes arise because the quotation gives one hourly rate but the final invoice contains fuel, operator overtime, repairs, transportation and standby charges. Decide whether each amount forms part of one bundled supply or is a genuinely separate supply.

Diesel included in the hire rate

If the supplier provides equipment and fuel for one consolidated consideration, the fuel cost may simply be an input cost of the supplier's service. The supplier normally accounts for its own input costs while charging GST on the taxable value of its supply. Do not automatically deduct the estimated diesel value from the invoice just because the supplier has shown a fuel component internally.

Diesel supplied by the hirer

If your company supplies diesel to the equipment owner/operator, record the issue with machine number, date, litres, site and meter/hour reading. Whether the diesel is merely a customer-provided input, a recoverable amount, or a separate supply depends on ownership, contract and consideration. Do not assume that every free issue of fuel is a taxable outward supply; equally, do not ignore a contractual recovery or transfer of ownership.

Operator and overtime

If operator wages are included in the supplier's single hire charge, they are generally part of the supplier's cost base. If a separate operator staffing agency supplies labour to your company, that is a separate service requiring its own classification and tax review. Check whether the arrangement is genuinely manpower supply or part of equipment rental.

Mobilisation and demobilisation

Transporting a large excavator, crane or paver to and from a project site may be charged as a one-time amount. If it is ancillary to the equipment rental and supplied by the same supplier as part of one contract, examine whether it forms part of the value of that supply. If a separate transporter is engaged under a separate contract, classify that transport service separately.

Repairs, breakdown and standby charges

  • Routine maintenance included: may be part of the supplier's overall rental commitment.
  • Repair charged separately: check whether it is a separate repair service, a contractual reimbursement or a deduction from rent.
  • Standby / idle hours: examine whether the amount is consideration for keeping equipment available or compensation for breach/cancellation. Contract wording and facts matter.
  • Damage recovery: distinguish a genuine price adjustment from compensation for damage. GST treatment is not decided by the label “penalty”.
Practical invoice rule: Ask the supplier to describe the equipment, hire period, machine hours, operator/fuel inclusion, site, mobilisation, standby and additional charges clearly. A single vague line called “machinery charges” makes later audit and ITC verification harder.

7. Reverse charge (RCM): does hiring from an unregistered owner create RCM?

This is a common misconception. A recipient should not assume that every machinery hire invoice from an unregistered individual or small equipment owner automatically attracts GST under reverse charge.

RCM applies only where the law specifically notifies the category of supply/recipient or another statutory provision makes the recipient liable. Section 9(4) is not a blanket reverse-charge rule for every purchase from an unregistered person; it operates for notified classes of registered persons and specified categories of supplies.

SituationPractical GST check
Registered equipment owner invoices a registered contractorSupplier generally charges GST under forward charge unless a specific RCM notification applies.
Unregistered individual rents an excavator without operator to a companyDo not automatically pay RCM. Check whether this exact service and recipient are covered by a current notification under Section 9(3) or 9(4).
Body corporate hires a passenger motor vehicle with operator from a specified non-body corporate supplierSpecific RCM entry may apply subject to the exact conditions and rate-notification options. This is not a general rule for earthmoving equipment.
Transport service supplied by a GTAAnalyse GTA classification, consignment note, recipient category and the applicable forward-charge/RCM option. A tipper hired by time is not automatically a GTA service.
Government/local authority supplies a serviceCheck the government-service RCM notification and its exclusions/recipient conditions; do not infer RCM simply because one party is Government.

Before booking RCM, document the exact notification entry, supplier category, recipient category, service description and effective date. If none of the notified conditions is met, do not create an RCM liability only because the supplier is unregistered.

Important: Reverse-charge entries are amended from time to time. The examples above are issue-spotting guidance, not a substitute for checking the notification in force on the transaction date.

8. Can the contractor claim ITC on machinery hire?

In many ordinary business situations, GST paid on genuine machinery hire used for taxable construction, mining, infrastructure or other business activity may be eligible as input tax credit, subject to Section 16 conditions and the specific blocked-credit provisions in Section 17(5).

For example, a road contractor hires an excavator to execute a taxable road project. If the service is received, the invoice is valid, the supplier reports the supply as required, tax is paid to the Government and the contractor satisfies the statutory conditions, the hire-service ITC may be available. The contractor must still examine whether any specific restriction applies to the particular vehicle or service.

Do not confuse equipment hire with purchase of a motor vehicle

Section 17(5)(a) restricts ITC in respect of specified motor vehicles for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity of not more than thirteen persons (including the driver), subject to exceptions. The restriction is not a blanket prohibition on all construction machinery or all vehicle-related expenditure.

Earth-moving machinery, tippers, dumpers, cranes and similar equipment need classification under the Motor Vehicles Act and the facts of their design/use. CBIC's sectoral FAQ has specifically addressed mining equipment such as tippers and dumpers and explains that such equipment is not included in the relevant motor-vehicle definition for that clarification. Do not extend that FAQ to every vehicle without checking its technical and statutory classification.

Section 17(5)(c) and (d): construction of immovable property

These clauses deal with specified works contract services and goods/services used for construction of immovable property on one's own account, subject to statutory exceptions including plant and machinery. A contractor using hired machinery to execute a taxable works contract for a customer is not automatically constructing an immovable property on its own account. On the other hand, a business hiring equipment/services for construction of its own office or building must examine the blocked-credit provisions and the plant-and-machinery definition carefully.

Use of hired equipmentITC review
Excavator hired to execute customer's taxable road/bridge contractGenerally examine normal Section 16 eligibility; no automatic block merely because the work relates to immovable property belonging to customer.
Crane hired to install plant and machinery for taxable business operationsReview business use, invoice and whether the activity is part of eligible plant/machinery installation or another supply.

ITC checklist for the hirer

  1. Confirm that the service was actually received and used in the course or furtherance of business.
  2. Obtain a valid tax invoice/debit note from the correct GST registration.
  3. Check supplier GSTIN, invoice details and reporting/communication in the applicable GST system.
  4. Ensure the credit is not blocked under Section 17(5).
  5. Pay the supplier within 180 days where the second proviso to Section 16(2) applies; monitor Rule 37 reversal and re-availment.
  6. Reconcile ITC claimed with purchase ledger, GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B.
  7. Retain the work order, machine log and project usage evidence.

9. GST and ITC for the equipment owner

The equipment owner may have purchased the machine with GST, taken a loan, paid insurance, bought spare parts and incurred repairs. If the owner uses the machine to make taxable rental supplies, the input tax credit on eligible business inputs and capital goods may be available subject to the normal conditions and restrictions.

However, the owner should not assume that every expense is eligible. Passenger motor vehicles, personal use, exempt supplies, blocked credits, capital goods used partly for non-business purposes and special rate conditions must be examined individually.

Example: owner buys a road roller and rents it out

Facts
A registered equipment company buys a road roller for ₹40 lakh plus GST. It rents the roller to multiple road contractors under taxable hire agreements. The company records the asset, uses it in its rental business and issues periodic invoices.

Review
Determine the correct goods classification and whether the machine is a motor vehicle or other equipment for the relevant statutory purpose. Apply Section 16 and Section 17(5), capital goods rules and any applicable rate-notification condition. Do not decide credit solely from the registration certificate or the word “roller”.

Where the supplier opts for a concessional rate that carries a no-ITC condition, the condition must be read exactly. A supplier cannot charge a concessional rate and simultaneously claim credit contrary to the notification condition.

10. Time of supply, continuous hire and invoice timing

Machinery hire is often a continuous or periodic service. The agreement may say rent is payable monthly, every 250 machine-hours, on certification of the log sheet or on completion of a milestone. GST timing must be considered under Section 13 and the continuous-supply provisions in Section 31.

Simple monthly hire example

Facts
Excavator hired from 1 November to 30 November. Contract says monthly rent of ₹2,00,000 is payable on the fifth day of the following month. The supplier issues an invoice on 5 December.

Practical treatment
Read the contract's due date and billing terms. For a continuous supply of services where payment is linked to a specified date, Section 31(5) requires invoice on or before the specified date. Time of supply under Section 13 is then determined using the invoice/payment rules, subject to the statutory provisions. Do not wait until cash is received if the invoice/tax point has already arisen.

Hourly hire and machine log certification

If payment depends on approved machine hours, the contract should state when the statement is issued and when payment becomes due. The supplier should issue invoices within the statutory time. A customer should not hold up all invoices indefinitely merely because the site engineer has not signed a log sheet, where the contractual/statutory billing point has already arisen.

Advance paid to equipment owner

For services, advance receipts can affect time of supply under Section 13. A registered supplier of services should assess tax on advances in accordance with the law; do not apply the general relaxation for advances on goods to equipment rental services.

11. Moving hired machinery to and from the project site

Large machinery often moves on trailers or low-bed vehicles. The equipment may be transported from the owner's yard to Site A, shifted from Site A to Site B, sent for repairs and returned after the hire period. These movements should be documented separately from the rental invoice.

E-way bill requirements under Rule 138 should be checked based on consignment value, movement, State-specific rules and applicable exemptions. Do not assume that a hired machine is exempt from movement documentation merely because ownership has not changed.

12. GST TDS and income-tax TDS – do not mix them up

GST TDS under Section 51

GST TDS is not deducted by every company hiring machinery. It applies to notified deductors, such as specified Government departments, establishments, local authorities, governmental agencies and other notified persons, when the statutory conditions are met. The threshold is based on the value of taxable supply under a contract exceeding ₹2.5 lakh, excluding GST and cess for this purpose, subject to the law and notified deductor categories.

Where applicable, the notified deductor generally deducts GST TDS at 2% in total (1% CGST + 1% SGST for intra-State supply, or 2% IGST for inter-State supply). The supplier should reconcile the deduction with the electronic cash ledger and the relevant TDS certificate/return records.

Income-tax TDS

Income-tax TDS on equipment rent is a separate law. The classification between plant/machinery rent and a contract for carrying out work can affect the applicable withholding provision and rate. A payment for mere use of plant or machinery may need to be examined under the rent provision; a composite execution contract may need a different analysis. Check the Income-tax law and threshold/rate applicable on the date of credit/payment, including the transition to the Income-tax Act, 2025 from 1 April 2026. Do not apply GST SAC or GST rate to decide income-tax TDS.

Finance control: Maintain separate fields in the vendor master and invoice workflow for GST, GST TDS and income-tax TDS. A GST TDS deduction is not a reduction in the taxable value of the machinery hire service for GST purposes.

14. Detailed practical cases

Case 1 – JCB dry hire for a road contractor

A contractor hires a JCB for 26 days. The owner delivers the machine; the contractor provides diesel and operator. The contract charges ₹7,000 per day. Treat this as an equipment-rental candidate. Check the applicable Heading 9973 entry and the rate on like goods, the GSTIN of the supplier and the actual contract. Retain daily log sheets and site deployment records.

Case 2 – Excavator with operator and diesel included

The owner provides excavator, operator, diesel and maintenance for ₹2,500 per hour. The contractor controls the site and directs the location where excavation is required. Determine whether the owner is simply making the equipment/operator available or undertaking excavation as an activity measured by output. The answer depends on the contract and actual performance. Do not use the 12% goods-carriage rental entry just because fuel is included; that entry is specific to renting of goods carriage.

Case 3 – Tipper hired monthly with driver

A tipper is made available to a mining contractor for a month, with driver and diesel included. This may fall for consideration under Heading 9966 as rental of a goods carriage with operator, subject to the exact vehicle and contract conditions. The current rate table contains a specific 12% entry for renting of goods carriage where fuel cost is included. If the arrangement is instead transport of goods per trip/tonne under a GTA or other transport model, analyse that separately.

Case 4 – Crane hired for a bridge project

A crane owner supplies a 100-tonne crane with operator and rigging team. The supplier bills ₹1.5 lakh per shift for lifting girders. If the supplier is responsible for executing a lifting operation rather than merely renting out a crane, the supply may need a service classification other than simple equipment rental. The purchase order should clearly state the scope, safety responsibility, rigging, lifting plan and payment basis.

Case 5 – Paver and roller hired for bituminous road work

A contractor hires a sensor paver and roller with operators. If the owner only provides equipment on hourly basis and the main contractor controls the laying operation, analyse equipment rental with operator. If the owner undertakes laying and compaction to a specified output/quality, examine whether it is a construction activity or subcontracted work package. The machine list alone cannot answer the GST question.

Case 6 – Unregistered local owner rents a compressor

A small individual owner, not registered under GST, rents a compressor to a registered company. Do not book RCM automatically. Check whether any current notification specifically covers that supply and recipient. Also review the supplier's registration liability based on aggregate turnover and applicable exemptions. A purchase from an unregistered person does not by itself create a general RCM liability.

Case 7 – Mobilisation charge and monthly hire in one agreement

The owner charges ₹50,000 to transport an excavator to site and ₹2 lakh per month as rent. If mobilisation is ancillary to the rental supplied by the same owner under one arrangement, assess whether it forms part of the taxable value of that supply. If a separate transporter is independently contracted, review that transport invoice on its own facts.

Case 8 – Machine breaks down and owner deducts idle hours

The owner invoices 180 hours instead of 220 hours because the machine was unavailable for 40 hours. This may be a reduction in consideration under the contract rather than a separate taxable penalty. Ensure the log sheet, credit note and contractual formula agree. If a separate amount is charged for breach or damages, analyse its legal and GST character separately.

Case 9 – Equipment hired for own office construction

A company hires a crane and concrete pump to construct its own office building. Even if the supplier charges GST correctly, the company must separately test ITC under Section 17(5)(c) and (d), the meaning of construction on own account and the plant-and-machinery exception. A taxable invoice does not guarantee that the recipient can claim ITC.

Case 10 – Inter-State movement of a hired machine

A crane owned by a supplier in Telangana is moved to a project in Karnataka. The movement is not automatically a sale, but delivery/return documents, place of supply, the supplier's registration, the recipient GSTIN and e-way bill requirements must be checked. If separate registrations or a distinct-person arrangement are involved, conduct a Schedule I and valuation review.

15. Contract, procurement and GST audit checklist

Before issuing the purchase order

  1. Identify equipment make, model, registration/serial number and capacity.
  2. State whether hire is dry or wet and who supplies operator, fuel, lubricants and maintenance.
  3. Specify whether payment is hourly, daily, monthly, per trip, per tonne, per cubic metre, per lift or per completed activity.
  4. Define who controls the equipment, operator, working hours, routes and work sequence.
  5. Allocate breakdown, standby, mobilisation, demobilisation, damage and insurance responsibilities.
  6. Agree GST classification and rate based on the actual scope, not only the vendor's old invoice.
  7. Obtain supplier GSTIN, PAN, bank details and registration status where applicable.

At the site and during the month

  1. Record machine arrival and return dates with hour-meter/odometer readings.
  2. Maintain daily log sheets signed by site engineer and supplier/operator.
  3. Record diesel issues, consumption, opening/closing balance and machine number.
  4. Certify idle hours, breakdowns, overtime and additional shifts.
  5. Maintain mobilisation/demobilisation challans and e-way bill records where applicable.
  6. Match supplier invoice hours with approved site log sheets and purchase order rates.

At finance and GST review

  1. Check SAC and rate notification effective on the time-of-supply date.
  2. Check whether the supply is rental, transport vehicle rental, transport, excavation or works contract service.
  3. Check forward charge versus a specifically notified RCM entry.
  4. Verify invoice, GSTIN, GSTR-2B and ITC eligibility under Sections 16 and 17(5).
  5. Review 180-day payment condition where applicable.
  6. Review GST TDS only if the customer is a notified deductor and threshold conditions are met.
  7. Reconcile equipment hire ledger with project-wise machinery deployment register.
Recommended control: Create a monthly “Plant & Machinery Hire Register” with columns for project, supplier GSTIN, equipment ID, dry/wet hire, operator, fuel responsibility, hire period, machine hours, rate, taxable value, SAC, GST rate, ITC status, RCM check, GST TDS, invoice number and payment date.

16. Frequently asked questions

1. Is GST on JCB hire always 18%?

No. First determine whether it is dry hire, equipment with operator or an excavation activity. For rental of goods without operator, the rate may be linked to the rate on like goods. For other rental categories, the relevant specific or residual entry must be checked.

2. Is wet hire always 18%?

No. Wet hire is a commercial description, not a rate entry. Transport vehicles with operator have specific entries, including a 12% entry for goods carriage where fuel is included. Other arrangements require their own classification.

3. Does diesel included in the invoice change the rate?

It can matter where a specific rate entry makes fuel inclusion a condition, such as the goods-carriage rental entry. For other equipment, fuel inclusion does not automatically produce the same rate.

4. If a machine is supplied with operator, is it automatically Heading 9966?

No. Heading 9966 is for rental services of transport vehicles with operators. Construction machinery that is not a transport vehicle may need a different analysis. A supplier performing excavation or construction work may be supplying an activity rather than merely renting equipment.

5. Is RCM payable when the machine owner is unregistered?

Not automatically. RCM applies only where a statutory provision or notification covers the supply and recipient. There is no general rule that every machinery hire from an unregistered person attracts RCM.

6. Can a road contractor claim ITC on excavator hire?

Generally examine normal Section 16 conditions and specific Section 17(5) restrictions. Hire used to execute a customer's taxable contract is different from equipment used to construct the recipient's own immovable property.

7. Is ITC blocked on every crane, tipper or dumper?

No blanket answer. Check the technical nature and statutory classification of the equipment, its use and the relevant Section 17(5) clause. CBIC has issued a sectoral clarification on certain mining equipment; do not extend it beyond its facts.

8. Can mobilisation be billed separately?

Yes, subject to the contract and tax invoice requirements. But a separate line does not automatically make it a separate supply. If ancillary to the rental, it may form part of the value of the principal supply.

9. Is GST TDS deducted by every company hiring machinery?

No. Section 51 applies to specified deductors and subject to the statutory contract-value threshold and other conditions.

10. What is the difference between machinery hire and excavation subcontract?

In hire, the customer typically obtains use/availability of equipment. In an excavation subcontract, the supplier may undertake a measured result or activity. The actual scope, control, risk and billing basis determine classification.

11. Does a monthly hire contract qualify as continuous supply of services?

It may, depending on the contract and recurring payment obligations. Apply the definition and Section 31(5) invoice rules to the actual agreement.

12. What documents should the hirer retain?

Agreement, PO, equipment details, delivery/return challans, e-way bill where applicable, daily log sheets, fuel records, invoice, GSTR-2B reconciliation, ITC working and payment proof.

17. Final decision framework

1. Identify equipment→2. Read scope→3. Operator/fuel?→4. Rental or activity?→5. Select SAC/rate→6. ITC/RCM/TDS

The safest approach is to classify the contract before the machine reaches the site. A purchase order that clearly explains what the owner supplies, who controls the equipment, how charges are measured and who bears operating costs will prevent many GST disputes later.

For contractors, the equipment register should connect the commercial agreement with daily deployment, machine hours, fuel consumption, supplier invoices, GST classification and project cost. That is how procurement, site operations and finance can arrive at one consistent GST position.

Legal and rate note: This article provides general educational guidance based on the CGST Act, 2017, the service-rate notification framework and CBIC material reviewed on 23 November 2026. Classification and rates depend on the precise contract, equipment, supplier/recipient status, place of supply and amendments effective on the relevant date. Verify the applicable notification and tax position before invoicing, claiming ITC or responding to a notice.
