1. Quick summary: ITC is a legal entitlement, not simply a ledger balance

Input tax credit reduces eligible GST paid on business inputs, input services and capital goods against output tax, subject to the CGST/SGST/IGST framework, statutory conditions, restrictions and prescribed procedures. A purchase entry or a figure appearing in GSTR-2B is important evidence, but neither should be treated as a complete substitute for checking eligibility.

Eligibility

Confirm business use, a valid tax document, receipt of goods/services, supplier-reported details where required, return filing and the applicable time limit.

Restrictions

Screen blocked credits under Section 17(5), personal/non-business use, exempt supplies, duplicate claims and capital-goods depreciation restrictions.

Reconciliation

Match purchase register to GSTR-2B/IMS, supplier invoices, goods receipt notes, service evidence, books and GSTR-3B.

Ongoing controls

Track unpaid invoices, supplier filing status, credit notes, amendments, reversals, reclaim eligibility and invoice-level audit evidence.

Important: ITC is not automatically disallowed merely because a portal row needs investigation; equally, a portal match alone does not prove that a blocked or non-business expense is eligible. Apply the law to the actual transaction and preserve evidence.

3. The core eligibility test: use this for every invoice

Before taking credit, assess the transaction through a consistent sequence. A single missing condition can affect entitlement even when the invoice is recorded in the books.

  1. Registered recipient: the claimant should be the person entitled to the credit under the GST registration and tax-document structure.
  2. Business purpose: the goods/services must be used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business.
  3. Valid document: retain the tax invoice, debit note, bill of entry, ISD document or other prescribed document applicable to the transaction.
  4. Supplier reporting: check whether the invoice/debit note details have been furnished and communicated as required by Section 16 and the relevant return mechanism.
  5. Receipt: prove actual receipt of goods/services. For goods received in lots, credit is generally subject to the statutory condition relating to receipt of the last lot/instalment.
  6. Tax and return conditions: verify the tax charged, applicable restrictions, and filing of the recipient's return.
  7. No disqualification: check Section 17(5), personal use, exempt supply attribution and other specific restrictions.
  8. Time limit: confirm the claim is within the statutory deadline, including any applicable special provision.
Illustration — likely eligible subject to all conditionsA registered manufacturer buys raw material for taxable production, has a valid invoice, receives the goods, records the inward supply, verifies the supplier-reported entry, and uses the material for taxable business supplies. The credit may be claimed after all applicable checks are satisfied.
Illustration — invoice alone is insufficientA company records a tax invoice for goods that never arrived and cannot show transport, receipt or consumption evidence. A ledger entry and portal appearance do not cure the lack of actual receipt.

  • GRN, gate entry, weighment and quality report
  • E-way bill, lorry receipt, transporter invoice
  • Stock register, consumption or issue record
  • Payment and vendor ledger
Services
  • Tax invoice and engagement/work order
  • Contract, deliverables and service completion evidence
  • Timesheets, reports, emails or acceptance certificates
  • Payment proof and vendor ledger
  • Business-purpose note where the nature is unusual
Capital goods
  • Invoice, fixed-asset register and commissioning record

  • Depreciation schedule showing tax component treatment
  • Asset transfer/disposal record if applicable
Import / RCM / ISD
  • Bill of entry and customs tax payment
  • RCM liability and payment evidence
  • ISD document and allocation working
  • Proof of eligibility and recipient registration

Best practice: assign each purchase invoice a stable internal key—supplier GSTIN + normalized invoice number + financial year—and link it to the ledger voucher, GSTR-2B row, receipt evidence, payment ageing and ITC decision.

5. Time limit for taking ITC: do not use a generic date without checking the period

Section 16(4) generally restricts taking credit on an invoice or debit note after the statutory deadline linked to the November return following the end of the relevant financial year, or filing of the relevant annual return, whichever occurs earlier, subject to the text applicable to that period. The Act has also included special provisions for specified earlier financial years; these should not be treated as a blanket extension for every year.

Practical deadline check
  1. Identify the invoice date and the financial year to which the invoice/debit note pertains.
  2. Identify the return deadline under the version of Section 16(4) applicable to that year.
  3. Check the annual-return filing date and whether it causes an earlier cut-off.
  4. Check if a specific retrospective or transitional provision applies to that particular year and fact pattern.
  5. Document the conclusion in the ITC register and do not rely solely on a portal allowing or preventing an entry.

Example: a company finds an unclaimed FY 2025–26 invoice during an October 2026 review. It must verify the statutory deadline applicable to that invoice and the annual return status before claiming credit. The fact that the invoice appears in a later system download does not by itself extend the legal time limit.

6. GSTR-2B, IMS and purchase-register reconciliation

Use GSTR-2B as an important statement for ITC reconciliation. The Invoice Management System (IMS) allows recipient action on specified supplier-reported records, but does not replace the statutory eligibility analysis. The correct process is to reconcile, investigate and decide—not to accept or reject every row mechanically.

Suggested invoice matching statuses

Reconciliation controls

  • Normalize invoice numbers without deleting meaningful characters that distinguish invoices.
  • Match supplier GSTIN first; then invoice number, date, taxable value and each tax head.
  • Separate original invoices from debit/credit notes and amendments.
  • Keep a month-wise opening mismatch, additions, resolved items and closing mismatch roll-forward.
  • Reconcile ITC actually availed in GSTR-3B, not merely the statement total.
  • Preserve IMS actions and any regenerated statement/version used for filing.
IMS caution: process and portal options can change. Check current GST Portal advisories for the relevant tax period, particularly for credit notes and amendment records.

7. Supplier reporting, non-payment of tax and recipient due diligence

Separate three questions: whether the recipient satisfies the statutory ITC conditions; whether supplier reporting and tax-related conditions are met; and what evidence the recipient has of a genuine transaction. Do not treat a supplier's default as a purely accounting issue, but also do not assume that every supplier-side discrepancy has the same legal consequence regardless of the provision, period and facts.

Practical supplier-risk file

  • Supplier GSTIN and registration-status checks at onboarding and periodically.
  • Purchase order, invoice, transport/receipt and service evidence.
  • Payment proof and vendor balance confirmation for material balances.
  • GSTR-2B availability and follow-up records for missing invoices.
  • Vendor declarations where commercially appropriate; these do not replace statutory conditions.
  • Escalation record for cancelled registration, suspected fictitious supply or repeated non-reporting.

Where the department alleges that a supplier is non-existent or the supply is fictitious, preserve evidence of actual supply, movement, delivery, business rationale, payment trail and subsequent use/resale. Obtain professional advice promptly because the facts and applicable law determine the defence.

8. Rule 37: payment to supplier within 180 days

Where the recipient has availed ITC but fails to pay the supplier the value of supply plus tax within 180 days from the invoice date, the applicable statutory reversal/payment mechanism may require an amount equivalent to the credit to be added to output tax liability, with interest as prescribed. The rule has exceptions and special treatment, including supplies on which tax is payable under reverse charge; apply the current text and facts rather than a one-line rule.

Example: partial payment

Invoice value ₹1,00,000 plus GST ₹18,000. The recipient pays ₹59,000 within 180 days and leaves ₹59,000 unpaid (including proportionate tax component). Determine the unpaid portion and related ITC using the statutory rule and invoice-level payment allocation; do not automatically reverse the entire invoice credit if the rule requires proportionate treatment.

Ageing process

  1. Calculate 180 days from each invoice date, not from the month-end by default.
  2. Match payments using an agreed allocation method supported by the vendor ledger and remittance details.
  3. Separate disputed amounts, debit notes, credit notes and advances; document the basis of allocation.
  4. Identify the portion of invoice value and tax still unpaid at the relevant date.
  5. Calculate reversal/payment and interest under the rule applicable to the period.
  6. Track the later payment and re-availment eligibility, with proof of settlement.

Important: a balance confirmation alone does not establish which invoice was paid. Maintain a FIFO or specifically documented allocation schedule, bank references and vendor account reconciliation.

9. Rule 37A: supplier has not furnished the relevant return

Rule 37A addresses specified situations where the supplier furnished outward-supply details but did not furnish the corresponding return within the prescribed framework. It has a distinct reversal and re-availment mechanism and is not the same as the 180-day payment rule. Confirm the exact period, return status, statutory due date, subsequent supplier filing and any applicable amendment before calculating a reversal.

Control design

Maintain separate flags for (a) invoice absent from GSTR-2B, (b) invoice reported but supplier return not filed, (c) recipient has not paid within 180 days, and (d) substantive ineligibility. These are different issues with different evidence and timelines.

Do not assume that a supplier filing after the relevant date automatically cures every consequence without checking the rule and timing. Save portal status reports, supplier follow-ups and the period-wise reversal/reclaim working.

10. Blocked ITC under Section 17(5): common categories and fact checks

Section 17(5) blocks specified categories of ITC, subject to statutory exceptions. The precise wording of the provision and the facts matter. The following table is a screening tool, not a substitute for reading the applicable clause.

Food, catering, health/beauty services, club membership and similar employee-related itemsOften restricted, subject to exceptionsWhether provision applies, employer obligation under law, and any specified outward-supply exception

Construction example

A developer constructs a commercial building for sale and buys cement, steel, design services and works-contract services. Do not decide all credits by the business label “construction”. Examine whether the property is constructed on own account, whether it is intended for sale, when consideration is received, the applicable Schedule II treatment, Section 17(5) wording and relevant statutory exceptions. Land/building and plant-and-machinery treatment can differ. Prepare a project-wise legal note.

Employee benefit example

A company incurs hotel, food and employee transport costs for a client event. Split the invoices by service type and purpose. Do not treat all employee-related costs identically: assess the specific blocked-credit clause, whether a statutory obligation or exception applies, and whether the expense is actually a taxable business supply input.

11. Business use, personal use and exempt supplies

Section 17(1) restricts credit attributable to non-business use; Section 17(2) restricts credit attributable to exempt supplies. Where a cost is directly attributable, identify it directly. Where common inputs or services support both eligible and ineligible activities, apply the prescribed apportionment rules rather than an unsupported estimate.

Directly eligible

Dedicated production input used exclusively for taxable outward supplies, subject to other conditions.

Directly ineligible

Expense clearly attributable to personal consumption, exempt activity or a blocked category.

Common credit

Shared rent, software, security, accounting or electricity supporting taxable and exempt activity; evaluate Rules 42/43.

Mixed purpose

Asset or expense partly for business and partly personal use; apply statutory allocation and retain the basis.

Examples of exempt-supply analysis may include interest income, sale of land, specified securities transactions and other transactions treated as exempt for the relevant calculation. The prescribed definition and exclusions control the calculation; do not simply divide all exempt income by total bank credits.

12. Rules 42 and 43: common-credit calculations

Rule 42 addresses inputs and input services used partly for non-business purposes or exempt supplies and partly for eligible supplies. Rule 43 deals with capital goods used commonly. These calculations require proper classification of directly attributable credit and common credit before applying the prescribed formula.

Monthly Rule 42 working: recommended structure

  1. Start with total ITC for inputs and input services for the tax period.
  2. Remove credit directly attributable to non-business purposes.
  3. Remove credit directly attributable to exempt supplies.
  4. Identify blocked credit and any other ineligible amount separately.
  5. Determine common credit and apply the prescribed formula and ratio for exempt supplies.
  6. Calculate eligible residual credit and add required reversal to output tax liability.
  7. Retain turnover definitions, source data, formula and reviewer approval.

Illustrative simplified example: Assume common input-service credit is ₹1,00,000 and the prescribed exempt-supply ratio for the relevant calculation is 10%. A simplified illustration would indicate ₹10,000 as the exempt-attributable component before considering the complete statutory formula, direct attribution, non-business component and other adjustments. This is not a substitute for the full Rule 42 working.

Annual true-up

At year-end, recalculate using final annual figures and compare with monthly reversals. If the prescribed final calculation produces additional reversal, pay it within the applicable period and calculate interest where the rule requires it. If the provisional reversal exceeds the final amount, claim the permitted excess credit within the prescribed timeline. Keep the monthly and annual workings linked.

Rule 43 capital goods

For common capital goods, track invoice-level tax, date of receipt/capitalisation, eligible use, useful-life mechanism prescribed by the rule, exempt turnover and residual life. Do not treat all capital goods as if they follow the input-service calculation or simply reverse the entire credit in every month.

13. Capital goods, fixed assets and depreciation

ITC on eligible capital goods may be available subject to the general conditions and specific restrictions. Under Section 16(3), if depreciation is claimed under the Income-tax Act on the GST component of the cost of capital goods or plant and machinery, ITC on that same tax component is not allowed. Accounting depreciation and income-tax depreciation are not interchangeable concepts; review the actual income-tax claim and asset records.

Fixed-asset control checklist

  • Match the asset invoice to the fixed-asset register and GSTIN that claimed the credit.
  • Confirm the asset is used for business and is not blocked under Section 17(5).
  • Confirm GST component was not included in the depreciable cost where ITC is claimed.
  • Identify shared use across taxable/exempt activity and apply Rule 43 where required.
  • Record transfers, disposal, change in use, destruction and sale of the asset.
  • Review construction-related assets separately from ordinary plant and machinery.

Example: a company buys equipment for ₹10,00,000 plus GST of ₹1,80,000. If it claims eligible ITC of ₹1,80,000, the same GST component must not also be included in the income-tax depreciable cost. Verify eligibility first; the arithmetic does not itself establish that the credit is allowable.

14. Reverse charge: liability payment and ITC are separate steps

For supplies subject to reverse charge, the recipient may have to discharge GST in cash as required by law. The recipient should not assume that the tax liability and input credit arise as a single net entry. Record the liability, pay it using the legally permitted method, satisfy ITC conditions and claim credit in the appropriate return period.

ControlEvidence
Identify RCM applicabilitySupplier category, service/goods description, place of supply and relevant notification

Do not use ITC to discharge a liability where the law requires cash payment. Check the current provisions and notifications for the exact category of reverse-charge supply.

15. Imports, bill of entry, SEZ and cross-border services

Import of goods

Reconcile bill of entry details, importer GSTIN, customs IGST, assessable value, amendments and goods receipt. Credit should be claimed by the eligible importer and should agree to customs records. A customs broker's summary is useful but should not replace the underlying bill of entry.

Import of services

Assess whether the transaction qualifies as an import of services and whether reverse charge applies. Preserve the agreement, supplier invoice, place-of-supply analysis, foreign remittance/payment documents and RCM payment evidence. Related-party and inter-company transactions may need separate valuation and supply analysis.

SEZ procurements

For supplies to or from an SEZ, check the statutory zero-rating framework, authorised operations where relevant, endorsements and prescribed documents. Do not infer entitlement from the counterparty's SEZ status alone.

Foreign currency and bank charges

Separate the foreign service itself from bank charges, withholding taxes and foreign exchange differences. Determine the GST treatment of each item and the correct recipient registration; do not claim credit merely because the expense is recorded as a foreign payment.

16. Credit notes, discounts, returns and amendments

Supplier credit notes and downward amendments can affect the tax and ITC position. Match each credit note to the original invoice, identify whether the recipient actually availed the corresponding ITC, check the supplier's tax adjustment and determine the recipient's required reversal. For commercial or financial credit notes without a GST tax adjustment, analyze the contract and statutory treatment rather than assuming every discount produces an automatic ITC reversal.

  • Map credit note to original invoice and original ITC claim.
  • Check whether the credit note is a GST credit note or a commercial credit note.
  • Verify whether the supplier reduced output tax and whether statutory conditions were met.
  • Use IMS actions and GSTR-2B impact where applicable; retain a record of the action taken.
  • For goods returns, match the sales return, transport, inventory receipt and supplier document.
  • For price revisions, confirm whether a debit/credit note or other adjustment document is legally appropriate.

Example: supplier issues a credit note for ₹10,000 taxable value plus ₹1,800 GST. The recipient should not mechanically reverse ₹1,800 without checking whether the original credit was claimed, the credit note's legal character, supplier reporting and the current statutory treatment.

17. ITC for exports, zero-rated supplies and refund claims

Zero-rated supplies are not the same as exempt supplies for ITC purposes. Eligible credit may be available for exports and qualifying supplies to SEZ for authorised operations, subject to the governing law and refund route. A refund application requires reconciliation of turnover, eligible ITC, tax payment, export evidence and any restrictions applicable to the selected route.

Refund readiness file

  • Shipping bills, export invoices and export general manifest/customs data as relevant.
  • Export of services: agreement, invoice, recipient/location evidence, place-of-supply and foreign remittance evidence as applicable.
  • SEZ: endorsement/authorised-operations documentation and prescribed evidence.
  • Statement of invoices and ITC ledger linked to the refund period.
  • Turnover and tax calculation, including adjustments and any applicable restrictions.
  • Bank account validation, declarations and supporting documents required for the refund category.

Do not apply one refund formula to every case. Refunds of unutilised ITC and refunds of tax paid follow different routes and restrictions; verify the current rules and notifications before filing.

18. ITC across industries: different fact patterns require different analysis

Construction and infrastructure

Review cement, steel, plant, fuel, subcontractor invoices, works-contract services, employee accommodation and project-office costs separately. Distinguish a contractor supplying works-contract services to a customer from a person constructing immovable property on own account. Project-wise cost codes help identify blocked, eligible and common credit.

Manufacturing

Match raw materials to GRNs, stores and production consumption; reconcile scrap, wastage, samples, job work and capital goods. Investigate invoice mismatch separately from physical non-receipt or stock shortage.

Trading and distribution

Trace inward invoices to inventory, sales, returns, discounts, stock transfers and e-way bills. Investigate duplicate invoices and goods received at branches with different GST registrations.

IT, consulting and professional services

Review software subscriptions, cloud services, office rent, professional fees, travel and employee benefits. Foreign vendor payments may require import-of-service and RCM analysis. Separate personal subscriptions from business accounts.

Real estate developers

Assess project-specific tax treatment, residential/commercial classification, rate conditions, restrictions on ITC and the relevant period. Keep separate ledgers and evidence for projects subject to different tax regimes or rate conditions.

Hospitals, education and financial services

Because exempt and taxable outputs may coexist, identify direct attribution and calculate common credit under the prescribed rules. Do not apply a simple ratio to all expenditure without first segregating direct credit and excluded amounts.

Hotels, restaurants and event businesses

Separate food/catering, room supplies, banquet services, employee meals, complimentary supplies and assets. Blocked-credit clauses and exceptions need transaction-specific review.

Transport, logistics and e-commerce

Reconcile freight, vehicle expenses, warehouse services, platform charges, TCS statements and multi-state registrations. Determine whether each expense is business input, blocked credit, RCM or common credit.

19. Worked examples: accounting entry, GST test and control

Example A — ordinary eligible purchase

Taxable purchase ₹2,00,000 plus GST ₹36,000 for taxable business use. Subject to all statutory conditions, the accounting entry may debit purchase/expense ₹2,00,000 and eligible ITC ₹36,000, crediting the supplier ₹2,36,000. Match the invoice to receipt, 2B and payment ageing.

Example B — mixed taxable and exempt activity

A business has direct credit for taxable production, direct credit for exempt activity and shared office expenses. Claim directly eligible credit, exclude directly ineligible credit, and compute the common component under Rules 42/43. Do not use a single turnover ratio against all ITC without the required segregation.

Example C — supplier credit note

Original ITC was claimed; supplier later issues a valid GST credit note. Link the note to the original invoice, determine the credit reversal, reconcile the supplier statement and the recipient return, and retain proof of the calculation.

Example D — invoice not in 2B

Books show a genuine purchase and goods receipt, but the invoice is absent from GSTR-2B. Flag it as a mismatch, request supplier correction, review the statutory conditions and time limit, and keep the evidence. Do not silently include it in eligible ITC just to force a reconciliation.

Example E — invoice unpaid beyond 180 days

Run invoice-level payment allocation, determine the unpaid value and related tax, apply Rule 37 as applicable, and track later payment and permitted re-availment. Keep this distinct from Rule 37A and from substantive blocked-credit issues.

Example F — asset with GST component

For a business machine, verify invoice and use, check whether any Section 17(5) restriction applies, determine common-credit treatment if relevant, and confirm the GST component is not also claimed as income-tax depreciation cost where ITC is taken.

20. Reversal, reclaim and return reporting controls

Every reversal should be linked to its legal reason, original invoice, original ITC claim, tax period, tax head, calculation, return disclosure and reviewer. A single generic “ITC reversal” ledger obscures whether the issue is a credit note, 180-day non-payment, supplier return default, common-credit apportionment or blocked credit.

Reclaim should not be made merely because a vendor promises to correct a return. Verify that the event required by law has actually occurred and that the recipient remains within the permitted time limit.

21. Responding to ITC mismatch notices and audit objections

For a notice alleging excess ITC, first classify the allegation: mismatch with 2B, blocked credit, non-receipt, supplier default, duplicate claim, 180-day non-payment, Rule 37A, common-credit error or time-barred claim. A single blanket reply is rarely effective where the notice combines multiple issues.

Suggested reply structure

  1. Reference the notice number, period, provision and date of service.
  2. Summarize the allegation and the amount proposed under each issue.
  3. Provide an issue-wise reconciliation from notice figure to books and returns.
  4. Explain facts and legal position separately for each disputed item.
  5. Attach indexed evidence: invoices, receipt, payment, 2B, ledger, calculation and correspondence.
  6. Identify amounts already reversed/paid and explain whether the payment is admitted or disputed.
  7. Request relied-upon documents, reasonable hearing and a speaking order where relevant.
  8. File through the prescribed channel within the stated deadline and preserve acknowledgement.
Do not ignore deadlines

The response deadline and procedural route depend on the document, provision, tax period and stage. A pre-notice intimation, show-cause notice, audit observation and adjudication order are not interchangeable. Verify the actual form and statutory provision before choosing the response.

22. Monthly ITC close checklist

  • Download and archive GSTR-2B and relevant IMS data.
  • Reconcile purchase register to 2B at invoice and tax-head level.
  • Check invoice dates, GSTIN, place of supply and tax rate.
  • Validate goods receipt/service evidence for material transactions.
  • Screen blocked credits and personal/non-business use.
  • Update Rule 37 payment ageing and Rule 37A supplier-return monitoring separately.
  • Review RCM liability and corresponding credit eligibility.
  • Review credit notes, amendments, debit notes and duplicate claims.
  • Prepare Rule 42/43 calculations and track annual true-up.
  • Reconcile ITC claimed, reversed and reclaimed in GSTR-3B to ledgers.
  • Document pending mismatches, vendor owners, target dates and next actions.
  • Obtain maker-checker approval and lock the monthly file after review.

Recommended ITC register fields

Financial year, month, GSTIN, supplier name, invoice number, invoice date, taxable value, IGST, CGST, SGST, cess, 2B status, IMS action, receipt status, business purpose, Section 17(5) flag, RCM flag, payment due date, 180-day status, Rule 37A status, ITC claimed, ITC reversed, reclaim date, evidence path, reviewer and remarks.

23. Common mistakes and how to prevent them

Claiming all 2B entries

Prevent with a separate eligibility and blocked-credit review.

Using only ledger balances

Trace invoices to receipt, business use, returns and payments.

Mixing reversal reasons

Use distinct reason codes for Rule 37, Rule 37A, Rule 42/43 and Section 17(5).

Ignoring credit notes

Match notes to original invoices and actual ITC availed.

Wrong time-limit assumption

Record the applicable legal deadline per financial year and invoice cohort.

Unsupported manual adjustments

Keep a calculation sheet, source data, maker-checker sign-off and return linkage.

24. Frequently asked questions

Does appearing in GSTR-2B automatically make ITC eligible?

No. The statement is a key reconciliation source, but the recipient must also satisfy the applicable eligibility conditions and restrictions.

Can ITC be claimed if goods were not received?

Generally, receipt is a core statutory condition. The evidence and specific legal circumstances must be examined; an invoice alone is not enough.

Are all employee expenses blocked?

No. Section 17(5) specifies categories and exceptions. Review each expense type, its purpose and the exact provision.

Is Rule 37 the same as Rule 37A?

No. Rule 37 concerns failure to pay the supplier within 180 days; Rule 37A addresses a specified supplier-return filing situation. Maintain separate tracking.

Can ITC be claimed on capital goods?

Potentially, subject to general conditions, blocked-credit restrictions, business use and the prohibition on claiming income-tax depreciation on the GST component for which ITC is taken.

Can credit be reclaimed after a reversal?

Sometimes, where the applicable provision permits it and the required event/conditions are satisfied. Keep evidence and check the time limit.

What should be done when a supplier does not report an invoice?

Reconcile the invoice, contact the supplier, document the follow-up, check current portal mechanisms and statutory time limits, and do not claim solely on the basis of a purchase ledger entry.

Can this guide be used for every financial year?

Use it as a practical framework, but verify the statutory text, notifications, circulars and deadlines applicable to the specific tax period. GST law and portal procedures can change.
