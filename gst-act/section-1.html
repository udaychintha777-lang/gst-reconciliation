CGST ACT • CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY

Section 1 — Short Title, Extent and Commencement

Section 1 is the opening provision of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017. It establishes the formal name of the legislation, its territorial reach and the legal mechanism by which its provisions come into force.

Section 1 at a Glance

ProvisionSection 1
ChapterChapter I — Preliminary
SubjectShort title, extent & commencement

Although Section 1 does not itself impose GST, prescribe a rate, determine input tax credit or create a procedural obligation, it is important whenever the applicability of the Act has to be established. In particular, commencement becomes significant when examining whether a particular statutory provision was legally in force on a particular date.

1. What does Section 1 do?

Section 1 performs three basic functions. First, it gives the legislation its formal short title. Second, it specifies the territorial extent of the Act. Third, it lays down the framework for bringing the Act, or individual provisions of the Act, into force.

This makes Section 1 different from the substantive provisions that follow it. It is a foundational provision used to understand the legal starting point of the statute and the date from which a particular provision can operate.

2. Short Title — Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017

Sub-section (1) provides the formal name of the legislation as the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017.

A short title is useful because legislation is frequently referred to in contracts, tax opinions, departmental communications, notices, orders, audit observations and judicial proceedings. The short title provides a consistent legal reference to the statute.

3. Territorial Extent — Whole of India

Sub-section (2) states that the Act extends to the whole of India. Therefore, the territorial scope of the CGST Act is not confined to a particular State or Union Territory.

The practical operation of GST, however, depends on the nature of the transaction and the interaction of the CGST Act with the other GST laws. For example, whether a supply is intra-State or inter-State can determine whether CGST with State GST or IGST becomes relevant. Section 1 should therefore be read as the territorial foundation of the Act rather than as a standalone charging provision.

4. Commencement — When does the Act come into force?

Sub-section (3) provides that the Act comes into force on the date appointed by the Central Government through a notification published in the Official Gazette.

This is important because the date on which Parliament enacted a law and the date on which a particular provision becomes operational are not necessarily the same. The commencement mechanism allows the Government to specify the effective date through notification.

Practical principle: For a historical GST issue, do not assume that every provision of the CGST Act became operational on the same date. The effective date of the particular provision must be checked.

5. Different Provisions Can Commence on Different Dates

The proviso to Section 1(3) expressly permits different dates to be appointed for different provisions of the Act. It also explains how a reference to the “commencement of this Act” is to be understood in a provision that has its own commencement date.

This means that commencement has to be examined at the provision level whenever the legal question depends upon a historical date. A section may have been enacted as part of the CGST Act but become enforceable only from the date specified in the relevant commencement notification.

6. Important Commencement Chronology

The CGST Act was brought into operation in stages. The initial commencement notifications appointed different dates for different groups of provisions. This historical distinction remains relevant when reviewing old transactions, returns, assessments, audit periods, notices and litigation.

22 June 2017
Certain provisions, including Sections 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 22 to 30, 139, 146 and 164, were brought into force from this date through Notification No. 01/2017-Central Tax dated 19 June 2017.
1 July 2017
A large group of substantive and procedural provisions was brought into force from 1 July 2017 through Notification No. 09/2017-Central Tax dated 28 June 2017.
1 October 2018
Sections 51 and 52 were subsequently brought into force from 1 October 2018 through the respective commencement notifications.
Why this matters: When analysing an old GST matter, the relevant law is the law applicable on the date under examination. A later amendment or a later commencement date should not automatically be applied to an earlier period.

7. Why the Commencement Date Matters in Practice

Commencement is especially important in professional tax work because GST disputes often relate to a specific financial year, tax period or transaction date.

  • Historical compliance: determine whether the provision was in force for the relevant tax period.
  • GST audit: verify the legal provision applicable during the period being audited.
  • Departmental notices: examine whether the provision relied upon was operational on the relevant date.
  • Assessments and adjudication: identify the law that legally applied when the event occurred.
  • Litigation: distinguish the original commencement date from later amendments and their effective dates.
  • Internal tax review: avoid applying present-day provisions mechanically to historical transactions.

8. Commencement of the Act vs. Effective Date of an Amendment

A later amendment to a GST provision can have its own effective date. Therefore, two separate questions should be kept in mind:

  1. When did the original provision come into force?
  2. When did the later amendment to that provision become effective?

This distinction is particularly important for historical tax positions. The fact that the law currently contains a particular wording does not by itself establish that the same wording applied during an earlier period.

9. Statutory Text of Section 1

The provision is reproduced below for ease of reference:

1. Short title, extent and commencement.—

(1) This Act may be called the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017.

(2) It extends to the whole of India.

(3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification
in the Official Gazette, appoint:

Provided that different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this Act and
any reference in any such provision to the commencement of this Act shall be construed
as a reference to the coming into force of that provision.

The statutory wording above is provided for legal-reference convenience. For any specific dispute, filing, notice or litigation matter, the applicable amended text, commencement notification and other relevant legal instruments should be verified.

10. How to Read Section 1 as a Tax Professional

Section 1 should be approached as a three-step legal check:

Step 1 — Identify the law

Confirm that the issue falls within the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017.

Step 2 — Identify the relevant date

Fix the transaction, tax period, notice or event date before selecting the applicable provision.

Step 3 — Verify commencement

Check whether the particular provision was in force on that date.

Step 4 — Check amendments

Where the provision was amended, determine the effective date of the amendment separately.

11. Key Takeaways

  • Section 1 is the opening provision of the CGST Act and establishes its basic legal framework.
  • The formal short title is the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017.
  • The Act extends to the whole of India.
  • The Central Government brings provisions into force through notification in the Official Gazette.
  • Different provisions can have different commencement dates.
  • For historical GST analysis, the commencement date of the specific provision is important.
  • A later amendment and its effective date must be distinguished from the original commencement of the provision.
  • Section 1 is therefore particularly relevant to audit, assessment, notices, historical compliance reviews and litigation.
