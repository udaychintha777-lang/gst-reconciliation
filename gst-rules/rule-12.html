CGST Rule 12 – Registration for Persons Required to Deduct or Collect Tax

A practical guide to GST registration for tax deductors under Section 51 and tax collectors under Section 52, including FORM GST REG-07, verification, three-working-day grant, non-physical-presence applications and cancellation through REG-08.

Section 51GST tax deduction

1. What Rule 12 Is About

Rule 12 provides the registration procedure for persons who are required to deduct GST under Section 51 or collect GST at source under Section 52. This is a specialised registration process and should not be confused with ordinary taxpayer registration under Rules 8 to 10.

Simple meaning: If an organisation is legally required to operate as a GST TDS deductor or GST TCS collector, it has a separate registration workflow using FORM GST REG-07.

CBIC's registration framework also makes clear that a separate GSTIN is required for a person registered as a tax deductor, even where that person already has another GST registration for its own taxable business.

2. Rule 12 – Clause-by-Clause Explanation

Rule 12(1) – Application in FORM GST REG-07

Any person required to deduct tax under Section 51 or collect tax at source under Section 52 submits an electronically signed/verified application in FORM GST REG-07 through the common portal, either directly or through a notified Facilitation Centre.

The registration is therefore tied to the statutory TDS/TCS responsibility. It is not simply an optional registration selected because an organisation wants another GSTIN.

Rule 12(1A) – No physical presence in the State/UT

Where a person applies for registration to deduct or collect tax in a State or Union territory in which the person does not have a physical presence, the application contains the State/UT information required under the rule.

In practical terms, the applicant identifies the State/UT for the TDS/TCS registration in Part A of REG-07 and provides the State/UT in which its principal place of business is located in Part B, which may be different.

Practical point: Do not reject the possibility of a TDS/TCS registration merely because the deductor/collector does not maintain a conventional physical office in the State where the relevant registration is required. Rule 12 specifically addresses this situation.

Rule 12(2) – Grant of registration

The proper officer may grant registration after due verification and issue the registration certificate in FORM GST REG-06 within three working days from submission of the application.

This is different from the ordinary registration workflow because Rule 12 specifically prescribes its own grant mechanism for TDS/TCS registrations.

Rule 12(3) – Cancellation when TDS/TCS liability ends

If, after enquiry or another proceeding under the GST law, the proper officer is satisfied that a registered person is no longer liable to deduct tax under Section 51 or collect tax under Section 52, the officer may cancel the registration.

The cancellation is communicated electronically in FORM GST REG-08, and the prescribed cancellation procedure must be followed.

If the underlying TDS/TCS obligation subsequently ceases, the registration should not simply remain dormant indefinitely. The continued legal basis for the registration should be reviewed and the applicable cancellation process followed where required.

5. Who Should Pay Particular Attention to Rule 12?

  • Government departments and specified government bodies responsible for GST TDS.
  • Specified local authorities and other entities covered by the applicable Section 51 provisions.
  • E-commerce operators and other persons covered by Section 52 TCS provisions.
  • Entities that have a TDS/TCS role in a State/UT different from the State where their principal business presence is located.
  • Finance teams managing multiple GST registrations where one registration is for the organisation's own business and another is for GST TDS/TCS.
Important: Whether an entity is actually required to deduct or collect GST depends on the applicable Section 51/52 provisions, notifications and the nature of the transaction/person involved. Rule 12 provides the registration mechanism; it does not independently create the underlying TDS/TCS liability.

6. Practical Example – Government TDS

Example: A government entity awards a works contract and is required to deduct GST TDS under Section 51. The entity should obtain the appropriate GST TDS registration using REG-07 and maintain the TDS registration separately from any GST registration it may hold for its own taxable activities.

The accounts team should then ensure that contracts, invoices, deductions, TDS payment, GST TDS returns and certificates are mapped to the correct TDS registration.

7. Practical Example – E-Commerce TCS

Example: An e-commerce operator covered by Section 52 is required to collect tax at source on supplies made through the electronic platform, subject to the statutory conditions. The operator uses the Rule 12 registration mechanism for the relevant TCS responsibility.

The TCS registration should be distinguished from the operator's own GST registration used for its taxable supplies and other business activities.

8. Multiple GST Registrations – Why the TDS/TCS GSTIN Matters

A large organisation may have several ordinary GSTINs because it operates in multiple States, plus a separate GST registration for its role as a TDS deductor or TCS collector. These registrations should not be merged in ERP merely because the PAN is common.

9. REG-07 Application – Internal Checklist

  1. Confirm whether the organisation is actually covered by Section 51 or Section 52.
  2. Identify the correct State/UT for the TDS/TCS registration.
  3. Confirm PAN and legal name details.
  4. Identify the authorised signatory.
  5. For a no-physical-presence case, correctly identify the State/UT information required by Rule 12(1A).
  6. Submit REG-07 electronically with the required verification.
  7. Save the application acknowledgement and registration trail.
  8. After approval, download and verify REG-06.
  9. Configure the TDS/TCS GSTIN separately in ERP and compliance systems.

10. Three-Working-Day Timeline – What It Means

Rule 12(2) states that the proper officer may grant registration after due verification and issue REG-06 within three working days from submission of the application.

The phrase “after due verification” matters. The timeline should not be understood as permission to ignore deficiencies or as a substitute for accurate application data.

Finance-team practice: Track the application from submission through approval and immediately verify REG-06. If the organisation has a statutory TDS/TCS obligation that is about to commence, registration should be planned in advance rather than filed after deductions/collections have already started.

11. What Happens When TDS/TCS Liability Stops?

Rule 12(3) addresses the situation where the registered person is no longer liable to deduct tax under Section 51 or collect tax under Section 52.

The proper officer may cancel the registration after enquiry or another proceeding under the Act, and cancellation is communicated in FORM GST REG-08.

From a governance perspective, the finance team should document the reason the underlying TDS/TCS role ended. This is especially important where an organisation, contract, platform model or statutory designation changes.

12. Common Mistakes Under Rule 12

  • Assuming an ordinary business GSTIN automatically covers GST TDS responsibility.
  • Failing to obtain the separate TDS/TCS registration when legally required.
  • Using the ordinary business GSTIN instead of the TDS/TCS GSTIN in compliance records.
  • Confusing income-tax TDS registration/TAN with GST TDS registration.
  • Ignoring the special procedure for a State/UT where the person has no physical presence.
  • Not verifying REG-06 after approval.
  • Continuing a TDS/TCS GST registration without reviewing whether the underlying statutory responsibility still exists.
  • Keeping TDS/TCS transactions mixed with ordinary outward-supply accounting in ERP.

13. GST TDS and Income-Tax TDS Are Different

This distinction is essential for finance teams. GST TDS under Section 51 is a GST mechanism. Income-tax TDS under the Income-tax Act is a separate tax mechanism with its own TAN, sections, rates, returns and compliance procedures.

14. Audit & Departmental Notice Relevance

During GST audit or departmental verification, an entity may be asked to establish why it was registered as a TDS/TCS deductor/collector, whether deductions/collections were correctly made and whether the registration remained relevant.

Maintain a dedicated Rule 12 file containing:

  • REG-07 application;
  • acknowledgement and registration details;
  • REG-06 certificate;
  • Section 51/52 applicability documentation;
  • TDS/TCS workings;
  • payment and return records;
  • certificates/communications issued where applicable; and
  • evidence supporting cessation if the registration is later cancelled.

16. Frequently Asked Questions

Is separate GST registration required for GST TDS?

Yes, persons required to deduct GST under Section 51 use the separate registration mechanism under Rule 12 and FORM GST REG-07. An ordinary GST registration does not simply substitute for the TDS registration.

Which form is used for GST TDS/TCS registration?

FORM GST REG-07 is used for the Rule 12 registration application.

Which certificate is issued?

Registration is issued in FORM GST REG-06 after the prescribed verification process.

How quickly can Rule 12 registration be granted?

Rule 12(2) provides that the proper officer may grant registration after due verification and issue REG-06 within three working days from submission of the application.

Can GST TDS registration be obtained where there is no physical presence in the State?

Yes. Rule 12(1A) specifically provides the application mechanism for such cases and requires the relevant State/UT details to be entered in REG-07.

What is REG-08?

REG-08 is the form through which cancellation of a Rule 12 registration is communicated when the person is no longer liable to deduct or collect tax under the relevant GST provisions.

Is GST TDS the same as income-tax TDS?

No. GST TDS under Section 51 and income-tax TDS are separate statutory mechanisms. Their registrations, returns, payments and compliance records should not be confused.

What is the next Rule after Rule 12?

Rule 13 deals with grant of registration to non-resident taxable persons under the GST registration framework.

18. Source & Verification Note

This page is based on the current Rule 12 framework for GST tax deductor and tax collector registration, including FORM GST REG-07, the three-working-day grant provision and cancellation communication in FORM GST REG-08. The practical explanations distinguish the registration mechanism from the underlying liability under Sections 51 and 52.

The existence and scope of a TDS/TCS obligation should always be checked against the applicable statutory provisions, notifications and the facts of the organisation. This page is an educational compliance guide and not a substitute for professional advice in a disputed matter.

19. Practical Takeaway

Rule 12 is the dedicated GST registration gateway for TDS deductors and TCS collectors. The key controls are simple but important: identify the correct Section 51/52 obligation, apply through REG-07, verify the State/UT details, obtain REG-06, keep the TDS/TCS GSTIN separate from ordinary business GSTINs, and review the registration when the underlying TDS/TCS responsibility ends.
