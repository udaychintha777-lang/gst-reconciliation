1. Why Section 110 matters

Section 109 explains the constitution and Bench structure of the GST Appellate Tribunal. Section 110 goes one level deeper: it deals with the people who constitute that Tribunal.

It sets eligibility standards for the President, Judicial Members, Technical Member (Centre) and Technical Member (State), provides the appointment mechanism, deals with service conditions and tenure, and establishes safeguards relating to resignation, removal and post-office restrictions.

This is important because GST litigation is not decided only by the existence of a forum. The credibility and functioning of an appellate tribunal also depend on the qualifications, selection process, tenure and independence of its members.

Practical takeaway: Section 110 is mainly an institutional provision, but it has a direct connection with the confidence businesses place in the GST appellate process.

2. Section 110 in simple language

In simple terms, Section 110 answers five questions:

Who can serve?It prescribes eligibility for the President and different categories of Members.
Who appoints them?The Government makes appointments based on recommendations of the statutory Search-cum-Selection Committee.
How long can they serve?The section prescribes a four-year term, subject to specified age limits and re-appointment rules.
When can they leave?It provides for resignation, removal and suspension in specified circumstances.

The current text also includes a minimum age requirement for appointment and specific committee structures for selecting Technical Members (State) and other Tribunal appointments.

5. Qualification for President

Section 110 provides that a person is not qualified for appointment as President unless the person has been a Judge of the Supreme Court or is or has been the Chief Justice of a High Court.

This is a deliberately senior judicial qualification. The President's role is not limited to hearing individual tax appeals; the President also has important responsibilities in the administration and functioning of the Tribunal.

Practical significance

  • The President's office is designed to have high-level judicial experience.
  • The President's position is important for allocation and administration of Tribunal business.
  • In a vacancy or temporary inability of the President, Section 110 provides an acting arrangement so that the Tribunal can continue functioning.

6. Qualification for Judicial Member

Section 110 permits appointment of a Judicial Member through specified judicial or litigation experience routes.

The current provision recognises:

  • a person who has been a Judge of a High Court;
  • a person who has served as a District Judge or Additional District Judge for a combined period of ten years; and
  • an advocate with ten years of substantial experience in litigation involving indirect taxes in the specified appellate/constitutional forums.

This is particularly relevant for GST because many disputes require a combination of statutory interpretation, precedent analysis, evidence assessment and procedural fairness.

Do not confuse general legal experience with the statutory qualification. Section 110 specifies the type and duration of experience required. A person's general professional seniority by itself does not automatically satisfy the provision.

7. Technical Member (Centre)

The Technical Member (Centre) qualification is designed around senior Central Government tax/service experience.

The current provision refers to membership of the Indian Revenue (Customs and Indirect Taxes) Service, Group A, or the specified All India Service route, together with the prescribed experience in administration of an existing law or GST in the Central Government and the required period of Group A service.

Why technical expertise matters

GST disputes can involve highly technical administration issues: return matching, classification, valuation, ITC conditions, tax payment records, exemption conditions and departmental procedures. A technical member brings administrative and tax-system experience that complements judicial analysis.

8. Technical Member (State)

The Technical Member (State) qualification is connected with senior State Government or All India Service experience and requires the prescribed rank and experience in GST, an existing law, or finance and taxation.

The provision also allows the State Government, on the recommendation of the GST Council, to relax the Group A service requirement in specified circumstances where an eligible person has the required overall Government service but the State does not have a person satisfying the normal Group A threshold.

Practical point: State-specific eligibility can depend on the applicable notification. Before relying on a qualification statement in a professional opinion, verify the latest notification applicable to the State.

9. Minimum age requirement

Section 110 contains a minimum age safeguard: a person who has not completed 50 years of age is not eligible for appointment as the President or Member.

This is separate from the maximum age limits applicable during the term of office. Therefore, an eligibility review should consider both the person's age on appointment and the applicable retirement/maximum-age provision.

10. Appointment and re-appointment

The President, Judicial Member, Technical Member (Centre) and Technical Member (State) are appointed or re-appointed by the Government on the recommendations of the applicable Search-cum-Selection Committee.

This means the appointment is not simply an administrative nomination by one department. Section 110 establishes a structured selection mechanism involving senior judicial and government representatives.

Why this matters for Tribunal users

  • It creates a defined statutory appointment process.
  • It separates eligibility from the final appointment decision.
  • It provides a formal mechanism for selecting candidates.
  • It supports continuity through re-appointment provisions within the statutory limits.

11. Search-cum-Selection Committee

Section 110 creates different committee arrangements depending on the position being filled.

A. Technical Member (State)

The committee includes senior judicial and State Government representatives. It is chaired by the Chief Justice of the High Court in whose jurisdiction the State Bench is located. The structure also includes the senior-most Judicial Member in the State, the State Chief Secretary and specified senior State officials.

B. Other appointments

For other Tribunal appointments, the committee is chaired by the Chief Justice of India or a Supreme Court Judge nominated by the Chief Justice of India. The statutory composition also includes nominated Central/State Government representatives, the relevant Tribunal President or specified former judicial authority, and the Revenue Department's Secretary as Member Secretary.

Why this is useful to understand: Section 110 is not only about individual qualifications. It also creates a selection architecture intended to bring judicial and executive perspectives into the appointment process.

12. Panel and committee safeguards

The Chairperson has the casting vote, while the Member Secretary does not have a vote.

The committee is required to recommend a panel of two names for appointment or re-appointment of the President or a Member.

The Act also provides that an appointment or re-appointment is not invalid merely because of a vacancy or defect in the constitution of the Search-cum-Selection Committee.

Practical interpretation

These provisions are aimed at ensuring that an administrative vacancy or procedural defect in the committee's composition does not automatically destabilise appointments already made under the statutory framework.

13. Salary and conditions of service

Section 110 provides that the salary of the President and Members is to be prescribed. Their allowances and other conditions of service are linked to those applicable to Central Government officers carrying the same pay.

The section also contains an important protection: after appointment, the salary, allowances and other terms and conditions of service should not be varied to the disadvantage of the President or Member.

A separate provision addresses house-rent reimbursement where the President or Member takes a house on rent, subject to prescribed limits and conditions.

Institutional significance: Service-condition protection helps separate the terms of office from short-term administrative decisions after appointment.

15. What happens if the President's office is vacant or the President cannot function?

Section 110 provides continuity arrangements.

If the President's office becomes vacant because of death, resignation or another reason, the Judicial Member of the Principal Bench, or in the absence of a Judicial Member, the senior-most Technical Member of the Principal Bench, acts as President until the newly appointed President assumes office.

Similarly, if the President cannot discharge functions because of absence, illness or another cause, the same statutory sequence applies until the President resumes duties.

Business impact: This prevents the Tribunal's administrative functioning from automatically stopping merely because the President is temporarily unavailable.

16. Resignation

The President or a Member may resign by giving written notice addressed to the Government.

However, resignation does not ordinarily mean immediate departure. The person continues in office until the earliest of:

  1. expiry of three months from the date the Government receives the notice;
  2. the duly appointed successor entering office; or
  3. expiry of the person's term.

Why this matters operationally

A controlled transition is important in a Tribunal because pending matters, administrative responsibilities and Bench continuity should not be disrupted unnecessarily.

17. Removal and suspension

Section 110 permits removal on specified grounds, on the recommendations of the Search-cum-Selection Committee.

The listed grounds include:

  • being adjudged an insolvent;
  • conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude;
  • becoming physically or mentally incapable of acting as President or Member;
  • acquiring a financial or other interest likely to affect the person's functions prejudicially; and
  • abusing the position in a manner that makes continuation prejudicial to the public interest.

For removal on the grounds relating to conflicting interests or abuse of position, the person must be informed of the charges and given an opportunity of being heard.

Where removal proceedings have been initiated, the Government may, on the Committee's recommendation, suspend the President or Judicial/Technical Member.

Important distinction: Suspension during removal proceedings is not the same thing as final removal. Section 110 provides separate mechanisms for each.

18. Restrictions after leaving office

Section 110 also contains a post-office restriction. Subject to Article 220 of the Constitution, a former President or Member is restricted from appearing, acting or pleading before the Principal Bench or State Bench where that person served as President or Member.

This is an important professional-conduct safeguard because the former office-holder may possess institutional knowledge of the Bench and its working.

Practical implication for tax professionals

If a business is considering engaging a former Tribunal office-holder or professional associated with the Tribunal, the engagement should be checked against the applicable statutory restrictions rather than relying only on general professional-eligibility assumptions.

19. Why businesses should care about Section 110

At first glance, Section 110 looks like a provision meant only for Tribunal administration. In practice, businesses should understand it because the Tribunal is an important stage in GST litigation.

20. Practical examples

Example 1 — ITC dispute involving thousands of invoices

A company receives an adverse appellate order involving ₹2 crore of disputed ITC. The management decides to pursue the matter further.

What the finance team should do:

  • freeze the disputed invoice population;
  • reconcile it with the purchase register and GSTR-2B;
  • identify which statutory condition is disputed for each group of invoices;
  • preserve supplier GSTIN, invoice, payment and receipt evidence;
  • calculate the financial exposure separately for tax, interest and penalty; and
  • give counsel a clean evidence index.

Example 2 — Classification dispute in manufacturing

A manufacturer disputes the department's classification of a product.

Better preparation: technical specifications, product literature, manufacturing process, tariff analysis, sample invoices and customer-use evidence should be organised before the Tribunal hearing.

Example 3 — Infrastructure company with several GSTINs

A contractor operates in Telangana, Maharashtra and Karnataka. A classification or ITC issue arises only in one GSTIN.

Correct approach: identify the exact order, jurisdiction, factual matrix and appeal history of that GSTIN. Do not automatically treat another State's litigation file as identical.

Example 4 — Change in professional representation

A business changes its tax counsel before Tribunal proceedings.

Best practice: hand over the complete case chronology, order copies, limitation calculation, grounds, evidence index, financial exposure and hearing history instead of giving the new counsel only the latest order.

21. Practical litigation impact

Section 110 does not itself tell a taxpayer how to file an appeal. That is primarily dealt with by the Tribunal appeal provisions and rules. But Section 110 tells the taxpayer something important about the forum: it is a structured statutory institution with defined judicial and technical membership.

Therefore, a strong Tribunal case should be built in a way that allows both the legal and technical aspects of the dispute to be understood clearly.

Legal issueWhat provision, notification or principle is disputed?
Commercial factsWhat actually happened in the business?
EvidenceWhich documents prove those facts?
Financial impactWhat tax, ITC, interest or penalty amount is involved?

23. Common mistakes

  1. Using the original 2017 qualification rules. Section 110 has been amended. Always use the current text.
  2. Ignoring the minimum age rule. Eligibility must be checked at appointment.
  3. Confusing qualification with appointment. Meeting eligibility does not itself create an appointment.
  4. Assuming every Tribunal member has the same professional background. Section 110 distinguishes Judicial, Technical (Centre) and Technical (State) Members.
  5. Ignoring State-specific notifications. Technical Member (State) requirements can involve State-level notification and prescribed relaxation.
  6. Assuming resignation is immediate. The three-month/successor/term mechanism must be considered.
  7. Ignoring post-office restrictions. Former members have statutory restrictions on appearing before specified Benches.
  8. Building a case without financial reconciliation. The legal team should know exactly how the disputed amount ties to the books and GST returns.

27. Frequently Asked Questions

What is Section 110 of the CGST Act?

Section 110 deals with the President and Members of the GST Appellate Tribunal, including their qualifications, appointment, conditions of service, tenure, resignation, removal and related matters.

Who can become President of the GST Appellate Tribunal?

The current Section 110 requires the President to have been a Judge of the Supreme Court or to be or have been the Chief Justice of a High Court.

What is the minimum age for appointment?

A person who has not completed 50 years of age is not eligible for appointment as President or Member under the current Section 110.

How long is the President's term?

The President holds office for four years from entering office or until reaching 70 years, whichever is earlier, with eligibility for re-appointment for a period not exceeding two years subject to the age limit.

How long do Tribunal Members serve?

Judicial, Technical (Centre) and Technical (State) Members hold office for four years or until reaching 67 years, whichever is earlier, with re-appointment possible for a period not exceeding two years subject to the age limit.

Can an advocate become a Judicial Member?

Yes. The current provision recognises an advocate with ten years of substantial experience in litigation involving indirect taxes in the specified forums, subject to the other statutory eligibility requirements.

Can a Technical Member (State) qualification be relaxed?

The section provides a mechanism for the State Government, on the recommendation of the GST Council, to relax the specified Group A service requirement in the circumstances described in the provision.

Can a Tribunal Member be removed?

Yes. Section 110 specifies grounds including insolvency, conviction involving moral turpitude, incapacity, prejudicial financial/other interest and abuse of position prejudicial to public interest, subject to the statutory procedure and hearing safeguard where prescribed.

Is resignation immediate?

No. The President or Member generally continues until three months after the Government receives the resignation notice, a successor enters office, or the term expires, whichever occurs earliest.

Does Section 110 tell a taxpayer how to file a GSTAT appeal?

No. Section 110 is mainly about the Tribunal's President and Members. The actual appeal mechanism is dealt with principally under Section 112 and the applicable rules.

Why should an accountant or finance manager understand Section 110?

Because Tribunal litigation often depends on finance-controlled evidence such as invoices, ledgers, reconciliations, contracts, tax payments and disputed-demand calculations. Understanding the appellate institution helps the finance team prepare a better litigation record.

29. Key takeaways

  • Section 110 deals with the President and Members of the GST Appellate Tribunal.
  • It prescribes distinct qualifications for the President, Judicial Member, Technical Member (Centre) and Technical Member (State).
  • A minimum age of 50 years applies for appointment as President or Member.
  • Appointments and re-appointments are made by the Government on recommendations of the statutory Search-cum-Selection Committee.
  • The section establishes different committee structures for Technical Member (State) and other Tribunal appointments.
  • The President has a four-year term or until 70 years, whichever is earlier, with limited re-appointment subject to the age limit.
  • Judicial and Technical Members have a four-year term or until 67 years, whichever is earlier, with limited re-appointment subject to the age limit.
  • Section 110 provides mechanisms for temporary continuity when the President's office is vacant or the President cannot perform functions.
  • Resignation, removal and suspension are governed by specific statutory conditions rather than informal administrative decisions.
  • Former office-holders are subject to specified restrictions on appearing before the Bench where they served.
  • For businesses, the practical lesson is to maintain a complete, evidence-based and financially reconciled GST litigation file before a matter reaches Tribunal stage.
