Section 152 of the CGST Act, 2017

Bar on disclosure of information. Understand how GST law protects information furnished for Sections 150 and 151, when information can be published or used in proceedings, the hearing safeguard and the public-interest exception.

Section 152 at a Glance

What does it protect?Information given for the purposes of Sections 150 and 151 from identifiable publication, subject to the statutory conditions and exceptions.
Consent ruleIdentifiable publication is generally restricted without previous written consent of the concerned person or authorised representative.
Hearing safeguardInformation cannot be used for proceedings under the CGST Act without giving the concerned person an opportunity of being heard, as provided in the current text.
In simple language: Section 152 is a confidentiality safeguard around information furnished under Sections 150 and 151. It restricts identifiable publication and regulates use of such information in GST proceedings, while allowing the Act's stated exceptions, including publication of information about a class of taxpayers or class of transactions where the Commissioner considers publication desirable in the public interest.

1. Current Statutory Text of Section 152

152. Bar on disclosure of information.—

(1) No information with respect to any matter given for the purposes of section 150 or section 151 shall, without the previous consent in writing of the concerned person or his authorised representative, be published in such manner so as to enable such particulars to be identified as referring to a particular person and no such information shall be used for the purpose of any proceedings under this Act without giving an opportunity of being heard to the person concerned.

(2) [Omitted.]

(3) Nothing in this section shall apply to the publication of any information relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions, if in the opinion of the Commissioner, it is desirable in the public interest to publish such information.
Current-law point: The current central CGST Act reflects the 2021 amendment changes effective from 1 January 2022: the words referring specifically to “any individual return or part thereof” were omitted, the hearing safeguard was inserted, and sub-section (2) was omitted.

2. Important 2022 Changes

Section 152 must be read in its post-1 January 2022 form. Older GST books and websites may reproduce the pre-amendment text and therefore can give an incomplete picture of the current provision.

AreaCurrent positionWhy it matters
Individual-return wordingThe words “of any individual return or part thereof” were omitted.The provision's wording was broadened beyond that older formulation.
Use in proceedingsThe current text says information shall not be used for proceedings under the Act without giving the concerned person an opportunity of being heard.This adds an express hearing safeguard.
Sub-section (2)Omitted with effect from 1 January 2022.Old explanations based on the former access restriction should not be presented as current Section 152(2).

These amendments were made through the Finance Act, 2021 provisions relating to Sections 151 and 152 and took effect from 1 January 2022.

Legal-library warning: If an article says Section 152(2) currently prohibits specified persons from accessing information collected under old Section 151, that is historical text. Current Section 152 does not contain that sub-section.

3. Section 152 Explained in Simple Language

Sections 150 and 151 allow GST authorities to obtain information. Section 152 addresses what happens to that information after it has been furnished.

The provision is based on a simple principle: information obtained for tax administration should not automatically become unrestricted public information identifying a particular person.

Information collectionSections 150 and 151 provide mechanisms for obtaining information.
Confidentiality / disclosureSection 152 restricts identifiable publication and regulates use in proceedings.
Public interestSection 152(3) permits publication of class-level information when the Commissioner considers it desirable in the public interest.

Therefore, Section 152 is not a general “taxpayer privacy law” covering every piece of information held by every GST officer. Its wording is specifically tied to information given for the purposes of Sections 150 and 151.

4. What Information Is Covered?

The current Section 152(1) applies to information “with respect to any matter” given for the purposes of Section 150 or Section 151.

Source provisionWhat it doesConnection with Section 152
Section 150Requires specified persons/record-holders to furnish information returns.Information furnished for that purpose receives the Section 152 protection.
Section 151Allows the Commissioner/authorised officer to order any person to furnish information connected with the Act.Information furnished pursuant to that power is within Section 152.

The provision therefore needs to be read together with the actual information request or information-return requirement. A person should identify whether the information was supplied for the purposes of Section 150 or 151 before automatically invoking Section 152.

5. Previous Written Consent

Section 152(1) says identifiable publication of covered information cannot ordinarily occur without the previous consent in writing of the concerned person or the person's authorised representative.

What does this mean?

  • The consent contemplated by the provision is previous consent.
  • It is required to be in writing.
  • It is linked to publication in a manner that enables the particulars to be identified as referring to a particular person.
Example: If information supplied for Section 150/151 purposes is proposed to be published in an identifiable manner, the consent requirement in Section 152(1) becomes relevant unless another statutory exception applies.
Do not overstate the rule: Section 152(3) separately permits publication relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions when the Commissioner considers it desirable in the public interest. The exact nature of the proposed publication therefore matters.

6. What Does “Identified” Mean?

The section is concerned with publication in a manner that enables particulars to be identified as referring to a particular person.

This is important because there is a difference between:

Identifiable informationClass-level information
Information that enables a particular person to be identified.Aggregated or class-based information relating to a group of taxpayers or transactions.
Section 152(1)'s consent restriction is directly relevant.Section 152(3) expressly addresses publication of class-level information where the Commissioner considers it desirable in the public interest.
Requires careful confidentiality review.May be publishable under the public-interest exception, subject to its conditions.
Practical caution: “Anonymous” does not necessarily mean non-identifiable. If a combination of published fields could reasonably reveal the particular taxpayer or transaction, confidentiality review may still be necessary.

7. Opportunity of Being Heard

A major current feature of Section 152(1) is that information covered by the provision cannot be used for proceedings under the CGST Act without giving the person concerned an opportunity of being heard.

What does this safeguard mean?

Where covered information is proposed to be used for proceedings under the Act, the affected person should have an opportunity to respond before the information is used for that statutory proceeding in the manner contemplated by Section 152.

This does not mean that every use of information requires a separate hearing before any administrative activity. The precise procedural requirements depend on the proceeding and the applicable provision. The important point is that Section 152 itself now contains an express hearing safeguard.

Example:

A third-party information record is furnished under Section 151 and later becomes relevant to a GST proceeding against a taxpayer. The authority should not simply treat the information as conclusively usable without regard to the Section 152 hearing safeguard and the procedure applicable to the underlying proceeding.

8. Public-Interest Publication Exception

Section 152(3) creates an important exception to the restriction in Section 152. It says that nothing in the section applies to publication of information relating to:

  • a class of taxable persons; or
  • a class of transactions;

where, in the opinion of the Commissioner, publication is desirable in the public interest.

QuestionSection 152(3) position
Can class-level information be published?Yes, where the statutory conditions are satisfied.
Does it have to identify one particular taxpayer?The exception is expressly framed around a class of taxable persons or class of transactions.
Who decides public-interest desirability?The Commissioner, according to the statutory wording.
Does this mean all Section 150/151 information is public?No. The exception is specific and should not be read as eliminating the general restriction in Section 152(1).

9. Omitted Sub-section (2)

One of the most important current-law points is that Section 152(2) has been omitted with effect from 1 January 2022.

Older versions contained a separate restriction concerning access to information or individual returns by persons not engaged in collection of statistics or compilation/computerisation under the old Section 151 framework.

That provision should not be reproduced as a current Section 152(2). The current section moves away from the old statistics-oriented wording and should be read in conjunction with the substituted Section 151 and the amended Section 152(1).

For accountants and tax professionals: When relying on a GST legal database, always check the amendment footnotes and effective dates. A pre-2022 PDF can be technically accurate for its date but misleading if presented as the current law.

11. Section 152 vs Section 151

Section 151Section 152
Power to call for information.Bar on disclosure of information.

Easy way to remember: Section 151 is about getting information; Section 152 is about protecting and controlling disclosure/use of certain information.

12. Section 152 vs Section 158

These provisions both concern information confidentiality, but their scopes are different.

Section 152Section 158
Specifically concerns information given for purposes of Sections 150 and 151.Deals more broadly with particulars contained in statements, returns, accounts, documents, evidence and records of proceedings under the Act, subject to statutory exceptions.
Focuses on identifiable publication and use in proceedings under the Act, with a hearing safeguard.Provides a broader confidentiality framework for public servants, with listed disclosure exceptions.
Includes a public-interest class publication provision.Also contains its own specific statutory disclosure exceptions, including public-interest class publication.

13. Section 152 vs Section 159

Section 159 separately deals with publication of information in respect of persons in certain cases. It is therefore important not to read Section 152 as an absolute prohibition on every publication of taxpayer-related information.

ProvisionCore idea
Section 152Controls disclosure/use of information furnished for Sections 150 and 151.
Section 159Allows publication of names and particulars in specified public-interest circumstances concerning proceedings or prosecution, subject to statutory conditions including the appeal-related safeguard for penalties.

Whether a particular publication is lawful depends on the exact statutory provision being used, the type of information and the applicable conditions.

14. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – Identifiable third-party information

A person provides transaction information under Section 151. The department proposes to publish the information in a way that identifies that particular person. Section 152(1)'s written-consent restriction becomes relevant unless a statutory exception applies.

Example 2 – Class-level statistics

The Commissioner wants to publish aggregated information about a class of taxable persons. Section 152(3) may permit such publication where the Commissioner considers it desirable in the public interest.

Example 3 – Class of transactions

The department publishes information about a broad category of transactions rather than identifying an individual taxpayer. The Section 152(3) public-interest provision should be examined.

Example 4 – Information used in proceedings

Information supplied under Section 151 becomes relevant to a proceeding under the CGST Act. The affected person must be given an opportunity of being heard as required by the current Section 152(1).

Example 5 – Old website article

A website says Section 152(2) prevents outsiders from accessing statistics collected under old Section 151. That may describe the pre-2022 law and should not be presented as the current Section 152(2), because sub-section (2) has been omitted.

Example 6 – Supplier data

A supplier's information is obtained through an information-return mechanism. A business should not assume that because the department has received the information it is automatically public or automatically conclusive for every tax question.

Example 7 – Internal publication

A company receives a tax-related information request. The finance team should control internal circulation of sensitive commercial records and provide access to employees based on business need and legal requirements.

Example 8 – Data anonymisation

A report removes the GSTIN but leaves a unique combination of transaction details that makes the taxpayer obvious. The team should assess whether the person can still be identified.

Example 9 – Public interest

A publication concerns a class of transactions and the Commissioner considers publication desirable in the public interest. Section 152(3) provides the statutory basis for examining such publication.

Example 10 – Consent

A person gives written consent for publication of specified information. The consent requirement in Section 152(1) should be considered along with the scope and wording of the consent and any other applicable law.

Example 11 – Information vs penalty

Information obtained under Section 151 does not itself become a penalty order. If a penalty proceeding follows, the taxpayer must be dealt with under the relevant substantive and procedural provisions.

Example 12 – Audit trail

A finance team sends information to the department under Section 151. It should retain the exact submitted file, acknowledgement, source reports and covering communication so that the disclosure can be reconstructed later.

15. Business Confidentiality Controls

Although Section 152 is primarily a statutory restriction on disclosure/use by the tax administration, businesses should also maintain disciplined controls when responding to information requests.

ControlRecommended practice
Access controlLimit sensitive GST response files to authorised personnel.
Submission registerRecord what information was provided, to whom, when and under which order/reference.
Source preservationKeep the source ledger/export/document from which the response was prepared.
Version controlDo not overwrite submitted files; preserve the final version exactly as furnished.
Confidentiality reviewIdentify trade secrets, customer information and other sensitive material before external submission.
Legal reviewEscalate complex disclosure questions to tax/legal personnel.
RetentionMaintain the order, response and acknowledgement according to the organisation's record-retention policy and applicable law.

16. Information Used in GST Proceedings

The current hearing safeguard is especially important when information furnished for Sections 150 or 151 is proposed to be used in proceedings under the CGST Act.

What a taxpayer should do

  1. Identify the information source.
  2. Compare it with the taxpayer's books and GST returns.
  3. Check whether the information relates to the correct GSTIN and period.
  4. Identify errors, duplicates, cancelled transactions or amended documents.
  5. Prepare supporting evidence.
  6. Respond within the applicable proceeding's deadline.
  7. Request clarification where the source or transaction is unclear.
  8. Maintain a complete response trail.
Important: Section 152's hearing safeguard does not mean every taxpayer automatically wins a dispute merely by questioning third-party data. It means the person concerned must be given the statutory opportunity of being heard before the covered information is used for proceedings under the Act.

17. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 – Treating Section 152 as an absolute secrecy rule

The section contains a general restriction but also expressly provides exceptions, including class-level publication in the public interest.

Mistake 2 – Using pre-2022 wording

Sub-section (2) was omitted and Section 152(1) was amended from 1 January 2022.

Mistake 3 – Ignoring the hearing safeguard

The current text expressly refers to an opportunity of being heard before covered information is used for proceedings under the Act.

Mistake 4 – Assuming consent is needed for every disclosure

The statutory wording specifically addresses identifiable publication and separately provides the public-interest class exception. Other GST provisions may also authorise disclosure.

Mistake 5 – Assuming aggregated information is always prohibited

Section 152(3) expressly addresses publication relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions.

Mistake 6 – Confusing Section 152 with Section 158

They have different scopes and different statutory structures.

Mistake 7 – Treating third-party data as conclusive

Information can be examined, reconciled and challenged with supporting evidence where appropriate.

Mistake 8 – Publishing internal tax data casually

Businesses should maintain their own confidentiality controls for sensitive information shared with tax authorities.

20. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 152 of the CGST Act?

Section 152 places restrictions on publication and use of information furnished for the purposes of Sections 150 and 151, subject to the statutory exceptions and safeguards.

2. What does “bar on disclosure of information” mean?

It means covered information cannot ordinarily be published in an identifiable manner without the previous written consent of the concerned person or authorised representative, subject to the statutory framework.

3. What information is covered by Section 152?

Information with respect to a matter given for the purposes of Section 150 or Section 151.

4. Is written consent required?

For publication in a manner that enables particulars to be identified as referring to a particular person, Section 152(1) requires previous written consent of the concerned person or authorised representative, unless the applicable statutory exception applies.

5. Can information be used in GST proceedings?

The current Section 152(1) says covered information shall not be used for proceedings under the Act without giving the concerned person an opportunity of being heard.

6. Does Section 152 prohibit all publication?

No. Section 152(3) permits publication of information relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions where, in the opinion of the Commissioner, publication is desirable in the public interest.

7. Is Section 152(2) currently in force?

No. Sub-section (2) was omitted with effect from 1 January 2022.

8. What changed from 1 January 2022?

The individual-return wording in Section 152(1) was omitted, the opportunity-of-being-heard safeguard was inserted, and sub-section (2) was omitted.

9. Does Section 152 apply to ordinary GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B data?

Section 152 is specifically framed around information given for the purposes of Sections 150 and 151. Other GST information may be subject to other confidentiality and disclosure provisions, including Section 158.

10. Is Section 152 the same as Section 158?

No. Section 152 specifically addresses information furnished for Sections 150 and 151, while Section 158 contains a broader confidentiality framework for particulars held in GST records and proceedings, subject to its exceptions.

11. Is Section 152 an absolute privacy right?

No. It is a statutory disclosure restriction with specific exceptions and should be read with other provisions of the CGST Act.

12. Can aggregated GST information be published?

Section 152(3) expressly permits publication of information relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions when the Commissioner considers it desirable in the public interest.

13. Does Section 152 protect information obtained under Section 151?

Yes. Section 152(1) expressly refers to information given for the purposes of both Section 150 and Section 151.

14. Does receiving a Section 151 order mean information will become public?

No. Section 152 specifically regulates disclosure and publication of information furnished for Section 151.

15. What should a business do when submitting information under Section 151?

Maintain a controlled submission process, preserve the exact data furnished and acknowledgement, and restrict internal access to sensitive response files.

16. Can a taxpayer challenge third-party information?

Where covered information is used in proceedings under the Act, the current Section 152(1) provides an opportunity of being heard. The taxpayer should reconcile the information with transaction records and respond under the applicable proceeding.

21. Quick Revision – Section 152

  • Section 152 = Bar on disclosure of information.
  • It specifically concerns information given for Sections 150 and 151.
  • Identifiable publication generally requires previous written consent of the concerned person or authorised representative.
  • Covered information cannot be used for proceedings under the Act without giving the concerned person an opportunity of being heard.
  • Section 152(2) is omitted with effect from 1 January 2022.
  • Section 152(3) permits publication relating to a class of taxable persons or class of transactions when the Commissioner considers it desirable in the public interest.
  • Do not use pre-2022 Section 152 wording as current law.
  • Section 152 is different from the broader confidentiality framework in Section 158.
  • Section 159 contains a separate statutory framework for publication in certain cases.
  • Section 152 is a disclosure safeguard, not a blanket prohibition on every form of GST information use.
