Rule 20 – Application for Cancellation of Registration

A practical guide to voluntarily applying for cancellation of GST registration. Understand FORM GST REG-16, the 30-day filing window, stock and capital-goods details, tax liability, reasons for cancellation, voluntary registration restrictions and the compliance steps before closing a GSTIN.

1. What is Rule 20?

Rule 20 prescribes the procedure for a registered person who seeks cancellation of GST registration under section 29(1). The application is made electronically in FORM GST REG-16 through the common portal.

Simple meaning: Rule 20 is the taxpayer-initiated cancellation route. It should be treated as a structured GST closure process—not simply as deleting a GSTIN from the accounting system.

Before applying, management should identify the legal reason for cancellation, determine the proposed cancellation date, calculate the tax/ITC liability connected with stock and capital goods, and preserve records required for the post-cancellation period.

3. When can cancellation be considered?

Section 29(1), read with the cancellation framework, covers circumstances such as:

  • discontinuance of business;
  • transfer of the business;
  • amalgamation, merger, demerger or other disposal;
  • change in constitution of the business;
  • death of a sole proprietor; or
  • the taxable person no longer being liable for registration under the applicable provisions.
Important: The reason for cancellation should be legally identified before filing REG-16. “Business closed” and “business transferred” are not interchangeable events because their tax and documentation consequences can differ.

4. FORM GST REG-16

FORM GST REG-16 is the prescribed application for cancellation of registration. The form captures the GSTIN, legal/trade name, principal-place details, correspondence details and the reason for cancellation.

Reasons provided in the form include

  • discontinuance/closure of business;
  • ceased to be liable to pay tax;
  • transfer due to amalgamation, merger, demerger, sale, lease or other disposal;
  • change in constitution resulting in change of PAN;
  • death of sole proprietor; and
  • other specified reasons.

5. The 30-day requirement

Rule 20 requires the cancellation application at the common portal within 30 days of the occurrence of the event warranting cancellation.

Therefore, once management decides that a cancellation event has occurred, the GST team should immediately identify the event date and start the cancellation workflow.

Best practice: Record three separate dates in the internal tracker: the business/legal event date, the proposed cancellation date and the REG-16 filing date. Do not assume these dates are automatically identical.

6. Voluntary registration – important restriction

The Rule 20 framework contains a restriction for a person who obtained registration voluntarily. The CBIC rule text provides that an application for cancellation is not to be considered before expiry of one year from the effective date of voluntary registration.

Practical implication: A business that voluntarily obtained GST registration should not assume it can immediately cancel the registration simply because its turnover is below the mandatory threshold.

Before relying on this provision, the taxpayer should check the current rule text and any applicable amendment/notification for the relevant date and facts.

7. Stock details are not optional

REG-16 requires details of inputs held in stock and inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods held in stock on the date from which cancellation is sought.

This is important because cancellation can trigger an ITC/tax consequence under section 29(5). The finance team should therefore perform a proper closing-stock exercise before submitting the application.

8. Capital goods and plant & machinery

Cancellation should not be treated as affecting only trading stock. Capital goods and plant and machinery must also be reviewed where input tax credit has been availed.

The finance team should prepare an asset-wise schedule containing:

  • asset description;
  • purchase date;
  • original taxable value;
  • GST charged;
  • ITC availed;
  • remaining relevant period/amount under the applicable rule;
  • current book value; and
  • tax payable under the cancellation provisions.

9. Example – closure of a trading business

A trader closes its business permanently on 30 September. It has inventory on that date and had availed ITC on the purchases.

The business should not simply file REG-16 stating “closure” and stop filing all GST compliance. It should:

  1. freeze the proposed cancellation date;
  2. prepare the closing inventory statement;
  3. identify ITC attributable to relevant stock;
  4. review capital goods;
  5. compute applicable tax/liability;
  6. make required payment/debit;
  7. file REG-16 with supporting information; and
  8. continue required GST compliance until the registration is actually cancelled and the applicable return obligations are completed.

10. Example – transfer of business

A company transfers its entire business to another legal entity.

The cancellation application should clearly identify the transfer event and preserve the underlying agreement, effective date and supporting records. The acquiring entity's registration and transaction treatment must be separately reviewed.

Key point: Cancellation of the transferor's GSTIN does not automatically determine the GST registration or tax treatment of the transferee. Those are separate compliance questions.

11. Example – construction/project business

A construction entity completes its only project and subsequently stops taxable business activity. The finance team proposes cancellation of the GSTIN.

Before filing, it should reconcile:

  • unbilled revenue and contract balances;
  • retention amounts and advances;
  • project-site inventory/materials;
  • plant and machinery;
  • subcontractor and vendor balances;
  • input tax credit ledger;
  • credit/debit notes pending; and
  • any ongoing GST litigation, notices or return obligations.

12. Cancellation does not erase past GST liabilities

Cancellation of registration is not a waiver of liabilities relating to periods before cancellation.

Section 29 expressly preserves liability to pay tax and other dues and to discharge GST obligations for periods prior to cancellation, whether those dues are determined before or after cancellation.

Therefore: Never treat GST cancellation as “closing the GST account and ending all liability.” Past returns, assessments, audits, notices, interest, penalties and other statutory obligations can continue after cancellation.

13. Cancellation date vs application date

The taxpayer should carefully distinguish the date on which REG-16 is filed from the date from which cancellation is sought.

The application captures the proposed cancellation date and the proper officer subsequently processes the cancellation under the applicable cancellation procedure.

A business should therefore continue its GST compliance workflow until it is clear what date the registration is actually cancelled from and what returns/transactions remain reportable.

Understanding these distinctions is important when responding to a departmental notice. A taxpayer-initiated cancellation application is not the same as a cancellation proceeding initiated by the officer.

15. What happens after REG-16?

Rule 20 is the application stage. The subsequent cancellation procedure is dealt with under the following rules.

The proper officer may examine the application and, where appropriate, issue the cancellation order in the prescribed form under the cancellation procedure.

Where the officer finds the application and facts satisfactory, the registration can be cancelled from the appropriate date. The taxpayer should retain the cancellation order and verify the effective cancellation date.

16. Suspension after applying for cancellation

The rules also contain a separate suspension mechanism. Where a registered person applies for cancellation under Rule 20, the registration can be deemed suspended from the date of submission of the cancellation application or the date from which cancellation is sought, whichever is later, pending completion of the cancellation proceedings.

This does not mean that the taxpayer can ignore the remaining procedural requirements. The business should monitor the application and comply with any outstanding obligations.

17. Audit and departmental notice relevance

Cancellation is often reviewed during later departmental proceedings. Common areas of review include:

  • whether the cancellation date is correct;
  • whether taxable supplies continued after the claimed closure date;
  • whether stock and capital-goods ITC consequences were correctly considered;
  • whether returns were filed up to the appropriate period;
  • whether invoices/credit notes were issued around the closure date;
  • whether there were pending advances or contract receipts;
  • whether the business actually ceased from the declared place; and
  • whether past tax liabilities remain outstanding.

19. Common mistakes under Rule 20

  1. Filing REG-16 without calculating stock/capital-goods liability.
  2. Choosing an incorrect reason for cancellation.
  3. Assuming cancellation immediately ends GST obligations.
  4. Ignoring invoices or receipts after the proposed cancellation date.
  5. Failing to reconcile ITC before cancellation.
  6. Forgetting project-site inventory and capital equipment.
  7. Not checking the one-year restriction for voluntary registration where applicable.
  8. Not retaining the cancellation order.
  9. Closing accounting records before determining the final GST compliance period.

22. Frequently Asked Questions

Which form is used to cancel GST registration?

FORM GST REG-16 is the application form under Rule 20.

Within how many days should cancellation be applied for?

Rule 20 generally requires the application within 30 days of the event warranting cancellation.

Do I need to disclose stock while applying for cancellation?

Yes. Rule 20 requires details of inputs in stock, inputs contained in semi-finished or finished goods, and capital goods on the relevant date, together with the associated liability and payment details.

Can a voluntarily registered person cancel immediately?

The CBIC Rule 20 text contains a restriction against considering such an application before one year from the effective date of voluntary registration. The current rule/notifications applicable to the relevant facts should be checked before filing.

Does cancellation remove previous GST liability?

No. Cancellation does not extinguish liabilities for periods before cancellation.

Does filing REG-16 itself mean the GSTIN is cancelled?

No. REG-16 is the application. The cancellation process and order must be completed under the applicable rules.

What happens to ITC on stock and capital goods?

The cancellation framework requires the relevant stock and capital-goods liability to be considered. The actual amount should be computed under section 29(5) and the prescribed rules.

24. Practical takeaway

Rule 20 is not just a form-filing provision. It is the starting point of a controlled GST exit process. Before filing REG-16, identify the legal reason, meet the 30-day requirement, calculate stock and capital-goods consequences, reconcile pending returns and liabilities, and preserve the complete cancellation trail. Cancellation does not erase past GST obligations.
