1. Why Section 42 matters

Searches for “Section 42 CGST Act”, “Section 42 GST”, “GST ITC matching”, “input tax credit matching”, “ITC mismatch”, “Section 42 omitted” and “GSTR-2B Section 42” require an important distinction: Section 42 is not a current operative provision, but it is highly relevant for understanding how India's GST return system evolved.

Section 42 was originally titled “Matching, reversal and reclaim of input tax credit.” It was designed around a two-way GST return architecture in which supplier-reported outward supplies and recipient-reported inward supplies were electronically matched.

Current position: Section 42 has been omitted. It should not be presented as the current statutory mechanism for GSTR-2B matching. Today's ITC reconciliation is governed by the operative provisions including Sections 16, 17, 38, 41 and the applicable Rules.

2. Current legal status of Section 42

As of September 2026, the current CGST Act lists Section 42 as “Omitted.” India Code also records that Section 42 originally came into force on 1 July 2017, except the proviso to sub-section (9), which had a separate commencement position. citeturn0search0

3. What original Section 42 did

The original Section 42 attempted to create an electronic matching process between:

  • details of inward supplies furnished by the recipient; and
  • corresponding outward-supply details furnished by the supplier.

The objective was to identify whether the recipient's ITC claim corresponded with the supplier's declared tax invoice/debit-note information.

The original framework therefore anticipated:

Match
Supplier and recipient data agreed.
Mismatch
Differences were communicated and could require correction.
Reversal
Persistent discrepancies could result in reversal/addition to recipient liability.
Reclaim
Credit could be restored when the discrepancy was subsequently rectified and matched.

4. Original statutory architecture

The original Section 42 contained a detailed multi-stage mechanism.

42(3)Communication of discrepancies/mismatches.

42(10)Consequences for contravention of the matching/reversal framework.

This architecture is historical. It should not be copied into a current GST compliance SOP as if the provision still operates.

5. Original ITC matching process

The original system envisaged a sequence similar to this:

  1. Supplier reports an outward supply.
  2. Recipient reports the corresponding inward supply/ITC.
  3. GST system compares the two datasets.
  4. Matched claims are accepted through the prescribed electronic process.
  5. Discrepancies are communicated to supplier and/or recipient.
  6. Uncorrected discrepancies could lead to reversal/additional output liability.
  7. Subsequent correction could permit reclaim.

The historical Rules contained detailed matching provisions in Rules 69 to 72, including matching criteria, final acceptance, discrepancy communication and duplicate-claim handling.

6. What was a mismatch?

The historical matching rules contemplated differences between the recipient's ITC claim and the corresponding supplier-reported data.

Examples included differences in:

  • GSTIN of supplier/recipient;
  • invoice or debit-note number;
  • invoice date;
  • taxable value; and
  • tax amount.

The historical Rule 69 framework also contained a tolerance concept for tax amounts, under which an ITC claim could be treated as matched where the claimed amount was equal to or less than the output tax paid on the corresponding invoice/debit note, subject to the prescribed matching rules.

Historical only: these original Section 42 matching rules should not be confused with today's GSTR-2B matching or ERP reconciliation logic.

7. Rectification of mismatch

Where a mismatch was communicated under the original framework, the supplier could correct the outward-supply statement and the recipient could correct its inward-supply/ITC information in the prescribed manner.

The purpose was to allow a genuine transaction to move from mismatch to match rather than permanently denying credit merely because the supplier and recipient initially reported different information.

This historical design is conceptually useful even today: invoice-level differences should be investigated and corrected rather than simply written off without analysis.

8. Reversal of unmatched ITC

Persistent discrepancies under the original Section 42 framework could result in the amount of discrepancy being added to the recipient's output tax liability in the prescribed return.

The original system therefore connected three concepts:

Mismatch
Supplier and recipient data differed.
Communication
The discrepancy was electronically communicated.
Non-rectification
The discrepancy remained unresolved.
Reversal
The prescribed amount was added to the recipient's liability.

9. Reclaim after matching

One of the distinctive features of the original Section 42 design was the ability to reclaim ITC after a previously unmatched claim became matched following rectification.

This created a “reversal and reclaim” cycle:

Claim → mismatch → reversal → supplier/recipient correction → subsequent matching → reclaim.

The current GST system does not operate through this original Section 42 cycle. Current re-availment situations arise under provisions such as Section 41 and specific Rules such as Rule 37A.

10. Interest consequences

The original Section 42 contained interest provisions linked to amounts added to output tax liability because of persistent discrepancies. The original law also contemplated refund of certain interest when the supplier subsequently corrected the position, subject to statutory conditions and limits.

This historical interest mechanism should not be mechanically applied to current ITC mismatches. Current interest must be determined under the operative provisions, particularly Section 50 and the applicable Rule.

11. Duplicate ITC claims

The original matching Rules also contemplated electronic communication of duplicate ITC claims. Rule 72 addressed a situation where ITC on the same invoice was claimed more than once.

Although Section 42 is omitted, duplicate-claim prevention remains a critical accounting control. Modern ERP systems should therefore use invoice number, supplier GSTIN, invoice date, taxable value and tax amount as duplicate-detection fields.

13. Original GSTR-2 concept

The original GST return design contemplated a recipient-side GSTR-2 containing inward-supply information. Section 42 was built around this two-way reporting model.

The practical architecture was broadly:

Supplier GSTR-1 → recipient GSTR-2 → matching under Section 42 → acceptance/reversal/reclaim.

This architecture did not become the long-term operational GST return model.

15. Why Section 42 was omitted

The GST return architecture changed significantly after implementation. The original GSTR-2/GSTR-3 matching model was not used as the permanent operational system.

As the return system evolved toward GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and system-generated GSTR-2B, the original statutory matching provisions became unsuitable for the operational model. The law was therefore restructured and Sections 42, 43 and 43A were omitted.

Important: The omission does not mean GST ITC reconciliation disappeared. It means the original statutory matching mechanism disappeared and the current system uses a different legal and technological architecture.

16. 2022 return-system restructuring

The omission of Sections 42, 43 and 43A formed part of the major CGST Act amendments brought into force from 1 October 2022.

Finance Act 2022 introduced the relevant statutory changes, and Notification 18/2022-Central Tax brought the relevant Finance Act provisions into force from 1 October 2022. The corresponding amendments also removed references to “unmatched under section 42 or section 43” from related provisions. citeturn0search2turn1search1

This is why current legal research must be careful when reading older GST articles: many pre-October-2022 explanations still describe Section 42 as though it were an operative provision.

17. Current ITC reconciliation

Today's ITC reconciliation should be built around the operative provisions rather than the old Section 42 mechanism.

Current provisionPractical role
Section 16Core ITC eligibility.
Section 17Apportionment and blocked credits.
Section 38System-generated ITC information.
Section 41Self-assessed ITC and supplier non-payment reversal/re-availment.
Rule 37ASpecific supplier GSTR-3B non-filing reversal/re-availment mechanism.
Rule 60GSTR-2B generation framework.

The modern workflow is therefore purchase register → GSTR-2B → legal eligibility → supplier compliance → GSTR-3B → reversal/re-availment monitoring.

18. Supplier compliance today

Under the current framework, the finance team should not simply ask whether an invoice is visible in GSTR-2B. It should also consider supplier compliance where the law requires it.

  • Supplier reported the invoice correctly.
  • Invoice belongs to the recipient.
  • Goods/services were received.
  • Section 16 conditions are satisfied.
  • Section 17 restrictions do not block the credit.
  • Supplier-return status is monitored where relevant.
  • Rule 37A is reviewed annually.

19. Rule 37A today

Rule 37A is one of the clearest examples of how the current law deals with a problem that historically sat within the broader Section 42 matching architecture.

Where the supplier has reported the relevant invoice/debit note but has not furnished the corresponding GSTR-3B by the Rule 37A cut-off, the recipient must reverse the affected ITC by the prescribed date. If the supplier subsequently furnishes the return, the recipient may re-avail the reversed credit according to the prescribed mechanism.

Do not call this “Section 42 reversal”. It is a current Rule 37A mechanism operating under the current Section 41 framework.

20. Practical examples

Example 1 — historical matching

Under the original architecture, supplier reports invoice ₹1,00,000 taxable value and ₹18,000 tax; recipient reports ₹18,000 ITC. The system matches the details and the claim moves through the acceptance process.

Example 2 — historical invoice-number mismatch

Supplier reports INV-100, while recipient reports INV-0100. The original matching framework could identify a discrepancy and communicate it for correction.

Example 3 — historical supplier correction

Supplier corrects the invoice details in its outward-supply statement. The discrepancy can subsequently move toward matching under the original Rules.

Example 4 — historical reversal

An unmatched claim continues unresolved under the original mechanism. The prescribed amount can be added to recipient liability and interest can arise under the historical Section 42 framework.

Example 5 — historical reclaim

The supplier later corrects the transaction and the claim becomes matched. The recipient could reclaim the amount under the historical statutory process.

Example 6 — current GSTR-2B

Today, an invoice appears in GSTR-2B. The recipient still performs Sections 16/17 eligibility checks before claiming ITC.

Example 7 — current Rule 37A

A supplier reports an invoice but does not furnish the corresponding GSTR-3B by the prescribed annual cut-off. The recipient follows Rule 37A rather than old Section 42.

Example 8 — current re-availment

The supplier later files the relevant GSTR-3B. The recipient monitors the event and re-avails the reversed ITC according to Rule 37A.

Example 9 — duplicate claim

A company accidentally claims the same invoice twice. The current ERP should detect the duplicate independently of any historical Section 42 mechanism.

Example 10 — construction company

A large contractor has thousands of subcontractor invoices. The ERP performs invoice-level GSTR-2B matching and maintains a separate supplier-compliance/Rule 37A watchlist.

23. GST Council background

The original GST return model was designed around extensive electronic matching between supplier and recipient data. As implementation progressed, the return architecture was simplified and redesigned.

The move away from the original GSTR-2/3 matching model ultimately made the detailed statutory Section 42 mechanism unnecessary for the operative system. The 2022 legislative restructuring formally removed Sections 42, 43 and 43A.

For current compliance research, the historical Council/return-reform material is useful for understanding why the architecture changed, but it should not be cited as authority for an operative Section 42 obligation.

27. Common mistakes

  • Publishing Section 42 as though it is currently operative.
  • Saying GSTR-2B is governed directly by Section 42.
  • Applying old Rules 69–72 to current GST return filing.
  • Confusing historical “matching” with current reconciliation.
  • Assuming every GSTR-2B mismatch automatically requires a Section 42 reversal.
  • Ignoring current Section 41 and Rule 37A.
  • Failing to distinguish historical and current tax periods.
  • Using old GSTR-2 terminology in current compliance SOPs.

28. Section 42 GST FAQs

What is Section 42 of the CGST Act?

Section 42 was the original statutory provision for matching input tax credit claims with corresponding supplier-reported outward supplies, including reversal and reclaim mechanisms.

Is Section 42 currently applicable?

No. Section 42 is currently omitted from the CGST Act.

When was Section 42 omitted?

The omission became effective from 1 October 2022 as part of the GST return-system restructuring.

Why was Section 42 omitted?

The GST return architecture evolved away from the original GSTR-2/GSTR-3 matching model toward the current GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and system-generated GSTR-2B framework.

Is GSTR-2B based on Section 42?

No. GSTR-2B is part of the current Section 38/Rule 60 framework. It should not be described as the current Section 42 matching mechanism.

What did Section 42 originally match?

It was designed to match recipient inward-supply/ITC information with corresponding supplier outward-supply information.

What happened to unmatched ITC under old Section 42?

The original law provided communication, rectification, reversal/addition to liability and, after subsequent correction, reclaim mechanisms subject to the prescribed conditions.

Can I use Section 42 for a current GST notice?

Because Section 42 is omitted, the applicable current provisions should be identified based on the relevant tax period and issue. Sections 16, 17, 38, 41 and the applicable Rules may be relevant instead.

What is the difference between Section 42 and Rule 37A?

Section 42 was the historical matching/reversal/reclaim provision. Rule 37A is a current mechanism dealing with specified supplier non-filing and ITC reversal/re-availment under the current Section 41 framework.

What did Rules 69 to 72 do?

They contained the original matching, final acceptance, discrepancy communication and duplicate-claim processes associated with the historical Section 42 framework.

Does Section 42 still matter for historical periods?

Yes. It can matter when researching historical GST positions, legacy records, old departmental communications or litigation relating to the period when the provision was operative.

What replaced Section 42?

There is not one single “replacement section”. The current ITC system is distributed across Sections 16, 17, 38 and 41 together with Rules such as Rule 37A and Rule 60.

Should a GST reconciliation ERP use Section 42?

For current reconciliation, it should not implement Section 42 as an operative legal rule. It can, however, retain historical matching logic for legacy-period analysis.

30. Key takeaways

  • Section 42 is currently omitted from the CGST Act.
  • It originally governed matching, reversal and reclaim of ITC.
  • The original system was built around GSTR-2 and supplier-recipient matching.
  • Sections 42, 43 and 43A were omitted with effect from 1 October 2022.
  • Do not describe current GSTR-2B reconciliation as “Section 42 matching”.
  • Current ITC reconciliation is based on Sections 16, 17, 38 and 41 and the applicable Rules.
  • Rule 37A provides a current supplier non-filing reversal/re-availment mechanism.
  • Historical Section 42 remains relevant for legacy-period research and litigation.
  • A GST ERP should distinguish historical Section 42 logic from current ITC reconciliation logic.
