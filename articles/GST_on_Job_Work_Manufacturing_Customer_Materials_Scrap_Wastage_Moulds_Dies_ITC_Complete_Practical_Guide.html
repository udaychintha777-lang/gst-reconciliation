1. The factory problem

Job work becomes difficult when the ERP sees one processing invoice but the factory has multiple physical movements.

Principal material→Job worker→Processing→Scrap/WIP→Return/next worker

Example: a manufacturer sends ₹50 lakh of steel to a fabricator. The fabricator uses its own consumables, generates scrap, records process loss and sends painted components to another processor. Finance must trace ownership, quantity, documentation, statutory ageing and tax treatment at every stage.

OwnershipWho owns raw material, WIP, output, scrap and tools?
MovementWhich movement is under Section 143 and which is a supply?
QuantityDoes input reconcile to output + scrap + documented loss?
EvidenceCan the ERP prove the entire chain?
Best management rule: reconcile job work by quantity + ownership + document + location + ageing.

2. What is job work?

Section 2(68) defines job work as treatment or processing undertaken on goods belonging to another registered person. The registered person whose goods are processed is the principal for Section 143.

Commercial labels such as conversion, fabrication, finishing, assembly or outsourcing are not conclusive. Check actual ownership and the contract.

Important: qualifying movement of principal-owned goods under Section 143 is not itself a taxable supply merely because possession changes. The job worker's processing service is a separate supply.

Where the processor purchases and owns the material for its own account, or ownership transfers before processing, the transaction needs a different analysis.

3. Section 143 — core framework

A registered principal may send inputs or capital goods to a job worker without payment of tax, subject to the prescribed conditions.

GoodsCore periodControl
Inputs1 yearAge from original dispatch
Capital goods other than specified exclusions3 yearsAge from original dispatch
Moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures and toolsExcluded from the specific 3-year deeming ruleStill track ownership/location

If covered goods are not returned or otherwise supplied within the statutory period, the deeming provision can treat the principal as having supplied them to the job worker on the original dispatch date, with tax and interest consequences.

Control: flag open challans months before the statutory deadline.

5. Customer-owned raw material

Physical possession at the job worker's factory does not by itself transfer ownership. The principal should maintain a separate “material at job worker” stock location or sub-ledger.

PrincipalTrack quantity/value sent, returned, consumed, scrapped and outstanding.
Job workerDo not treat customer-owned material as ordinary purchase inventory merely because it is received physically.
ERP tip: customer-owned stock should have its own location, batch and ageing fields.

6. Job worker's own materials

A job worker may use its own consumables or other inputs. The contract should state what the principal supplies and what the job worker supplies.

Do not automatically split every invoice into material plus labour. First determine whether the commercial substance is processing service alone or includes an independent goods supply, then apply the relevant classification and rate.

7. Delivery challan and movement documents

Rule 45 provides for movement to job workers under a challan issued by the principal, including direct dispatches, and provides a trail for movements between job workers.

9. The one-year / three-year clock

Build the statutory ageing report from the original dispatch date. Do not reset the clock merely because the goods move from one job worker to another.

Example: ₹40 lakh of input material is dispatched on 10 September 2026. Finance should calculate the applicable one-year deadline from the original dispatch and monitor the batch until it is returned or otherwise supplied in the permitted manner.

Ageing should show challan date, quantity, value, job worker, current location, return quantity, scrap/loss, outstanding quantity, statutory deadline and days remaining.

10. Moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures and tools

Section 143 specifically excludes moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures and tools from the particular three-year deeming rule applicable to other capital goods.

That is not a reason to stop tracking them. Maintain ownership, asset register, physical location, repair responsibility, insurance and disposal records.

QuestionControl
Who owns it?Contract + asset register
Where is it?Location-wise register
Who pays repair?Agreement + invoice

11. Normal vs abnormal wastage

“Wastage” should not be a dumping account for unexplained shortages. Determine standard yield, process loss, identifiable scrap and abnormal variance.

Example: 1,000 kg input = 930 kg finished output + 60 kg scrap + 10 kg technical process loss. The 60 kg should have a scrap trail; the 10 kg should be supported by the production process.
  1. Document standard yield.
  2. Record actual output.
  3. Separate recoverable scrap/by-product.
  4. Investigate abnormal variance.
  5. Identify owner of scrap.

12. Scrap generated during job work

Section 143(5) specifically addresses waste and scrap. A registered job worker may supply scrap directly from its premises on payment of tax. If the job worker is not registered, the principal supplies the scrap on payment of tax.

Registered job workerDirect scrap supply is permitted by Section 143(5), subject to applicable GST.
Unregistered job workerPrincipal supplies the scrap and pays applicable GST.
Control: reconcile scrap generated with material issued and production output by batch.

13. Second job worker

Goods may move from one job worker to another. This is common in machining → heat treatment → painting → assembly chains.

Principal→Worker A→Worker B→Principal

The documentation must preserve principal ownership and connect the second movement to the original job-work trail. Rule 45 contains challan endorsement provisions for such movements.

Common failure: Worker A raises a sale invoice to Worker B even though ownership never transferred. This can create an artificial supply trail.

Do not apply one blanket GST answer to every loss. The applicable ITC restrictions and supply provisions depend on the facts and statutory category.

16. Direct dispatch from job worker

Section 143 permits supply from the job worker's premises subject to its conditions. The law also contains an additional-place-of-business requirement with specified exceptions, including cases involving a registered job worker.

Example: Principal sends components to a registered job worker for final assembly and instructs direct delivery to its customer. Documents should clearly establish who is supplying the customer and that the job worker is performing the processing service, unless the actual contract says otherwise.

17. ITC for principal and job worker

Section 19 contains specific credit provisions for inputs and capital goods sent for job work, subject to statutory conditions. The principal should maintain evidence supporting the credit position.

The job worker may claim eligible ITC on its own business inputs and services subject to normal Section 16 conditions and restrictions. Customer-owned material should not be mixed into the job worker's own purchase inventory.

18. Job-work service rate

There is no universal “job-work GST rate.” The applicable rate depends on the relevant activity, goods and rate notification. Maintain a classification memo for each process.

Rate master: product/HSN, process, relevant entry, rate, effective date, notification and approval. Recheck when product or process changes.

19. Detailed manufacturing case studies

Auto-component machining

Principal sends forged blanks worth ₹80 lakh. Job worker machines them using its own coolant and tools. Finished parts return and metal chips become scrap. Track principal-owned blanks separately, invoice processing service, reconcile output and scrap, and preserve the challan/ITC-04 trail.

Plastic injection moulding

Principal supplies polymer and retains a ₹30 lakh mould. Runners and rejects arise. Polymer movement is tested under Section 143; the mould needs separate asset/location control; scrap/rejects need production and disposal evidence.

Steel fabrication + painting

Worker A cuts and welds principal-owned steel and sends it to Painter B. The movement between A and B must preserve the principal's ownership and original job-work trail.

Chemical processing

A batch produces 94% usable output, 4% recoverable by-product and 2% process loss. The yield sheet should distinguish by-product/scrap from technical loss.

Fire at job worker

Customer-owned material is destroyed. Prepare incident, insurance, physical-stock and reconciliation records and separately test any ITC consequence.

Job worker buys additional steel

Principal supplies 80% of material and worker buys 20%. The contract must identify the independent goods component before the invoice is classified as pure processing.

23. Month-end checklist

  1. Reconcile opening job-worker stock.
  2. Match all dispatch challans.
  3. Match job-worker receipts.
  4. Match returns to principal.
  5. Identify second-job-worker movements.
  6. Reconcile output, scrap and process loss.
  7. Verify scrap invoices and GST.
  8. Verify mould/die/tool locations.
  9. Age open challans.
  10. Reconcile ITC-04 with ERP.
  11. Review job-work invoice classification/rate.
  12. Document abnormal variances.
  13. Obtain high-value confirmations where appropriate.

24. FAQs

Is sending goods to a job worker itself a taxable supply?

Not where the movement qualifies under Section 143 and its conditions are followed. The processing service is separately a supply.

Can goods go directly from principal to job worker?

Yes, Rule 45 covers direct dispatches subject to the prescribed documentation.

Can goods move from one job worker to another?

Yes, subject to the Section 143 and Rule 45 trail.

What if inputs are not returned within one year?

The deeming provision can apply from the original dispatch date, with tax and interest implications.

What about capital goods after three years?

The three-year rule can apply to covered capital goods; moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures and tools are excluded from that particular deeming rule.

Who pays GST on scrap?

A registered job worker may supply scrap directly and pay applicable GST; if unregistered, the principal supplies the scrap and pays applicable GST.

Is every production loss taxable?

No blanket answer should be used. Separate normal process loss, identifiable scrap, abnormal shortage and destruction.

Can the job worker use its own material?

Yes, but test whether the arrangement also contains an independent goods supply.

25. Final takeaway

The largest job-work GST risk is usually not the processing invoice. It is the gap between physical material, legal ownership, statutory time limits and the ERP trail.

OwnershipKnow whose goods are at every location.
QuantityReconcile input → output + scrap + loss.
TimeAge every challan from original dispatch.
Management conclusion: Operate job work like a controlled supply-chain ledger. If every kilogram/piece can be traced from dispatch to return, scrap or permitted supply, GST disputes become easier to identify, quantify and defend.
