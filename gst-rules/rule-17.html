Rule 17 – Assignment of Unique Identity Number to Certain Special Entities

A practical guide to GST Unique Identity Numbers for UN bodies, embassies and other notified special entities. Understand FORM GST REG-13, UIN, REG-06, the three-working-day process, refund relevance and the controls needed for proper GST documentation.

1. What is Rule 17?

Rule 17 deals with the assignment of a Unique Identity Number (UIN) to certain special entities referred to in section 25(9) of the CGST Act.

Unlike an ordinary GST registration, a UIN is designed for specified entities that require a special identification mechanism under GST. The principal examples include certain United Nations bodies, embassies, consulates and other persons or classes of persons covered by the statutory framework.

Simple meaning: UIN is not an ordinary GSTIN for carrying on taxable business. It is a special GST identification mechanism used by eligible entities for the purposes prescribed under GST law, particularly where the law provides special treatment such as refund of eligible taxes.

2. Statutory structure of Rule 17

ProvisionPractical meaning
Rule 17(1)Eligible persons required to receive a UIN under section 25(9) may apply electronically in FORM GST REG-13.
Rule 17(1A)A UIN granted to a person covered by section 25(9)(a) applies to the territory of India.
Rule 17(2)The proper officer may assign the UIN and issue FORM GST REG-06 within three working days from submission of the application; the rule also permits action after filling the form or receiving a recommendation from the Ministry of External Affairs where applicable.

The important point is that Rule 17 operates together with section 25(9). Therefore, the first question is always whether the entity falls within the statutory category eligible for UIN.

3. Who can obtain a UIN?

Section 25(9) is the starting point for identifying eligible entities. Broadly, the UIN framework covers specified special organisations and diplomatic or consular entities, including:

  • a specialised agency of the United Nations Organisation or a multilateral financial institution and organisation notified under the United Nations (Privileges and Immunities) Act, 1947;
  • consulates or embassies of foreign countries; and
  • other persons or classes of persons as may be notified by the Government.
Eligibility must not be assumed merely because an organisation is foreign or diplomatic. The entity should first be mapped to the relevant statutory category and supporting notification/documentation.

5. FORM GST REG-13 – Application for UIN

The prescribed application is FORM GST REG-13. It is submitted electronically and is duly signed or verified through the prescribed electronic verification mechanism.

The form is specifically titled as the application/form for grant of UIN to UN Bodies / Embassies / others.

Typical information captured

  • Name of the entity;
  • PAN, where applicable;
  • authorised signatory details;
  • contact information;
  • type of entity;
  • country;
  • Ministry of External Affairs recommendation details, where applicable.
Practical control: The legal name, entity category and supporting diplomatic/organisational documentation should be consistent across REG-13, invoices, refund claims and internal records.

6. Rule 17(2) – Assignment of UIN

After submission of REG-13, the proper officer may assign the UIN. The rule also contemplates assignment after the officer fills the prescribed form or after receiving a recommendation from the Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India, where applicable.

The prescribed certificate is FORM GST REG-06.

Timeline: Rule 17(2) provides for issuance of the UIN certificate within three working days from the date of submission of the application.

7. What is the purpose of a UIN in practical GST compliance?

The UIN allows an eligible special entity to be identified in the GST ecosystem without treating it simply as an ordinary taxable business.

One of the most important practical areas is the mechanism for claiming refund of eligible taxes paid on specified inward supplies, subject to the applicable statutory conditions and refund rules.

Therefore, finance teams dealing with UIN entities should maintain a clear audit trail from:

  1. supplier invoice;
  2. supplier GSTIN;
  3. UIN of the recipient entity;
  4. payment proof;
  5. nature of goods/services;
  6. eligibility of the tax for refund; and
  7. refund application and supporting documentation.

8. Example – UN organisation

A qualifying UN-related organisation operating in India purchases office equipment and professional services on which GST is charged.

The organisation should not simply assume that the supplier invoice automatically becomes refundable. Its finance team should verify:

  • whether the organisation is covered by section 25(9);
  • whether it has the appropriate UIN;
  • whether the supplier has correctly reported the relevant transaction;
  • whether the inward supply qualifies under the applicable refund framework;
  • whether the invoice contains the required particulars; and
  • whether the refund claim is supported by payment and other prescribed records.

9. Example – Embassy / Consulate

A foreign embassy in India obtains a UIN and receives services from an Indian registered supplier. GST is charged on the service invoice.

The embassy's finance team should maintain a separate UIN-controlled ledger for eligible purchases. It should not mix UIN transactions with ordinary commercial transactions of another entity merely because the supplier is the same.

Supplier-side control: The supplier should verify the recipient's correct legal name and UIN documentation rather than relying only on an email instruction to treat the transaction as eligible for special treatment.

10. UIN and GST refund – why documentation matters

For eligible entities, refund claims can involve a large volume of supplier invoices. A weak invoice trail can create reconciliation problems even when the underlying eligibility is genuine.

11. Rule 17(1A) – Territorial application

Rule 17(1A) specifically provides that the UIN granted under Rule 17 to a person covered by section 25(9)(a) shall be applicable to the territory of India.

This is relevant for qualifying entities with activities or purchases across different locations in India. The organisation should nevertheless maintain location-wise records where required for invoice, refund and internal control purposes.

12. UIN does not automatically make every GST expense refundable

This is one of the most important practical points for finance teams.

Having a UIN is an identification condition; it does not by itself establish that every GST amount appearing on every invoice can be refunded.

The refund claim must still be tested against the applicable law, refund rules, notifications and conditions governing the particular eligible entity and supply.

Common misconception: “We have UIN, therefore all GST paid is refundable.” This is not a safe compliance conclusion.

13. Supplier-side practical checklist

When supplying goods or services to a UIN entity, the supplier's accounts team should consider:

  • Confirm the recipient's legal name.
  • Obtain and verify the UIN details.
  • Apply the correct GST treatment based on the actual supply and applicable exemption/refund framework.
  • Ensure invoice particulars are accurate.
  • Report the transaction correctly where GST reporting is required.
  • Retain supporting documents.
  • Avoid treating a UIN as if it were an ordinary GSTIN of a taxable business.

15. Audit and departmental relevance

UIN-related transactions can involve high-value procurement and refund claims. Therefore, documentation quality is important even though the entity is not operating like an ordinary taxable supplier.

Keep an audit file containing

  • REG-13 application;
  • REG-06 UIN certificate;
  • entity eligibility/supporting documentation;
  • supplier invoices;
  • payment records;
  • refund applications;
  • refund sanction/rejection communications;
  • invoice-wise reconciliation;
  • internal approval records; and
  • clarifications submitted to the department.

16. Common mistakes under Rule 17

  1. Confusing UIN with an ordinary GSTIN.
  2. Applying without first confirming section 25(9) eligibility.
  3. Incorrect legal name in REG-13 and supplier invoices.
  4. Using outdated entity or authorised-signatory information.
  5. Assuming every GST-paid invoice is automatically refundable.
  6. Failing to reconcile invoices before refund filing.
  7. Duplicate inclusion of invoices across refund claims.
  8. Poor retention of diplomatic/organisational supporting documents.
  9. Not maintaining a dedicated UIN register.

20. Frequently Asked Questions

What is UIN under GST?

UIN is a Unique Identity Number assigned under the GST registration framework to specified special entities covered by section 25(9).

Which form is used to apply for UIN?

FORM GST REG-13.

Which certificate is issued?

FORM GST REG-06 is used for the certificate.

How quickly can UIN be assigned?

Rule 17(2) provides for assignment and issue of the certificate within three working days from submission of the application, subject to the prescribed process.

Is UIN the same as GSTIN?

No. UIN is a special identification mechanism for eligible entities and should not be treated as an ordinary taxable-person GSTIN.

Does having UIN make every GST payment refundable?

No. Refund eligibility depends on the applicable statutory provisions, rules, notifications and conditions governing the entity and the particular supply.

Can an embassy apply for UIN?

Eligible embassies and consulates fall within the special-entity framework, subject to the requirements of section 25(9) and the prescribed procedure.

22. Important numbering clarification

Do not confuse Rule 17 with physical verification. Older GST material and some older online pages may show physical verification under Rule 17. The current numbering places the physical-verification framework under Rule 25. Current Rule 17 deals with UIN assignment.

This distinction is important when preparing legal opinions, compliance manuals, GST notices or website content because quoting the old rule number can create an avoidable legal-reference error.

23. Practical takeaway

Rule 17 provides the GST mechanism for assigning a Unique Identity Number to specified special entities. The process begins with eligibility under section 25(9), followed by electronic application in REG-13 and assignment of UIN through REG-06. For finance teams, the most important practical area is maintaining a clean invoice-wise trail and not assuming that UIN status automatically makes every GST expense refundable.
