GST ACT — SECTION 52

Section 52 CGST Act — GST TCS on E-Commerce, GSTR-8 & TCS Credit

Section 52 deals with Tax Collection at Source (TCS) by electronic commerce operators. This detailed guide explains the provision first in everyday language and then in technical detail: who collects TCS, when it applies, how “net value of taxable supplies” is calculated, GSTR-8, supplier credit, refunds, late filing and modern e-commerce arrangements such as multiple operators and ONDC.

1. Why Section 52 matters

Searches such as “Section 52 GST”, “GST TCS rate”, “GSTR-8”, “GST TCS on Amazon/Flipkart/e-commerce”, “who has to collect GST TCS?”, “TCS credit in GST cash ledger” and “GST TCS calculation” all come back to one basic question: when does an e-commerce operator have to collect GST TCS from a supplier?

Section 52 creates a collection mechanism for certain e-commerce transactions. The operator collects a prescribed amount from the supplier's taxable supplies and pays it to the Government. The supplier then gets the amount as a credit in the electronic cash ledger.

Simple chain: Customer buys → ECO facilitates/collects consideration → ECO calculates TCS → ECO deposits and reports TCS → supplier gets the TCS amount in the GST electronic cash ledger.

2. Section 52 in very simple language

Imagine an online marketplace where an independent seller lists products and the marketplace collects the buyer's money.

The marketplace does not simply pass the entire amount to the seller. Under Section 52, where the statutory conditions are satisfied, it keeps the prescribed TCS amount, deposits it with the Government and reports it in GSTR-8.

1 — Supplier sells

A registered supplier makes a taxable supply through the electronic platform.

2 — ECO collects money

The consideration for that supply is collected by the electronic commerce operator.

3 — TCS is collected

The operator calculates TCS on the net taxable value covered by Section 52.

4 — Supplier gets credit

The TCS deposited and reported is reflected as credit in the supplier's electronic cash ledger.

Important: TCS is not an additional GST charged to the customer. It is a compliance collection from the supplier's transaction amount and is later available as cash-ledger credit to that supplier.

3. What is GST TCS?

GST TCS means Tax Collection at Source under Section 52 of the CGST Act.

It applies to an electronic commerce operator (ECO), not being an agent, when the statutory conditions are met.

The section authorises collection at a rate not exceeding 1% of the net value of taxable supplies made through the operator by other suppliers where the consideration is to be collected by the operator. citeturn0search0

4. Electronic commerce and ECO

Section 2(44) broadly defines electronic commerce as supply of goods or services, including digital products, over a digital/electronic network. Section 2(45) defines an electronic commerce operator as a person who owns, operates or manages the digital/electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce.

Therefore, “ECO” is about the platform/facility role. But being an ECO under the definition does not mean every transaction automatically attracts Section 52 TCS.

Very important: Section 52 has its own conditions. Always ask: Is the supply by another supplier? Is the consideration collected by the ECO? Is it a taxable supply covered by the section? What is the net value after permitted reductions?

6. Who must collect TCS?

Section 52 applies to an electronic commerce operator, not being an agent, where the statutory conditions are satisfied.

It is therefore normally the platform/operator, not the individual supplier, who collects the Section 52 TCS.

PersonTypical Section 52 role
Independent seller on marketplaceSupplier whose qualifying sales are made through the ECO.
Marketplace/ECOCollects TCS where Section 52 applies.
CustomerUsually pays the transaction consideration; customer is not the Section 52 collector merely because they purchase online.

7. When does Section 52 apply?

There are several conditions. A useful checklist is:

  1. There must be an electronic commerce operator.
  2. The operator must not be acting as the “agent” excluded by Section 52(1).
  3. The supply must be made through the operator by another supplier.
  4. The supply must be a qualifying taxable supply.
  5. The consideration for that supply must be collected by the operator.
  6. The amount must be included in the calculation of net value of taxable supplies.
Do not stop at “the sale happened online”. Online presence alone is not the complete Section 52 test.

8. Net value of taxable supplies

This is the heart of Section 52.

“Net value of taxable supplies” means the aggregate value of taxable supplies of goods/services/both, other than supplies notified under Section 9(5), made during the month by registered persons through the operator, reduced by the aggregate value of taxable supplies returned to suppliers during that month.

Net taxable value = Taxable supplies through ECO − Taxable supplies returned during the month

This definition is contained in the explanation to Section 52(1). citeturn0search0

9. Returned supplies

Returns are important because Section 52 does not simply say “take every sale made during the month”. The statutory definition specifically reduces the taxable-supply value by taxable supplies returned to suppliers during that month.

TransactionEffect
Taxable sale through ECONormally increases net taxable value.
Taxable supply returned during the monthReduces net taxable value as provided in Section 52.
Non-taxable/exempt supplyNot part of taxable supply value.
Section 9(5) notified supplyExcluded from the statutory definition of net value under Section 52(1).

10. GST TCS rate

The Act permits a rate not exceeding 1%, but the notified rate must be checked for the relevant period.

For the current framework reflected in the GST Council's 53rd-meeting material:

Intra-State taxable supplies
0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST/UTGST = 1% total.
Inter-State taxable supplies
1% IGST.

The 53rd GST Council material specifically records the then-current reduced TCS rates and Notification 02/2018-Integrated Tax. citeturn0search1

Historical caution: TCS rates changed from the original framework. When calculating an old month, use the rate applicable to that month rather than today's rate.

11. Operator selling its own goods

This is one of the easiest ways to understand the words “by other suppliers”.

If an ECO sells its own products through its own website, Section 52 TCS is not collected merely because the website is an electronic-commerce platform.

GST Council/official FAQ material explains that where a person sells its own products through its website, Section 52 TCS does not apply to those supplies. The supplies themselves remain subject to normal GST rules. citeturn0search7

12. When consideration is collected by ECO

Section 52 specifically refers to supplies where the consideration is to be collected by the operator.

That condition matters in platform models where the seller may generate the invoice but the platform collects the customer's money.

CBIC's TCS FAQ explains that even where the ECO does not provide the invoicing solution and the seller issues the invoice directly, if the ECO collects the consideration, the TCS mechanism can apply to the qualifying net taxable value. citeturn0search7

13. GSTR-8

GSTR-8 is the monthly statement through which the ECO reports the Section 52 TCS collection.

The operator should maintain supplier-wise and transaction-level reconciliation between:

  • orders;
  • taxable supplies;
  • returns;
  • consideration collected;
  • TCS calculated;
  • TCS actually deposited;
  • GSTR-8; and
  • supplier-wise cash-ledger credit.

The prescribed form is linked to Rule 67. GST Council material on GSTR-8 also records supplier GSTIN, supplies attracting TCS and TCS amount as core reporting information. citeturn0search4

14. Deposit and due date

Section 52 requires the operator to pay the amount collected to the Government within the prescribed period. The monthly statement and payment process operate together.

The operator therefore needs a monthly close process rather than treating TCS as an annual compliance.

Practical control: Close the TCS month only after order data, returns, payment collections, TCS calculation, cash payment and GSTR-8 all reconcile.

15. Supplier's TCS credit

Suppose a marketplace collects ₹2,000 as GST TCS from a supplier.

The supplier does not lose this amount permanently. After the ECO reports and pays it, the amount is reflected in the supplier's electronic cash ledger.

The GST Council has expressly explained that TCS collected by the ECO and paid through GSTR-8 gets reflected in the supplier's electronic cash ledger and may be used for tax, interest or penalty payments, with refund possible for eligible unutilised cash-ledger balance under Section 54. citeturn0search1

17. Corrections and mismatches

A mismatch can occur when the ECO's TCS does not match the supplier's books or GST portal.

Typical causes include:

  • wrong supplier GSTIN;
  • incorrect taxable value;
  • returns not captured;
  • duplicate transaction;
  • wrong tax period;
  • incorrect rate;
  • GSTR-8 not filed/paid correctly; or
  • transaction reported by one system but not the other.

The ECO should maintain an amendment/correction trail, while the supplier should reconcile the reflected TCS with its own sales and settlement reports.

18. Refund of TCS balance

TCS credited to the electronic cash ledger can become part of the supplier's cash balance. If the balance remains unutilised after the supplier's due liabilities are dealt with, refund may be available under Section 54, subject to the statutory conditions.

GST Council material specifically notes that a supplier can claim refund of an unutilised cash-ledger balance after paying due tax liabilities. citeturn0search1

19. Late fee and default

GSTR-8 is a return/statement covered by the GST late-fee framework. Section 47 was amended to expressly cover failure to furnish a return under Section 52 by the due date.

The 52nd GST Council material records the introduction of the GSTR-8 late-fee table and the related Rule/Form changes. citeturn0search4

Do not confuse late fee for delayed GSTR-8 with interest for failure to collect/pay TCS. They are different consequences.

20. Annual statement

Section 52 also contains an annual-statement framework for the operator covering supplies effected through it, returned supplies and the amount collected during the financial year, subject to the current prescribed form and procedure.

Because GST return architecture has evolved, the operator should always verify the current Rule 67/form instructions before assuming that a historical annual statement requirement is still filed in exactly the same format.

21. Unregistered suppliers and composition suppliers

This area requires particular care because Section 52 itself and later GST amendments/rules have evolved.

Supplies by unregistered persons through ECOs and supplies by composition taxpayers have been subject to separate legislative and procedural developments, especially following changes considered by the GST Council.

The 49th GST Council agenda records recommendations for special procedures concerning supplies of goods through ECOs by unregistered persons and composition taxpayers, including corresponding GSTR-8 reporting changes. citeturn0search3

Professional approach: Do not assume that every supply by an unregistered or composition supplier automatically follows the original Section 52 TCS model. Check the current notification/rules for the relevant period.

22. Section 9(5) vs Section 52

These two provisions are often confused because both involve e-commerce operators.

Section 52Section 9(5)
ECO collects TCS.ECO can become liable to pay GST on specified services notified under Section 9(5).
Supplier generally remains liable for GST on its supply, subject to the applicable framework.ECO itself pays GST on the specified notified service.
TCS becomes supplier's cash-ledger credit.Tax paid by ECO is not simply a TCS credit.
Section 52 is a collection/reporting mechanism.Section 9(5) is a tax-payment liability mechanism.

The definition of net value under Section 52 specifically excludes supplies notified under Section 9(5). citeturn0search0

23. Multiple ECOs and ONDC

Modern e-commerce does not always have one platform doing everything. A transaction can involve a buyer-side ECO, seller-side ECO, payment/technology layers and other participants.

This created an important question: which ECO should comply with Section 52?

The GST Council considered the issue in the context of ONDC and multiple-ECO transactions. The official agenda explains that both buyer-side and seller-side participants may qualify as ECOs, creating a need to identify the operator responsible for Section 52 compliance. citeturn0search8

The practical lesson is to examine the actual commercial flow rather than assuming that the most visible marketplace is automatically responsible for TCS.

24. Construction and business examples

Section 52 is mainly an e-commerce provision, so it is less central to traditional construction businesses. However, it can become relevant where a construction/business entity sells materials or other taxable supplies through an ECO.

Example — Construction company sells surplus material online

If a construction company sells its own surplus goods through its own website, Section 52 TCS does not automatically arise merely because the website is an electronic platform. The “other suppliers” and consideration-collection conditions must be examined.

Example — Independent supplier sells construction materials through marketplace

If an independent registered supplier sells taxable construction materials through an ECO and the ECO collects the consideration, the ECO should examine Section 52 TCS.

Example — Returns

If taxable construction materials sold through the ECO are returned during the month, the returned taxable value is relevant to the net-value calculation under Section 52.

25. Easy numerical examples

Example 1 — ₹10 lakh taxable sales

Qualifying taxable supplies through ECO = ₹10,00,000. If no returns and the applicable total TCS rate is 1%, TCS = ₹10,000.

Example 2 — ₹10 lakh sales and ₹2 lakh returns

Net taxable value = ₹10,00,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹8,00,000. At 1%, TCS = ₹8,000.

Example 3 — GST excluded

Taxable value ₹5,00,000 and GST ₹90,000. TCS is calculated on the qualifying taxable value, not ₹5,90,000.

Example 4 — Own products

An ECO sells its own products through its own website. Section 52 TCS does not arise merely because the sale happens through a digital platform.

Example 5 — Seller issues invoice, ECO collects money

The seller issues the invoice directly, but the ECO collects the buyer's consideration. Section 52 can still apply if all statutory conditions are satisfied.

Example 6 — No consideration collected by ECO

If the platform only provides technology/listing services and does not collect the consideration for the supplier's supply, the Section 52 condition concerning collection of consideration must be examined carefully.

Example 7 — TCS is not ITC

Supplier receives ₹20,000 TCS credit. It is reflected in the electronic cash ledger, not the credit ledger.

Example 8 — TCS refund

A supplier has excess cash-ledger balance consisting partly of TCS and satisfies the Section 54 conditions. A refund may be claimed for eligible unutilised balance.

Example 9 — GSTR-8 mismatch

ECO reports ₹15,000 TCS under the wrong GSTIN. The supplier should not simply claim it. The error must be corrected through the prescribed ECO/GST system process.

Example 10 — Multiple ECO transaction

A buyer-side and seller-side ECO both participate in an ONDC transaction. The parties must identify the ECO responsible for Section 52 compliance under the applicable clarification/framework.

26. Accounting treatment

For an ECO, TCS should be tracked separately from the supplier's sales consideration and the ECO's own output GST.

For a supplier, TCS is a recoverable/cash-ledger-type GST balance rather than ITC.

32. Common mistakes

  • Assuming every online sale attracts GST TCS.
  • Applying TCS to the ECO's own sales without checking the “other suppliers” condition.
  • Including GST in the taxable-value base.
  • Ignoring returned supplies.
  • Ignoring the Section 9(5) exclusion in the net-value definition.
  • Using today's rate for an old tax period.
  • Confusing TCS with ITC.
  • Failing to reconcile GSTR-8 with settlement data.
  • Reporting TCS under the wrong supplier GSTIN.
  • Assuming the largest/visible platform is always the responsible ECO in a multiple-ECO transaction.
  • Ignoring GSTR-8 late fee.
  • Not maintaining a correction trail.

33. Section 52 GST FAQs

What is Section 52 of the CGST Act?

Section 52 deals with Tax Collection at Source by electronic commerce operators on qualifying taxable supplies made through them by other suppliers where the consideration is collected by the operator.

What is GST TCS?

GST TCS is the amount collected by an ECO under Section 52 and deposited with the Government.

What is the GST TCS rate?

The Act permits up to 1%. The notified rate must be checked for the relevant period. Under the current framework reflected in official GST Council material, intra-State supplies are 0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST/UTGST and inter-State supplies are 1% IGST.

What is GSTR-8?

GSTR-8 is the monthly statement filed by the electronic commerce operator for Section 52 TCS.

Who files GSTR-8?

The electronic commerce operator responsible for Section 52 TCS compliance files GSTR-8.

What is net value of taxable supplies?

It is qualifying taxable supplies through the ECO during the month reduced by qualifying taxable supplies returned to suppliers during that month, subject to the statutory exclusions.

Is GST included in TCS value?

The Section 52 net-value concept is based on the value of taxable supplies; GST charged separately is not added as a second tax base.

Is GST TCS an input tax credit?

No. It is reflected in the supplier's electronic cash ledger.

Can TCS be used to pay GST?

Yes, after it is credited to the electronic cash ledger, subject to the normal payment framework.

Can TCS be refunded?

Eligible unutilised cash-ledger balance can be claimed through the Section 54 refund framework, subject to its conditions.

Does an ECO collect TCS on its own products?

Not merely because it operates a website. Section 52 refers to supplies made through the ECO by other suppliers and contains the consideration-collection condition.

Does TCS apply if the ECO only provides a platform?

The consideration-collection condition must be checked. Platform participation alone is not the complete Section 52 test.

Does a returned sale reduce TCS?

Qualifying taxable supplies returned during the month reduce the net value under the statutory definition.

What is the difference between GST TCS and Section 9(5)?

Section 52 is a TCS collection mechanism. Section 9(5) can make an ECO liable to pay GST on specified notified services.

Does Section 52 apply to unregistered sellers?

The framework has evolved and special procedures have been considered/notified for different categories. Always check the current rules and notifications for the relevant period.

What happens if GSTR-8 is filed late?

Late fee can apply under the Section 47 framework.

What happens if an ECO collects TCS but does not pay it?

The operator can face the statutory tax/interest and other compliance consequences under Section 52 and related provisions.

What happens when two ECOs participate in one transaction?

The actual transaction structure must be examined. GST Council/CBIC materials have specifically addressed multiple-ECO/ONDC arrangements.

35. Key takeaways

  • Section 52 is the GST TCS provision for qualifying e-commerce transactions.
  • TCS is collected by the ECO, not normally by the supplier.
  • The section applies to qualifying supplies made through the ECO by other suppliers where the ECO collects consideration.
  • Net value means qualifying taxable supplies less qualifying taxable returns during the month.
  • Supplies covered by Section 9(5) are excluded from the Section 52 net-value definition.
  • GST TCS is not ITC; it becomes electronic cash-ledger credit for the supplier.
  • GSTR-8 is the core monthly TCS statement.
  • Rates have changed historically, so old-period calculations need the rate applicable to that period.
  • Modern platform models such as ONDC can involve multiple ECOs, so responsibility must be determined from the actual transaction structure.
  • Section 52 should always be read with Sections 2(44), 2(45), 9(5), 47, 49 and 54 and the current Rule 67 framework.
