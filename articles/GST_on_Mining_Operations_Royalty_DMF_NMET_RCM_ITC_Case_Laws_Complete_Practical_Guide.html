1. Why mining GST is different from ordinary GST

A mining business does not have one GST transaction called “mining”. It has a chain of legally different transactions: Government mining rights, royalty and other statutory amounts, contract mining, drilling, blasting, overburden removal, loading, transport, processing and mineral sale.

The difficult part is identifying what the supply is, who supplied it, what the consideration is, whether RCM applies, what rate/classification applies, whether ITC survives Section 17(5), and what evidence proves the position.

Management question: A ₹500 crore mining operation can have several high-value GST decisions in the same month. A single ERP tax code cannot safely decide all of them.

2. The legal foundation: what is supplied by Government?

The GST framework treats specified services supplied by Central/State Government, Union territory or local authority to a business entity as subject to notified RCM. Notification 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate), Entry 5, is the central RCM framework for this category.

GST Council material describes the mining-lease transaction as the Government granting the right to explore and mine minerals, not simply leasing a tangible good. The established service classification is SAC 997337 — licensing services for the right to use minerals including exploration and evaluation.

Rate: The GST Council's Broadson Commodities appellate material records 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST for an intra-State supply) for grant of mining rights.

This classification is important because royalty is analysed as consideration connected with the mining-right service.

3. Royalty: complete practical treatment

Step 1 — Read the mining lease

Identify the Government authority, mineral, lease period, area, royalty mechanism and recipient of payment.

Step 2 — Apply RCM

CBIC's mining FAQ states that GST applies on royalty paid to Government for a mining lease under RCM and that GST paid under RCM can be eligible as ITC subject to normal conditions.

Example: Royalty ₹10,00,000. At 18%, GST = ₹1,80,000. For an intra-State supply, CGST ₹90,000 + SGST ₹90,000. The recipient discharges RCM and may claim eligible ITC after satisfying the applicable conditions.
Royalty / mining-right cost Dr ₹10,00,000 Input CGST – RCM Dr ₹90,000 Input SGST – RCM Dr ₹90,000 To GST RCM payable ₹1,80,000 To Royalty payable / Bank ₹10,00,000

Financial-accounting classification may differ depending on whether royalty is expensed, capitalised or included in inventory cost. Do not confuse accounting treatment with GST liability.

CBIC also states that the 180-day consideration condition does not apply where tax is payable under RCM; other ITC conditions still apply.

4. Historical rate/classification issue

Mining-right classification created a historical rate issue for the initial GST period. GST Council material records the Council's view that grant of mineral exploration/mining rights is appropriately classified under SAC 997337 and that the intended rate was 18%. Broadson Commodities AAAR recorded 18% for 1 July 2017–31 December 2018 and thereafter.

Audit rule: never use today's rate to close a historical royalty dispute without checking the notification and clarification applicable to that tax period.

5. DMF: the major 2026 change in practical treatment

CBIC Circular 206/18/2023-GST clarified that District Mineral Foundation Trusts set up by State Governments are Governmental Authorities and eligible for the relevant GST exemptions applicable to Governmental Authorities, based on the activities and structure addressed by the circular.

The 2026 Telangana AAAR in the Singareni Collieries matter applied this later clarification and held the DMF contribution not liable to GST in the case before it.

Example: Royalty ₹10 crore; DMF ₹3 crore. Do not automatically calculate GST on ₹13 crore. First determine the legal treatment of the DMF Trust, applicable exemption and tax period.
Do not overstate the ruling: an AAAR decision should not be presented as a universal exemption for every taxpayer and every DMF arrangement.

6. NMET: why it must be separated from DMF

NMET is a separate statutory mechanism. The 2026 Telangana AAAR expressly distinguished NMET from DMF. It held that the CBIC clarification applicable to DMF did not extend to NMET and upheld GST liability on NMET contribution in that case.

8. Royalty collection contractor / ERCC model

Some States use royalty collection contractor arrangements. There can be two relationships: Government → ERCC for assignment of collection rights, and ERCC → lease holder for collection/settlement of statutory amounts.

The GST Council's mining agenda material analysed these as separate transactions. A 2026 AAR also considered an ERCC model and the conditional exemption framework for assignment of royalty-collection rights, while analysing the liability of mining lease holders on statutory levies.

Practical point: the party collecting the money is not automatically the supplier of the underlying mining-right service. Map each contractual relationship separately.

9. Mining contractors: classification starts with the contract

A mining contract can include drilling, blasting, overburden removal, excavation, raising, loading, haulage and mineral extraction. Classification should start with the contract, BOQ, mine plan, payment metric and actual scope—not the invoice description.

The 24 April 2026 CESTAT decision in Dholu Construction & Projects Ltd dealt with integrated lignite extraction involving overburden removal, excavation and loading. The Tribunal focused on the predominant mining activity and held that incidental activities could not be artificially separated from the comprehensive mining contract.

Example: A ₹50 crore contract has drilling ₹5 crore, overburden ₹15 crore, excavation ₹10 crore, loading ₹5 crore and mineral raising ₹15 crore. If the contractor's actual obligation is integrated mineral extraction under mine plans and payment is linked to mineral production, the finance team should not assume five independent supplies merely because the BOQ has five lines.

13. Royalty RCM ITC and cash-flow timing

CBIC's mining FAQ states that GST paid under RCM on royalty can be eligible as ITC.

Example: Royalty ₹1 crore; GST at 18% = ₹18 lakh. The company must discharge the RCM liability in the prescribed manner. If the mining business and credit satisfy the normal conditions, the ₹18 lakh may enter eligible ITC.

This is not necessarily cash-neutral on the same day. The tax payment and ITC claim occur through the statutory mechanism and can create a timing effect. Reconcile royalty → RCM → cash payment → GSTR-3B → ITC.

15. Foundations and structural supports — 2026 ITC development

Mining and mineral-processing plants often require engineered foundations for crushers, conveyors, screens, pumps and other heavy machinery.

The 2026 Gujarat AAR in Cadila Pharmaceuticals held ITC available for foundation and structural support used for plant and machinery where the machinery was fixed to earth through the supports and the structures were integral to load, vibration, stability and functioning.

That is a pharmaceutical case, not a mining case. Its statutory reasoning may be relevant by analogy where a mining plant has genuinely machine-specific foundations, but the facts must be proved.

Evidence pack: engineering drawings, equipment foundation drawings, manufacturer specifications, structural calculations, commissioning report, asset-register mapping and a technical note explaining why the support is integral to the identified machine.
Do not conclude: “all mine civil works are plant and machinery.” Buildings, roads and general civil structures need separate analysis.

17. Mineral processing and job work

Map ROM mineral → crushing → screening → washing/beneficiation → finished mineral → sale. Determine whether processing is captive, outsourced, job work or part of an integrated contract.

Example: A coal company sends 10,000 tonnes to a processor for washing. Before approving the GST treatment, establish ownership of the material, possession, movement, return of processed goods, wastage, consideration and contractual scope. The word “processing” alone does not answer the GST question.

20. Royalty ITC and inverted-duty refund: separate questions

Some mining businesses may accumulate ITC where GST paid on royalty/other inputs is higher than the GST burden on outward mineral supplies. This can create a working-capital issue.

In Amarjyoti Granites India Private Limited, the Odisha AAR in June 2026 considered an application concerning refund of accumulated ITC under the inverted-duty structure arising in part from mining royalty RCM. The AAR rejected the application on jurisdictional grounds; it was not a merits ruling that every such accumulation is refundable.

Key distinction: “RCM tax is eligible ITC” and “accumulated ITC is refundable” are separate legal questions.

21. 2026 litigation: royalty GST remains a live issue

The prevailing administrative position continues to require GST under RCM on mining royalty, but constitutional challenges remain active.

In Kotha Kishore Kumar Reddy v. Deputy Assistant Commissioner, the Andhra Pradesh High Court's common order dated 23 June 2026 dealt with multiple connected petitions challenging GST on mining royalty and related amounts. The order recorded continuation of earlier interim orders and listed the matters for further hearing. It was not a final merits judgment declaring GST on royalty invalid.

The connected cases also included challenges involving DMF/NMET and royalty/seigniorage.

Management position: do not stop RCM merely because litigation exists. Any change from the prevailing compliance position should be based on the actual court order, jurisdiction and specific legal advice.

27. Detailed FAQs

Is GST currently payable on mining royalty?

The prevailing administrative position reflected in CBIC's mining FAQ is yes, under RCM. Constitutional challenges remain pending in some courts, so pending litigation should not be confused with a final invalidation.

What is the GST rate on mining rights?

The established classification is SAC 997337 and the GST Council's Broadson material records 18%.

Is royalty a lease of goods?

GST Council material treats the Government transaction as granting rights to explore and mine minerals, not leasing tangible goods.

Can RCM GST on royalty be ITC?

Yes, CBIC's mining FAQ states that it can be eligible subject to normal conditions.

Is DMF exempt?

The 2026 Telangana AAAR held DMF not liable to GST in the case before it after applying CBIC Circular 206/18/2023-GST. Applicability must be tested for the particular Trust, period and facts.

Is NMET also exempt?

No. The same 2026 AAAR distinguished NMET and upheld GST liability on it.

Is overburden removal always a separate excavation service?

No. In an integrated mining contract it may form part of the predominant mining service. Dholu Construction is a useful 2026 decision on this principle.

Can all mining civil works claim ITC?

No. Section 17(5) must be tested component-wise. Machine-specific foundations can require a different analysis from buildings and roads.

Can hired warehouses/stockyards have ITC?

CBIC's mining FAQ states that hiring land, office, warehouse, processing unit and stockyards used in mining business can be eligible subject to applicable provisions.

Does an AAR bind every mining company?

No. Binding effect is governed by the GST law and applicant/jurisdiction framework.

Does eligible royalty ITC automatically mean refund?

No. Amarjyoti Granites shows that refund eligibility and AAR jurisdiction are separate issues.

Can a company stop RCM because royalty litigation is pending?

Not automatically. The June 2026 Andhra Pradesh High Court order continued interim orders but did not finally strike down the levy.

28. Final decision framework

1. Identify transaction
Government levy, mining service, transport, asset, processing or sale?
2. Identify supplier
Government, contractor, GTA, equipment supplier or processor?
3. Identify consideration
Royalty, tonnes, fixed fee, premium, freight or milestone?
4. Apply mechanism
Forward charge, RCM, exemption or outside scope?
5. Test ITC
Section 16 → 17 → 17(5) → time limit.
6. Preserve evidence
Contract + technical record + invoice + reconciliation.
Best practice: approve the GST treatment of every new mining contract before the first invoice. Correcting a wrong classification after hundreds of crores of invoices is much harder.
