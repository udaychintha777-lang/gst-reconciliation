GST E-Invoicing on RCM from 1 January 2027

A practical guide to the new requirement for recipient-generated self-invoices covering specified domestic purchases from unregistered persons and import of services under the Reverse Charge Mechanism.

Quick summary: what changes from 1 January 2027?

The Government has extended the e-invoicing framework to two additional categories of inward supplies where the recipient is responsible for paying GST under reverse charge. For eligible taxpayers, the recipient will have to report the relevant self-invoice/document on an authorised Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and obtain an Invoice Reference Number (IRN).

Important: This does not mean every purchase from an unregistered person becomes subject to RCM or e-invoicing. The underlying supply must first be one on which the recipient is legally liable to pay GST under RCM. The change expands the documents covered by e-invoicing; it does not itself expand the list of RCM supplies.

1. Notifications and legal basis

The amendment is based on two Central Tax notifications dated 26 October 2026:

NotificationWhat it doesEffective date relevant to this change
Notification No. 04/2026–Central TaxAmends Notification No. 13/2020–Central Tax issued under Rule 48(4) of the CGST Rules, 2017. It adds (a) supplies received from unregistered persons where the recipient pays tax under RCM and (b) import of services.1 January 2027
Notification No. 05/2026–Central TaxAmends the CGST Rules and updates FORM GST INV-01 to Schema Version 1.2, adding the supply-type codes RCMDOM and RCMIMP.Schema amendment: 1 January 2027

Notification No. 04/2026 substitutes the opening coverage paragraph of Notification No. 13/2020. The notified categories now include supplies to registered persons, exports, specified inward supplies from unregistered persons under RCM, and import of services.

The operative legal text is Notification No. 04/2026–Central Tax, read with the relevant amendments in Notification No. 05/2026–Central Tax. Always read the full notifications and the existing e-invoice provisions together.

2. What transactions are covered?

A. Domestic supplies from unregistered persons – RCMDOM

Where a registered recipient receives goods or services from a supplier who is unregistered, and the recipient is liable to pay GST under Section 9(3) or Section 9(4) of the CGST Act (or the corresponding IGST provisions, as applicable), the recipient's self-invoice/document falls within the new e-invoice reporting framework from 1 January 2027, subject to the recipient being within the notified e-invoice class.

B. Import of services – RCMIMP

Where a person in India receives a service from a supplier located outside India and the transaction qualifies as an import of services on which the Indian recipient is liable to pay IGST under reverse charge, the recipient-generated document is covered by the new reporting category, subject to the same e-invoice applicability framework.

Practical testFirst determine whether the transaction is legally liable to RCM. Only then determine whether the recipient is covered by the e-invoice mandate and whether the document falls under RCMDOM or RCMIMP.

3. Turnover threshold and existing exemptions

The January 2027 amendment changes the types of documents/transactions covered. It does not, by itself, replace the existing e-invoice turnover threshold or remove the exemptions already prescribed under the e-invoice framework.

  • The general e-invoice mandate currently applies to notified registered persons whose aggregate annual turnover has exceeded ₹5 crore in any relevant preceding financial year from 2017-18 onwards, subject to the applicable notification and exclusions.
  • The existing notified exemptions continue to be relevant. A person should not conclude that e-invoicing applies merely because the portal facility is visible or enabled.
  • Check the PAN-level aggregate turnover history, the relevant GST registrations and the current notified class/exemption position before deciding applicability.
Do not read the ₹5 crore threshold as a new RCM-specific threshold. It is part of the existing e-invoice applicability framework. Notification No. 04/2026 adds RCM document categories; it does not announce a separate turnover limit for RCM.

For a final applicability decision, refer to Notification No. 13/2020–Central Tax as amended, the current e-invoice portal guidance and the exemptions applicable to the taxpayer's category.

6. Self-invoice: who prepares and reports it?

Section 31(3)(f) of the CGST Act requires a registered person liable to pay tax under reverse charge to issue an invoice for goods or services received from a supplier who is not registered, within the prescribed period. This is commonly called a self-invoice.

For the new categories, the recipient is the party responsible for generating/reporting the relevant document under the e-invoice framework. This is a significant operational change because the document is created by the recipient rather than by a domestic supplier issuing an ordinary B2B invoice.

Receive supply / service→Determine RCM→Prepare self-invoice/document→Report to IRP→Obtain IRN and QR code
Do not confuse the e-invoice reporting date with the time of supply. The time of supply under the relevant RCM provisions must be determined independently. The new IRN requirement does not replace the time-of-supply rules or extend statutory payment deadlines.

For domestic unregistered supplies, also review the prescribed time limit for issuing the self-invoice under the applicable CGST Rules. Keep the underlying supplier document, receipt evidence, self-invoice number, IRN acknowledgement and tax-payment working together.

8. What is not covered by this new extension?

  • Every unregistered purchase: Unregistered status alone does not create RCM liability. Section 9(4) applies only to notified classes/categories and conditions.
  • Import of goods: The new code is specifically for import of services. Import of goods continues to follow customs documentation, including the bill of entry.
  • All RCM transactions from registered suppliers: The new domestic code is specifically worded for inward supplies from unregistered persons. For a registered supplier's invoice, apply the existing supplier-side e-invoice provisions where relevant.
  • Tax-rate changes: The amendment does not itself change the applicable GST rate, taxable value or the underlying RCM liability.
  • Automatic ITC entitlement: Obtaining an IRN does not by itself make the tax credit eligible. Section 16 and other ITC conditions/restrictions continue to apply.

9. Recommended compliance workflow from January 2027

  1. Identify the taxpayer's e-invoice status. Confirm PAN-level aggregate turnover and whether any notified exemption applies.
  2. Build a dedicated RCM register. Capture supplier GSTIN/status, country, nature of supply, invoice date, receipt date, taxable value, RCM section/notification and tax rate.
  3. Separate domestic and import transactions. Use RCMDOM for qualifying domestic inward supplies from unregistered persons and RCMIMP for import of services.
  4. Prepare the underlying document. Ensure the self-invoice/document is complete, sequentially numbered and supported by the original supplier invoice or other evidence.
  5. Generate IRN on the authorised IRP. Use the new supply type and the updated schema/validation rules. Preserve the signed response and QR code.
  6. Post RCM liability correctly. Reconcile the document with the RCM liability reported in the applicable GST return and discharge the tax in the prescribed manner.
  7. Review ITC separately. Claim credit only after checking eligibility, payment of tax and all statutory conditions.
  8. Reconcile monthly. Match the RCM register, self-invoice series, IRN records, books, tax payment and ITC workings.

10. RCM tax payment and ITC treatment

The new e-invoice requirement is a document-reporting requirement. It does not convert RCM tax into a supplier-charged tax or change the normal reverse-charge payment mechanism.

RCM liabilityDetermine the applicable taxable value, place of supply, time of supply and rate. Pay the reverse-charge tax in the manner prescribed under GST law.
Input tax creditAfter payment, evaluate ITC under Section 16 and other applicable provisions. Business use, blocked-credit provisions, exempt supplies and other restrictions remain relevant.

For example, if an eligible recipient incurs ₹1,00,000 for an imported service taxable at 18% under RCM, the illustrative IGST liability is ₹18,000. The recipient must report the relevant document under RCMIMP if covered by the e-invoice mandate, pay the RCM tax as required, and separately determine whether the ₹18,000 is eligible as ITC. The IRN alone does not establish ITC eligibility.

12. Frequently asked questions

1. From which date is RCM e-invoicing mandatory?

From 1 January 2027 for the two newly added categories, subject to the existing e-invoice applicability conditions.

2. Is every RCM transaction required to have an e-invoice?

No. The new categories are recipient-generated documents for specified domestic supplies received from unregistered persons under RCM and import of services. The recipient must also fall within the notified e-invoice applicability framework.

3. Does every purchase from an unregistered person attract RCM?

No. RCM applies only where a specific provision/notification makes the recipient liable. Unregistered status by itself is not enough.

4. What is RCMDOM?

It is the new supply-type code for a domestic supply from an unregistered person under RCM.

5. What is RCMIMP?

It is the new supply-type code for import of services.

6. Has the ₹5 crore threshold changed?

Notification No. 04/2026 does not announce a new threshold. The existing e-invoice applicability threshold and exemptions continue to be examined.

7. Does the change cover import of goods?

No. The new category specifically covers import of services. Import of goods continues under the customs documentation framework.

8. Does an IRN automatically make RCM ITC eligible?

No. ITC eligibility must be evaluated independently under Section 16, Section 17 and the applicable rules. The tax must be paid and all conditions must be satisfied.

9. Does the amendment change the RCM rate or tax liability?

No. It extends e-invoice reporting to additional document categories. The applicable RCM provisions determine tax liability and rate.

10. Can businesses test before 1 January 2027?

GSTN's advisory dated 17 November 2026 states that sandbox functionality is available for testing. It also indicates planned production enablement from 15 December 2026, with voluntary generation from 15 to 31 December 2026 for eligible taxpayers. Mandatory applicability begins on 1 January 2027.
