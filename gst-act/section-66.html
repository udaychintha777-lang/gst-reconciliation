GST ACT — SECTION 66

Section 66 CGST Act — Special Audit by CA or CMA

Section 66 provides a special audit mechanism where, during scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or another proceeding, an officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner believes that the declared value or input tax credit requires special examination because of the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue. This guide explains ADT-03, ADT-04, Commissioner approval, the 90-day report period, extension, hearing and the tax proceedings that may follow.

1. Why Section 66 matters

Searches such as “Section 66 CGST Act”, “GST special audit”, “special audit under GST”, “ADT-03”, “ADT-04”, “GST audit by CA”, “GST audit by CMA”, “special audit 90 days” and “GST special audit notice” relate directly to this provision.

Section 66 is different from the normal GST departmental audit under Section 65. It is a special audit used when the case is sufficiently complex and the officer considers that the declared value or ITC requires an independent professional examination having regard to the interest of revenue.

Simple idea: Section 65 is a departmental audit. Section 66 is a special audit where the GST department directs a Commissioner-nominated chartered accountant or cost accountant to examine the registered person's records.

2. Section 66 in very simple language

Imagine the GST department is already examining a company. During that examination, the officer finds a complicated valuation structure, unusual related-party transactions, very large ITC, complex accounting or another issue that cannot be satisfactorily examined through an ordinary review.

If the officer believes that the value has not been correctly declared or the ITC availed is not within normal limits, considering the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue, the officer can seek the Commissioner's prior approval for a special audit.

Scrutiny/inquiry/investigation → complex case + value/ITC concern → Commissioner approval → ADT-03 → CA/CMA special audit → ADT-04

3. What is a special audit?

A special audit is an examination of the registered person's records, including books of account, by a chartered accountant or cost accountant nominated by the Commissioner.

The professional does not independently decide to conduct a GST special audit. The statutory trigger comes from the tax proceeding, the authorised officer and the Commissioner's approval.

FeatureMeaning
NatureSpecial examination within an existing GST proceeding.
ProfessionalCA or CMA nominated by the Commissioner.
TriggerOfficer's opinion based on nature/complexity, value or ITC and revenue interest.
ApprovalPrior approval of Commissioner.
ReportNormally within 90 days, subject to permitted extension.

4. When can Section 66 be invoked?

Section 66 can be used when an officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner, at any stage of scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or any other proceedings before him, forms the statutory opinion that the special audit is necessary.

Two specific concerns are mentioned in the provision:

Value concern

The value declared by the registered person has not been correctly declared.

ITC concern

The input tax credit availed is not within the normal limits.

Nature and complexity

The nature and complexity of the case justify a special professional examination.

Interest of revenue

The officer's opinion must also have regard to the interest of revenue.

Section 66 is therefore not intended to be an automatic audit for every registered taxpayer.

5. Prior Commissioner approval

The officer cannot simply direct a Section 66 special audit on his own. The provision requires prior approval of the Commissioner.

This approval is an important statutory safeguard because the special audit is a significant intervention involving an independent professional examination of the taxpayer's records.

Important: “The case is complicated” by itself is not the complete statutory test. The officer's opinion must be connected with the value/ITC concerns, the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue.

6. Who conducts the special audit?

The Commissioner nominates either a:

  • Chartered Accountant; or
  • Cost Accountant.

The nominated professional examines and audits the records specified in the direction and submits the signed and certified report to the Assistant Commissioner within the statutory period.

This is why Section 66 is often described as a CA/CMA special audit under GST.

7. ADT-03 direction

Rule 102 provides that the officer referred to in Section 66 issues FORM GST ADT-03 directing the registered person to get the records audited by the CA or CMA specified in the direction.

The taxpayer should carefully read ADT-03 and identify:

  • the proceeding from which the special audit arises;
  • the professional nominated;
  • the records to be examined;
  • the nature of the issues requiring special audit; and
  • the practical arrangements for producing records.

8. What is examined?

Section 66 refers specifically to the registered person's records, including books of account. The exact scope will depend on the issues identified in the proceeding and the direction issued.

AreaPossible examination
RevenueSales, contracts, invoices and revenue recognition.
ValuationRelated-party pricing, discounts, reimbursements, bundled transactions and other valuation issues.
ITCLarge or unusual ITC, eligibility, reversals and utilisation.
BooksLedgers, trial balance, journals and supporting records.
ContractsCommercial terms affecting GST value or liability.
ReconciliationsBooks vs GST returns and transaction-level evidence.

9. 90-day audit report period

Section 66(2) provides that the nominated CA or CMA must submit the signed and certified special-audit report to the Assistant Commissioner within 90 days.

The report must contain the particulars required under the law/direction.

ADT-03 → Special audit → Report within 90 days

10. Extension by another 90 days

The Assistant Commissioner may extend the 90-day period by a further period of 90 days where an application is made by:

  • the registered person;
  • the chartered accountant; or
  • the cost accountant;

or where there is any material and sufficient reason.

The extension is not an automatic extra 90 days. There must be an application or material and sufficient reason supporting the extension.

11. Can there already be another audit?

Yes. Section 66(3) expressly states that the special-audit provision applies notwithstanding that the accounts of the registered person have been audited under any other provision of the CGST Act or any other law for the time being in force.

Therefore, the existence of a statutory financial audit, internal audit, tax audit or another GST audit does not by itself prevent a Section 66 special audit.

But: This does not mean that every previous audit is irrelevant. In practice, the taxpayer should provide prior audit reports and explain already-examined matters where they are relevant to the special-audit scope.

12. Opportunity of hearing

Section 66(4) provides an important safeguard.

If material is gathered through the special audit and that material is proposed to be used in proceedings against the registered person under the GST Act or Rules, the registered person must be given an opportunity of being heard in respect of that material.

Practical meaning: A special-audit report is not a substitute for the taxpayer's right to respond to material that the department proposes to use against the taxpayer.

13. Who pays the special-audit cost?

Section 66(5) provides that the expenses of the examination and audit, including the remuneration of the CA or CMA, are determined and paid by the Commissioner.

The Commissioner's determination of the expenses is final under the section.

This is an important distinction from privately engaged professional audits, where the company normally appoints and pays the professional itself.

14. ADT-04 special-audit findings

Rule 102 provides that after conclusion of the special audit, the registered person is informed of the findings in FORM GST ADT-04.

FormPurpose
ADT-03Direction to get records audited by nominated CA/CMA.
ADT-04Communication of special-audit findings.

ADT-04 should be preserved along with the complete special-audit report, management explanations and supporting records.

15. What happens if tax discrepancy is found?

Section 66(6) provides that where the special audit results in detection of:

  • tax not paid;
  • tax short paid;
  • tax erroneously refunded; or
  • input tax credit wrongly availed or utilised;

the proper officer may initiate the applicable tax-determination proceedings.

For current law, the applicable framework depends on the tax period and facts, including Section 73, Section 74 or Section 74A.

16. Section 73/74/74A after special audit

ProvisionBroad current framework
Section 73Determination for periods up to FY 2023-24 in cases other than fraud/wilful misstatement/suppression, subject to statutory conditions.
Section 74Determination for periods up to FY 2023-24 involving fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression, subject to statutory conditions.
Section 74ACurrent determination framework for applicable periods from FY 2024-25 onward.

The special audit is therefore an evidentiary/examination stage; it is not itself the final tax-determination mechanism for every discrepancy.

20. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Complex related-party valuation

A company supplies goods/services to related entities under multiple commercial arrangements. The officer finds that the declared value requires detailed examination. A Section 66 special audit may be considered if the statutory conditions are satisfied.

Example 2 — Unusually high ITC

A registered person reports very large ITC compared with the nature and scale of its business. The officer may examine whether the credit is within normal limits and whether a special audit is warranted.

Example 3 — Complex construction contracts

Large infrastructure contracts contain advances, variations, retention, free issue materials and multiple subcontractors. A professional examination may be needed to determine the correct GST treatment.

Example 4 — Prior statutory audit

The company's financial statements have already been audited. Section 66 can nevertheless operate because the Act expressly says that the special-audit provision applies notwithstanding an audit under another law.

Example 5 — Taxpayer requests extension

The taxpayer has millions of transaction records and requires additional time to provide the data. An extension can be considered through the statutory mechanism where the required conditions are met.

Example 6 — Special audit finds short payment

The CA identifies a GST short-payment. The department can use the report as relevant material and initiate the applicable tax-determination proceedings.

Example 7 — Taxpayer disputes report

The taxpayer believes the special auditor classified exempt transactions as taxable. The taxpayer should provide the relevant agreements, invoices, exemption provisions and reconciliation during the subsequent proceedings.

Example 8 — ITC reversal

The special audit identifies ITC that was availed and utilised without satisfying the statutory conditions. The taxpayer should reconcile invoice-wise eligibility and reversals.

Example 9 — Multiple GST registrations

A company has GSTINs in several States. The special-audit direction and records should be examined carefully to identify the registered person and scope covered by the proceeding.

Example 10 — Report vs final demand

The special-audit report identifies an issue, but the taxpayer's opportunity of hearing and the applicable tax-determination provisions still matter before a final demand is established.

21. Construction and infrastructure examples

RA bills and contract valuation

Large works contracts can contain multiple components, variations, escalation clauses and different billing stages. The special audit may examine whether the declared taxable value correctly reflects the applicable GST rules.

Mobilisation advance

Advance registers, bank receipts and tax invoices should reconcile with the time-of-supply position.

Retention money

Contractual retention clauses should be reviewed together with invoice and revenue records to determine the actual GST treatment.

Free issue material

Where the principal supplies materials to a contractor, the agreements and accounting treatment should be examined carefully because valuation and supply-characterisation issues can become complex.

Subcontractors

Subcontractor invoices, RCM where applicable, ITC and project-wise payments should be reconciled.

22. Valuation and complex transactions

Section 66 specifically refers to a situation where the officer believes that the value has not been correctly declared.

Therefore, valuation is one of the most important areas to prepare for.

Related parties
Examine relationship and valuation provisions.
Discounts
Review invoice and post-supply discount documentation.
Reimbursements
Separate taxable consideration from genuine exclusions where legally supportable.
Bundled transactions
Determine composite/mixed supply and principal supply where relevant.
Free supplies
Check whether contractual terms affect taxable value.
Cross-border services
Review import-of-service and related-party valuation issues.

23. ITC and special audit

The other express trigger in Section 66 is that the credit availed is not within the normal limits.

This does not mean that every high ITC amount is automatically wrong. A large ITC can be completely legitimate for a capital-intensive business, manufacturer, infrastructure company or business with large taxable purchases.

The real issue is whether the credit is properly supported and legally eligible.

ITC areaAudit question
InvoiceIs the required tax document available?
ReceiptWere goods/services actually received?
Supplier reportingDoes the relevant GST data support the transaction?
Business useIs the credit connected with business/taxable supplies?
Blocked creditDoes Section 17(5) restrict the credit?
ReversalWere applicable reversals made?
UtilisationWas credit used according to the prescribed order?

27. Common mistakes

  • Confusing Section 66 special audit with Section 65 departmental audit.
  • Assuming every large ITC figure automatically justifies special audit.
  • Ignoring the requirement of prior Commissioner approval.
  • Not understanding the exact scope of ADT-03.
  • Providing incomplete books or transaction-level data.
  • Not reconciling related-party valuation.
  • Failing to explain complex construction contracts.
  • Assuming a statutory financial audit prevents a Section 66 audit.
  • Ignoring the taxpayer's hearing right regarding material proposed to be used against it.
  • Treating the special-audit report itself as the final tax demand.

29. Section 66 GST FAQs

What is Section 66 of the CGST Act?

Section 66 provides for a special audit where, during scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or another proceeding, an authorised officer considers that the value has not been correctly declared or ITC is not within normal limits, having regard to the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue.

Who conducts a Section 66 special audit?

A chartered accountant or cost accountant nominated by the Commissioner conducts the special audit.

Is Commissioner approval required?

Yes. The officer must obtain prior approval of the Commissioner before directing the special audit.

What is ADT-03?

ADT-03 is the direction issued to the registered person to get the records audited by the CA or CMA specified in the direction.

What is ADT-04?

ADT-04 is the communication of the findings of the special audit to the registered person.

How long does a Section 66 special audit take?

The nominated professional must normally submit the report within 90 days. The Assistant Commissioner can extend the period by another 90 days on application or for material and sufficient reason.

Who pays the CA/CMA special-audit fees?

The expenses, including professional remuneration, are determined and paid by the Commissioner under Section 66(5).

Can a company be subjected to Section 66 after statutory audit?

Yes. Section 66 expressly applies notwithstanding that the accounts have been audited under another provision of the GST Act or another law.

Does a special audit automatically create a GST demand?

No. If the special audit detects tax not paid/short paid, erroneous refund or wrongly availed/utilised ITC, the proper officer may initiate the applicable tax-determination proceedings.

Does the taxpayer get a hearing?

Yes. Where material gathered through the special audit is proposed to be used against the registered person in GST proceedings, Section 66(4) requires an opportunity of being heard regarding that material.

Is Section 66 the same as GST departmental audit?

No. Section 65 is departmental audit by tax authorities. Section 66 is a special audit by a Commissioner-nominated CA or CMA.

Can Section 66 be used only for ITC?

No. The section covers both a value that has not been correctly declared and ITC that is not within normal limits, subject to the other statutory conditions.

Can Section 66 apply to construction companies?

Yes, where the statutory conditions are satisfied. Complex valuation, contracts, advances, related parties, large ITC or other difficult GST issues may require detailed examination.

31. Key takeaways

  • Section 66 is a special audit provision, not the normal departmental audit.
  • It can arise during scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or another GST proceeding.
  • The officer must be not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.
  • The case must involve the statutory value/ITC concerns, nature and complexity and interest-of-revenue considerations.
  • Prior Commissioner approval is required.
  • The special audit is conducted by a Commissioner-nominated CA or CMA.
  • ADT-03 directs the special audit and ADT-04 communicates its findings.
  • The normal report period is 90 days, with a possible further 90-day extension under the statutory conditions.
  • The taxpayer has a hearing right regarding material gathered through the special audit that is proposed to be used against it.
  • Special-audit expenses are determined and paid by the Commissioner.
  • A special-audit finding can lead to Section 73, 74 or 74A proceedings, depending on the applicable period and facts.
