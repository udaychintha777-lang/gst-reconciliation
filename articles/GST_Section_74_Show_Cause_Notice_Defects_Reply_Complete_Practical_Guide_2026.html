1. What is a GST Show Cause Notice under Section 74?

A Show Cause Notice (SCN) is the Department's formal statement of the proposed liability and the basis on which the taxpayer is being asked to explain why tax, interest and applicable penalty should not be determined. It is not the final demand order.

Section 74 is the fraud/wilful misstatement/suppression route for the relevant statutory framework. Because it carries a longer limitation mechanism than the ordinary non-fraud route, the Department must do more than simply insert the words “fraud”, “wilful misstatement” or “suppression of facts” into the notice.

2026 Supreme Court principle: In Tata Steel Ltd. v. Union of India, the Supreme Court held that the foundational facts leading to the inference of fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression should be evident from the SCN itself. Mechanical use of those words is not enough.
Important: A defective SCN does not mean every tax allegation disappears automatically. First identify the defect, then examine whether it goes to jurisdiction, statutory ingredients, limitation, natural justice, quantification or only a curable procedural issue.

2. The first hour: extract these 12 items from the SCN

1. Provision
Is it Section 73, 74 or another provision?
2. Tax period
Which month/quarter/FY is involved?
3. Tax amount
Is the proposed tax quantified?
4. Interest
How has interest been calculated?
5. Penalty
Which section and clause?
6. Allegation
What exactly did the taxpayer allegedly do?
7. Section 74 ingredient
Where are fraud/suppression facts?
8. Evidence
What documents/statements are relied upon?
9. Invoice list
Are transactions identified invoice-wise?
10. Limitation
Why is the notice within the applicable period?
11. Authority
Who issued it and under what assignment?
12. Deadline
What is the date for reply/hearing?
Do not start by writing a 50-page factual reply. First create an SCN control sheet. A good defence often becomes much clearer once the allegations, amount, evidence and statutory ingredients are mapped separately.

3. How to test whether Section 74 has actually been made out

QuestionWeak SCNStronger SCN
What was concealed?“The taxpayer suppressed facts.”Identifies the fact/information allegedly withheld.
What was misrepresented?“Wilful misstatement was made.”Identifies the representation, document or return entry.
Where is intent?No factual explanation.Facts relied on to infer deliberate conduct are identified.
How did it cause tax loss?Generic statement.Transaction-wise link between conduct and tax effect.
What is the evidence?“Investigation revealed…”Specific records/statements/data are identified.
Why Section 74?Only the statutory words.Foundational facts support the chosen provision.

The Supreme Court's August 2026 decision in Tata Steel is particularly important because it rejected the idea that simply using statutory expressions can extend the limitation period. The foundational facts must be apparent from the notice.

6. How to prepare a professional Section 74 reply

A strong reply should allow the adjudicating officer to understand the defence without reconstructing the taxpayer's books.

Recommended structure

  1. Covering submission — notice number, date, tax period and taxpayer.
  2. Preliminary objections — jurisdiction, limitation, statutory ingredients and procedural defects.
  3. Brief facts — business model and relevant transaction flow.
  4. Allegation-wise response — reproduce/identify each allegation and answer it.
  5. Invoice-wise reconciliation — especially for ITC matters.
  6. Evidence index — map every defence statement to an annexure.
  7. Legal submissions — explain why the invoked provision does or does not apply.
  8. Interest calculation — dispute incorrect period/base where applicable.
  9. Penalty — address statutory ingredients separately.
  10. Prayer — request dropping of proceedings or appropriate relief.
  11. Personal hearing — expressly request it.
Best presentation: Put a one-page executive summary before the detailed reply. Then provide a numbered response corresponding exactly to the SCN paragraphs.

7. Personal hearing: do not treat it as a formality

Where a hearing is required, prepare a hearing note that contains the five points the officer must decide:

1. Jurisdiction

Why the proceeding is legally maintainable or not.

2. Ingredients

Why Section 74's fraud/suppression ingredients are or are not established.

3. Evidence

Why the Department's evidence proves or fails to prove the allegation.

4. Quantification

Why the proposed tax, interest and penalty are correct or incorrect.

In appropriate cases involving third-party statements, specifically record why cross-examination is necessary. A July 2026 Kerala High Court decision in Emas Gold and Diamonds LLP considered both block assessment issues and the denial of cross-examination in Section 74 proceedings.

8. Important 2026 case-law developments

1. Tata Steel Ltd. v. Union of India — Supreme Court — August 2026

The Supreme Court set aside the Section 74 SCN and consequential order because the notice lacked foundational facts supporting fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression. The Court stressed that statutory expressions cannot be mechanically inserted merely to invoke the extended limitation framework.

2. Shree Hanumant Steel Traders v. Assistant Commissioner — Chhattisgarh High Court — 9 September 2026

The Court followed the Supreme Court principle and quashed Section 74(9) notices where sufficient reasons/foundational facts for the fraud allegation were not assigned. The Department was left at liberty to issue fresh notices strictly according to law.

3. Rohit Garg / Siwon Enterprises batch — Delhi High Court — 7 September 2026

The batch involved challenges concerning officer competence, Section 122 proceedings, evidentiary material, third-party statements, cross-examination, personal hearing, multiple penalties and the statutory appeal route. The Court treated several issues as matters that could be raised before the statutory appellate authority rather than automatically bypassing Section 107.

4. Emas Gold and Diamonds LLP v. Joint Commissioner — Kerala High Court — July 2026

The judgment is useful for analysing block assessments across periods and procedural fairness, including cross-examination issues in proceedings involving Section 74.

5. S. S. Traders v. Union of India — J&K High Court — March 2026

The Court treated a challenge to Section 74 SCNs as premature and emphasised the statutory reply and appeal route, while granting time to file replies. This is an important counterbalance to cases where a notice itself contains a patent jurisdictional defect.

11. What if the adjudication order goes against you?

The SCN reply is only the first stage. If an adverse order is passed, immediately compare the SCN → reply → hearing record → order → DRC-07.

Check 1 — Did the order answer the reply?

Major submissions should not be ignored without reason.

Check 2 — Did the order stay within the SCN?

Compare allegations and amounts line-by-line.

Check 3 — Was natural justice followed?

Review documents, hearing and cross-examination issues.

Check 4 — Is Section 74 actually supported?

Re-test fraud, wilful misstatement and suppression findings.

Appeal vs writ: A taxpayer should not assume that every SCN or adjudication error belongs immediately before the High Court. Recent 2026 decisions show courts may direct parties toward the statutory Section 107 appeal where factual/legal issues can be examined there. Conversely, a patent jurisdictional defect or serious denial of natural justice can require a different analysis.

13. Frequently asked questions

Does receiving a Section 74 SCN mean fraud has been proved?

No. An SCN sets out the proposed case and gives the taxpayer an opportunity to respond. The Department still has to sustain the statutory ingredients through the adjudication process.

Can the Department simply write “suppression of facts” and invoke Section 74?

The Supreme Court's August 2026 Tata Steel decision says foundational facts leading to that inference should be evident from the notice; mechanical recital is insufficient.

Can I challenge an SCN directly in High Court?

Sometimes exceptional jurisdictional or natural-justice issues can justify writ intervention, but courts frequently emphasise the statutory reply and appeal route. The facts matter.

What if the SCN relies on a supplier's statement?

Obtain the relied-upon statement and assess whether the Department has corroborating transaction-specific evidence. Where appropriate, specifically request cross-examination and explain why it is necessary.

What if the Department refuses cross-examination?

Record the request and the prejudice caused. The legal effect depends on the statutory provision, the nature of the evidence and the facts of the proceeding.

Can one SCN cover multiple financial years?

Do not assume that it is automatically valid or invalid. Analyse the statutory scheme, limitation and determination requirements for each period. A period-wise working is essential.

What if I already paid the disputed amount through DRC-03?

Disclose and reconcile it. A payment does not eliminate the need to check whether the demand calculation is correct or whether the payment has the proper statutory treatment.

Can the adjudicating officer demand more than the SCN?

The comparison between notice and order is critical. If the final order travels beyond the grounds or amount in the notice, raise the issue through the appropriate remedy.

Should my reply only contain legal case laws?

No. For a finance/tax defence, the strongest reply normally combines legal submissions with transaction-level evidence and a clear quantification reconciliation.

What is the single biggest mistake?

Waiting until the last day and then filing a generic denial. Start immediately with an allegation-to-evidence matrix.

Final takeaway — treat the SCN as a structured defence exercise

A Section 74 SCN should never be answered with a generic paragraph saying that the taxpayer has not committed fraud. The professional approach is to break the notice into allegations, statutory ingredients, periods, amounts and evidence, then answer each component with a clear factual and legal basis.

Practical next step: Create an “SCN Defence Master Sheet” with five columns — SCN paragraph → allegation → Department evidence → taxpayer evidence → legal response. This single working paper can become the backbone of the final reply and hearing note.
