GST ACT — SECTION 84

Section 84 CGST Act — Continuation of GST Recovery After Appeal, Revision & Demand Changes

Section 84 explains what happens to GST recovery proceedings when a demand has already been served and that demand is later enhanced or reduced through an appeal, revision or other proceeding. It prevents the recovery process from unnecessarily starting from zero and provides a mechanism for updating recovery to the legally effective amount.

1. Why Section 84 matters

Searches such as “Section 84 CGST Act”, “Section 84 GST”, “GST recovery after appeal”, “GST demand reduced in appeal”, “GST demand enhanced in appeal”, “continuation of recovery proceedings under GST”, “GST recovery after revision order” and “does GST need a fresh demand notice after appeal” all point to the practical issue addressed by this provision.

GST litigation does not always end with the original demand order. An appeal, revision or another statutory proceeding may increase or decrease the amount payable.

Section 84 provides the bridge between that changed legal position and the recovery machinery. It tells the Commissioner what to do with recovery that has already started.

Simple idea: If the demand changes after appeal/revision, Section 84 updates the recovery process without unnecessarily restarting the entire recovery exercise.

2. Section 84 in very simple language

Suppose the department originally determines GST dues of ₹10 lakh and serves a demand notice. Recovery proceedings begin.

The taxpayer appeals and the appellate authority changes the demand.

  • If the demand becomes ₹13 lakh, the extra ₹3 lakh needs a fresh demand notice, while recovery of the original ₹10 lakh can continue from the stage already reached.
  • If the demand becomes ₹6 lakh, the Commissioner does not need to issue a fresh demand notice for ₹6 lakh. The taxpayer and the authority handling recovery are informed of the reduction, and recovery continues for ₹6 lakh from the earlier stage.
Original demand → recovery starts → appeal/revision → demand changes → Section 84 adjusts continuation of recovery

3. Complete statutory structure

Section 84 applies where a notice of demand for tax, penalty, interest or any other amount payable under the CGST Act has been served and an appeal, revision application or other proceeding is initiated in respect of those Government dues.

SituationStatutory result
Government dues enhancedCommissioner serves another demand notice for the amount of enhancement. Recovery of the original dues can continue from the stage at which it stood before disposal of the appeal/revision/other proceeding, without another fresh demand notice for the original amount.
Government dues reducedNo fresh demand notice is necessary. Commissioner informs the taxpayer and the authority with which recovery is pending. Recovery can continue for the reduced amount from the same stage.

4. Meaning of “Government dues”

Section 84 expressly defines the expression for this section broadly as tax, penalty, interest or any other amount payable under the CGST Act.

This is wider than looking only at the basic tax amount. A recovery file should therefore reconcile:

Tax
Principal GST amount.
Interest
Applicable statutory interest.
Penalty
Penalty confirmed under the law.
Other payable amount
Any other amount legally falling within the statutory wording.

5. When Section 84 becomes relevant

There are four basic elements:

✓ Demand notice has been served
✓ Government dues exist under the CGST Act
✓ Appeal/revision/other proceeding is initiated
✓ That proceeding changes or affects the Government dues

Section 84 is therefore not normally the starting point of a GST demand. It becomes important when a demand already exists and a later proceeding changes the amount or otherwise affects it.

6. Demand enhanced in appeal or revision

When Government dues are enhanced, Section 84(a) requires the Commissioner to serve another notice of demand for the amount by which the dues are enhanced.

This avoids an important procedural problem: the taxpayer must receive a demand for the additional amount that did not exist in the original demand.

Example

Original demand = ₹10 lakh. Appeal results in demand = ₹14 lakh. The enhanced portion is ₹4 lakh.

The Commissioner serves a demand notice for the ₹4 lakh enhancement. Recovery proceedings relating to the original ₹10 lakh can continue from the stage at which they stood immediately before disposal of the appeal.

7. Demand reduced in appeal or revision

Section 84(b) creates a different mechanism where the Government dues are reduced.

Three important consequences follow:

  1. No fresh demand notice is necessary for the reduced amount.
  2. The Commissioner must intimate the taxpayer and the authority with whom recovery proceedings are pending about the reduction.
  3. Recovery can continue for the reduced amount from the stage at which the proceedings stood before the appeal/revision/other proceeding was disposed of.
Why this matters: If ₹10 lakh was under recovery and an appeal reduces it to ₹6 lakh, recovery does not continue for ₹10 lakh. The recovery authority must act on the legally reduced ₹6 lakh.

8. When is a fresh demand notice required?

SituationFresh demand notice?
Original dues continue unchangedSection 84 allows existing recovery to continue from the existing stage.
Dues are enhancedYes — for the amount of enhancement.
Dues are reducedNo fresh demand notice is required for the reduced amount; intimation is given.

This distinction is one of the most important practical points in Section 84.

9. Meaning of continuation from the same stage

The words “continued from the stage at which such proceedings stood immediately before such disposal” prevent unnecessary duplication.

For example, if a recovery process had already reached a particular stage before the appeal was decided, the department does not have to start the recovery process again merely because the appellate order changes the amount.

But the recovery must be aligned with the legally effective amount after the appeal/revision.

11. Section 84 and GST appeals

Section 84 should be read with the GST appellate framework. An appellate authority may confirm, reduce, enhance or otherwise affect the demand depending on the statutory powers applicable to the appeal.

Once the appellate outcome is legally operative, the recovery file must reflect that outcome.

Finance teams should maintain separate records for:

  • original order;
  • amount demanded;
  • amount paid;
  • appeal filed;
  • pre-deposit, where applicable;
  • appellate order;
  • revised balance; and
  • recovery status.

12. Section 84 and revision

The provision expressly refers to a revision application. Therefore, the same basic recovery logic applies where the Government dues are changed through a valid revision proceeding.

If the dues are enhanced, the enhanced amount receives the specific demand treatment under Section 84(a). If reduced, the reduction is communicated and recovery continues for the reduced amount.

13. Other proceedings

Section 84 is not limited to an appeal or revision. Its wording also refers to “any other proceedings” initiated in respect of the Government dues.

The exact proceeding must therefore be identified before applying the section. Do not assume that every communication from the department is automatically an “other proceeding” for Section 84.

14. Appropriate authority

When dues are reduced, Section 84(b)(ii) requires the Commissioner to intimate the appropriate authority with whom recovery proceedings are pending.

This is practically important because a taxpayer may obtain a favourable appellate order while the recovery machinery still holds records reflecting the older amount.

The recovery authority must therefore receive the updated position.

15. Is Section 84 a new demand?

No. Section 84 is primarily a continuation and validation mechanism for recovery after a demand is affected by a later proceeding.

It does not independently create the original tax liability. The underlying demand comes from the relevant determination provision and order.

Remember: Section 84 manages the recovery consequences of a changed demand; it is not the normal provision for creating the original demand.

16. Section 84 vs Section 79

Section 79Section 84
Main GST recovery machinery.Continuation/validation when existing Government dues are affected by appeal, revision or other proceedings.
Contains methods of recovery.Determines how existing recovery is adjusted after the later proceeding.
Can be used to recover legally payable dues.Prevents unnecessary restart and adjusts enhanced/reduced dues.

Easy memory: Section 79 tells you how recovery can happen; Section 84 tells you how an existing recovery process is affected when the demand later changes.

17. Section 84 vs Section 78

Section 78 deals with the period for payment after an order before recovery proceedings are initiated, subject to its statutory framework. Section 84 operates later when Government dues already subject to a demand/recovery framework are affected by an appeal, revision or other proceeding.

Demand order → Section 78 payment period → recovery → appeal/revision outcome → Section 84 adjustment/continuation

18. Section 84 with Sections 73, 74 and 74A

The original demand may arise under the applicable determination provision. Which provision applies depends on the tax period and the statutory framework applicable to that period.

For relevant periods, this may involve Sections 73 or 74. For periods to which the newer Section 74A framework applies, the current law must be checked.

Section 84 does not replace those demand provisions. It deals with what happens to recovery after a demand has been affected by a later proceeding.

22. Finance and accounting implications

Section 84 can change the amount that finance teams should show as outstanding Government dues.

After an appellate/revisional order, the finance team should reconcile:

ItemReview
Original demandAmount in original DRC-07/order.
Appeal/revision resultEnhanced or reduced amount.
Payments madeCash ledger, challans and recovery adjustments.
Interest/penaltyUpdated amount according to the legally effective position.
Recovery statusAmount actually being pursued.

Where financial statements contain provisions or contingent-liability disclosures, the accounting team should update the position based on the current legal status and applicable accounting standards.

23. Construction and infrastructure examples

Works contract demand reduced

A contractor receives a ₹5 crore GST demand relating to project expenses. Appeal reduces it to ₹2 crore. Recovery should be updated to ₹2 crore under Section 84, with the required intimation to the recovery authority.

Large project demand enhanced

An original ₹3 crore demand is enhanced to ₹4.2 crore. The ₹1.2 crore enhancement requires the additional demand treatment under Section 84(a), while the original recovery can continue from its existing stage.

Multiple GSTINs

A construction group operates several GST registrations. The Section 84 analysis should be done GSTIN-wise and demand-wise rather than assuming an appeal for one registration changes another registration's dues.

Bank recovery

If recovery had already progressed to a bank-account action and the demand is later reduced, the recovery authority must be informed of the reduced amount so that recovery does not continue for the superseded amount.

24. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Demand reduced from ₹10 lakh to ₹6 lakh

No fresh demand notice is required for ₹6 lakh. The Commissioner informs the taxpayer and recovery authority, and recovery continues for ₹6 lakh from the existing stage.

Example 2 — Demand enhanced from ₹10 lakh to ₹13 lakh

A demand notice is served for the additional ₹3 lakh. Recovery of the original ₹10 lakh can continue from the stage reached before the appeal was disposed of.

Example 3 — Recovery had not yet started

If the original demand had not entered active recovery before the appellate decision, the recovery authority applies the resulting legally effective amount under the normal recovery framework.

Example 4 — Bank account already attached

If recovery action has already affected a bank account and an appeal reduces the dues, the recovery authority must be informed of the reduction and the action should correspond to the reduced amount.

Example 5 — Partial enhancement

An original ₹50 lakh demand becomes ₹62 lakh. The enhancement is ₹12 lakh and receives the additional-demand treatment.

Example 6 — Partial reduction

An original ₹50 lakh demand becomes ₹30 lakh. The ₹20 lakh reduction is communicated; no fresh demand notice is required for the ₹30 lakh reduced amount.

Example 7 — Penalty reduced

If the appellate order reduces the penalty while tax remains unchanged, the recovery file must reflect the legally revised Government dues.

Example 8 — Interest recalculation

Where the appellate outcome changes the tax base or amount payable, the associated interest should be reconciled according to the applicable statutory rules rather than simply carrying forward the old figure.

Example 9 — Further appeal

If a taxpayer challenges the appellate order further, the finance team should maintain a new litigation stage and avoid treating the matter as finally settled merely because one appeal was decided.

Example 10 — Departmental revision

A revision proceeding enhances the dues. Section 84(a) applies to the enhanced portion and the original recovery can continue according to the statutory mechanism.

Example 11 — Insolvency process

Where insolvency proceedings affect the taxpayer, the recovery position should also be reviewed under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code and applicable Government instructions. Section 84 does not override the separate insolvency framework.

Example 12 — Multiple demands

A company has separate demands for several financial years. An appeal for one year does not automatically alter another year's demand. Section 84 must be applied demand-by-demand.

28. Department/recovery officer perspective

From the recovery side, Section 84 is intended to preserve continuity while ensuring that recovery corresponds to the legally effective amount.

For an enhanced demand:

  • issue demand for the enhancement;
  • continue recovery of the original dues from the existing stage; and
  • maintain a clear record of the enhanced amount.

For a reduced demand:

  • do not insist on a fresh demand notice for the reduced amount;
  • inform the taxpayer;
  • inform the appropriate recovery authority; and
  • continue recovery for the reduced amount from the existing stage.

29. Insolvency and IBC considerations

Section 84 itself does not create a separate insolvency priority rule. Where the taxpayer is under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, the recovery position must be considered together with the applicable IBC framework and the nature/stage of insolvency proceedings.

CBIC has also issued clarification concerning treatment of GST statutory dues after proceedings are finalised under the IBC. The clarification reproduces Section 84's reduced-demand mechanism and explains that where Government dues are reduced, recovery continues for the reduced amount after the required intimation.

30. Legislative development

PeriodDevelopment
1 July 2017Section 84 came into force as part of the original CGST recovery framework.
2018The GST Council considered clarifying Section 84(b)(i) by expressly referring to “the taxable person or any other person”.
Current lawThe operative provision covers a demand served on a taxable person or any other person and provides separate mechanisms for enhancement and reduction.

The GST Council's 25th meeting agenda records the proposed clarification to Section 84(b)(i), reflecting the need to make the reduced-demand communication framework apply clearly beyond only the taxable person.

31. Administrative clarification

CBIC Circular No. 224/18/2024-GST, dated 7 November 2024, provides guidelines for recovery of outstanding dues in cases where the first appeal has been disposed of while the Appellate Tribunal was not yet operational. It is useful background when studying how recovery interacts with appellate proceedings.

Separately, CBIC's IBC clarification discusses Section 84 in the context of reduced Government dues following insolvency proceedings.

These administrative materials should be read as implementation guidance alongside the statute and Rules, not as replacements for the statutory text.

32. Common mistakes

  • Assuming Section 84 creates the original GST demand.
  • Failing to distinguish enhanced dues from reduced dues.
  • Issuing/expecting a fresh demand notice for a reduced amount when Section 84(b) does not require one.
  • Ignoring the requirement of a fresh demand for the enhanced portion.
  • Continuing recovery for the original higher amount after a valid reduction.
  • Failing to inform the authority handling recovery.
  • Not reconciling pre-deposits and payments.
  • Mixing separate GSTINs or financial years.
  • Ignoring further appeal/revision proceedings.
  • Confusing Section 84 with Section 79.
  • Treating Section 84 as a substitute for the original demand provision.

33. Section 84 GST FAQs

What is Section 84 of the CGST Act?

Section 84 deals with continuation and validation of certain recovery proceedings when Government dues are affected by an appeal, revision or other proceeding.

What happens if GST demand is reduced in appeal?

No fresh demand notice is required for the reduced amount. The Commissioner informs the taxpayer and the authority handling recovery, and recovery can continue for the reduced amount from the existing stage.

What happens if GST demand is enhanced in appeal?

The Commissioner serves another demand notice for the amount by which the dues are enhanced. Recovery of the original dues can continue from the existing stage.

Is a fresh demand notice required after every appeal?

No. Section 84 specifically distinguishes enhancement from reduction. Enhancement requires a demand notice for the enhanced amount; reduction does not require a fresh demand notice for the reduced amount.

Can GST recovery continue after an appeal?

Yes, subject to the applicable appellate and recovery framework. Section 84 specifically allows recovery to continue from the stage reached before disposal of the appeal/revision, with the legally revised amount applied.

What does “continued from the stage” mean?

It means the department does not have to restart the recovery process from the beginning merely because the demand has been changed by the later proceeding.

Does Section 84 apply to revision?

Yes. Revision applications are expressly included.

Does Section 84 apply to other proceedings?

Yes, the statutory text also refers to other proceedings initiated in respect of the Government dues, subject to the facts and applicable law.

Does Section 84 create tax liability?

No. The underlying liability arises under the applicable demand/determination provision. Section 84 governs continuation and validation of recovery after the dues are affected by a later proceeding.

What are Government dues under Section 84?

Tax, penalty, interest and any other amount payable under the CGST Act are included in the expression used by Section 84.

What if the original demand was ₹20 lakh and appeal reduces it to ₹8 lakh?

The Commissioner informs the taxpayer and recovery authority of the reduction. Recovery can continue for ₹8 lakh from the stage reached before the appeal was disposed of.

What if ₹20 lakh becomes ₹25 lakh?

A demand notice is served for the additional ₹5 lakh. Recovery of the original ₹20 lakh can continue from its existing stage.

Is Section 84 the same as Section 79?

No. Section 79 provides the recovery machinery. Section 84 deals with how recovery is continued or adjusted after appeal, revision or other proceedings change the Government dues.

Does Section 84 apply to every GST demand?

It applies when the statutory conditions are met: a demand has been served and an appeal, revision or other proceeding is initiated in respect of the Government dues.

Does Section 84 stop recovery during appeal?

Section 84 itself is not a general stay provision. Whether recovery is stayed depends on the applicable appellate law, statutory pre-deposit requirements, orders of competent authorities and the facts.

What should a finance team do after an appellate order?

Reconcile the original demand, revised demand, tax/interest/penalty, payments, pre-deposits and recovery action, and update the litigation and accounting records.

35. Key takeaways

  • Section 84 is a bridge between GST litigation and GST recovery.
  • It applies after a demand has been served and an appeal, revision or other proceeding affects the Government dues.
  • If dues are enhanced, a demand notice is required for the enhanced amount.
  • If dues are reduced, a fresh demand notice is not required for the reduced amount.
  • For reduced dues, the Commissioner must inform the taxpayer and the authority handling recovery.
  • Recovery can continue from the stage at which it stood before the appeal/revision/other proceeding was disposed of.
  • Section 84 does not itself create the original tax demand.
  • Section 79 remains the main recovery machinery.
  • Finance teams should reconcile the revised demand with payments, pre-deposits and recovery actions.
  • Each GSTIN, financial year and demand should be tracked separately.
  • Where further appeal, revision or insolvency proceedings exist, the complete legal position should be reviewed before concluding that the dues are final.
