Rule 7 – Rate of Tax of the Composition Levy

Understand the current composition-tax rate table, which taxpayer category each rate applies to, how the calculation works, and why the rate must never be selected without first checking eligibility.

At a glance

What Rule 7 does

Rule 7 prescribes the rate at which eligible registered persons pay tax under the composition levy under Section 10 and the composition chapter of the CGST Rules.

Important: The rate must be read together with the taxpayer's eligibility under Section 10 and the other composition provisions. Rule 7 tells you the rate after the legal category has been established; it does not independently grant eligibility.

The current CBIC rules show four categories: specified manufacturers, suppliers making supplies covered by paragraph 6(b) of Schedule II, other eligible suppliers under Sections 10(1)/(2), and the special Section 10(2A) category.

The four-category structure and the prescribed percentages are reflected in the CBIC version of the CGST Rules.

2. Rule 7 in simple language

Rule 7 answers one practical question: “Once I am legally eligible for composition, what percentage applies to my category?”

Manufacturer
Ordinary eligible manufacturer category: 0.5% CGST on the prescribed turnover base.
Schedule II paragraph 6(b)
The specified category carries a 2.5% CGST rate.
Other eligible supplier
Ordinary eligible supplier category carries 0.5% CGST on the prescribed taxable-supply turnover base.
Section 10(2A)
Special category carries 3% of turnover of supplies of goods and services in the State/UT.
Do not choose the rate first. A business cannot say “I will use 0.5% because it is lower.” The legal category and eligibility must be established first.

3. How to calculate composition tax

Step 1 — Identify the Section 10 route

Determine whether the taxpayer is using Sections 10(1)/(2) or the special Section 10(2A) route.

Step 2 — Identify the Rule 7 category

Classify the taxpayer as the specified manufacturer, Schedule II paragraph 6(b) supplier, other eligible supplier, or Section 10(2A) taxpayer.

Step 3 — Establish the correct turnover base

Read the exact wording of the applicable row. The table does not use identical wording for every category.

Step 4 — Apply the rate

Apply the prescribed composition rate to the relevant base and separately consider the corresponding State/UT component for intra-State supplies.

Finance control: Maintain a monthly Rule 7 working showing the category, turnover base, CGST composition tax and corresponding State/UT liability.

6. Business impact of the rate

Pricing
Composition treatment affects how the business presents its prices because the taxpayer generally does not collect GST as a normal registered supplier.
ITC economics
Composition status changes the input-tax-credit position, so management should compare the total tax cost rather than only the output percentage.
Customer profile
A B2B customer that expects input tax credit may view composition status differently from a final consumer.
Business changes
New products, services or activities can change eligibility and therefore the applicable tax treatment.

7. Common mistakes

  1. Using 0.5% for every composition taxpayer. Rule 7 has different categories.
  2. Confusing CGST with the complete intra-State tax. The State/UT component must also be considered.
  3. Applying the rate before checking eligibility.
  4. Ignoring the different turnover wording between rows.
  5. Mixing ordinary Section 10(1)/(2) taxpayers with Section 10(2A) taxpayers.
  6. Failing to update ERP rates after a change in status.
  7. Comparing composition only on output tax and ignoring ITC and commercial impact.

8. Audit and GST notice relevance

In an audit, the rate calculation should be capable of being traced from the legal category to the turnover working and finally to the tax paid.

  • Document the taxpayer's composition category.
  • Keep the Rule 7 rate determination working.
  • Reconcile turnover with books and GST records.
  • Keep separate workings for different business activities where classification matters.
  • Preserve the effective-date and eligibility analysis under Rules 4–6.
  • Reconcile composition tax paid with the relevant returns and accounting ledgers.
Notice risk: An incorrect category can make an otherwise mathematically correct percentage calculation legally incorrect. The strongest defence is a clear category-and-turnover trail.

11. Frequently asked questions

What is GST Rule 7?

Rule 7 of the CGST Rules prescribes the rates of tax for eligible composition taxpayers under Section 10 and the composition provisions.

What is the composition rate for manufacturers?

The Rule 7 table prescribes 0.5% for the specified manufacturer category under Sections 10(1) and 10(2), subject to the applicable eligibility conditions.

What is the 2.5% composition rate?

The 2.5% CGST rate applies to suppliers making supplies referred to in clause (b) of paragraph 6 of Schedule II, subject to the applicable composition provisions.

What is the Rule 7 rate for other eligible suppliers?

The table prescribes 0.5% of the turnover of taxable supplies of goods and services in the State or Union territory for the specified other-supplier category under Sections 10(1) and 10(2).

What is the rate under Section 10(2A)?

Rule 7 prescribes 3% of turnover of supplies of goods and services in the State or Union territory for the Section 10(2A) category.

Does Rule 7 itself make a business eligible for composition?

No. Eligibility must first be established under Section 10 and the applicable composition conditions and restrictions.

Is 0.5% the total GST payable?

Rule 7 prescribes the composition tax rate in the CGST Rules. For intra-State supplies, the corresponding State/UT component also needs to be considered.

12. Practical Rule 7 checklist

  • Confirm composition eligibility.
  • Identify the correct Section 10 route.
  • Identify the exact Rule 7 category.
  • Determine the correct turnover base.
  • Apply the prescribed rate.
  • Consider the corresponding State/UT component.
  • Reconcile the working with books and GST records.
  • Document the rate determination for audit purposes.

13. Practical takeaway

Rule 7 is best understood as the rate-selection stage of the composition process.

Correct sequence: Eligibility → Section 10 route → Rule 7 category → Turnover base → Rate → CGST/State or UT component → Reconciliation.

If the category is wrong, the calculation can be wrong even when the arithmetic is perfect. For finance teams, the safest approach is to document the category and turnover base and maintain a monthly reconciliation.

14. Source & verification
