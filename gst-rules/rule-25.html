1. Rule 25 at a glance

Rule 25 of the CGST Rules, 2017 deals with physical verification of business premises in certain cases. The rule is important because GST registration is not always completed solely through document review and electronic verification. In specified circumstances, the proper officer can require a physical verification of the declared place of business.

Current structure: Following the 2023 substitution of Rule 25, the rule now operates in two connected situations: physical verification required before grant of registration in circumstances specified in the proviso to Rule 9(1), and physical verification required after grant of registration.

The verification report, supporting documents and photographs are uploaded electronically in FORM GST REG-30.

3. Physical verification before grant of registration

Rule 25(2) applies where physical verification is required before grant of registration in the circumstances specified in the proviso to Rule 9(1).

The present registration framework includes risk-based and Aadhaar-related circumstances in which physical verification may be required. CBIC instructions also direct officers to initiate physical verification promptly where the applicable Rule 9 conditions require it.

What does the verification establish?

  • Whether the declared principal place of business exists.
  • Whether the address corresponds with the registration application.
  • Whether the business premises can be located and identified.
  • Whether the documents and factual position support the declared place of business.
  • Whether photographs and other verification evidence support the report.
Business preparation: Keep the address, ownership/lease evidence, utility documents, name board/signage where appropriate, books/records and responsible-person contact details consistent with the registration application.

4. Physical verification after grant of registration

Under Rule 25(1), after registration has been granted, the proper officer may get physical verification of the place of business carried out where the officer is satisfied that such verification is required.

This is important because physical verification is not limited to the initial registration stage. A registered taxpayer can therefore face a premises verification later, including where the department considers verification necessary based on the circumstances of the case.

Why might post-registration verification matter?

  • Verification of the declared principal or additional place of business.
  • Clarification of registration records.
  • Risk-based departmental verification.
  • Investigation of inconsistencies in registration particulars.
  • Other circumstances in which the proper officer considers physical verification necessary.
Do not assume: Physical verification automatically means that the registration will be cancelled. The verification report is evidence for the department's assessment; the legal consequence depends on the facts and the applicable statutory provisions.

5. FORM GST REG-30 — verification report

Rule 25 requires the physical verification report, along with other documents including photographs, to be uploaded in FORM GST REG-30 on the common portal.

StageRule 25 requirement
Before registrationReport to be uploaded at least five working days before completion of the applicable Rule 9 time period.
After registrationReport to be uploaded within fifteen working days following the date of verification.

CBIC's 2025 instructions also emphasise recording whether the declared principal place exists, documenting efforts where the entity is found non-existing and uploading GPS-enabled site photographs and other relevant documents in cases requiring physical verification.

For taxpayers: A business should retain its own evidence of the premises and its operations rather than relying only on the officer's report.

6. What can the officer verify?

Rule 25 itself focuses on physical verification of the place of business and the resulting report. In practice, the verification exercise can involve checking the factual existence and identity of the declared premises against the registration information and supporting records.

7. Aadhaar authentication and risk-based verification

Physical verification must be read together with the current Rule 9 registration process. The circumstances requiring physical verification before grant of registration can include failure to undergo Aadhaar authentication or not opting for Aadhaar authentication, as well as specified risk-based cases.

CBIC's instructions also provide for risk-based identification of applicants and require officers to follow the prescribed verification process where the case is flagged or physical verification is otherwise authorised.

Key point: Aadhaar authentication and physical verification are related but not identical concepts. Failure to authenticate or not opting for authentication can be one trigger within the registration framework, while physical verification may also arise from other specified risk or departmental reasons.

8. Practical business examples

Example 1 — Office address verification

A company applies for GST registration for an office in Hyderabad. The registration application contains a lease document for the same address. If the application falls within a prescribed physical-verification route, the officer may visit the premises and record the findings in REG-30.

Example 2 — Construction company

A construction company has a registered office and multiple project locations. The finance team should distinguish the registered principal/additional places of business from temporary project sites and maintain the relevant registration documentation for each declared location.

Example 3 — Post-registration verification

A registered business changes its premises but does not update the GST registration particulars. If a later physical verification finds a mismatch, the taxpayer may face registration/compliance issues. The better control is to update registration particulars promptly when there is a genuine change.

Example 4 — Premises shared by multiple entities

Several entities operate from the same commercial address. The taxpayer should maintain clear documentation showing its right to use the premises and ensure that its GST registration particulars are factually accurate.

9. How a taxpayer should prepare for verification

  1. Confirm the GST registration address in the portal and compare it with the actual premises.
  2. Keep occupancy evidence ready—ownership, lease, consent or other applicable documents.
  3. Keep address-linked documents available where relevant.
  4. Ensure the business can be identified at the declared premises.
  5. Keep the authorised/responsible person informed about the verification process.
  6. Check additional places of business separately.
  7. Correct genuine registration amendments instead of allowing outdated addresses to remain.
  8. Preserve your own evidence of the premises and business operations.
Management principle: The registration master should be treated as a controlled legal master-data record, not merely a form filed at the time of incorporation.

12. Common mistakes

  • Assuming physical verification can occur only before registration.
  • Using an outdated Rule 25 version in current compliance guidance.
  • Keeping the GST address different from the actual premises without updating registration records.
  • Not maintaining lease/ownership/consent documentation.
  • Ignoring additional-place-of-business registration requirements.
  • Assuming physical verification itself equals cancellation of registration.
  • Failing to preserve evidence after an officer's verification visit.

13. FAQs

What is Rule 25 of the CGST Rules?

Rule 25 provides for physical verification of business premises in specified cases, both before grant of registration through its linkage with Rule 9 and after registration where the proper officer considers verification necessary.

What form is used for the physical verification report?

The current Rule 25 requires the verification report and supporting documents, including photographs, to be uploaded in FORM GST REG-30.

How long does the officer have to upload the report after post-registration verification?

Rule 25(1) specifies fifteen working days following the date of verification.

Is physical verification mandatory for every GST registration?

No. It is required in specified circumstances under the registration framework; it is not a universal physical inspection of every applicant.

Can physical verification happen after GST registration is granted?

Yes. Rule 25(1) expressly covers physical verification after grant of registration where the proper officer is satisfied that it is required.

Does a physical verification visit mean GST registration will be cancelled?

No. Verification is a fact-finding mechanism. Any later consequence depends on the findings and the applicable statutory provisions.

Why should construction companies pay attention to Rule 25?

Construction businesses often operate across multiple offices, sites and States. Keeping the principal/additional-place registration data and supporting address documents accurate reduces the risk of avoidable verification issues.

14. Compliance checklist

  • ☐ Verify principal-place address in the GST registration.
  • ☐ Verify all additional places of business.
  • ☐ Maintain ownership/lease/consent evidence.
  • ☐ Maintain address-linked supporting documents.
  • ☐ Review GST registration whenever premises change.
  • ☐ Keep a GSTIN-wise premises master in the ERP/MIS.
  • ☐ Preserve physical-verification communications and reports.
  • ☐ Check that current guidance uses the post-2023 Rule 25 wording.
