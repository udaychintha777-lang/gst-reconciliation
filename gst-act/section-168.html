CGST Act Section 168 – Power to Issue Instructions or Directions

Section 168 empowers the Board to issue orders, instructions or directions to central tax officers for uniformity in the implementation of the CGST Act and also contains a special rule explaining the meaning of “Commissioner” for specified provisions.

1. Section 168 at a Glance

Who can issue instructions?The Board.
Why?When necessary or expedient for uniformity in implementation of the CGST Act.
Who must observe them?Central tax officers and other persons employed in implementation of the Act, as stated in sub-section (1).
In simple language: Section 168 is an administrative implementation provision. It allows the Board to issue directions so that the CGST Act is implemented consistently across central tax formations. It should be distinguished from the Act itself, rules, regulations and individual taxpayer orders.

3. Statutory Text of Section 168

168. Power to issue instructions or directions.— (1) The Board may, if it considers it necessary or expedient so to do for the purpose of uniformity in the implementation of this Act, issue such orders, instructions or directions to the central tax officers as it may deem fit, and thereupon all such officers and all other persons employed in the implementation of this Act shall observe and follow such orders, instructions or directions. (2) The Commissioner specified in clause (91) of section 2, sub-section (3) of section 5, clause (b) of sub-section (9) of section 25, sub-sections (3) and (4) of section 35, sub-section (1) of section 37, sub-section (2) of section 38, sub-section (6) of section 39, sub-section (5) of section 66, sub-section (1) of section 143, sub-section (1) of section 151, clause (l) of sub-section (3) of section 158 and section 167 shall mean a Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board and such Commissioner or Joint Secretary shall exercise the powers specified in the said sections with the approval of the Board.
Important: Section 168 has two distinct functions. Sub-section (1) concerns Board orders, instructions or directions for uniformity. Sub-section (2) specifies who is meant by “Commissioner” for the particular provisions listed there.

4. Meaning in Simple Language

The GST Act contains substantive legal provisions, but tax administration also requires consistent implementation across many offices and officers. Section 168 gives the Board a mechanism to promote that consistency.

Under sub-section (1), the Board may issue orders, instructions or directions to central tax officers when it considers this necessary or expedient for uniformity in implementing the Act. Once issued, the provision says the relevant officers and other persons employed in implementation shall observe and follow them.

Sub-section (2) serves a different purpose. For a specified list of provisions, the word “Commissioner” refers to a Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board, and that person exercises the specified powers with the approval of the Board.

Easy memory: 168(1) = uniform implementation. 168(2) = special meaning of “Commissioner” for listed provisions.

5. Section 168(1) – Instructions, Orders and Directions

Sub-section (1) contains four important components:

ComponentWhat it means
BoardThe power belongs to the Board.
Necessary or expedientThe Board may act where it considers the instruction/direction necessary or expedient.
PurposeThe stated purpose is uniformity in implementation of the CGST Act.
Recipients and complianceThe provision directs central tax officers and other persons employed in implementation to observe and follow the orders, instructions or directions.

The provision is therefore aimed primarily at administrative uniformity. It helps avoid materially different implementation approaches across different central tax formations.

6. What Does “Uniformity in Implementation” Mean?

Uniformity means that the Board can communicate a common administrative approach on issues arising in the implementation of the Act. This is particularly relevant where field formations may otherwise adopt inconsistent procedures.

Common procedureHelps officers follow a consistent administrative approach.
ClarificationMay explain how the Board expects a statutory provision to be implemented.
Field-level consistencyHelps reduce different administrative approaches across formations.

Official CBIC material contains examples of directions issued expressly under Section 168(1). For example, Circular No. 33/07/2018-GST states that the Board acted under Section 168 to issue directions concerning disputed transitional CENVAT credit.

CBIC has also issued instructions under Section 168(1) to promote uniformity in procedures relating to interception, inspection, detention, seizure and release of goods and conveyances.

7. Section 168(2) – Special Meaning of “Commissioner”

Sub-section (2) is separate from the general instruction power in sub-section (1). It says that, for the listed provisions, the Commissioner means a Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board.

The list in Section 168(2) includes provisions relating to, among other matters, powers under Sections 5, 25, 35, 37, 38, 39, 66, 143, 151, 158 and 167.

FeatureSection 168(2)
Person coveredCommissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board.
Where applicableOnly for the specific provisions listed in sub-section (2).
ApprovalThe specified Commissioner or Joint Secretary exercises the powers with approval of the Board.
PurposeProvides a statutory meaning of “Commissioner” for the listed provisions.

8. Why Section 168(2) Matters

A common mistake is to read the word “Commissioner” in a listed section without checking whether Section 168(2) changes its statutory meaning for that provision.

For the provisions enumerated in Section 168(2), the statutory meaning is specifically linked to a Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board, with Board approval for exercise of the specified powers.

Important: Section 168(2) is not a general definition of “Commissioner” for every provision of the CGST Act. Its wording applies to the provisions specifically listed in the subsection.

9. Legal Character of Section 168 Instructions

For compliance and litigation work, it is important to identify what a Section 168 document actually is.

InstrumentGeneral functionDo not confuse it with

CGST RuleDelegated legislation made under the Act.A circular or internal direction.
RegulationBoard regulation under Section 165.A Section 168 instruction.
Section 168 order/instruction/directionBoard administrative direction for uniformity in implementation.The parent charging provision itself.

Practical rule: If a notice or departmental action relies on a Section 168 document, retain the exact document and identify the statutory provision it is implementing.

10. Practical Examples

Example 1 – Uniform procedure

The Board issues an instruction to central tax officers so that a particular statutory procedure is handled consistently across field formations.

Example 2 – Transitional credit

CBIC has historically issued Section 168 directions on transitional-credit implementation. The legal file should retain the actual circular rather than relying on a summary.

Example 3 – Movement of goods

CBIC has issued Section 168 instructions concerning interception and inspection of goods and conveyances to promote uniform implementation.

Example 4 – Officer follows instruction

A central tax officer follows a Board instruction in administering a provision. The instruction can explain the administrative approach, but the underlying Act remains the statutory source.

Example 5 – Taxpayer receives notice

If a notice cites a Section 168 circular, the taxpayer can identify the circular, its date, subject and statutory basis before preparing the response.

Example 6 – Circular amended

If a circular is amended or superseded, the historical version applicable to the transaction should be identified.

Example 7 – Section 168(2)

When a provision listed in Section 168(2) uses the term “Commissioner”, the special statutory meaning in Section 168(2) should be considered.

Example 8 – Board approval

For the specified powers in Section 168(2), the subsection states that the Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board exercises those powers with Board approval.

Example 9 – Circular versus rule

A finance team should not configure an ERP as though a Section 168 circular were itself a CGST Rule. Record the legal source separately.

Example 10 – Multiple GSTINs

A company with multiple registrations can maintain a central register of material Section 168 instructions and identify which processes they affect.

Example 11 – Historical audit

For an old tax period, retain the instruction that applied at that time rather than relying solely on a later consolidated circular.

Example 12 – Litigation

If the legal effect of a Board instruction is disputed, the case file should include the instruction, parent statutory provision, relevant rules and applicable judicial interpretation.

Example 13 – Officer authority

Where a proceeding involves a provision listed in Section 168(2), check whether the special meaning of “Commissioner” affects the authority relevant to the action.

Example 14 – Internal SOP

A company may incorporate an applicable Section 168 instruction into its SOP, but should preserve the original Board document as the source.

11. Finance & Litigation Workflow

  1. Identify the issue: return, ITC, assessment, audit, investigation, movement of goods or other GST process.
  2. Find the underlying Act section: identify the statutory provision being implemented.
  3. Identify the Board instruction: note circular/order/instruction/direction number and date.
  4. Check Section 168: verify whether the document expressly relies on Section 168.
  5. Check amendments: determine whether the instruction has been amended, rescinded or superseded.
  6. Check historical applicability: match the instruction to the relevant tax period and proceeding date.
  7. For Section 168(2): if a listed provision is involved, verify the statutory meaning of “Commissioner” and Board-approval requirement.
  8. Retain evidence: archive the exact circular/instruction used for the position.

14. Common Mistakes

  • Confusing a Section 168 instruction with a CGST Rule.
  • Assuming every GST circular is issued under Section 168.
  • Ignoring the purpose of uniformity stated in sub-section (1).
  • Applying a superseded circular to a current transaction.
  • Using a current circular to interpret an old tax period without checking its applicability.
  • Ignoring Section 168(2) when a listed provision uses the term “Commissioner”.
  • Assuming a departmental instruction can independently replace the parent Act.
  • Failing to preserve the exact version of the Board instruction relied upon.
  • Confusing delegation under Section 167 with instructions under Section 168.
  • Treating a portal advisory as automatically having the same legal character as a Section 168 direction.

16. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 168 of the CGST Act?

Section 168 gives the Board power to issue orders, instructions or directions to central tax officers when necessary or expedient for uniformity in implementation of the CGST Act. Sub-section (2) also defines the meaning of “Commissioner” for the specified provisions listed there.

2. Why does the Board issue instructions under Section 168?

The stated purpose in sub-section (1) is uniformity in the implementation of the CGST Act.

3. Who must follow Section 168 instructions?

Sub-section (1) states that central tax officers and all other persons employed in implementation of the Act shall observe and follow the orders, instructions or directions.

4. Can Section 168 create a new tax liability?

Section 168(1) is an administrative direction power aimed at uniform implementation. The substantive charging provisions and taxpayer liabilities must be traced to the Act and applicable delegated legislation.

5. Are Section 168 instructions the same as CGST Rules?

No. Rules are made under the rule-making power. Section 168 concerns Board orders, instructions or directions for uniformity in implementation.

6. Are Section 168 instructions the same as notifications?

No. The legal character and statutory source should be identified for each instrument.

7. Can a Section 168 direction be relevant to a taxpayer?

Yes. Although addressed to central tax officers, the direction can affect how officers administer and implement the Act, so it may be relevant when understanding departmental practice.

8. What is the purpose of Section 168(2)?

It specifies that “Commissioner” in the listed provisions means a Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board, who exercises the specified powers with Board approval.

9. Does every Commissioner become a Commissioner under Section 168(2)?

No. Sub-section (2) specifically refers to the Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board for the listed provisions and requires Board approval for exercise of those powers.

10. What is the difference between Section 167 and 168?

Section 167 concerns delegation of powers by the Commissioner through notification. Section 168 concerns Board orders, instructions or directions for uniform implementation and separately defines the Commissioner for listed provisions.

11. Can officers ignore a Section 168 direction?

Sub-section (1) states that the relevant central tax officers and other persons employed in implementation shall observe and follow such orders, instructions or directions.

12. Does Section 168 apply to State GST officers?

The text of sub-section (1) refers to central tax officers and persons employed in implementation of the Act. State GST administration has its own statutory framework; applicability should be checked rather than assumed.

13. Can a taxpayer challenge an action merely because it follows an instruction?

A specific challenge depends on the Act, the instruction, the facts and applicable judicial interpretation. The instruction should not be treated as replacing the statutory provisions.

14. What should be checked when a notice cites a Section 168 instruction?

Check the exact instruction, issuing authority, date, statutory basis, subject, relevant period and whether it is an order, instruction or direction under Section 168.

15. Why is Section 168 important for GST administration?

It provides a statutory mechanism for promoting uniform implementation across central tax formations.

16. Is Section 168 still relevant to current GST law?

Yes. The provision appears in the CGST Act and is used in official CBIC directions and circulars for uniform implementation.

17. Should companies maintain a Section 168 circular register?

For material GST compliance and litigation work, maintaining the source, date, subject, affected process and current status of relevant Board instructions is a useful control.

18. Is this article legal advice?

No. It is an educational explanation. For a particular dispute or notice, verify the current Act, rules, notifications, instructions and applicable judicial interpretation.

17. Quick Revision – Section 168

  • Subject: Power to issue instructions or directions.
  • Authority under 168(1): Board.
  • Purpose: Uniformity in implementation of the CGST Act.
  • Instruments: Orders, instructions or directions.
  • Persons required to follow: Central tax officers and other persons employed in implementation, as stated in sub-section (1).
  • 168(2): Defines “Commissioner” as a Commissioner or Joint Secretary posted in the Board for specified listed provisions.
  • Approval: The specified Commissioner/Joint Secretary exercises those powers with Board approval.
  • Compliance lesson: Always identify the exact instruction, legal source, date, applicable period and whether it has been amended or superseded.
