GST QRMP Scheme – Eligibility, IFF, PMT-06, 35% Challan, Due Dates & Practical Examples

1. QRMP in one minute – what changes and what does not?

QRMP means Quarterly Return, Monthly Payment of Taxes. An eligible registered person files GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly, but generally deposits tax for the first two months through Form GST PMT-06.

Returns

One quarterly GSTR-1 and one quarterly GSTR-3B for each quarter.

Monthly payment

For month 1 and month 2, use the fixed-sum method or self-assessment method through PMT-06, subject to the prescribed conditions.

Optional invoice reporting

Use IFF for selected B2B invoices in the first two months if customers need earlier reflection for ITC.

Monthly discipline remains

Maintain sales, purchases, ITC, RCM, e-invoice and cash-flow records monthly. Quarterly filing does not mean quarterly bookkeeping.

Important distinction QRMP changes the frequency of GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B. It does not change GST rates, the substantive ITC eligibility rules, invoice time-of-supply rules or the obligation to maintain accurate monthly records.

2. Who is eligible for QRMP?

A registered person required to furnish GSTR-3B may opt for QRMP where aggregate turnover on a PAN basis is within the ₹5 crore limit for the relevant preceding/current financial-year tests under the scheme. The latest filed GSTR-3B must be furnished before opting, and the portal must permit the selection.

TestPractical explanation
Turnover limitAggregate annual turnover up to ₹5 crore, calculated on PAN basis across registrations, not GSTIN-by-GSTIN in isolation. Include the relevant supplies forming part of aggregate turnover under the Act.
Return obligationTaxpayer should be a person required to furnish GSTR-3B and otherwise covered by the notified class.
Return filing statusLast return due as on the date of opting should have been filed.
Multiple GSTINs on one PANQRMP selection is GSTIN-wise. One GSTIN can use QRMP while another GSTIN under the same PAN remains monthly, subject to eligibility.
SEZ unit/developerEligible categories include regular taxpayers and SEZ units/developers, subject to the scheme conditions.
Composition taxpayerQRMP is not the composition scheme. A taxpayer paying tax under the composition scheme follows the separate composition return/payment procedure.
Do not test the ₹5 crore limit only using one GSTIN's sales. Aggregate turnover is PAN-based and can include turnover from registrations in different States. Monitor it throughout the year.

3. How to opt in or opt out – portal steps and time window

  1. Log in to the GST portal using the taxpayer's credentials.
  2. Navigate to Services → Returns → Opt-in for Quarterly Return.
  3. Select the relevant financial year/quarter and review the filing frequency shown for the GSTIN.
  4. Choose quarterly or monthly filing, as permitted by the portal.
  5. Submit the selection and save the acknowledgement/screenshot in the compliance folder.
QuarterQRMP option windowLast date
April–June1 February to 30 April30 April
July–September1 May to 31 July31 July
October–December1 August to 31 October31 October
January–March1 November to 31 January31 January

The option, once exercised, generally continues for subsequent quarters unless changed within the applicable window or the taxpayer becomes ineligible. The portal requires the latest due GSTR-3B to be filed before allowing the option. A GST practitioner may assist with compliance but cannot independently exercise the option on behalf of the taxpayer; the taxpayer/authorised signatory must do so.

Current date example: For the January–March 2027 quarter, the ordinary selection window is 1 November 2026 to 31 January 2027. Check the GST portal for the GSTIN's current status and any specific extension/advisory.

5. IFF – what it is, who should use it and its ₹50 lakh limit

Invoice Furnishing Facility is an optional facility for QRMP taxpayers to report specified outward supply details to registered recipients for the first two months of a quarter. It helps the recipient see eligible invoice details earlier in its GSTR-2A/GSTR-2B flow and plan ITC reconciliation.

What can be reported?

  • Specified B2B invoices and debit/credit notes for registered recipients.
  • Eligible document details covered by the IFF functionality and applicable rules.
  • Do not use IFF to report B2C supplies; report those in the quarterly GSTR-1 as applicable.
IFF rulePractical position
Optional or mandatory?Optional. Use it where customers need earlier invoice visibility.
Time limitBy the 13th day of the succeeding month for the previous month.
Value capAggregate value of supplies furnished through IFF cannot exceed ₹50 lakh in each of the first two months.
After 13thIFF for that month closes; unreported invoices can be included in quarterly GSTR-1.
Repeat reporting?Invoices already furnished through IFF should not be reported again in quarterly GSTR-1.
Example – supplier with large B2B customers A supplier issues 40 B2B invoices in April. Five customers require early ITC visibility. The supplier can select eligible invoices within the IFF limit and file IFF by 13 May. The remaining invoices are reported in the quarterly GSTR-1. This is not an additional return for all invoices.

When should you skip IFF?

If customers do not need early ITC visibility, or the supplier has no eligible B2B documents to report, it can skip IFF and report the quarter's details in GSTR-1. Maintain an IFF-vs-GSTR-1 control to avoid omissions and duplication.

6. PMT-06 – two methods for monthly tax payment

FeatureFixed Sum Method (35% challan)Self-Assessment Method
BasisSystem-generated amount based on previous cash tax payment history.Actual current-period tax liability after considering eligible ITC and applicable rules.
Monthly calculationSame fixed-sum amount for each of first two months, based on prescribed previous-period basis.Calculate each month using actual books and available eligible ITC.
Best suited forTaxpayers with stable tax profile and who want a simple prefilled payment.Taxpayers with fluctuating sales, ITC, seasonality or high-value transactions.
Interest protectionNo interest on the difference between actual monthly liability and fixed-sum deposit if the fixed sum is paid by due date and full quarterly liability is discharged by quarterly GSTR-3B due date.Interest may apply on tax short-paid or paid late for month 1/month 2, as per Section 50 and the applicable rules.

For either method, generate Form GST PMT-06 and select the reason corresponding to monthly payment by a quarterly taxpayer. Payment is ordinarily due by the 25th of the month following the relevant month.

Do not confuse PMT-06 with GSTR-3B. PMT-06 deposits money into the electronic cash ledger. It is not a monthly GSTR-3B return and does not itself file or finally offset the quarter's return liability.

7. 35% fixed-sum method – calculation with examples

The fixed sum is based on the cash tax paid in the immediately preceding period, depending on the taxpayer's previous return frequency.

Previous return frequencyBasis for PMT-06 fixed sum in each of first two months
Previous quarter filed quarterly35% of the tax paid through the electronic cash ledger in the preceding quarter's GSTR-3B.
Previous period filed monthlyAmount equal to tax paid through the electronic cash ledger in the last month of the immediately preceding quarter's monthly GSTR-3B.

Example A – previous quarter was quarterly

In the preceding quarter, cash tax paid in GSTR-3B was ₹90,000. The fixed-sum challan for each of the first two months of the next quarter is:

CalculationAmount
Previous quarter cash tax paid₹90,000
35% of ₹90,000₹31,500
Month 1 PMT-06₹31,500
Month 2 PMT-06₹31,500
Total deposits for first two months₹63,000

Example B – previous period was monthly

If cash tax paid in the last monthly GSTR-3B of the immediately preceding quarter was ₹24,000, the fixed-sum amount for each of the first two months is ₹24,000 (not 35% of ₹24,000).

Key point: The 35% label applies to the quarterly-return history method. Where the immediately preceding filing frequency was monthly, the fixed sum is the cash tax paid in the last month of that quarter. The portal's prefilled challan should be checked against the taxpayer's filed return history.

8. Self-assessment method – calculate actual tax month by month

Under self-assessment, determine the month's tax liability on outward supplies, reduce it by eligible ITC available under the law, and deposit the balance through PMT-06. Do not simply pay 35% of last quarter's cash tax.

Use eligible ITC only after checking Section 16 conditions, Section 17 restrictions, supplier reporting, receipt of goods/services, payment conditions and reversals. Monthly GSTR-2B data can support the assessment; reconcile it with purchase records rather than blindly accepting every credit.

9. When can a QRMP taxpayer avoid a PMT-06 deposit?

A taxpayer need not make an additional deposit for a month where the electronic cash ledger and/or electronic credit ledger balance is sufficient to cover the tax due for that month, or where there is nil tax liability.

SituationPractical treatment
Month 1 has nil tax liabilityNo PMT-06 deposit is required for that month.
Month 1 liability is fully covered by available ledger balanceNo fresh deposit is required to the extent the relevant ledger balance is adequate.
Month 2 liabilityCheck cumulative tax due for months 1 and 2 against available cash/credit ledger balances. Deposit only the shortfall, if any.
Fixed sum method selected but sufficient ledger balance existsApply the circular's ledger-balance/nil-liability relaxation; do not create an unnecessary duplicate cash deposit.
Keep evidence. Save the electronic cash ledger, credit ledger, working of tax due and PMT-06 challan/payment reference. A nil payment should be supported by a proper monthly calculation, not merely by an assumption that QRMP means no monthly compliance.

10. Interest and late fee – important difference

Payment / filing situationInterestLate fee
Fixed-sum PMT-06 paid by 25th and full quarter liability paid through quarterly GSTR-3B by due dateNo interest merely because actual month-wise liability exceeded the fixed-sum deposit.No late fee for a difference in monthly deposit alone.

Self-assessed monthly tax is short-paid or paid lateInterest applies to tax remaining unpaid/paid late for the relevant first/second month, subject to Section 50.No late fee merely for delayed PMT-06 payment.

Interest under Section 50 is generally 18% per annum for delayed payment of tax, calculated for the relevant period and on the legally prescribed base. Verify the applicable rate, notification, tax period and facts before computing.

11. ITC, GSTR-2B and quarterly GSTR-3B – how to reconcile

QRMP does not relax ITC eligibility. The taxpayer must claim credit in the quarterly GSTR-3B only after applying the normal GST conditions, restrictions and reversal rules.

  • Download/review the available GSTR-2B information for the relevant months and quarter.
  • Match supplier GSTIN, invoice number/date, taxable value and tax amount with the purchase register.
  • Identify invoices reported through IFF for month 1 and month 2 and avoid duplicate reporting or duplicate claim.
  • Review imports, RCM, ISD credits, credit notes, amendments and ineligible/blocked ITC separately.
  • Check payment to suppliers within 180 days and reverse/reclaim credit where Rule 37 applies.
  • Prepare a quarterly ITC summary and reconcile it with the auto-populated GSTR-3B before filing.
Recipient's practical issue: If a supplier is under QRMP and does not use IFF, some B2B invoices may not be visible in the recipient's GSTR-2B until the supplier files quarterly GSTR-1. Ask important suppliers about their filing frequency and IFF practice before closing monthly ITC.

At quarterly GSTR-3B, ABC reports total output tax of ₹5,55,000 and eligible ITC of ₹3,20,000, resulting in net liability of ₹2,35,000. After adjusting the ₹1,60,000 already deposited in the cash ledger and applying the actual ledger balances/offset rules, the remaining amount to be discharged is ₹75,000, subject to the return's final tax-head-wise set-off.

Close the quarter only after a full reconciliation. Reconcile monthly PMT-06 deposits, electronic cash ledger, ITC ledger, GSTR-1/IFF, purchase register, sales register and quarterly GSTR-3B. The sum of monthly deposits is not itself the final tax computation.

14. What if aggregate turnover crosses ₹5 crore?

If aggregate turnover on the PAN crosses ₹5 crore during a quarter in the current financial year, the taxpayer ceases to be eligible for QRMP from the succeeding quarter. It should move to monthly filing from that next quarter and ensure that the portal filing frequency is updated.

Example

A PAN has aggregate turnover of ₹4.80 crore up to September 2026. During October–December 2026, aggregate turnover crosses ₹5 crore in November. The taxpayer should not continue QRMP for January–March 2027; it should follow monthly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B from the succeeding quarter, subject to the applicable portal transition and notifications.

Do not wait for year-end. Track aggregate turnover across all GSTINs monthly. If the limit is crossed during a quarter, flag the next quarter's return frequency immediately and review IFF/PMT-06 tasks that will no longer apply.

15. Common mistakes and frequently asked questions

  • Checking the ₹5 crore limit GSTIN-wise instead of PAN-wise.
  • Assuming quarterly returns mean tax payment can also wait until quarter-end.
  • Using 35% of prior quarter cash tax even when the previous return frequency was monthly.
  • Paying fixed-sum challan without checking sufficient cash/credit ledger balance or nil liability.
  • Missing IFF deadline on the 13th or exceeding its ₹50 lakh limit.
  • Reporting IFF invoices again in quarterly GSTR-1.
  • Assuming IFF is mandatory for every QRMP taxpayer.
  • Claiming ITC only from books without supplier reporting and statutory eligibility checks.
  • Not moving to monthly filing after crossing the turnover threshold.
  • Confusing PMT-06 deposit with filing GSTR-3B.

1. Is QRMP compulsory for taxpayers below ₹5 crore?

No. Eligible taxpayers may choose the quarterly option or continue monthly filing, subject to the portal's option window and conditions.

2. Is IFF compulsory?

No. IFF is optional and is useful when B2B recipients need earlier invoice visibility for ITC.

3. What is the IFF limit?

The aggregate value of supplies furnished through IFF is limited to ₹50 lakh for each of the first two months of a quarter.

4. What is the due date for IFF?

The 13th day of the month succeeding the relevant month, subject to any notified extension.

5. Is the 35% challan always 35% of the previous quarter's cash tax?

No. If the previous return frequency was quarterly, it is 35% of cash tax paid in that quarter. If the previous return frequency was monthly, it is equal to the cash tax paid in the last month of the immediately preceding quarter.

6. Can I use self-assessment in one month and fixed sum in the next?

Yes. The taxpayer may use either method in either of the first two months, subject to the scheme conditions.

7. Do I need to pay PMT-06 if there is no tax liability?

No deposit is required where the liability is nil. The circular also permits no fresh deposit where the relevant cash/credit ledger balance is sufficient to cover the liability.

8. Does QRMP change ITC eligibility?

No. Normal Sections 16 and 17 conditions and other applicable rules continue to apply.

9. What are the quarterly GSTR-3B due dates?

Ordinarily, the 22nd or 24th of the month following the quarter, depending on the State/UT category. Check the current notification and GST portal for the relevant period.

10. What happens if turnover crosses ₹5 crore?

The taxpayer becomes ineligible from the succeeding quarter and should move to monthly filing from that quarter.

11. Can different GSTINs under the same PAN choose different filing frequencies?

Yes. The option is GSTIN-wise, subject to each registration's eligibility.

12. Is late fee payable for late PMT-06 payment?

No late fee is levied merely for delay in monthly PMT-06 payment. Interest may apply. Late fee is relevant to delayed filing of the prescribed quarterly return or outward-supply details, subject to the law.

16. Monthly close checklist and legal references

Monthly checklist for the accounts team

  1. Update PAN-level aggregate turnover tracker across all GSTINs.
  2. Confirm QRMP status and current quarter selection on the portal.
  3. Close sales register and reconcile tax rate, B2B/B2C, credit/debit notes and amendments.
  4. Decide whether IFF is needed for B2B customers; complete it by the 13th if used.
  5. Reconcile purchase register with GSTR-2B and identify eligible ITC, reversals and RCM.
  6. Calculate PMT-06 under fixed sum or self-assessment and document the selected method.
  7. Check cash/credit ledger balances and nil-liability conditions before depositing.
  8. Pay PMT-06 by the 25th and save challan/CIN and ledger evidence.
  9. At quarter-end, reconcile IFF + quarterly GSTR-1, ITC and PMT-06 deposits with GSTR-3B.
  10. Review interest, late fee and any portal notices before filing.

Practical takeaway

QRMP reduces return filing frequency, not monthly responsibility. Check eligibility at PAN level, use IFF only when earlier B2B invoice visibility is useful, calculate PMT-06 correctly, preserve monthly ITC and cash-ledger workings, and reconcile all three months before filing quarterly GSTR-3B.
