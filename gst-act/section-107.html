1. Why Section 107 matters

Section 107 is one of the most important practical provisions in GST litigation. When an adjudicating authority passes an order that adversely affects a taxpayer, the taxpayer generally has a statutory route to challenge that order before the Appellate Authority.

For a finance or tax team, an appeal is not just a legal document. It can affect tax payable, interest, penalty, ITC, refund, cash flow, recovery proceedings, customer/vendor balances and financial-statement provisions.

Most important control: Do not wait until the last few days of the appeal period. The limitation clock is linked to communication of the decision or order, so the date of receipt/communication should be recorded immediately.

2. Section 107 in simple language

Section 107 provides the principal first appellate remedy against a decision or order passed by an adjudicating authority under the CGST Act, SGST Act or UTGST Act, subject to the statutory conditions.

3. Who can appeal?

Section 107(1) applies where a person is aggrieved by a decision or order passed by an adjudicating authority. In practical terms, this can include a taxpayer or another person whose rights or liabilities are affected by the order, depending on the facts and the nature of the proceeding.

Commissioner-directed appeal

Section 107 also contains a separate mechanism under which the Commissioner can call for and examine the record of an adjudication proceeding and direct a subordinate authorised officer to apply to the Appellate Authority on specified points, subject to the statutory time limit.

4. What kind of orders can be appealed?

The appeal route applies to decisions or orders passed by an adjudicating authority under the relevant GST enactments. The first task after receiving an order is therefore to identify exactly what order has been issued and under which provision.

Before deciding to appeal, prepare an order map

  • Order number and date.
  • Date and method of communication.
  • Tax period involved.
  • Demand/ITC/refund/penalty/interest components.
  • Sections under which the order was passed.
  • Issues accepted and issues rejected.
  • Whether any part of the order is admitted.
  • Potential financial-statement and cash-flow impact.
Do not appeal blindly: Read the findings paragraph by paragraph. An appeal should challenge the actual reasoning and quantified consequences in the order.

5. The three-month appeal time limit

For an appeal under Section 107(1), the normal period is three months from the date on which the decision or order is communicated to the person.

Example

If an order is communicated on 10 September, the tax team should immediately calculate the statutory filing deadline under the applicable limitation rules and build an internal deadline several working days earlier. Do not use the date printed on the order as a substitute for analysing the communication date.

6. Commissioner review route under Section 107

Section 107 is not limited to taxpayer-filed appeals. Under sub-section (2), the Commissioner may, on own motion or at the request of the relevant Commissioner, call for and examine the record of proceedings to examine the legality or propriety of an adjudicating authority's decision/order.

The Commissioner may then direct a subordinate officer to apply to the Appellate Authority on specified points. Section 107(3) says that such an application is dealt with as an appeal and the authorised officer is treated as the appellant for that purpose.

This is a separate departmental appellate mechanism. A taxpayer should therefore monitor the final adjudication order and subsequent departmental communications rather than assuming only the taxpayer's own appeal can move the matter forward.

7. Form GST APL-01 and filing

The CGST Rules prescribe FORM GST APL-01 for an appeal to the Appellate Authority under Section 107(1). The rules also require the relevant decision/order and supporting documentation in the prescribed manner. citeturn0search1turn0search28

Build the appeal before opening the portal

  1. Obtain the complete adjudication order.
  2. Prepare issue-wise findings and counterarguments.
  3. Calculate admitted and disputed amounts.
  4. Calculate the applicable pre-deposit.
  5. Prepare statement of facts.
  6. Prepare grounds of appeal.
  7. Compile supporting evidence.
  8. Obtain management/authorised sign-off.
  9. File and preserve acknowledgement/reference numbers.
Best practice: The portal filing should be the final execution step, not the place where the tax team first starts drafting the appeal.

8. Pre-deposit and recovery protection

Section 107(6) contains the financial pre-condition for filing an appeal under sub-section (1). In broad terms, the appellant must pay the amount of tax, interest, fine, fee and penalty admitted by it and the prescribed portion of the remaining disputed tax. The statutory text provides the detailed calculation.

Section 107(7) provides that once the appellant has paid the amount required under sub-section (6), recovery proceedings for the balance amount are deemed to be stayed until disposal of the appeal. citeturn0search27

Finance-team calculation

Because the exact percentage and procedural requirements are statutory matters, calculate the amount from the current Section 107 text and applicable rules/notices at the time of filing.

9. How to prepare strong grounds of appeal

A strong appeal is not simply a statement that “the officer is wrong”. Each ground should identify the finding, explain why it is legally or factually incorrect, and connect the argument to evidence.

Weak groundBetter approach
“The demand is incorrect.”Identify the specific finding, provision, factual error and supporting document.
“ITC should be allowed.”Explain eligibility condition-by-condition and reconcile the disputed invoices with records.
“There is no suppression.”Address the factual findings and evidence relied upon by the adjudicating authority.
“Penalty should be deleted.”Address the statutory basis, facts, conduct, disclosure and applicable legal principles.

Recommended structure

  1. Background
  2. Statement of facts
  3. Issues involved
  4. Grounds of appeal
  5. Ground-wise evidence
  6. Tax/interest/penalty reconciliation
  7. Prayer/request

10. Hearing and additional evidence

The Appellate Authority can make further inquiry as necessary before deciding the appeal. The appeal file should therefore be capable of answering factual questions raised during the hearing.

Prepare a hearing pack

  • Original adjudication order.
  • Appeal form and grounds.
  • Statement of facts.
  • Issue-wise paper book.
  • Relevant contracts and invoices.
  • Reconciliation statements.
  • Case-specific legal submissions.
  • Calculation of disputed demand and pre-deposit.
  • Chronology of notices, replies and hearings.

If additional evidence is proposed, explain why it is relevant and why it was not already placed before the adjudicating authority where that explanation is necessary.

11. What can the Appellate Authority do?

Section 107(11) gives the Appellate Authority power, after making necessary further inquiry, to pass an order it considers just and proper, including confirming, modifying or annulling the decision or order appealed against. It cannot remand the case back to the original adjudicating authority under that provision. citeturn0search0

OutcomePractical meaning
ConfirmOriginal order remains substantially upheld.
ModifySome part of the decision/order is changed.
AnnulThe challenged decision/order is set aside to the extent decided.

12. Enhancement and adverse orders — why the taxpayer must be prepared

The Appellate Authority has statutory safeguards around certain adverse outcomes. Section 107(11) requires a reasonable opportunity of showing cause before an order enhancing fee/penalty/fine, confiscating goods of greater value, or reducing refund or ITC is passed.

It also contains a separate safeguard where the Authority proposes to require payment of tax or ITC that was not paid/was short-paid/erroneously refunded or wrongly availed/utilised: the appellant must be given notice and an opportunity to respond, subject to the statutory limitation framework. citeturn0search0

Practical lesson: An appeal file should analyse not only “what do we want reduced?” but also “what findings could create an adverse consequence if the Authority examines the matter further?”

13. What should an appellate order contain?

Section 107(12) requires the appellate order to be in writing and to state the points for determination, the decision on those points and the reasons for the decision. citeturn0search0

14. Practical finance examples

Example 1 — ITC demand

A tax officer disallows a large block of ITC. The taxpayer has purchase invoices, GSTR-2B records, goods-receipt evidence and payment records.

Appeal approach: Build an invoice-level reconciliation showing disputed invoices, supplier GSTIN, invoice number, taxable value, tax, 2B status, payment evidence and the specific reason the officer rejected the credit.

Example 2 — Tax classification

The order treats a service under a different classification/rate than the taxpayer used.

Appeal approach: Connect the actual contract scope, service description, customer agreement, invoice wording and relevant legal provisions. Do not rely only on the invoice description.

Example 3 — Penalty dispute

The tax demand is partly accepted but penalty is disputed.

Appeal approach: Separate the tax calculation from the penalty issue. Identify the statutory basis of penalty and the facts supporting the taxpayer's conduct.

Example 4 — Refund reduction

An adjudicating authority reduces a refund claim.

Appeal approach: Reconcile the refund calculation to returns, ledgers, invoices, payment records and the exact reason for rejection. Track cash-flow impact separately.

17. Common mistakes in Section 107 appeals

  1. Missing the limitation date. The biggest avoidable error.
  2. Confusing order date with communication date. Record the actual communication evidence.
  3. Filing grounds without reading the complete order. Every finding should be addressed systematically.
  4. Not reconciling the demand. The appeal should identify the exact amount under dispute.
  5. Ignoring pre-deposit cash-flow impact. Arrange funds and approvals early.
  6. Using generic grounds. Grounds should be issue-specific and evidence-backed.
  7. Not preparing for adverse findings. Understand the possible consequences of further inquiry.
  8. Failing to update books after the order. The finance team should record the outcome and revise provisions/recoveries as appropriate.

20. Section 107 vs Section 108 vs Section 112

ProvisionPurposeSimple memory aid
Section 107Appeal to Appellate Authority against adjudication order.First appeal
Section 108Revision by Revisional Authority in specified circumstances.Revision route
Section 112Appeal to Appellate Tribunal against specified orders including Section 107 orders.Next appellate level

For the taxpayer, the practical sequence may be adjudication → Section 107 appeal → further statutory remedy where available. The exact next step depends on the order, applicable law and current availability/jurisdiction of the appellate forum.

21. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the normal time limit for a taxpayer's Section 107 appeal?

Three months from the date on which the decision or order is communicated. Section 107 also permits the Appellate Authority to allow a further one month where sufficient cause prevented filing within the normal period. citeturn0search27

Which form is used for a Section 107(1) appeal?

The CGST Rules prescribe FORM GST APL-01 for an appeal under Section 107(1), along with the prescribed supporting documents and procedure. citeturn0search1turn0search28

Does filing an appeal automatically stay the entire demand?

No. The statutory recovery protection is linked to the payment requirements under Section 107(6). Section 107(7) provides deemed stay of recovery proceedings for the balance after the required payment is made. citeturn0search27

Can the Appellate Authority increase the taxpayer's liability?

Section 107 contains specific safeguards for certain adverse outcomes, including enhancement of fee/penalty/fine, reduction of refund or ITC and proposals involving tax or wrongly availed/utilised ITC. The appellant must receive the prescribed opportunity to show cause before such orders are passed. citeturn0search0

Can the Appellate Authority send the case back to the original adjudicating authority?

Section 107(11) states that the Appellate Authority may confirm, modify or annul the decision/order and shall not refer the case back to the adjudicating authority that passed it under that provision. citeturn0search0

How should finance teams account for a disputed GST demand?

Maintain an issue-wise reconciliation of the order, appeal position, admitted amount, pre-deposit, disputed amount and management's accounting assessment. The accounting treatment should follow the applicable financial-reporting framework and professional advice.

Can an appeal be filed at the last moment?

Technically the statutory deadline controls, but operationally it is poor practice. Portal issues, document gaps, approval delays or payment problems can create avoidable risk. Set an internal deadline substantially earlier.

23. Key takeaways

  • Section 107 is the principal first appellate route against specified adjudication orders.
  • A taxpayer normally has three months from communication of the order to appeal.
  • A further one month may be allowed where sufficient cause prevented timely filing.
  • FORM GST APL-01 is the prescribed form for the taxpayer's Section 107(1) appeal under the CGST Rules.
  • Pre-deposit requirements are critical because the statutory recovery protection for the balance is linked to them.
  • A strong appeal is built around issue-wise facts, law, evidence and quantified financial impact.
  • Finance teams should maintain a complete appeal master file and limitation register.
  • After the appellate order, immediately assess the financial, GST-return, accounting and further-appeal consequences.
