What is GST reconciliation?

GST reconciliation is the process of comparing purchase records in your books with supplier-reported invoice data available in GSTR-2B. It helps you identify records that agree, records that need investigation and invoices appearing on one side but not the other.

Why reconcile Purchase Register with GSTR-2B?

Differences can arise because of invoice number variations, document type differences, tax value differences, supplier filing timing or missing entries in the books. A structured reconciliation makes these exceptions easier to review before GST return and ITC decisions.

⚠️
Mandatory Rule 1 — Document Type must match.
The Invoice Type / Document Type entered in Purchase Register and GSTR-2B must be the same. If GSTR-2B shows a Credit Note, the Purchase Register must also show Credit Note. The same rule applies to Debit Notes and Invoices. Reconciliation will be blocked if the same GSTIN + invoice reference is found with different document types.
➕
Mandatory Rule 2 — Enter values as positive/non-negative amounts.
Do not enter negative numbers in Taxable Value, IGST, CGST or SGST. For Credit Notes and Debit Notes, enter the amounts as positive values and identify the document through the Document Type field. Use 0 only where a tax component is genuinely not applicable.

How to Use

Follow these steps every time you perform a reconciliation.

1. Download the Master Excel Template

Use the Download Excel Template button. The workbook contains separate Purchase Register and GSTR-2B sheets plus an instruction sheet.

2. Mandatory: Keep Document Type identical

If an invoice is shown as Credit Note in GSTR-2B, it must also be entered as Credit Note in the Purchase Register. Do not classify the same document as Invoice in one file and Credit Note in the other.

3. Mandatory: No negative values

Enter Taxable Value, IGST, CGST and SGST as positive/non-negative amounts only. For Credit Notes or Debit Notes, enter positive amounts and use the Document Type field to identify the document. Do not use minus signs.

4. Upload the Purchase Register

Upload .xlsx, .xls or .csv. If an Excel file contains multiple sheets, select the correct Purchase Register sheet after upload.

5. Upload GSTR-2B

Upload the GSTR-2B workbook/CSV and select the correct sheet. The tool reads the columns from the selected sheet.

6. Check mandatory fields

Use consistent GSTIN, Invoice Number, Document Type, Invoice Date, Taxable Value, IGST, CGST and SGST. Do not change column meanings between the two files.

7. Run Reconciliation

The tool validates the data and then compares records using GSTIN, document type, invoice number and tax values. Close invoice-number variations can be considered in the second matching pass.

8. Review before relying on the result

Matched records should still be reviewed against the source documents and your accounting/tax records. A reconciliation result is a working tool output and does not itself constitute a GST filing decision.

GST Reconciliation Tool – Frequently Asked Questions

What is a GST Reconciliation Tool?

A GST Reconciliation Tool compares purchase records with GST portal data such as GSTR-2B and identifies matched, unmatched and differing invoice records. It reduces manual invoice-by-invoice comparison and produces a structured reconciliation result for review.

How do I reconcile my Purchase Register with GSTR-2B?

Upload the Purchase Register and the relevant GSTR-2B file, select the required sheets if prompted, run the reconciliation and review the matched and exception categories. The results can then be exported to Excel for further analysis.

What is the difference between Purchase Register and GSTR-2B?

The Purchase Register represents purchase transactions recorded in your books, while GSTR-2B contains the static, auto-drafted input tax credit information made available from supplier filings and other GST data for the relevant period. Reconciliation compares the two datasets to identify differences.

What does “Not in GSTR-2B” mean?

It means a Purchase Register record did not have a corresponding record identified in the uploaded GSTR-2B data. The reason may require investigation, such as supplier filing timing, invoice details or differences between the two datasets.

What does “Not in Purchase Register” mean?

It means a GSTR-2B record did not have a corresponding Purchase Register record identified by the reconciliation process. The item should be reviewed against the books and source documents.

What is a partial match in GST reconciliation?

A partial match indicates that a related invoice has been identified but one or more compared details differ or require review. The exact result should be checked against the source documents before any accounting or ITC decision.

Can I download the GST reconciliation report in Excel?

Yes. After reconciliation, the tool provides an Excel export containing the reconciliation categories and invoice-level comparison information, including Purchase Register and GSTR-2B values.

Is this GST reconciliation tool free?

This reconciliation tool is provided as a free browser-based utility. You can use the available upload, reconciliation and Excel export functions without changing the existing reconciliation workflow.

Can I use Excel for GSTR-2B reconciliation?

Yes. The tool is designed around workbook-based reconciliation and provides Excel template and export functionality. Always use the required input structure and keep the original source files unchanged.

Does the tool replace professional GST review?

No. Reconciliation software helps identify data relationships and exceptions. Taxability, eligibility of ITC, reversals, blocked credits and filing decisions should be independently reviewed under the applicable GST provisions and source documents.
