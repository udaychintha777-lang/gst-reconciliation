1. Why Section 108 matters

A GST order passed by a subordinate officer is not necessarily beyond further departmental scrutiny merely because it has been issued. Section 108 gives a Revisional Authority a statutory mechanism to call for and examine such proceedings in specified circumstances.

The provision is particularly important where an order is considered erroneous and prejudicial to the interest of revenue, where it is illegal or improper, or where material facts were not taken into account. It can therefore affect a business even after an adjudication order has been received and the taxpayer has started planning its next steps.

Practical message: Section 107 appeal and Section 108 revision are different mechanisms. A finance team should track both the taxpayer's appellate rights and the possibility of departmental revision where applicable.

2. Section 108 in simple language

QuestionSimple answer
Who exercises revision?The Revisional Authority specified under the GST framework.
Whose order can be examined?A decision/order passed by an officer subordinate to the Revisional Authority under the relevant GST law.
Why?Because the order may be erroneous and prejudicial to revenue, illegal/improper, or may have failed to consider material facts, among the statutory grounds.
Can the order be stayed?Yes. The Revisional Authority may stay operation of the decision/order for a period considered appropriate.
Can the order be changed?Yes, after the required opportunity of hearing and further inquiry, the Authority may enhance, modify or annul the decision/order.
Is there a time limit?Section 108 contains a three-year outer framework from the passing of the decision/order, subject to the statutory restrictions and specific exclusion provisions.

3. Conditions that make revision relevant

Section 108 is not simply a general power to reopen every GST order. The Revisional Authority must examine the statutory conditions.

1. Existing orderThere must be a decision/order within the provision.
2. Subordinate officerThe order must be by an officer subordinate to the Revisional Authority.
3. Error/revenue prejudiceThe statutory revenue-related condition must be satisfied.
4. Legal/procedural issueThe order may be illegal, improper or omit material facts as specified.

The exact wording of Section 108 should be checked for the particular proceeding because revision is subject to Section 121 and the rules made under the Act. citeturn0search21

4. “Erroneous” and “prejudicial to the interest of revenue”

One of the most important practical ideas in Section 108 is that the statutory test is not simply “the officer made a mistake”. The provision links revision to an order being erroneous in a manner prejudicial to revenue and also addresses orders that are illegal, improper or fail to take account of material facts.

SituationWhy it may attract revision
Tax was not charged because the officer overlooked an applicable provision.Potential revenue prejudice and legal error.
A material document was ignored while deciding the case.Order may not reflect relevant facts.
Incorrect classification resulted in lower tax.Potential revenue prejudice.
Order contains a legal/procedural defect affecting the conclusion.May fall within the statutory grounds depending on facts.
Important: A taxpayer should not assume that every favourable adjudication order is permanently safe. The order should be analysed for possible departmental review/revision risk as part of litigation management.

5. Material facts and revision

Section 108 expressly covers a situation where the decision/order has not taken into account certain material facts, whether those facts were available when the order was issued or not.

This is important because a later-discovered document can potentially matter. For example, a contract amendment, accounting record, technical document or transaction record may demonstrate that the factual foundation used in the original order was incomplete.

Finance-team control

  • Keep the complete case file even after the order is issued.
  • Record new documents that become available after adjudication.
  • Escalate material new facts to the tax/legal team.
  • Do not alter or retrospectively create documents to support a preferred position.

6. How can revision begin?

The Revisional Authority can act on its own motion. Section 108 also permits revision on information received by the Authority or on a request from the Commissioner of State Tax or Commissioner of Union Territory Tax, as applicable.

SourcePractical meaning
Own motionThe Revisional Authority independently examines the record.
Information receivedInformation may bring a possible error/revenue issue to the Authority's attention.
Request from State/UT CommissionerThe relevant Commissioner can request examination under the statutory framework.

CBIC has also issued procedural clarifications concerning identification of Revisional Authorities for certain Common Adjudicating Authority orders, demonstrating that the identity of the correct Revisional Authority matters in practice. citeturn0search18

7. Can the Revisional Authority stay the order?

Yes. Section 108 permits the Revisional Authority, where necessary, to stay operation of the decision or order for such period as it considers appropriate.

A stay is different from a final revision. It is an interim procedural measure intended to preserve the position while the revision is considered.

If a business receives communication about a revision or stay, the finance team should immediately identify the order affected, the amount involved, the accounting provision, recovery position and any pending appeal.

8. Hearing and further inquiry

Before passing an order under Section 108(1), the Revisional Authority must give the person concerned an opportunity of being heard and may make further inquiry as necessary.

How to prepare for a revision proceeding

  1. Obtain the original adjudication order and complete case record available to the taxpayer.
  2. Identify the exact point proposed for revision.
  3. Prepare a chronology from notice to adjudication order.
  4. Collect contracts, invoices, ledgers and factual evidence.
  5. Quantify the potential additional tax/ITC/penalty exposure.
  6. Prepare a point-by-point response.
  7. Ensure the authorised representative understands the operational facts.

9. What can the Revisional Authority do?

After the required hearing and inquiry, Section 108 permits the Revisional Authority to pass an order it considers just and proper, including enhancing, modifying or annulling the original decision or order.

OutcomeBusiness impact
EnhanceTax/interest/penalty or another adverse component may increase where legally justified.
ModifyThe original findings may be changed in specified respects.
AnnulThe original order may be set aside.
Do not assume revision is only beneficial to the taxpayer. The statutory power can result in enhancement or other adverse changes. Management should therefore treat a revision notice as a significant tax-risk event.

10. When the Revisional Authority cannot exercise the power

Section 108(2) contains important restrictions. Revision cannot be exercised in specified situations, including where the order has already been subject to an appeal under Section 107, 112, 117 or 118.

RestrictionPractical meaning
Order already subject to specified appealRevision is generally blocked by Section 108(2)(a), subject to the statutory proviso for points not raised and decided in an appeal.
Section 107(2) period has not expiredThe Revisional Authority cannot exercise revision during the protected period specified by Section 108(2)(b).
More than three years have passedSection 108(2)(b) contains a three-year outer limit, subject to the statutory exclusion provision.
Order already taken up for revision earlierRepeated revision is restricted.
Order itself was passed under Section 108(1)Section 108(2)(d) restricts another revision of that order.

These restrictions are expressly stated in the CGST Act. citeturn0search21

11. What happens if the taxpayer has already appealed?

Section 108 generally prevents revision where the order has been subject to an appeal under the specified provisions. However, the proviso to Section 108(2) creates a specific possibility concerning a point that was not raised and decided in the appeal.

Therefore, a company should maintain an issue-wise map of what was actually raised and decided in its appeal.

IssueAdjudication orderSection 107 appealDecided in appeal?
ClassificationYesGround 1Yes
ITC eligibilityYesGround 2Yes
Another issueYesNot challengedNo

The exact consequences depend on the statutory conditions and dates. Never assume that filing an appeal automatically blocks every conceivable revision point.

12. The three-year limitation framework

Section 108(2)(b) prevents exercise of revision where more than three years have expired after the passing of the decision or order sought to be revised, subject to the special exclusion rule in sub-section (4).

This is different from the taxpayer's Section 107 appeal period. Section 107 focuses on the taxpayer's appeal timeline from communication of the order, while Section 108 contains a separate departmental revision framework.

Practical control: Maintain two separate dates in your litigation register: the taxpayer's appeal deadline and the departmental revision outer limit.

13. Special exclusion from the three-year calculation

Section 108(4) provides a special exclusion where the decision/order involves an issue on which the Appellate Tribunal or High Court has given a decision in another proceeding and an appeal against that decision is pending before the High Court or Supreme Court, as applicable. The specified period spent between those decisions is excluded from the limitation calculation.

This means the three-year calculation should not be treated as a simple “order date + three years” exercise in every case.

Best practice: Where a revision issue is linked to a larger legal issue being litigated at Tribunal/High Court/Supreme Court level, obtain a fresh limitation calculation before concluding that the revision power has expired.

14. Finality of a revision order

Section 108(3) states that every order passed in revision is, subject to the further statutory remedies referred to in the provision, final and binding on the parties.

Therefore, after receiving a revision order, the company should immediately identify whether a further appeal or other statutory remedy is available and calculate the corresponding deadline.

16. Practical examples

Example 1 — Tax treatment accepted by adjudicating officer

An adjudicating officer accepts a taxpayer's classification and determines a low tax liability. Later, the department identifies a material legal issue and considers the order erroneous and prejudicial to revenue.

Lesson: A favourable order can still be examined through the statutory revision mechanism if the conditions of Section 108 are satisfied.

Example 2 — Material contract not considered

A demand order does not consider a material contract amendment that affects the nature of the supply. The amendment becomes relevant to the legality/propriety of the original decision.

Lesson: Maintain the full contract history and immediately assess whether a revision proceeding could arise.

Example 3 — Taxpayer already filed Section 107 appeal

The taxpayer appeals classification and the appellate order decides that issue. A separate issue in the original order was never raised or decided.

Lesson: Section 108(2) and its proviso must be analysed issue-by-issue; simply saying “an appeal was filed” is not enough.

Example 4 — Revision near the three-year limit

An order is approaching three years from its date, but the same legal issue has been litigated before the Tribunal and a further appeal is pending.

Lesson: The special exclusion in Section 108(4) may affect the limitation calculation. Obtain a precise legal calculation.

17. Construction & infrastructure example

For infrastructure companies, an adjudication order may involve project-specific classification, subcontractor ITC, valuation, works contract treatment, exemptions or place-of-supply questions. A later revision can therefore affect multiple projects and periods.

Project-level revision file

  • Original contract and BOQ.
  • Variation orders.
  • Subcontract agreements.
  • Project invoices and measurement records.
  • Purchase register and GSTR-2B reconciliation.
  • Ledger and GST return workings.
  • Original adjudication order.
  • Section 107 appeal, if any.
  • Any revision notice/order.
  • Issue-wise tax exposure calculation.
Practical control: Link every major GST litigation matter to the relevant GSTIN, project, financial year, order number and ledger account. This prevents an order relating to one project from being incorrectly implemented across unrelated projects.

18. Records and response file

When a revision proceeding starts, create a separate Revision Master File.

  1. Original order and communication proof.
  2. Complete adjudication proceedings.
  3. Section 107 appeal status.
  4. Revision notice/communication.
  5. Issue-wise allegation or proposed revision.
  6. Contract and factual evidence.
  7. Tax/ITC/interest/penalty calculation.
  8. Written response.
  9. Hearing notes and evidence submitted.
  10. Revision order.
  11. Further appellate remedy and deadline.

19. Common mistakes

  1. Assuming a favourable order is permanently final. Check the revision framework.
  2. Confusing appeal and revision. They have different triggers, actors and limitations.
  3. Ignoring the three-year calculation. Special exclusions may matter.
  4. Failing to track what was actually decided in appeal. This can matter for Section 108(2).
  5. Not quantifying revenue exposure. Management needs a financial impact, not only a legal note.
  6. Changing ERP treatment before the legal position is finalised. Coordinate tax and finance implementation.
  7. Missing a further appellate deadline after revision. Recalculate deadlines immediately on receipt of the revision order.

22. Section 107 vs Section 108 vs Section 112

ProvisionWho/what?Core purpose
Section 107Aggrieved person / departmental appeal mechanismFirst appeal against adjudication decision/order.
Section 108Revisional AuthorityExamine and revise specified subordinate orders that meet statutory conditions.
Section 112Appellate TribunalFurther appeal against specified appellate/revisional orders.

Easy memory rule: Section 107 = appeal. Section 108 = departmental revision. Section 112 = Tribunal appeal.

23. Frequently Asked Questions

Can the department revise every GST order?

No. Section 108 contains specific statutory conditions and restrictions. Revision is not an unlimited reopening power.

Can the Revisional Authority increase the demand?

Yes. Section 108 permits an order including enhancement, modification or annulment after the prescribed hearing and inquiry requirements.

Can a taxpayer's order be revised after the taxpayer files an appeal?

Section 108(2)(a) generally restricts revision where the order has been subject to specified appeal proceedings, subject to the statutory proviso dealing with points not raised and decided in an appeal.

Is the three-year period counted from communication of the order?

Section 108(2)(b) refers to more than three years after the passing of the decision or order. This is different from Section 107's taxpayer appeal period, which is linked to communication of the order.

Can the Revisional Authority stay an order?

Yes. Section 108(1) permits the Revisional Authority, where necessary, to stay operation of the decision/order for the period it considers appropriate.

Does the taxpayer get a hearing?

Yes. Before passing an order under Section 108(1), the person concerned must be given an opportunity of being heard.

What if the same issue is pending before a higher court?

Section 108(4) contains a specific exclusion from the three-year calculation for the period described there when the issue has been decided by the Tribunal or High Court in another proceeding and the specified further appeal is pending.

What should finance teams do after receiving a revision notice?

Create a litigation file, check the jurisdiction and statutory restrictions, calculate limitation, quantify exposure, reconcile the order to books and returns, gather evidence and coordinate with the tax/legal team before responding.

25. Key takeaways

  • Section 108 provides a separate departmental revision mechanism.
  • The Revisional Authority can call for and examine specified subordinate orders when the statutory conditions are satisfied.
  • The provision addresses orders that are erroneous and prejudicial to revenue and also refers to illegal/improper orders and material facts not taken into account.
  • The Revisional Authority may stay an order and, after hearing and inquiry, enhance, modify or annul it.
  • Section 108 contains important restrictions where appeals have been filed, where the Section 107(2) period has not expired, where three years have passed, or where revision has already occurred.
  • The three-year calculation can be affected by the special exclusion in Section 108(4).
  • Finance teams should maintain separate appeal and revision trackers and immediately assess tax, ITC, accounting and cash-flow exposure.
