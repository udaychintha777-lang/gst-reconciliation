GST ACT — SECTION 72

Section 72 CGST Act — Officers to Assist Proper Officers

Section 72 creates a statutory framework for assistance to GST proper officers. Police, Railways, Customs, officers engaged in land-revenue collection, State tax officers and Union territory tax officers are required to assist proper officers in implementing the CGST Act. The Government can also notify other classes of officers to assist when called upon by the Commissioner.

1. Why Section 72 matters

Searches such as “Section 72 CGST Act”, “Section 72 GST”, “officers to assist proper officers”, “police assistance in GST”, “customs assistance GST”, “State GST officer assistance”, and “GST enforcement assistance” relate directly to this provision.

Section 72 may look short, but it performs an important administrative function. GST officers do not operate in isolation. Implementation of GST can require information, coordination and assistance from other government departments.

Simple idea: Section 72 creates the legal bridge through which specified government officers assist GST proper officers in implementing the CGST Act.

2. Section 72 in very simple language

Imagine a GST officer needs assistance in implementing the GST law. The issue may involve movement of goods, customs information, railway-related movement, land-revenue records, State GST or law-enforcement support.

Section 72 says that specified categories of government officers shall assist the proper officers in implementation of the Act.

GST proper officer + statutory assistance from other government officers = coordinated implementation of CGST law

This does not mean every officer from every department automatically becomes a GST proper officer. Section 72 is primarily an assistance provision.

3. What the section actually says

Section 72(1) states that all officers of Police, Railways, Customs, officers engaged in collection of land revenue including village officers, officers of State tax and officers of Union territory tax shall assist the proper officers in implementation of the CGST Act.

Section 72(2) gives the Government power, by notification, to empower and require any other class of officers to assist proper officers when called upon to do so by the Commissioner.

The current consolidated statutory text continues to contain these two subsections without a substantive amendment to the wording identified in the current law. citeturn0search1

5. Is assistance mandatory?

The wording of Section 72(1) is significant: the listed officers “shall assist” the proper officers.

Therefore, for the categories expressly listed in Section 72(1), the provision creates a statutory assistance mandate rather than merely saying that assistance may be provided as a matter of convenience.

However, the scope of the assistance should still be understood as assistance in implementation of the CGST Act. Section 72 does not independently grant every GST power to every assisting officer.

Important: Assistance is not the same thing as independent exercise of GST powers. The officer's own statutory authority and the particular proceeding must always be considered.

6. Government's power under Section 72(2)

Section 72(2) gives the Government an additional mechanism to involve other classes of officers.

The Government may, by notification:

  • identify another class of officers;
  • empower that class to assist proper officers;
  • require that class to provide assistance; and
  • make the assistance available when called upon by the Commissioner.

This means the list in Section 72(1) is not necessarily the only possible source of inter-departmental assistance.

7. Role of the Commissioner

The Commissioner is important to Section 72(2). The Government's notified class of officers can be called upon to assist by the Commissioner.

This creates a structured chain:

Government notification → class of officers empowered/required → Commissioner calls upon them → assistance to proper officer

The provision therefore combines Government-level notification power with Commissioner-level operational coordination.

8. Section 72 and Section 6

Section 6 and Section 72 perform different functions.

Section 6Section 72
Deals with authorisation/cross-empowerment of officers under the GST framework.Deals with assistance by specified government officers.
Can make officers of another tax administration function as proper officers subject to statutory conditions.Does not by itself make Police/Railway/Customs officers GST proper officers.
Concerns exercise of specified GST functions.Concerns assistance in implementation.

This distinction is important when analysing whether an officer is merely assisting or independently exercising a statutory GST power.

9. Section 72 and search/inspection

Section 67 contains the specific GST framework for inspection, search and seizure. Section 72 can provide an assistance mechanism, but it does not replace the statutory requirements of Section 67.

Therefore, if a GST search is being conducted, the legal authority for the search must still be examined under Section 67 and the applicable rules. Section 72 should not be treated as a standalone search warrant.

10. Section 72 and arrest

Section 69 contains the power to arrest in specified Section 132 offence situations. Section 72 does not itself create a general power for Police, Customs or other listed officers to arrest a person for GST offences.

If arrest is involved, the specific Section 69 framework, the applicable Section 132 offence and other procedural safeguards must be examined.

11. Section 72 and summons

Section 70 gives the proper officer the power to summon persons for evidence/documents in an inquiry. Section 72, by contrast, concerns assistance from other government officers.

12. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Goods movement

A GST investigation concerns movement of goods through a railway route. Railway-related information or assistance may be relevant within the statutory framework.

Example 2 — Import transaction

A GST officer needs customs-related information to verify an import transaction. Customs officers are expressly included in Section 72(1).

Example 3 — State GST coordination

A transaction involves Central and State GST administration. State tax officers are expressly included in the assistance framework.

Example 4 — UTGST coordination

A transaction concerns a Union Territory administration. UT tax officers fall within Section 72(1).

Example 5 — Police assistance

Where lawful assistance of Police is required in implementation of a GST proceeding, Police officers are one of the categories expressly covered.

Example 6 — Additional class notified

The Government may notify another class of officers under Section 72(2), after which the notified class can be empowered/required to assist when called upon by the Commissioner.

Example 7 — Fake invoice investigation

A GST investigation may involve multiple agencies and movement records. Section 72 supports coordinated assistance, while the underlying GST investigation powers continue to come from the relevant provisions.

Example 8 — Warehouse movement

Where goods movement and government records need coordination, the relevant assisting department may provide information or assistance within its lawful functions.

Example 9 — Construction project

A major infrastructure project has goods moving through several jurisdictions. Central and State tax officers can coordinate within their respective statutory roles.

Example 10 — Assistance is not independent GST authority

An assisting officer cannot simply assume every power available to a GST proper officer merely because Section 72 permits assistance. The specific legal authority for the action must still be identified.

Section 72 helps provide the administrative bridge for such cooperation; it does not replace the specific substantive provisions governing e-way bills, customs, search, seizure or arrest.

14. Customs and import/export situations

Customs officers are expressly included in Section 72(1). This is particularly relevant because GST administration interacts with customs processes for imported and exported goods.

For example, a GST officer investigating input tax or import-related transactions may require information that sits within the customs administration. Section 72 supports inter-departmental assistance, while the specific customs/GST provisions determine what information or action is legally available.

15. State GST coordination

State tax officers are expressly listed in Section 72(1). This is consistent with the dual GST structure in India, where Central and State tax administrations operate together.

Section 6 is relevant where cross-empowerment is involved. Section 72 is relevant where one administration assists another. These concepts should not be mixed.

16. Police assistance

Police officers are expressly included in Section 72(1). The practical importance of this provision should be understood carefully.

Section 72 does not mean that every GST matter becomes a Police matter. It provides a statutory assistance framework for implementation of the CGST Act.

Where a specific GST enforcement action requires police assistance, the underlying GST provision authorising the action and the legal basis for police assistance should both be considered.

17. What businesses should understand

A business should understand that GST administration can involve more than the GST department alone. Government records and assistance may come from multiple departments.

  • Maintain accurate GST records.
  • Keep e-way bill and transport documentation complete.
  • Maintain import/export documentation.
  • Reconcile customs-related data where relevant.
  • Keep Central and State GST records aligned.
  • Maintain a clear transaction chronology during investigations.

18. Practical safeguards

  • Identify which GST proceeding is taking place.
  • Ask your internal tax/legal team to identify the statutory provision being exercised.
  • Distinguish an assistance action from an independent search, seizure or arrest power.
  • Preserve original records and electronic evidence.
  • Do not obstruct lawful government assistance.
  • Do not assume that assistance gives another officer unlimited GST powers.
  • Document material interactions during an investigation.
  • Obtain professional legal advice where enforcement action affects personal liberty or involves seizure/criminal proceedings.

21. Common mistakes

  • Thinking Section 72 itself creates a search power.
  • Thinking every Police officer automatically becomes a GST proper officer.
  • Confusing Section 72 assistance with Section 6 cross-empowerment.
  • Assuming assistance means unlimited authority.

  • Assuming a multi-agency investigation automatically means tax evasion has been established.

23. Section 72 GST FAQs

What is Section 72 of the CGST Act?

Section 72 is titled “Officers to assist proper officers”. It requires specified categories of government officers to assist proper officers in implementing the CGST Act.

Which officers are covered by Section 72?

Police, Railways, Customs, officers engaged in collection of land revenue including village officers, State tax officers and Union territory tax officers are expressly covered.

Is Section 72 mandatory?

For the categories listed in Section 72(1), the statutory wording says they “shall assist” proper officers. Section 72(2) separately provides a Government notification mechanism for other classes of officers.

Does Section 72 give Police GST powers?

No. Section 72 provides an assistance framework. It does not by itself make Police officers GST proper officers or give them every GST power.

Can Customs officers assist GST officers?

Yes. Customs officers are expressly included in Section 72(1).

Can State GST officers assist Central GST officers?

Yes. State tax officers are expressly included in Section 72(1), subject to the statutory context of the assistance.

What is Section 72(2)?

It allows the Government, by notification, to empower and require another class of officers to assist proper officers when called upon by the Commissioner.

What is the difference between Section 72 and Section 6?

Section 6 concerns cross-empowerment/authorisation within the GST officer framework. Section 72 concerns assistance by specified government officers.

Is Section 72 the same as Section 67 search?

No. Section 67 contains the search, seizure and inspection framework. Section 72 is an assistance provision.

Is Section 72 the same as Section 69 arrest?

No. Section 69 is the arrest provision for specified GST offence situations.

Does Section 72 mean every government department can assist GST officers?

Section 72(1) lists specific categories. Section 72(2) allows the Government to notify additional classes of officers.

When did Section 72 come into force?

Section 72 came into force on 1 July 2017 through Notification No. 09/2017-Central Tax dated 28 June 2017.

Does Section 72 itself impose tax liability?

No. It is an administrative assistance provision. Tax liability arises under the substantive GST provisions.

25. Key takeaways

  • Section 72 is an inter-departmental assistance provision.
  • Police, Railways, Customs, land-revenue officers, State tax officers and UT tax officers are expressly included.
  • The listed officers “shall assist” proper officers in implementation of the CGST Act.
  • The Government can notify additional classes of officers under Section 72(2).
  • The Commissioner can call upon a notified class to assist.
  • Assistance does not automatically mean that the assisting officer becomes a GST proper officer.
  • Section 72 should not be confused with Section 6 cross-empowerment.
  • Section 72 itself does not replace the separate legal requirements for search, seizure, summons or arrest.
  • Businesses should maintain strong transport, customs, GST and transaction records because GST administration can involve multiple government departments.
  • The current statutory text has two subsections and has been in force since 1 July 2017.
