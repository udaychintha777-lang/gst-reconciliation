GST ACT — SECTION 77

Section 77 CGST Act — Wrong GST Tax Head, IGST vs CGST/SGST & Refund

Section 77 solves one of the most practical GST problems: the taxpayer has paid the wrong type of GST because the transaction was initially treated as intra-State when it was actually inter-State, or treated as inter-State when it was actually intra-State. This detailed guide explains the mistake, correction, refund, interest protection, Rule 89(1A), the two-year refund period and the interaction with Section 19 of the IGST Act.

1. Why Section 77 matters

Searches such as “Section 77 CGST Act”, “Section 77 GST”, “wrong GST tax head”, “IGST paid instead of CGST SGST”, “CGST SGST paid instead of IGST”, “GST wrong tax paid”, “Section 77 refund”, “Rule 89(1A) GST”, “GST tax head correction” and “Circular 162/18/2021-GST” all lead to this provision.

GST has three major tax heads for ordinary transactions: CGST, SGST/UTGST and IGST. The correct head depends largely on whether the supply is intra-State or inter-State and on the applicable place-of-supply rules.

A mistake in determining the nature of supply does not necessarily mean that the taxpayer has permanently lost the tax already paid. Section 77 creates a specific statutory mechanism for correcting this type of wrong-head payment.

Simple idea: If you paid the right amount of GST under the wrong tax head, you generally need to pay the tax under the correct head and then claim refund of the tax that was wrongly paid, subject to the prescribed procedure and time limit.

2. Section 77 in very simple language

Imagine a supplier makes a ₹10 lakh taxable supply.

The supplier initially believes it is an intra-State supply and charges:

  • CGST ₹90,000; and
  • SGST ₹90,000.

Later it is established that the supply was actually inter-State. The correct tax should have been:

  • IGST ₹1,80,000.

The taxpayer should not be forced to bear ₹3,60,000 merely because the first tax-head decision was wrong. The law provides a route to pay the correct IGST and seek refund of the wrongly paid CGST and SGST.

Wrong head paid → Correct head paid → Refund of wrong-head tax → Reconcile books and GST returns

3. The problem Section 77 solves

The GST system depends on the correct identification of the place of supply and the nature of the transaction. A taxpayer may make an honest mistake because:

  • the place of supply was initially interpreted incorrectly;
  • the customer's registration status was misunderstood;
  • the movement of goods was analysed incorrectly;
  • a service place-of-supply rule was misunderstood;
  • the transaction was initially treated as local and later determined to be inter-State; or
  • the transaction was initially treated as inter-State and later determined to be intra-State.

Section 77 prevents this classification mistake from permanently creating tax under two heads, provided the statutory correction process is followed.

4. Section 77 clause-wise explanation

ProvisionWhat it does
Section 77(1)Provides refund where CGST + SGST/UTGST was paid on a transaction treated as intra-State but it is subsequently held to be inter-State.
Section 77(2)Provides that where IGST was paid on a transaction treated as inter-State but it is subsequently held to be intra-State, interest is not required on the CGST + SGST/UTGST payable.

Section 77 must be read with the corresponding Section 19 of the IGST Act, 2017. Together they create the wrong-head correction mechanism for both directions of error.

5. Intra-State treated as inter-State

This is the situation covered by Section 77(1).

The taxpayer originally treated the transaction as intra-State and paid:

CGST
Central tax paid.
SGST/UTGST
State or Union territory tax paid.

Later, the transaction is established as inter-State. The taxpayer must pay the correct IGST.

After paying the correct IGST, the taxpayer can claim refund of the CGST and SGST/UTGST wrongly paid, subject to Section 77 and Rule 89(1A).

This direction is often described as CGST + SGST → IGST.

6. Inter-State treated as intra-State

This is the reverse situation.

The taxpayer originally treated the transaction as inter-State and paid IGST. Later, the transaction is established as intra-State.

The taxpayer then needs to pay the correct:

  • CGST; and
  • SGST or UTGST, as applicable.

Section 77 itself provides the important interest protection: the taxpayer is not required to pay interest on the CGST/SGST/UTGST payable in this situation.

The reverse direction is often described as IGST → CGST + SGST/UTGST.

7. The two correction flows

Original treatmentLater findingCorrect taxWhat happens to wrong tax?
Intra-StateInter-StateIGSTCGST + SGST/UTGST can be claimed as refund under Section 77(1) after paying correct IGST.
Inter-StateIntra-StateCGST + SGST/UTGSTIGST is refundable under the corresponding Section 19 mechanism after correct tax is paid.
The objective is tax-head correction, not a second tax burden.

8. Meaning of “subsequently held”

This phrase became important because taxpayers wanted clarity on whether Section 77 applies only when an officer or appellate authority changes the tax treatment, or also when the taxpayer independently discovers the mistake.

The GST Council's 45th meeting discussion and CBIC Circular No. 162/18/2021-GST clarified that “subsequently held” covers both situations:

  • the taxpayer itself subsequently discovers that the transaction was actually inter-State/intra-State; and
  • the tax officer or another proceeding subsequently establishes the correct nature of the supply.
Practical result: A taxpayer does not have to wait for a departmental order before correcting an identified wrong-head payment, provided the statutory conditions and procedure are followed.

9. Taxpayer discovers the mistake

Suppose the taxpayer reviews its place-of-supply position and discovers that a transaction reported as intra-State should have been inter-State.

A professional approach is:

  1. document the reason for reclassification;
  2. identify every affected invoice;
  3. calculate correct IGST;
  4. pay IGST under the correct head;
  5. identify the CGST and SGST/UTGST originally paid;
  6. prepare the refund application under Rule 89(1A);
  7. retain the payment and invoice evidence; and
  8. reconcile the refund with books and GST returns.

10. Officer discovers the mistake

The same mechanism can operate where the taxpayer originally treated the transaction one way and a tax officer subsequently holds that the correct tax head is different.

For example, an officer may conclude that a supply treated as intra-State is actually inter-State based on the place-of-supply provisions. The taxpayer then needs to pay the correct IGST and pursue refund of the CGST/SGST wrongly paid, subject to the prescribed conditions.

The important point is that the correction mechanism is not restricted to self-discovered errors.

11. Refund mechanism under Rule 89(1A)

Rule 89(1A) was inserted to prescribe the procedure and time limit for refunds under Section 77 of the CGST Act and Section 19 of the IGST Act.

Where a transaction originally treated as intra-State is subsequently held to be inter-State, the refund application is made electronically in FORM GST RFD-01.

Basic sequence

✓ Determine correct nature of supply
✓ Pay tax under correct head
✓ Prepare wrong-head tax reconciliation
✓ File RFD-01
✓ Attach required evidence
✓ Track ARN/application
✓ Respond to deficiency if issued
✓ Reconcile sanctioned refund

The rule links the normal refund procedure with the special wrong-tax-head situation.

12. Two-year time limit

One of the most important practical developments was the insertion of Rule 89(1A) through Notification No. 35/2021-Central Tax dated 24 September 2021.

For Section 77 refunds, the rule provides a two-year period measured from the date on which the tax under the correct head was paid.

For the CGST Section 77(1) situation, that means:

Two years from payment of correct IGST → Section 77 refund application

This is a critical distinction. The period is not simply calculated from the date of the original invoice or the date on which the wrong tax was originally paid.

Practical control: Record the date on which the correct tax was paid and immediately calculate the refund deadline.

13. Payments made before Rule 89(1A)

The rule also contains transitional treatment for cases where the correct tax had already been paid before Rule 89(1A) came into force.

CBIC Circular No. 162/18/2021-GST explains that where the correct tax had been paid before 24 September 2021, the refund application could be filed within two years from 24 September 2021.

This transitional rule was important for old wrong-head cases because the specific Rule 89(1A) procedure did not exist when the original tax was paid.

14. Interest protection

Interest is one of the most important differences between ordinary tax correction and the Section 77 framework.

Where intra-State was treated as inter-State

CGST + SGST/UTGST was paid first. When the transaction is later treated as inter-State, the taxpayer pays IGST. The refund of wrong-head CGST/SGST is then claimed according to Section 77 and Rule 89(1A).

Where inter-State was treated as intra-State

IGST was paid first. When the transaction is later held to be intra-State, Section 77(2)/Section 19 provides that interest is not required on the CGST + SGST/UTGST payable in the correction.

Key principle: A genuine wrong-head classification should not result in an interest burden merely because the correct tax head is determined later, subject to the statutory conditions.

15. Correcting the tax head vs refund

These are two separate steps and should not be mixed up.

StepPurpose
1. Determine correct tax headEstablish whether the supply is intra-State or inter-State.
2. Pay correct taxDischarge the actual liability under the correct head.
3. Claim refundRecover the tax wrongly paid under the original head.
4. ReconcileEnsure books, returns, electronic ledgers and refund records agree.

A taxpayer should therefore not assume that simply paying the correct tax automatically generates a refund of the wrong tax. The refund application must be separately completed.

16. Section 77 vs Section 19 of the IGST Act

Section 77 of the CGST Act and Section 19 of the IGST Act are companion provisions.

CGST Act Section 77IGST Act Section 19
Primarily addresses tax wrongly paid as CGST + SGST/UTGST when the transaction is subsequently held to be inter-State, and the corresponding interest protection.Addresses the reverse situation where IGST was paid on a transaction considered inter-State but subsequently held to be intra-State.
Refund of wrong central/state tax follows prescribed procedure.Refund of wrong IGST follows the prescribed procedure.

In practice, the two provisions should be read together whenever a transaction moves from one tax head to another.

17. Section 77 vs Section 50 interest

Section 50 is the general GST interest provision. Section 77/Section 19 provide a specific protective rule for the wrong-head situation.

For example, if IGST was initially paid and the transaction is subsequently held to be intra-State, the taxpayer should not calculate ordinary interest on the replacement CGST + SGST/UTGST merely because those taxes are being paid later, where Section 77/Section 19 applies.

Similarly, if the transaction was initially treated as intra-State and later held inter-State, the correct IGST payment and wrong-head refund should be worked through the Section 77 mechanism.

18. Section 77 vs ordinary Section 54 refund

Section 54 is the general GST refund provision. Section 77 is a special wrong-tax-head situation with its own Rule 89(1A) procedure.

Section 54Section 77
General refund provision covering many categories.Specific refund arising from wrong tax head.
Relevant-date rules depend on refund category.Rule 89(1A) specifically links the two-year period to payment of correct tax.
Different refund categories have different documentation.Wrong-head evidence and correct-head payment are central.
The special Section 77/Rule 89(1A) mechanism should not be confused with an ordinary excess-payment refund simply because both are filed through RFD-01.

20. Accounting treatment

The accounting entries depend on the circumstances and the accounting policy of the business, but the GST reconciliation should clearly distinguish:

  • tax originally charged;
  • tax originally paid;
  • tax determined to be payable under the correct head;
  • refund receivable for wrong-head tax; and
  • refund actually received.

For finance teams, a separate wrong-tax-head reconciliation register is useful where multiple invoices or GST registrations are affected.

21. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Telangana supplier to Maharashtra customer

A Telangana supplier initially treats a transaction as intra-State and pays CGST + SGST. Later the transaction is established as inter-State. The supplier pays the correct IGST and claims refund of the CGST + SGST under Section 77 and Rule 89(1A).

Example 2 — IGST incorrectly paid on local supply

A supplier treats a Telangana transaction as inter-State and pays IGST. Later it is established as intra-State. The supplier pays CGST + SGST and uses the Section 19/Section 77 interest protection and refund mechanism for the IGST originally paid.

Example 3 — Place of supply of services initially misunderstood

A service provider initially applies an inter-State place-of-supply rule and pays IGST. Later the correct place-of-supply rule establishes an intra-State supply. The tax-head correction must be documented through the applicable provisions.

Example 4 — Customer's GST registration status changes the analysis

A supplier treats the recipient as belonging to another State based on incomplete information. Later the contractual and registration facts establish a different place of supply. The original tax head should be reconciled with the correct position.

Example 5 — Officer determines wrong tax head

The taxpayer independently believed the supply was intra-State, but during proceedings the officer establishes that it was inter-State. Section 77 can still be relevant because “subsequently held” is not limited to taxpayer self-discovery.

Example 6 — Multiple invoices across a financial year

A place-of-supply interpretation is found to be wrong across 100 invoices. Prepare invoice-wise evidence, pay the correct tax and maintain a consolidated refund working supporting the RFD-01 claim.

Example 7 — Tax paid correctly but refund delayed

The taxpayer paid the correct tax but did not file the Section 77 refund application promptly. The two-year period from correct-head payment must be monitored.

Example 8 — Correct tax paid before 24 September 2021

For old cases covered by the transitional rule, Circular 162/18/2021-GST explains the special calculation of the refund filing period for payments made before Rule 89(1A) came into force.

Example 9 — Construction project across States

A contractor supplies services connected with an immovable property/project and initially applies the wrong place-of-supply rule. Because the place-of-supply analysis directly affects whether IGST or CGST + SGST applies, the entire contract and project-location evidence should be retained.

Example 10 — Wrong GSTIN and wrong tax head

A transaction is reported under the wrong GST registration and the wrong tax head. The taxpayer should first separate the GSTIN issue from the intra-State/inter-State issue and then determine the correct correction and refund route.

Example 11 — E-invoice already generated

An e-invoice reflects IGST but subsequent legal review establishes intra-State treatment. The business should not simply alter its accounting ledger. It should coordinate invoice correction, return reporting, correct tax payment and refund documentation under the applicable rules.

Example 12 — Advance and subsequent supply

A tax head was applied to an advance based on the initial place-of-supply understanding, but the final transaction is subsequently determined differently. The advance, invoice and final tax treatment must be traced together.

22. Construction and infrastructure examples

Works contract involving an immovable property

Construction and infrastructure transactions require careful place-of-supply analysis. The project location, recipient status, contract terms and nature of service should be documented before deciding IGST versus CGST + SGST.

HAM road project

For a highway project, maintain the concession agreement, project location, customer GSTIN, RA bills, tax invoices and GST return reconciliation. If a wrong tax head was used, these documents establish the factual basis for correction.

Multi-State EPC contract

A single customer may have projects in multiple States. The finance team should not assume that one tax treatment applies to every RA bill. Project-wise place-of-supply analysis is safer.

Pipeline project

Where the contract involves installation or work connected with immovable property, maintain project-location evidence and the exact service description used to determine place of supply.

Subcontractor tax-head mismatch

If a subcontractor charged IGST while the correct treatment was CGST + SGST, the principal should reconcile the invoice, place of supply, ITC treatment and supplier correction before finalising the ITC position.

23. Place-of-supply connection

Section 77 does not itself decide whether a supply is intra-State or inter-State. That question must be answered using the applicable charging and place-of-supply provisions.

CGST Act
Sections 7/8 of the CGST Act help determine whether a supply is intra-State or inter-State in conjunction with the IGST framework.
IGST Act
Sections 7 and 8 of the IGST Act classify inter-State and intra-State supplies, while Sections 10–13 contain place-of-supply rules.
Goods
Movement, delivery and location of goods can materially affect the analysis.
Services
Recipient location and the specific service-category place-of-supply rule can be decisive.

Therefore, a Section 77 refund file should contain not only payment evidence but also the legal and factual basis for the corrected place-of-supply conclusion.

26. Common mistakes

  • Paying the correct tax but forgetting to claim the wrong-head refund.
  • Calculating the two-year period from the original invoice date instead of the correct-head payment date.
  • Waiting for the department to discover the error instead of correcting it proactively.
  • Assuming “subsequently held” requires an assessment order.
  • Ignoring Section 19 of the IGST Act when the wrong payment was IGST.
  • Failing to document the corrected place-of-supply position.
  • Not reconciling the wrong tax with GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
  • Mixing up wrong tax-head refund with ordinary excess-tax refund.
  • Not tracking the RFD-01 ARN and deficiency communications.
  • Ignoring old transitional cases covered by the 2021 clarification.

28. Section 77 GST FAQs

What is Section 77 of the CGST Act?

Section 77 provides relief where a registered person pays CGST + SGST/UTGST on a transaction treated as intra-State but later it is held to be inter-State. It also provides interest protection for the reverse situation through Section 77(2).

What is wrong tax head in GST?

Wrong tax head means paying IGST when CGST + SGST/UTGST was actually applicable, or paying CGST + SGST/UTGST when IGST was actually applicable.

Can I get a refund of CGST and SGST wrongly paid instead of IGST?

Yes, Section 77(1) read with Rule 89(1A) provides the mechanism where the transaction was initially treated as intra-State but subsequently held to be inter-State, subject to the prescribed conditions.

Can I get a refund of IGST wrongly paid instead of CGST and SGST?

Yes. The reverse situation is addressed through Section 19 of the IGST Act read with the applicable refund rules.

Do I need to wait for a GST officer to identify the mistake?

No. The 2021 clarification states that “subsequently held” covers a situation where the taxpayer itself later discovers the correct nature of the supply.

What is Rule 89(1A)?

Rule 89(1A) prescribes the procedure and time limit for refund of tax paid under the wrong head in the Section 77/Section 19 situations.

What is the two-year time limit under Section 77?

For the Section 77 refund mechanism, the two-year period is generally counted from the date the tax under the correct head was paid, subject to the transitional rule for older payments.

What if I paid the correct tax before 24 September 2021?

The transitional mechanism explained in CBIC Circular 162/18/2021-GST allows the refund application within two years from 24 September 2021 for the specified older payments.

Is interest payable when IGST is changed to CGST and SGST?

Section 77(2) provides that interest is not required on the CGST and SGST/UTGST payable in the specified situation where IGST was initially paid on a transaction later held to be intra-State.

Does Section 77 decide place of supply?

No. Section 77 provides the correction/refund mechanism. The actual place-of-supply conclusion must be established under the applicable CGST/IGST provisions.

Is Section 77 refund the same as normal GST refund?

No. Although the application is made through the prescribed refund system, Section 77/Rule 89(1A) is a specific wrong-tax-head refund category.

Which form is used for Section 77 refund?

Rule 89(1A) provides for electronic application in FORM GST RFD-01.

What documents should be attached?

The taxpayer should maintain the original invoice, place-of-supply analysis, original tax payment, correct-head tax payment, return reconciliation and other evidence prescribed for the refund claim.

Does Section 77 apply to construction projects?

Yes. Construction and infrastructure businesses should be particularly careful because place-of-supply analysis can materially affect whether IGST or CGST + SGST applies.

What is Section 77 vs Section 19?

They are companion provisions. Section 77 is in the CGST Act and Section 19 is in the IGST Act, together covering the two directions of wrong tax-head payment.

30. Key takeaways

  • Section 77 is the GST wrong-tax-head correction provision.
  • It mainly addresses CGST + SGST/UTGST paid when the supply is later held to be inter-State.
  • Section 19 of the IGST Act addresses the reverse IGST-to-CGST/SGST situation.
  • “Subsequently held” includes a taxpayer discovering the error itself as well as a finding in departmental proceedings.
  • The correct tax should be paid under the correct head before claiming the wrong-head refund under the prescribed mechanism.
  • Rule 89(1A) provides the specific refund procedure and generally measures the two-year period from payment of the correct tax.
  • The 2021 transitional rule is important for older cases where correct-head tax was paid before Rule 89(1A) came into force.
  • Section 77(2) provides important interest protection for the reverse wrong-head situation.
  • Place-of-supply evidence is the foundation of a strong Section 77 refund claim.
  • Construction and multi-State businesses should maintain project-wise tax-head controls.
