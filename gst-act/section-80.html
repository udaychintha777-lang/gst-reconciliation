GST ACT — SECTION 80

Section 80 CGST Act — GST Tax Payment in Instalments & Extension of Time

Section 80 provides a practical relief mechanism for a taxable person who is unable to pay an eligible GST amount within the ordinary payment period. On application, the Commissioner may, for reasons recorded in writing, extend the time for payment or allow payment in monthly instalments, subject to interest and prescribed conditions. This detailed guide explains Section 80 in simple language, including the important exclusions, the maximum 24 instalments, Rule 158, DRC-20, DRC-21 and what happens if an instalment is missed.

1. Why Section 80 matters

Searches such as “Section 80 CGST Act”, “Section 80 GST”, “GST payment in instalments”, “GST demand instalment facility”, “GST extension of time for payment”, “GST DRC-20”, “GST DRC-21”, “how to pay GST demand in instalments” and “24 instalments GST” are directly connected with this provision.

GST liabilities can sometimes become large because of an adjudication order, audit, scrutiny, investigation, interest, penalty or other statutory event. Even when a business accepts the liability, immediate payment of the entire amount may create serious working-capital pressure.

Section 80 provides a statutory mechanism for dealing with that situation. It does not cancel the tax. It does not automatically waive interest. It does not give every taxpayer an unconditional right to instalments. Instead, it allows the Commissioner, on application and subject to the law, to grant additional time or permit monthly instalments.

Simple idea: Section 80 is not a tax waiver. It is a controlled payment-time facility for eligible amounts when the taxpayer cannot or does not want to pay the entire eligible liability at once.

2. Section 80 in very simple language

Imagine a company receives an eligible GST demand of ₹1.20 crore. The company accepts that the amount is payable but cannot arrange the entire amount immediately without seriously disrupting its operations.

Instead of simply allowing the demand to become overdue and enter recovery, the company can examine Section 80.

Eligible GST amount → Application → Commissioner considers reasons → Extension / monthly instalments → Pay as approved

The facility is discretionary: the taxpayer applies; the Commissioner considers; and the final payment schedule is governed by the order and the prescribed conditions.

The Rules provide for a formal electronic application and order mechanism. Rule 158 provides for FORM GST DRC-20 and, where the request is allowed, FORM GST DRC-21.

3. What the law actually says

Section 80 is titled “Payment of tax and other amount in instalments.”

Its central structure is:

Part of Section 80Meaning
ApplicationThe taxable person must apply for the facility.
Commissioner's decisionThe Commissioner may grant extension/instalments and must record reasons in writing.
Eligible amountThe facility covers an amount due under the Act, but excludes the liability self-assessed in a return.
Number of instalmentsMonthly instalments can be allowed up to a maximum of 24.
InterestPayment remains subject to interest under Section 50.
DefaultIf one instalment is missed, the whole outstanding balance becomes due immediately and can be recovered without another notice.

The current CBIC tax-information version states that Section 80 is enforced from 1 July 2017. The statutory wording has remained focused on instalment/payment-time relief rather than creating a general tax-deferral entitlement.

4. Who can apply?

The statutory applicant is a taxable person.

That means Section 80 is not a general facility for every person who may have a dispute with the department. The applicant must fall within the statutory framework and must have an eligible amount due under the Act.

Before applying, the taxpayer should establish:

✓ Correct GSTIN
✓ Exact demand/liability
✓ Legal provision creating liability
✓ Amount eligible for instalment request
✓ Cash-flow difficulty
✓ Proposed payment schedule
✓ Current recovery status
✓ Existing instalment history

5. The most important exclusion

The most important point that many summaries miss is that Section 80 specifically excludes the amount due as per the liability self-assessed in any return.

In simple terms, Section 80 is not intended to convert an ordinary self-assessed GST return liability into an instalment loan.

SituationSection 80 treatment
Eligible amount determined under an orderMay be considered, subject to conditions.
Other eligible amount due under the ActMay be considered, subject to conditions.
Liability self-assessed in a GST returnExcluded by Section 80.
InterestDoes not disappear; Section 50 applies.
Critical distinction: A business should not prepare a Section 80 application merely because its GSTR-3B cash liability is difficult to fund. The statutory exclusion for self-assessed return liability must be considered first.

6. Maximum 24 monthly instalments

Section 80 permits monthly instalments not exceeding twenty-four.

This means the statutory ceiling is 24 monthly instalments. It does not mean every applicant will receive 24 instalments.

The Commissioner may decide the number of instalments after considering the application, the jurisdictional officer's report and the applicable conditions.

DemandIllustrative schedule
₹24 lakh24 instalments could average ₹1 lakh each, before considering interest and the actual order.
₹60 lakh24 instalments could average ₹2.50 lakh each, before interest and conditions.
₹1.20 crore24 instalments could average ₹5 lakh each, before interest and conditions.

These are only mathematical illustrations. The Commissioner is not required to approve an equal instalment schedule merely because the taxpayer proposes one.

7. Extension of payment time

Section 80 contains two related forms of relief:

Extension of time
The Commissioner may allow additional time for payment.
Payment by instalments
The Commissioner may permit payment in monthly instalments.

Therefore, an application does not necessarily have to be framed only as “24 instalments”. If the business has a credible reason and payment plan, the request can explain the required additional time and/or proposed instalment schedule.

The final order governs what is actually permitted.

8. Interest under Section 50

Section 80 expressly states that the facility is subject to payment of interest under Section 50.

Therefore, instalment approval should never be interpreted as an interest waiver.

Instalment facility ≠ Interest waiver

The taxpayer should calculate the interest exposure separately and include it in cash-flow planning. The applicable interest depends on the nature of the underlying liability and the relevant Section 50 framework.

A company should model both the principal instalment and the continuing interest cost before submitting its cash-flow proposal.

9. Conditions and limitations

Section 80 itself says the facility is subject to prescribed conditions and limitations. Rule 158 provides the principal procedural conditions.

The Commissioner therefore does not simply approve an instalment plan because a taxpayer says that it has insufficient funds. The application must be considered within the statutory and Rules framework.

A strong application should demonstrate:

  • the exact eligible amount;
  • why immediate full payment would create genuine financial difficulty;
  • current liquidity and working-capital position;
  • expected collections;
  • the proposed monthly payment capacity;
  • that the taxpayer understands the interest consequence; and
  • that the proposed plan is realistic.

Rule 158 is the key procedural Rule for Section 80.

11. DRC-20 application

FORM GST DRC-20 is the electronic application used to seek extension of time for payment or payment by instalments under Section 80.

A professional application should not be treated as a mere formality. The supporting explanation should make it easy for the department to understand:

Demand
Exact amount and legal basis.
Cash flow
Why full immediate payment is difficult.
Collections
Expected inflows and dates.
Proposal
Amount and number of monthly instalments requested.
Interest
Recognition that Section 50 interest applies.
Compliance
Current GST compliance position.

12. Jurisdictional officer report

Rule 158 requires the Commissioner to call for a report from the jurisdictional officer regarding the taxable person's financial ability to pay.

This is important because it shows that the instalment decision is not intended to be based solely on a taxpayer's unsupported statement of financial difficulty.

The taxpayer should therefore make the application internally consistent with its:

  • bank statements;
  • management accounts;
  • receivable ageing;
  • payable ageing;
  • cash-flow forecasts;
  • GST liability position;
  • loan/working-capital commitments; and
  • project collection schedule.

13. DRC-21 order

Where the Commissioner allows the request, Rule 158 provides for an order in FORM GST DRC-21.

The approved order should be read carefully for:

  • total amount covered;
  • number of instalments;
  • amount of each instalment;
  • due dates;
  • conditions;
  • interest implications; and
  • consequences of default.
Control: Do not rely only on the original DRC-20 application. The actual approved DRC-21 order determines the payment schedule granted by the Commissioner.

14. Cases where instalment facility is not allowed

Rule 158(3) contains important restrictions.

RestrictionMeaning
Recovery process already on after defaultThe facility is not allowed where the taxable person has already defaulted on payment of an amount under the GST laws for which recovery process is on.
Instalments already allowed in preceding financial yearThe facility is not allowed where the taxable person was already allowed payment in instalments in the preceding financial year under the specified GST laws.
Amount below ₹25,000The facility is not allowed where the amount for which instalment facility is sought is less than ₹25,000.

These restrictions are specifically set out in Rule 158(3). They are therefore not merely departmental preferences.

A taxpayer should apply early. Waiting until recovery has already commenced can make the Rule 158(3)(a) restriction particularly important.

15. What happens if one instalment is missed?

This is one of the most important parts of Section 80.

If the taxpayer defaults in payment of any one instalment on its due date, the whole outstanding balance payable on that date becomes due and payable immediately.

Importantly, the law states that this happens without any further notice and the balance becomes liable for recovery.

Missed one instalment → Entire outstanding balance becomes due → Recovery exposure

Example

A company is allowed 12 monthly instalments of ₹5 lakh each. After paying seven instalments, it misses the eighth instalment. The remaining outstanding balance does not continue automatically as a protected nine-to-five-month schedule. The statutory consequence under Section 80 is that the outstanding balance becomes due and liable for recovery.

Finance control: Instalment schedules must be treated like statutory payment commitments. A single missed date can have consequences far beyond the missed instalment itself.

16. Section 78 and Section 80

Easy memory: Section 78 gives the ordinary payment window. Section 80 can provide a structured alternative for eligible liabilities where the statutory conditions are satisfied.

17. Section 79 and Section 80

Section 79 is the GST recovery provision. Section 80 is a payment-facilitation provision.

This distinction matters because the taxpayer should ideally address a cash-flow problem before recovery action begins.

Section 79
Recovery machinery after an amount becomes recoverable and remains unpaid.
Section 80
Application-based additional time/instalment mechanism subject to conditions.

Rule 158 specifically prevents the instalment facility where the taxpayer has already defaulted and recovery is on, subject to the exact wording and applicable law.

18. Self-assessed return liability

Section 80 specifically excludes an amount due as per the liability self-assessed in a return.

This is especially important for regular GSTR-3B liabilities. A taxpayer should not assume that an ordinary monthly output-tax liability can be converted into a 24-month Section 80 schedule simply because the business is short of cash.

For self-assessed liabilities, the taxpayer should instead examine the ordinary payment provisions, interest, return filing requirements and any other legally available relief or arrangement.

Example: If a company has ₹40 lakh self-assessed GST liability in its current return but cannot fund it, Section 80 is not the statutory route to turn that return liability into 24 instalments.

19. GST demand orders

Section 80 is more naturally relevant where a taxpayer has an eligible amount due under the Act that is not merely the self-assessed liability in a return.

Examples can include an amount confirmed through an adjudication process, subject to the exact statutory circumstances and whether the amount remains eligible under Section 80.

Before applying, identify:

  • order number;
  • tax period;
  • tax component;
  • interest;
  • penalty;
  • amount already paid;
  • appeal/pre-deposit status;
  • stay status; and
  • amount for which instalment is actually requested.

23. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — ₹24 lakh demand

A company has an eligible ₹24 lakh amount due. It requests 12 monthly instalments of ₹2 lakh. The Commissioner considers the application, financial report and prescribed conditions before deciding the schedule.

Example 2 — Request for maximum 24 months

A business requests 24 monthly instalments because its project collections are spread over two years. The request does not automatically guarantee 24 instalments; the Commissioner decides the permitted period.

Example 3 — Self-assessed GSTR-3B liability

A taxpayer has ₹15 lakh self-assessed liability in its return. Section 80 does not provide an instalment route for that liability because the section expressly excludes liability self-assessed in a return.

Example 4 — Recovery already started

A taxpayer waits until recovery proceedings have commenced and then seeks Section 80. Rule 158(3)(a) becomes a critical restriction because the prescribed facility is not allowed where the relevant recovery process is already on following default.

Example 5 — Instalment facility in previous year

A taxpayer was already allowed an instalment facility in the preceding financial year. A fresh request may be barred by Rule 158(3)(b), subject to the exact statutory framework.

Example 6 — Amount below ₹25,000

A taxpayer seeks instalments for ₹20,000. Rule 158(3)(c) specifically excludes an amount below ₹25,000.

Example 7 — Missed instalment

A taxpayer receives approval for 10 instalments but misses instalment number 6. The remaining balance becomes due immediately and becomes liable for recovery without another notice under Section 80.

Example 8 — Interest planning

A taxpayer plans the principal instalments but ignores Section 50 interest. The cash-flow plan becomes insufficient. Interest should be separately modelled from the beginning.

Example 9 — Construction company

A contractor receives an eligible demand of ₹1.20 crore. Its major project receivables are expected over 18 months. It prepares a detailed collection forecast and proposes a realistic instalment schedule, subject to Commissioner approval.

Example 10 — Government project collections

A road contractor expects certified RA bills from government departments. The cash-flow forecast should show expected certification/payment dates rather than simply stating “cash flow is tight”.

Example 11 — Multiple GSTINs

A group has a demand in one GSTIN but cash available in another. Section 80 should be analysed registration-wise because each GSTIN's liability and payment position must be properly identified.

Example 12 — Appeal pending

A taxpayer is considering appeal against an order but also has cash-flow concerns. It should first determine the appellate/pre-deposit/stay position before deciding whether an instalment application is appropriate for the amount that remains legally payable.

Example 13 — Large working-capital facility

A manufacturing company has a bank working-capital facility but temporary receivable delays. Its application should reconcile the actual bank position, receivables and committed payments instead of claiming an unsupported inability to pay.

Example 14 — Group company guarantees

A company may have inter-company receivables and guarantees. The cash-flow proposal should reflect actual legally available funds and not assume that group-company money can automatically be used to pay the applicant's GST liability.

24. Construction and infrastructure examples

RA bill cycle

Infrastructure companies often receive money only after measurement, certification and processing of RA bills. A Section 80 cash-flow proposal can explain this cycle with evidence rather than simply stating that payments are delayed.

Retention money

Retention reduces immediate cash availability. The cash-flow model should show expected release dates and not treat the entire retention balance as immediately collectible.

Mobilisation advance

Where mobilisation advances are received, the company should distinguish contractual receipts from available free cash because advances may carry project obligations and future adjustment.

Subcontractor payments

A construction company should include committed subcontractor payments and statutory deductions in its forecast. The objective is to demonstrate realistic repayment capacity while continuing project execution.

Equipment finance

EMIs for cranes, batching plants, heavy vehicles and other project equipment can materially affect monthly liquidity. They should be included in the cash-flow schedule.

Multiple projects

Use project-wise collections and costs. A consolidated number without project support is weaker than a clear project-by-project cash-flow forecast.

25. Accounting and finance treatment

Section 80 does not eliminate the underlying liability. The accounting team should therefore maintain the full demand liability while separately tracking the approved payment schedule.

28. Common mistakes

  • Assuming Section 80 is available for ordinary GSTR-3B self-assessed liability.
  • Waiting until recovery proceedings have already started.
  • Assuming the Commissioner must approve 24 instalments.
  • Ignoring Section 50 interest.
  • Submitting a cash-flow statement that does not agree with the books.
  • Failing to check whether instalments were already allowed in the preceding financial year.
  • Requesting instalments for an amount below ₹25,000.
  • Missing one instalment and assuming only that instalment becomes overdue.
  • Not reading the conditions in DRC-21.
  • Failing to reconcile instalment payments with the GST portal.
  • Using a generic “financial difficulty” statement without evidence.
  • Not involving treasury/finance early enough.

30. Section 80 GST FAQs

What is Section 80 of the CGST Act?

Section 80 allows the Commissioner, on application and subject to conditions, to extend the time for payment or allow eligible GST amounts to be paid in monthly instalments.

How many instalments can be allowed under Section 80?

Monthly instalments can be allowed for a maximum of 24 months.

Is Section 80 an automatic right?

No. The taxpayer must apply and the Commissioner may allow the facility after considering the application and prescribed conditions.

Can GSTR-3B self-assessed tax be paid under Section 80 instalments?

No. Section 80 expressly excludes the amount due as per the liability self-assessed in a return.

Does Section 80 waive interest?

No. Section 80 expressly makes the facility subject to payment of interest under Section 50.

What is DRC-20?

DRC-20 is the electronic application used under Rule 158 to seek extension of payment time or payment in instalments under Section 80.

What is DRC-21?

DRC-21 is the order used under Rule 158 when the Commissioner allows further time and/or monthly instalments.

Can I ask for 24 instalments?

You can request a schedule up to the statutory maximum, but approval of 24 instalments is not automatic. The Commissioner decides the permitted schedule.

What happens if one instalment is missed?

The whole outstanding balance payable on that date becomes due immediately and is liable for recovery without any further notice.

Can Section 80 be used after recovery has started?

Rule 158(3)(a) restricts the facility where the taxpayer has already defaulted on a specified GST amount for which the recovery process is on.

What if I already received instalment facility in the previous financial year?

Rule 158(3)(b) restricts the facility where instalments were already allowed in the preceding financial year under the specified GST laws.

Is there a minimum amount for Section 80?

Rule 158(3)(c) states that the facility is not allowed where the amount for which instalment facility is sought is less than ₹25,000.

Can the Commissioner extend the payment time without instalments?

Yes. Section 80 permits extension of time as well as payment through monthly instalments, subject to the statutory framework.

Does Section 80 apply to interest?

The underlying facility is subject to payment of interest under Section 50. The taxpayer should separately calculate and monitor interest.

Does Section 80 cancel a GST demand?

No. It only provides a payment-time/instalment mechanism for eligible amounts. The underlying liability remains unless reduced or cancelled by a competent legal process.

Can a company use Section 80 because of temporary cash-flow problems?

Temporary financial difficulty can be explained as part of an application, but approval depends on the statutory conditions, financial assessment and Commissioner's decision.

What should be attached to a Section 80 application?

Use the prescribed electronic application and support the request with the demand details, financial position, cash-flow forecast, receivables, committed payments and proposed instalment plan as appropriate.

Is Section 80 useful for construction companies?

It can be particularly relevant where an eligible demand is large and project collections are spread over several months, but the company must satisfy the statutory conditions and demonstrate a credible repayment capacity.

What is the difference between Section 78 and Section 80?

Section 78 provides the ordinary payment period after an order; Section 80 provides a possible extension or instalment mechanism for eligible amounts.

What is the difference between Section 80 and Section 79?

Section 80 is a payment-facilitation provision; Section 79 is the principal recovery provision when recoverable amounts remain unpaid.

32. Key takeaways

  • Section 80 provides a possible payment-time/instalment facility for eligible GST amounts.
  • It is not an automatic right; the taxpayer must apply and the Commissioner decides.
  • The maximum number of monthly instalments is 24.
  • Liability self-assessed in a return is expressly excluded.
  • Interest under Section 50 continues to apply.
  • Rule 158 is the key procedural Rule.
  • DRC-20 is the application and DRC-21 is the approval order.
  • Rule 158 requires consideration of the taxpayer's financial ability to pay.
  • The facility is restricted where recovery is already on following default, where instalments were allowed in the preceding financial year, and where the amount is below ₹25,000.
  • Missing one instalment can make the entire outstanding balance immediately due and liable for recovery.
  • A realistic cash-flow forecast is one of the most important supporting documents for a business seeking payment flexibility.
  • Construction and infrastructure companies should connect the instalment proposal with project collections, RA bills, retention, equipment finance and other committed cash outflows.
