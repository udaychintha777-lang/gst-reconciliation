CGST Act Section 171 – Anti-Profiteering Measure

Section 171 addresses the passing on of GST rate-reduction benefits and input tax credit benefits to recipients through a commensurate reduction in prices.

3. Statutory Text of Section 171

171. Anti-profiteering measure.— (1) Any reduction in rate of tax on any supply of goods or services or the benefit of input tax credit shall be passed on to the recipient by way of commensurate reduction in prices. (2) The Central Government may, on recommendations of the Council, by notification, constitute an Authority, or empower an existing Authority constituted under any law for the time being in force, to examine whether input tax credits availed by any registered person or the reduction in the tax rate have actually resulted in a commensurate reduction in the price of the goods or services or both supplied by him. (3) The Authority referred to in sub-section (2) shall exercise such powers and discharge such functions as may be prescribed.
Important: The statutory text is concise, but its practical application can require detailed transaction, tax-rate, ITC and pricing analysis.

4. Section 171 in Simple Language

GST can affect the economics of a supply in two important ways specifically identified by Section 171: the applicable rate of tax can be reduced, or a registered person can obtain a benefit from input tax credit.

Section 171(1) states that the benefit is to be passed on to the recipient by way of a commensurate reduction in prices.

This means Section 171 is not simply a rule saying “reduce the price by the same percentage in every case.” The relevant benefit has to be identified and the corresponding price effect examined.

Example in principle: If a tax-rate reduction decreases the tax burden on a supply, the business should not simply retain the entire resulting benefit without examining the Section 171 requirement. The actual calculation depends on the supply, price structure and relevant tax/ITC data.

5. What Benefits Are Covered?

Do not confuse: A reduction in tax rate and an increase in ITC are different sources of benefit. A proper analysis should identify which benefit occurred, when it occurred and how it affected the relevant supplies.

6. Section 171(1) – Passing on the Benefit

6.1 Reduction in rate of tax

When the rate applicable to a supply is reduced, Section 171(1) requires the resulting benefit to be passed to the recipient by way of commensurate reduction in prices.

6.2 Benefit of input tax credit

The provision separately addresses the benefit of input tax credit. Therefore, anti-profiteering analysis is not limited to changes in output tax rates.

6.3 Recipient-focused outcome

The statutory mechanism described in Section 171(1) focuses on the recipient receiving the benefit through the price of the goods or services. Businesses should therefore retain evidence connecting the relevant tax/ITC benefit with the pricing of affected supplies.

Identify the benefitDetermine whether the benefit arose from tax-rate reduction, ITC or both.
Identify affected suppliesMap the benefit to the relevant goods/services and period.
Measure price effectAnalyse whether the recipient received the commensurate reduction.

7. Section 171(2) – Authority to Examine

Section 171(2) gives the Central Government a statutory mechanism to establish the authority framework. On the recommendations of the GST Council, the Central Government may by notification:

  • constitute an Authority; or
  • empower an existing Authority constituted under any law for the time being in force.

The authority is concerned with examining whether input tax credits availed by a registered person or a reduction in the tax rate has actually resulted in a commensurate reduction in the price of goods or services or both supplied by that person.

Three important components: GST Council recommendation → Central Government notification → Authority empowered to examine the specified question.

8. Current Authority Framework

Notification No. 23/2022-Central Tax dated 23 November 2022 was issued under Section 171(2). It empowered the Competition Commission of India (CCI) to examine whether input tax credit benefits or tax-rate reductions had resulted in a commensurate reduction in prices. The notification took effect from 1 December 2022.

This is important when reading older anti-profiteering material. The authority arrangement changed from the earlier framework, so historical cases should be considered with reference to the law and notification applicable during the relevant period.

The CCI's own annual report records the same statutory mandate and effective date.

9. Section 171(3) – Powers and Functions

Section 171(3) states that the Authority referred to in sub-section (2) shall exercise such powers and discharge such functions as may be prescribed.

This means the Act itself establishes the core anti-profiteering principle and authority mechanism, while the detailed powers and functions are connected to the prescribed framework.

Practical reading: To understand a specific anti-profiteering proceeding, reading Section 171 alone may not be enough. The applicable rules, notifications, procedural provisions and the facts of the case should also be reviewed.

10. Understanding “Commensurate Reduction in Prices”

The expression “commensurate reduction” is central to Section 171(1). It indicates that the reduction in price should correspond to the benefit covered by the provision.

Why a universal percentage is not stated in Section 171

The section does not say that every tax-rate reduction or every ITC benefit must produce the same percentage reduction in the final customer price. The relevant supply may have a particular base price, tax component, ITC structure, discounts, credit notes and other transaction facts.

11. Records and Evidence for Businesses

A business that may be affected by a Section 171 review should be able to reconstruct its pricing and tax position for the relevant period.

Recommended records

  • Applicable GST rate notifications and effective dates.
  • Product/service master with tax rates.
  • Sales invoices and credit/debit notes.
  • Price lists before and after the relevant change.
  • Discount and promotional records.
  • Purchase register and eligible ITC workings.
  • GSTR-2B/GSTR-3B reconciliation where relevant to the ITC analysis.
  • Tax ledgers and output-tax workings.
  • Cost and commercial records needed to explain price changes.
  • Customer-level or supply-level data where appropriate.
  • ERP configuration changes and effective dates.
  • Management approvals for price revisions.
Best practice: Build a contemporaneous reconciliation instead of attempting to reconstruct every price and ITC movement years later.

12. Practical Examples

Example 1 – GST rate is reduced

A product was supplied at an applicable tax rate that is subsequently reduced. Section 171(1) requires the benefit of that reduction to be passed on to the recipient through a commensurate reduction in prices.

Example 2 – Input tax credit benefit

A registered supplier receives an additional benefit from eligible input tax credit. Section 171(1) addresses passing on the benefit of input tax credit to the recipient through commensurate price reduction.

Example 3 – Tax-rate reduction and ITC together

Where both a tax-rate change and an ITC benefit are relevant, the analysis needs to identify the effect of each benefit rather than assuming that the entire change in selling price is attributable to one factor.

Example 4 – No automatic fixed percentage

Section 171 does not itself state that every supplier must reduce the selling price by one universal fixed percentage. The statutory expression is 'commensurate reduction'.

Example 5 – Different product margins

Two products may have different pre-change prices, tax rates, ITC patterns and commercial structures. A compliance analysis therefore needs transaction-level or appropriate comparable data rather than a blanket arithmetic assumption.

Example 6 – Commercial price increase after tax reduction

A supplier may have other commercial cost changes. Such circumstances do not by themselves answer the Section 171 question; the relevant benefit and its commensurate passing-on must be examined under the applicable framework.

Example 7 – ITC changes over time

Where ITC eligibility or quantum changes between periods, the supplier's working papers should identify the relevant period, eligible credits and corresponding pricing data.

Example 8 – Multiple GST registrations

A business operating multiple GSTINs should preserve GSTIN-wise tax-rate, ITC, sales and pricing data so that the relevant supply and recipient records can be identified.

Example 9 – Real-estate/project supplies

For a project with supplies occurring over different periods, the analysis can require project-level records, invoices, tax rates, eligible ITC and amounts collected from recipients.

Example 10 – Consumer-facing business

A retailer should retain price lists, invoices and tax configuration before and after a tax-rate change so that any price adjustment can be explained.

Example 11 – B2B customer

For a business customer, the recipient's identity and invoice trail may be readily available. The supplier should preserve the relevant invoice, tax and pricing records for the affected period.

Example 12 – Accounting system change

If an ERP changes tax configuration, finance should retain the old and new tax settings and test invoices around the effective date so that the price impact can be demonstrated.

Example 13 – Refund or return

Returns, discounts, credit notes and debit notes can affect the amount actually realised. The analysis should therefore use complete transaction records rather than only original invoices.

Example 14 – Evidence of pass-through

A supplier may maintain price lists, invoices, discount policies, cost sheets, ITC workings and tax-rate change documentation to demonstrate how a benefit was treated.

Example 15 – Historical case review

A business reviewing an older anti-profiteering matter should identify the law, rules, notifications and authority framework applicable during the relevant period instead of applying today's procedural framework retrospectively.

Example 16 – Benefit already reflected in price

If the pricing structure already incorporated the relevant tax/ITC benefit, the supporting contemporaneous records become important in explaining the price movement.

Example 17 – Supply with different tax components

Where supplies have different tax treatments, the relevant supply-wise data should be separated instead of combining unrelated products into one average.

Example 18 – Management review

A tax team can prepare a Section 171 file containing the affected tax change, ITC movement, sales data, price before/after, discounts, credit notes, cost movements and reconciliation.

13. Section 171 Compliance Workflow

  1. Identify the change: Determine whether a GST rate reduction or ITC benefit occurred.
  2. Determine effective date: Fix the period from which the benefit applies.
  3. Identify affected supplies: Map products/services and recipients.
  4. Calculate the benefit: Quantify the relevant tax-rate or ITC effect using appropriate records.
  5. Analyse pricing: Compare relevant prices after considering discounts, returns and other transaction adjustments.
  6. Prepare reconciliation: Link the benefit to the amount passed on through price.
  7. Review exceptions: Investigate unusual price movements and incomplete data.
  8. Preserve evidence: Keep source records, calculations and approvals.
  9. Respond to proceedings: If an authority seeks information, provide a complete, internally reconciled response after appropriate professional review.

15. Historical vs Current Reading

Anti-profiteering material often refers to the National Anti-profiteering Authority (NAA) because that body formed part of the earlier GST anti-profiteering framework. For current legal research, however, the authority arrangement must be checked against the later notifications.

Notification No. 23/2022-Central Tax expressly empowered the CCI under Section 171(2) with effect from 1 December 2022.

Therefore, a useful research habit is to separate:

QuestionWhat to verify
When did the benefit arise?Tax-rate/ITC change and effective date.
Which authority framework applied?Notifications and rules applicable to that period.
Which recipient/supplies were affected?Invoice and pricing records.
What benefit was identified?Tax-rate reduction and/or ITC benefit.
How was it passed on?Price and transaction-level evidence.

16. Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every GST rate reduction automatically means the same percentage reduction in the final invoice price.
  • Ignoring the separate ITC-benefit limb of Section 171.
  • Using only list prices without considering discounts and credit notes.
  • Combining different products, tax treatments or periods into one unexplained average.
  • Failing to preserve pre-change and post-change price evidence.
  • Failing to reconcile ITC data with the relevant period.
  • Applying the current authority framework to a historical matter without checking the relevant date.
  • Assuming Section 171 itself contains every procedural rule for an investigation.
  • Not maintaining an audit trail for ERP tax-rate changes.
  • Confusing a commercial price increase with the separate statutory question of whether a qualifying GST benefit was passed on.

18. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 171 of the CGST Act?

Section 171 is the anti-profiteering provision. It states that a reduction in the rate of tax or the benefit of input tax credit is to be passed on to the recipient by way of commensurate reduction in prices.

2. What are the two main benefits covered by Section 171(1)?

The provision covers a reduction in the rate of tax on a supply and the benefit of input tax credit.

3. What does 'commensurate reduction in prices' mean?

It means the price reduction should correspond to the relevant benefit covered by Section 171. The section itself does not prescribe one universal percentage for every transaction.

4. Does Section 171 apply only when the GST rate is reduced?

No. It expressly covers both a reduction in the rate of tax and the benefit of input tax credit.

5. Who can examine whether the benefit was passed on?

Section 171(2) empowers the Central Government, on the recommendation of the GST Council, to constitute an Authority or empower an existing Authority to examine the matter.

6. Who was empowered to examine anti-profiteering cases from 1 December 2022?

Notification No. 23/2022-Central Tax, dated 23 November 2022, empowered the Competition Commission of India to examine the matters specified in Section 171(2), effective from 1 December 2022.

7. Does Section 171 itself prescribe a fixed price-reduction percentage?

No. The statutory language uses the concept of a commensurate reduction. The calculation depends on the relevant tax-rate or ITC benefit and the facts of the supply.

8. Does every price increase after a tax-rate reduction mean Section 171 was violated?

Not necessarily. The statutory issue concerns whether the relevant benefit was passed on through commensurate price reduction. Other commercial factors may require examination in the particular case.

9. What records should a business maintain?

Useful records include tax-rate notifications, price lists, invoices, credit/debit notes, discount records, ITC workings, purchase records, sales data, cost records and reconciliation workings.

10. Does Section 171 change the GST rate?

No. It is a benefit-pass-through provision. The applicable tax rate is determined under the relevant rate notification and GST law.

11. Is anti-profiteering the same as a normal GST rate reduction?

No. A rate reduction changes the tax applicable to the supply. Section 171 addresses passing the resulting benefit to recipients through commensurate price reduction.

12. Can ITC benefit be relevant even when the tax rate does not change?

Yes. Section 171(1) separately refers to the benefit of input tax credit.

13. Does Section 171 apply to old transactions?

Historical transactions can be subject to the law and procedural framework applicable to their relevant period. The applicable notifications, rules and authority arrangements should be checked.

14. What changed in December 2022?

Notification No. 23/2022-Central Tax empowered the Competition Commission of India under Section 171(2), with effect from 1 December 2022.

15. What happened to the earlier National Anti-profiteering Authority framework?

The statutory provision allowed the Central Government to constitute an Authority or empower an existing Authority. The authority arrangement changed with Notification No. 23/2022-Central Tax. Historical cases should therefore be read with the framework applicable to the relevant period.

16. Can a business prepare a Section 171 reconciliation?

Yes. A structured reconciliation of tax rate, ITC, sales, price, discounts and relevant transaction data can help identify and document the effect of a benefit.

17. Is Section 171 relevant to real estate?

It can be relevant where the statutory conditions and a qualifying tax/ITC benefit apply. Project-level records are often important because supplies, construction periods, ITC and collections may span different periods.

18. Is this article legal advice?

No. It is an educational explanation. Anti-profiteering matters can involve detailed facts, historical rules, notifications and adjudicatory decisions, so the applicable framework should be verified for the relevant period.
