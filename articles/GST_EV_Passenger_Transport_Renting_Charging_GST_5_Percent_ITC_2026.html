GST on EV Passenger Transport, Vehicle Renting & Charging – 5% vs 18% and ITC Rules

Operating electric taxis, employee transport fleets, app-based EV cabs or a public charging station? From 1 November 2026, a new rate option applies to specified EV passenger transport and renting services where battery charging is included in the price. But the 5% option comes with strict ITC restrictions. EV charging-station services are a separate supply and should not be confused with passenger transport.

1. Executive summary – separate three different GST supplies

The most common mistake is to treat an EV charging station, an EV taxi operator and a vehicle rental company as if they supply the same service. They do not. Identify the actual supply before selecting a rate or claiming ITC.

EV passenger transport

Transport of passengers by a qualifying electrically operated vehicle, where battery charging cost is included in the consideration: 5% with restricted ITC or 18% under the alternative rate, subject to the notification conditions.

Renting an EV with operator

Renting a passenger EV with an operator and charging cost included: 5% with the prescribed ITC restrictions or 18% as the alternative.

Public EV charging station

Battery charging service is a separate service. The GST Council's official discussion records the existing treatment as a taxable service at 18%; it is not automatically exempt electricity merely because electricity is consumed.

EV charger / charging station equipment

Sale of a charger or charging station as goods is a different transaction from charging a vehicle. Check the applicable goods classification and rate entry.

Critical date The new EV passenger transport and renting entries apply from 1 November 2026 under Notification No. 06/2026-Central Tax (Rate) and Notification No. 06/2026-Integrated Tax (Rate), dated 27 October 2026.

2. What changed from 1 November 2026?

Earlier, the concessional passenger transport and vehicle-renting entries were linked to the cost of fuel being included in the consideration. Electricity used to charge an EV was not treated as fuel for that wording. This created a rate mismatch between otherwise similar services using conventional vehicles and electric vehicles.

The 2026 amendments insert specific entries for passenger transportation by an electrically operated vehicle and renting of an electrically operated vehicle with an operator, where the cost of battery charging is included in the consideration.

ServiceNew rate optionCondition
Passenger transport by EV designed to carry passengers5% or 18%Battery charging cost included in consideration. 5% option has restricted ITC; 18% option follows normal ITC provisions.
Renting of passenger EV with operator5% or 18%Battery charging cost included in consideration. 5% option has prescribed ITC restrictions.
Renting of EV for transportation of goods with operator5% or 18%Battery charging cost included in consideration. 5% option has prescribed ITC restrictions.
EV charging station serviceSeparate classification/rate treatmentDo not apply the passenger transport rate to charging station invoices.
Rate expression: 5% generally means 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST for intra-State supply, or 5% IGST for inter-State supply. The 18% alternative generally means 9% + 9%, or 18% IGST.

Vehicle definition: The notification defines an electrically operated vehicle as a road vehicle running solely on electrical energy derived from an external source or one or more batteries fitted to the vehicle, including e-bicycles. Hybrid vehicles using both fuel and electricity should not automatically be treated as EVs under this definition.

4. How should an EV fleet operator choose 5% or 18%?

The new entries provide a rate choice for qualifying EV passenger transportation and specified renting services. The lower 5% rate is not a free tax saving: it comes with restrictions on ITC. The 18% option may be commercially preferable for a fleet with significant vehicle-related, charging, repair, technology and other input costs, depending on eligibility.

Practical approach Prepare a monthly or annual comparison of output GST under both rates against eligible ITC under the 18% option. Select a consistent, documented rate treatment after reviewing the applicable notification and commercial contracts.

5. ITC restrictions under the 5% option

The 5% entries require that ITC on goods and services used in supplying the service is not taken, except the specific same-line input service credit described in the notification. This restriction is broader than merely blocking ITC on the EV itself.

Input cost incurred by EV operatorUnder 5% optionUnder 18% option
Purchase of EV / vehicle accessoriesDo not claim ITC under the concessional entry conditions; also examine Section 17(5).Claim only if Sections 16 and 17 permit it; passenger motor vehicle restrictions remain relevant.
Charging station invoices / charging serviceITC restricted, except where the exact same-line service rule applies (normally it does not for charging services).Potentially eligible subject to normal provisions.
Tyres, spare parts and repairsITC restricted under the 5% entry.Potentially eligible subject to normal provisions and Section 17(5).
GPS, booking software, payment gateway and fleet managementITC restricted under the 5% entry.Potentially eligible subject to normal provisions.
EV transport/renting service purchased from another providerMay qualify as same-line input service; credit capped at 5% of its value where the supplier charged more than 5%.Normal ITC rules.
Do not mix up two separate restrictions. The notification's 5% rate condition restricts ITC on inputs and input services used for that service. Section 17(5) independently restricts certain motor vehicle credits. Passing one test does not automatically satisfy the other.

6. Same-line input service – how the 5% ITC cap works

The notification allows credit of input services in the same line of business, such as passenger transportation by EV or renting of an EV with operator, but where the input service supplier charges tax above 5%, the recipient's credit is limited to the amount calculated at 5% of the value of that input service.

Official-style illustration

Operator B charges customer A ₹1,000 for an EV passenger trip. B hires an EV with operator from C for ₹800. C charges B 18% GST of ₹144. B chooses the 5% rate and charges A ₹50 GST.

ParticularsAmount
Input service value paid to C₹800
GST charged by C at 18%₹144
Maximum same-line ITC at 5% of ₹800₹40
Input GST not creditable under this cap₹104
Output GST charged by B at 5% of ₹1,000₹50

B cannot claim the full ₹144 merely because the supplier has charged it in a valid invoice. The special condition limits the credit to ₹40. The uncreditable amount becomes a cost, subject to accounting treatment.

Important: The 5% cap applies to qualifying same-line input service credit under the entry. It is not a general 5% cap on every ITC invoice, and it does not convert ordinary inputs such as tyres or electricity into eligible credit.

7. GST on public EV charging stations – a separate supply

Charging an EV at a public charging station is not automatically treated as exempt sale of electricity. The GST Council's official deliberations record that the charging station consumes electricity to charge the customer's battery and provides a charging service; it does not perform electricity transmission or distribution merely by charging the vehicle.

TransactionGST treatment to examine
Public charging station charges EV owner for charging session / kWh plus service feeCharging service is generally treated as taxable at 18% under the current classification approach recorded by the GST Council.
Electricity supplied by licensed distribution utility to charging stationSupply of electrical energy by the utility is exempt under the relevant exemption entry.
Charging station sells charger equipmentSupply of goods; apply the applicable HSN rate for charger/charging station equipment.
Charging station provides separate parking or other facilityDetermine whether it is an independent supply or part of a composite charging service; examine contract and billing structure.
Do not split the bill artificially. Charging session, electricity consumed, parking, membership and convenience fees must be evaluated using the actual contractual supply. A separate line item does not automatically make electricity an exempt independent supply.

8. Battery swapping, subscription plans and electricity recovery

Battery swapping and battery subscription arrangements need a fact-based classification. The contract may involve a supply of a battery as goods, a battery leasing service, a charging service, or a composite arrangement. The label used on an invoice is not decisive.

Business modelQuestions to analyse
Customer pays for battery charging at stationIs the supplier providing charging service, rather than sale of electricity? Identify SAC and rate.
Customer exchanges a depleted battery for a charged batteryIs there a transfer of ownership, exchange of goods, or a service arrangement? Identify who owns the battery and what the customer pays for.
Monthly battery subscriptionIs it a lease/rental of battery, access service or composite supply with maintenance and charging? Analyse contract terms and principal supply.
EV fleet operator recovers charging cost from corporate customerIf charging cost is part of consideration for the qualifying passenger transport/renting service, include it in taxable value and check the 5%/18% entry conditions.
Charging station bills fleet operator separatelySeparate inward charging-service invoice; ITC depends on the fleet operator's chosen rate and eligibility.

For a battery swap, retain battery ownership records, swap logs, serial numbers, charging records, subscription agreements and evidence of any refundable deposit. Do not assume the same GST treatment for every battery-as-a-service model.

9. Purchase of EVs and Section 17(5) – can the operator claim vehicle ITC?

Section 17(5)(a) generally blocks ITC on motor vehicles for transportation of persons having approved seating capacity of not more than thirteen persons (including the driver), subject to specified exceptions including further supply of such vehicles, transportation of passengers and imparting training on driving.

Use of passenger EVITC issue

EV used by a registered taxi operator to provide taxable passenger transportMay fall within the passenger transportation exception, subject to Section 16 and other conditions.
EV purchased by a dealer for further taxable saleFurther-supply exception may apply, subject to statutory conditions.

Goods carriage EVPassenger vehicle restriction may not apply in the same way; verify vehicle classification, registration and business use.
Important interaction: Even if a vehicle purchase is covered by a Section 17(5) exception, the operator choosing the 5% rate may still be unable to claim ITC under the separate rate-notification condition. The 18% option must also satisfy Section 17(5) and all normal ITC conditions.

11. EV goods transport and delivery fleets

The 2026 notification also inserts an entry for renting an electrically operated vehicle for transportation of goods where battery charging cost is included in the consideration. This is distinct from a general goods transportation service and from renting without an operator.

ServicePotential treatment under amended entry
Renting an electric goods vehicle with operator, charging included5% with prescribed ITC restrictions or 18% alternative.
EV logistics company transports goods using its own EV fleetAnalyse the exact transport service and applicable rate entry; do not automatically use the vehicle-renting entry.
Self-drive electric goods vehicle leaseNot covered by the with-operator entry; examine underlying goods/lease treatment.
EV delivery service supplied through an e-commerce operatorCheck separate delivery service and Section 9(5) rules, including the amended entries effective from 1 November 2026; do not assume the passenger transport entry applies.
Classification discipline: The new entry is specifically for renting an EV for goods transportation with the battery charging cost included. The outward supply must actually be that service; vehicle ownership alone does not decide the GST rate.

14. Invoice and documentation checklist

EV passenger transport / renting supplier

  • Identify whether the supply is passenger transport or renting with operator.
  • Record vehicle registration number, vehicle category and evidence that it is an electrically operated vehicle.
  • Contract must clearly state who bears battery charging cost and whether it is included in consideration.
  • Choose the 5% or 18% option based on the applicable entry and ITC model.
  • Invoice should describe the service correctly and show the applicable GST rate.
  • Maintain trip sheets, route logs, customer booking records, charging statements and fleet records.
  • Maintain a separate ITC register showing disallowed credits and capped same-line input service credit.

EV charging station

  • Maintain charger-wise and session-wise transaction records.
  • Separate electricity input cost from customer billing only where the contract and actual supply support the treatment.
  • Identify charging, parking, subscription, membership and other fees correctly.
  • Issue invoices with service description, taxable value, GST rate and place-of-supply details.
  • Reconcile charger software records, payment gateway settlements, invoices and GSTR-1.

15. Common mistakes and frequently asked questions

  • Applying 5% GST to a public EV charging station merely because EV passenger transport gets a 5% option.
  • Claiming full ITC on tyres, repairs, electricity and software while using the 5% concessional entry.
  • Claiming full 18% input GST on subcontracted same-line EV transport when the 5% cap applies.
  • Applying the new 5% entry to self-drive EV leasing without an operator.
  • Assuming all hybrid vehicles qualify as electrically operated vehicles.
  • Failing to include charging cost in the consideration where the entry requires it.
  • Ignoring Section 17(5) when claiming ITC on passenger vehicles.

1. From which date does the new EV passenger transport GST option apply?

From 1 November 2026 under Notification No. 06/2026-Central Tax (Rate) and Notification No. 06/2026-Integrated Tax (Rate), dated 27 October 2026.

2. Is EV passenger transport taxable at 5% or 18%?

For qualifying passenger transport by EV where battery charging cost is included, the amended entry provides a 5% option with ITC restrictions or an 18% alternative.

3. Can an EV taxi operator claim ITC on repairs and tyres under the 5% option?

The 5% entry generally restricts ITC on goods and services used in providing the service, except the specified same-line input service credit. The separate Section 17(5) restrictions must also be checked.

4. Can an operator claim ITC on subcontracted EV trips?

Potentially, as same-line input service credit. If the supplier charges more than 5%, the credit is capped at 5% of the value of that input service when the outward service is taxed at the concessional rate.

5. Is EV charging station service exempt because electricity is exempt?

No automatic exemption. The GST Council's official deliberations treat EV battery charging at a public charging station as a service, not a further supply or distribution of electricity. The current treatment recorded is 18%.

6. Does sale of an EV charger attract the same GST rate as charging service?

No. Sale of a charger is supply of goods and must be classified under the applicable HSN goods rate entry. Charging a customer's vehicle is a service.

7. Does the 5% option apply to self-drive EV rental?

No, the new renting entry requires an electrically operated vehicle with an operator and charging cost included. Self-drive leasing is treated separately.

8. Can an EV goods vehicle rental operator use the new option?

The notification includes renting of an electrically operated vehicle for transportation of goods where battery charging cost is included, with 5% restricted ITC or 18% alternative.

9. Can a company claim ITC on EV cars used for employee transport?

Examine Section 17(5), the actual passenger transport/renting arrangement and the chosen output-rate option. The 5% concessional entry can independently restrict ITC even where a Section 17(5) exception may otherwise be relevant.

10. Is a hybrid vehicle an electrically operated vehicle?

Not automatically. The notification defines an electrically operated vehicle as one that runs solely on electrical energy from an external source or battery. A vehicle that also uses petrol or diesel may not meet this definition.

11. Can the supplier charge 5% on the trip and 18% on charging as separate lines?

Do not split the consideration artificially. Determine the actual supply and whether charging cost is included in the passenger transport consideration. A separately contracted charging service supplied by another charging station is a distinct inward supply.

12. Is 18% always better because ITC is available?

No. Compare output tax, eligible ITC, contract pricing, customer profile and cash flow. ITC under the 18% option remains subject to Sections 16 and 17.

Monthly close checklist

  1. Identify each revenue stream: passenger transport, renting with operator, self-drive lease, goods transport, charging or equipment sale.
  2. Verify EV definition and whether battery charging cost is included in the service consideration.
  3. Review the 5% versus 18% selection and document the rate basis.
  4. Calculate restricted ITC and same-line input service cap separately.
  5. Review vehicle ITC under Section 17(5) separately from rate-notification restrictions.
  6. Reconcile trip logs, charging sessions, invoices, payment gateway and GSTR-1.
  7. Reconcile output tax and eligible ITC with GSTR-3B.

Practical takeaway

Do not apply one GST rate to the entire EV ecosystem. Passenger transport and renting of EVs with operators may choose between 5% with restricted ITC and 18% under the amended entries from 1 November 2026, where charging cost is included. Public charging station service is separate and remains subject to its own classification and rate. Maintain separate revenue and ITC ledgers for each activity.
