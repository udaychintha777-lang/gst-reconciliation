Section 128 of the CGST Act, 2017 – Power to Waive Penalty or Fee or Both

A detailed, public-friendly explanation of Section 128: when the Government can waive GST penalties or late fee, why a notification is required, which penalties and late fee are covered, the role of GST Council recommendations, and how Section 128 differs from the special waiver mechanism under Section 128A.

CGST Act Section 128Penalty WaiverLate Fee WaiverGST NotificationsPractical Guide
At a glance

What does Section 128 mean?

In simple words: Section 128 gives the Government the power, through a notification, to waive fully or partly certain penalties and the late fee under Section 47 for specified classes of taxpayers and specified mitigating circumstances, based on the recommendations of the GST Council.

This means a taxpayer cannot normally say, “Section 128 allows me to cancel my penalty.” Section 128 is an enabling power. The actual waiver becomes available when the Government issues the relevant notification and the taxpayer falls within its conditions.

Key idea: Section 128 is not a blanket waiver provision. It allows targeted relief for specified taxpayers and circumstances. Always check the exact notification, effective period, conditions, exclusions and procedural requirements before claiming a waiver.

Law explained

1. Statutory text of Section 128

Section 128 – Power to waive penalty or fee or both.

The Government may, by notification, waive in part or full, any penalty referred to in section 122 or section 123 or section 125 or any late fee referred to in section 47 for such class of taxpayers and under such mitigating circumstances as may be specified therein on the recommendations of the Council.

The wording establishes several important conditions: the Government acts by notification; the waiver may be partial or complete; the provision covers penalties referred to in Sections 122, 123 and 125 and late fee referred to in Section 47; and the notification specifies the class of taxpayers and mitigating circumstances. The power is exercised on the recommendations of the GST Council. The current wording is reproduced in recent GST bare-law compilations.

2. Section 128 explained in very simple language

Suppose the Government wants to give relief to a particular group of taxpayers because there was a genuine difficulty, special circumstance, administrative issue, natural calamity, system-related problem or another circumstance considered appropriate under the relevant notification.

Section 128 provides the legal power to create that relief through a notification. The notification can specify who gets the benefit, what penalty or late fee is covered, how much is waived, for what period, and what conditions must be satisfied.

Therefore, Section 128 works more like a framework for targeted waiver rather than an automatic taxpayer right available whenever a penalty or late fee exists.

Section 128 gives the power

The Act authorises the Government to issue a notification granting specified waiver.

Notification gives the actual relief

The taxpayer must check the particular notification to know whether the waiver applies.

GST Council recommends

The statutory wording links the Government's notification power to recommendations of the GST Council.

Conditions matter

A waiver may apply only to a specified class, period, circumstance, return or compliance situation and may have conditions.

Important limitation: Section 128 does not say that every penalty or every GST fee can be waived under this section. Its text specifically refers to penalties under Sections 122, 123 and 125 and late fee under Section 47.

4. How does a Section 128 waiver actually work?

  1. A circumstance or compliance issue is identified. There may be a reason why targeted relief is considered appropriate.
  2. The GST Council considers/recommends relief. Section 128 expressly refers to recommendations of the Council.
  3. The Government issues a notification. This is essential because Section 128 says the waiver is by notification.
  4. The notification defines the scope. It may identify taxpayers, periods, forms, amounts, dates, circumstances and conditions.
  5. The taxpayer checks eligibility. The taxpayer must compare its facts against every condition.
  6. The waiver is applied according to the notified mechanism. Depending on the notification, the relief may be automatic through the system or may require a specified compliance/payment/action.
  7. Records should be retained. Keep the notification, relevant return/ledger details and evidence of satisfying conditions.

The important lesson is that Section 128 alone is not the calculation sheet. The notification made under it is what tells the taxpayer how the particular waiver operates.

5. What are “mitigating circumstances”?

The Act deliberately uses the expression “such mitigating circumstances as may be specified therein.” This means the Government's notification determines the circumstances for the particular waiver.

Therefore, a taxpayer should not assume that every personal or business difficulty automatically qualifies. The relevant notification must be checked.

Examples of circumstances that may be relevant to a notified waiver

  • A specified category of taxpayers facing a defined compliance difficulty.
  • A specified period during which a particular compliance requirement was affected.
  • A particular class of return or statutory filing covered by the notification.
  • Special circumstances recognised through Government/GST Council action.
  • Any specific condition expressly stated in the notification.
Do not rely on a generic “reasonable cause” argument alone. Section 128 is notification-based. If a notification says a particular class or circumstance qualifies, the taxpayer should demonstrate that it falls within that exact category.

6. Why is the notification so important?

The difference between “Section 128 exists” and “I qualify for a waiver” is the notification.

QuestionWhat to check in the notification
Who?Whether the taxpayer class, registration type, turnover category or other specified class is covered.
What?Whether the particular penalty or late fee is covered.
Which period?The relevant tax period, financial year, filing period or date range.
How much?Whether the waiver is full, partial, capped or subject to a specified amount.
What conditions?Whether filing, payment, application, correction or another action is required.
By when?Any deadline for satisfying the waiver conditions.
Exclusions?Taxpayers, situations, returns or amounts specifically excluded.

For this reason, a professional GST working paper should record the notification number/date and the exact clause under which the taxpayer claims relief.

7. Late fee under Section 47 – how Section 128 connects to it

Section 47 deals with late fee for delay in furnishing specified returns/details under the CGST framework. Section 128 specifically gives the Government power to waive, in part or full, any late fee referred to in Section 47, for the class of taxpayers and mitigating circumstances specified in the notification.

This is different from simply saying that the taxpayer was late. A late fee may arise under the law, while a subsequent notification under Section 128 may provide relief if the taxpayer satisfies the notified conditions.

Simple example: A taxpayer has a late fee liability for a specified filing. If the Government subsequently issues a Section 128 notification covering that taxpayer class, filing period and circumstances, the taxpayer may receive the notified waiver. If one of those conditions is not satisfied, the waiver cannot simply be assumed.

8. Penalties under Sections 122, 123 and 125

Section 122

Section 122 contains penalties for specified offences/contraventions. Section 128 can be used by the Government to waive, partly or fully, a penalty referred to in Section 122 where the relevant notification covers the case.

Section 123

Section 123 concerns the penalty for failure to furnish an information return under the statutory framework. Section 128 expressly includes this section in the list of penalties that can be waived by notification.

Section 125

Section 125 is the general penalty provision. It applies where a person contravenes the CGST Act or Rules and no separate penalty is provided, with the penalty capable of extending to ₹25,000. Section 128 expressly permits waiver of a penalty referred to in Section 125.

Do not confuse Section 128 with Section 126. Section 126 lays down general disciplines related to penalty. Section 128 gives the Government a notification-based waiver power for specified penalties and late fee.

9. Section 128 does not erase the underlying law automatically

A waiver notification does not mean that the underlying GST provision never existed or that every compliance failure is permanently forgiven.

Usually, the legal effect is narrower: a specified penalty or late fee is waived to the extent and under the conditions stated in the notification.

For example, if a notification waives a late fee for a specified filing period subject to filing by a specified date, it does not necessarily waive the tax payable, interest payable, or other unrelated statutory consequences.

Tax

Whether tax is payable is a separate question and depends on the relevant charging/demand provisions.

Interest

A penalty/late-fee waiver does not automatically mean interest is waived unless the relevant notification or another statutory provision says so.

Penalty

Only the penalty covered by the applicable waiver provision/notification receives the specified relief.

Late fee

Section 128 can provide notification-based waiver of late fee referred to in Section 47.

10. Section 128 vs Section 128A – very important distinction

Sections 128 and 128A both concern relief from penalties/fees or interest-related consequences, but they are not interchangeable.

PointSection 128Section 128A
Basic natureGeneral enabling power for specified waiver by Government notification.Specific statutory waiver mechanism for interest or penalty, subject to its own conditions, relating to specified Section 73 demands and tax periods.
What is covered?Specified penalties under Sections 122, 123, 125 and late fee under Section 47.Interest or penalty or both relating to qualifying demands raised under Section 73 for the specified tax periods and subject to the statutory conditions.
MechanismGovernment notification based on GST Council recommendation.Detailed statutory conditions and associated rules/notifications/circulars govern eligibility and implementation.
Nature of reliefMay be partial or full as specified.Relief operates under the special Section 128A framework.

Section 128A was inserted with effect from 1 November 2024 as a separate provision concerning waiver of interest or penalty or both relating to qualifying Section 73 demands for specified tax periods. The GST Council's materials describe the original recommendation and scope, while CBIC subsequently issued implementation clarifications.

Practical warning: Do not use a Section 128A waiver checklist to claim a Section 128 waiver, or vice versa. First identify the legal provision under which the relief is being claimed.

11. Practical examples in layman language

Example 1 – Late filing covered by a waiver notification

A taxpayer files a specified return late and late fee becomes payable. Later, the Government issues a Section 128 notification covering that taxpayer class and filing period, subject to specified conditions. If the taxpayer satisfies those conditions, the specified late fee can be waived to the extent stated in the notification.

Example 2 – Partial waiver

A notification provides for partial waiver rather than complete waiver. The taxpayer should not treat the entire late fee as zero. The working should calculate the amount remaining after applying the exact notified relief.

Example 3 – Full waiver

A notification specifies full waiver for a defined class of taxpayers under defined circumstances. A taxpayer within that class may receive full relief for the covered penalty/late fee, subject to the conditions and period specified.

Example 4 – Taxpayer outside the notified class

A waiver notification applies only to a particular category of taxpayers. Another taxpayer reads the notification and assumes it applies to everyone. That assumption is incorrect. The taxpayer must satisfy the class condition.

Example 5 – Correct period, wrong condition

A taxpayer's filing period is covered, but the notification requires filing by a specified date and the taxpayer misses that date. The taxpayer cannot ignore the condition merely because the period is covered.

Example 6 – Penalty under a section not listed in Section 128

A taxpayer faces a penalty under a provision other than Sections 122, 123 or 125. Section 128's text does not automatically extend its waiver power to every other penalty provision. The taxpayer must identify the specific legal basis for any available relief.

Example 7 – Late fee and tax are different

A waiver notification removes a specified late fee. That does not by itself cancel the tax liability in the return. The taxpayer must separately comply with tax payment requirements.

Example 8 – Interest and late fee are different

A taxpayer sees a late-fee waiver and assumes interest is also waived. That conclusion is unsafe unless the applicable notification or provision expressly provides interest relief.

Example 9 – Section 125 penalty

A taxpayer has a penalty under Section 125. If a Section 128 notification specifically covers that Section 125 penalty and the taxpayer meets the notified conditions, the specified penalty may be waived.

Example 10 – Section 128A is a different route

A taxpayer has a qualifying old Section 73 demand and is considering the special waiver mechanism under Section 128A. The taxpayer should test Section 128A's separate conditions rather than treating it as an ordinary Section 128 notification.

Example 11 – Multiple GST registrations

A company has GST registrations in several States. A waiver notification may contain conditions that have to be tested registration-wise, return-wise or taxpayer-class-wise. The finance team should not assume that relief for one GSTIN automatically means every GSTIN is covered.

Example 12 – Audit working paper

An auditor or finance manager sees that a late fee has been reduced after a waiver. The working paper should retain the relevant notification and the calculation showing original amount, waiver amount and balance, instead of merely recording “waived under Section 128.”

Example 13 – Portal amount still visible

A taxpayer sees an amount on the GST portal and assumes that no waiver exists. Conversely, a taxpayer sees an announcement and assumes the portal balance is automatically corrected. The correct approach is to read the operative notification and follow the specified implementation procedure.

Example 14 – Old notification

A taxpayer finds an internet article discussing a historical late-fee waiver. That article may relate to an expired or period-specific notification. Always verify the actual notification and its effective dates before applying it to a current filing.

Example 15 – Waiver does not mean “no compliance required”

A notification may waive a penalty or fee subject to filing or another action. Relief and compliance can therefore be linked. The taxpayer should complete the underlying compliance exactly as required.

12. Practical workflow for a GST department / finance team

  1. Identify the liability. Determine whether it is tax, interest, penalty or late fee.
  2. Identify the statutory section. For Section 128, check whether the penalty is referred to in Section 122, 123 or 125, or whether the amount is late fee referred to in Section 47.
  3. Search the operative notification. Do not rely only on a secondary article or social-media summary.
  4. Check the issue date and effective period. A historical waiver may not apply to a later period.
  5. Check taxpayer class. Confirm the GSTIN/taxpayer falls into the category specified.
  6. Check mitigating circumstances/conditions. Match every condition against actual records.
  7. Calculate relief. Show original amount, waived amount and remaining amount.
  8. Complete required compliance. File return, pay tax or perform another action if required by the notification.
  9. Reconcile the portal. Verify the electronic liability records after the relevant relief is implemented.
  10. Retain evidence. Keep notification, calculation and supporting documents.

14. Common mistakes taxpayers make

Mistake 1: “Section 128 automatically waives all GST penalties.”

No. The section is limited to the categories expressly referred to and operates through notifications specifying taxpayers and circumstances.

Mistake 2: “Any late fee can be waived whenever I ask.”

No. Relief depends on the Government's notification and the conditions specified under it.

Mistake 3: “A waiver of late fee also waives interest.”

Not automatically. Interest is a separate liability unless the applicable provision/notification provides otherwise.

Mistake 4: “Section 128 and 128A are the same.”

No. Section 128 is a general notification-based waiver power for specified penalties/late fee; Section 128A is a separate special waiver provision relating to qualifying Section 73 demands for specified tax periods.

Mistake 5: “An internet article is enough evidence.”

Use the operative Government notification and relevant official clarification as the primary reference for eligibility.

Mistake 6: “If the notification covers the period, I automatically qualify.”

Not necessarily. Taxpayer class, filing date, payment condition and other requirements may also apply.

Mistake 7: “The waiver removes the need to file the return.”

Usually the underlying filing obligation remains unless the relevant law or notification provides otherwise.

Mistake 8: “Section 128 is a discretionary power of the officer.”

The text gives the waiver power to the Government by notification, on recommendations of the Council. It is not simply an individual officer deciding to waive a taxpayer's penalty under Section 128.

16. Management-level GST compliance checklist

  • Maintain a current register of penalty and late-fee liabilities.
  • Separate tax, interest, penalty and late fee in MIS reporting.
  • When a waiver is announced, identify the exact notification before changing the liability working.
  • Record the notification number, date and effective period.
  • Test eligibility GSTIN-wise where necessary.
  • Maintain a before-and-after waiver calculation.
  • Do not write off a liability in books merely because a waiver is expected; confirm the legal and system position.
  • Retain evidence of all conditions satisfied.
  • Review whether the relief is under Section 128 or the separate Section 128A mechanism.
  • Reconcile the GST portal after implementation.

17. Frequently Asked Questions about Section 128

What is Section 128 of the CGST Act?

It gives the Government power, by notification, to waive partly or fully specified penalties under Sections 122, 123 and 125 and late fee referred to in Section 47, for specified classes of taxpayers and mitigating circumstances, on the recommendations of the GST Council.

Who can waive a GST penalty under Section 128?

The Government may issue the waiver by notification. The section links this power to recommendations of the GST Council.

Can Section 128 waive a penalty fully?

Yes. The section permits waiver “in part or full,” subject to the notification.

Can Section 128 waive late fee?

Yes. It specifically refers to late fee under Section 47.

Can Section 128 waive interest?

Section 128 itself refers to specified penalties and Section 47 late fee, not a general waiver of interest. Section 128A is a separate provision dealing with specified interest/penalty relief in qualifying Section 73 demand cases.

Does Section 128 automatically waive Section 125 penalty?

No. A notification must provide the relevant waiver and the taxpayer must satisfy its conditions.

Does Section 128 cover every penalty under the CGST Act?

No. The text specifically refers to penalties under Sections 122, 123 and 125.

What does “mitigating circumstances” mean?

The particular Government notification specifies the circumstances in which the waiver is available. A taxpayer should therefore read the operative notification rather than applying a general definition.

Is a Section 128 waiver automatic?

Not merely because Section 128 exists. The relevant notification and its conditions determine how the relief applies.

What is the difference between Section 128 and Section 128A?

Section 128 is a general notification-based waiver power for specified penalties and Section 47 late fee. Section 128A is a separate special waiver mechanism for qualifying Section 73 demands for specified tax periods.

Does a late-fee waiver mean the return need not be filed?

No. A waiver of late fee does not by itself remove the underlying return-filing obligation.

Does penalty waiver mean tax is waived?

No. A penalty waiver and tax liability are separate matters unless another specific provision provides tax relief.

What should a company maintain as proof of waiver?

Keep the relevant notification, taxpayer eligibility working, original liability calculation, waiver calculation, filing/payment proof and portal records.

Can a historical waiver be applied to a current return?

Only if the notification's period and conditions cover the current return. Historical waiver announcements should not be assumed to remain available.

Where should I verify a GST waiver notification?

Use official Government/CBIC/GST Council sources and the actual notification rather than relying solely on third-party summaries.

19. Quick revision – Section 128 in 60 seconds

  • Purpose: Power to waive specified penalty or fee.
  • Who acts: Government, by notification.
  • GST Council: Waiver is based on recommendations of the Council.
  • Penalties covered: Penalties referred to in Sections 122, 123 and 125.
  • Late fee covered: Late fee referred to in Section 47.
  • Extent: Waiver may be partial or full.
  • Eligibility: Only the class of taxpayers and mitigating circumstances specified in the notification.
  • Important: Section 128 is not a blanket waiver of every GST penalty, interest or tax.
  • Do not confuse: Section 128A is a separate special waiver provision for qualifying Section 73 demands.
