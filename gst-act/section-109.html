1. Why Section 109 matters

Section 109 establishes the institutional framework for the Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal, commonly known as GSTAT. The Tribunal is the next major appellate forum in the GST dispute-resolution structure after the first appellate stage.

For a finance or tax team, this matters because a Section 107 appeal is not necessarily the end of a dispute. A taxpayer may need to take the matter further against an order covered by Section 112. The Tribunal's constitution and bench structure under Section 109 determine the forum in which those disputes are heard.

Practical message: Do not treat “Tribunal” as a single undifferentiated office. Section 109 creates different types of Benches, and the correct jurisdiction and bench configuration matter when planning an appeal.

2. Section 109 in simple language

Section 109 provides for constitution of the GST Appellate Tribunal and its Benches. The statutory structure includes the National Bench, Regional Benches, State Bench and Area Benches, with different member compositions and jurisdictional roles.

StructureBasic role
National BenchSituated at New Delhi and constituted with the President, one Technical Member (Centre) and one Technical Member (State).
Regional BenchesConstituted as required; the statutory composition includes a Judicial Member, one Technical Member (Centre) and one Technical Member (State).
State BenchFunctions within the State-level Tribunal structure as provided by the Act and applicable notifications/rules.
Area BenchesBenches associated with the State Bench and serving the territorial structure prescribed under the GST framework.

The statutory text provides that the Tribunal hears appeals against orders passed by the Appellate Authority or Revisional Authority. citeturn0search24

3. What is GSTAT?

GSTAT is the Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal. It is the specialist appellate forum for GST disputes at the Tribunal level.

The Tribunal is important because GST disputes can involve highly technical issues such as input tax credit, classification, valuation, place of supply, exemptions, demand, interest and penalties. The statutory Tribunal structure is designed to combine judicial expertise with technical expertise.

Simple hierarchy: Adjudicating Authority → First Appellate Authority / Revision → GST Appellate Tribunal → High Court (where statutory conditions are met) → Supreme Court in appropriate cases.

The exact appellate route depends on the type of order and the provisions applicable to the particular dispute.

4. Understanding the Tribunal structure

National BenchLocated at New Delhi and headed by the President.
Regional BenchConstituted as required with judicial and technical members.
State BenchState-level Tribunal structure.
Area BenchTerritorial bench serving the prescribed area.

The purpose of multiple Benches is practical: GST disputes arise across India, and the Tribunal needs a structure capable of dealing with taxpayers within the relevant territorial and statutory framework.

5. National Bench

Section 109(3) provides for the National Bench at New Delhi. It is presided over by the President and consists of one Technical Member (Centre) and one Technical Member (State).

The National Bench should not be confused with the Regional or State-level Benches. The actual jurisdiction and allocation of cases must be checked against the Act, notifications and the applicable Tribunal framework for the matter in question.

Why this matters to businesses

  • A national-level business may have disputes involving multiple registrations and States.
  • Jurisdiction should be confirmed before preparing filing strategy.
  • Tax counsel should use the applicable Tribunal rules and current notifications rather than relying on an old understanding of bench location.

6. Regional Benches

Section 109 provides for Regional Benches as required. The statutory composition specified in sub-section (4) includes a Judicial Member, one Technical Member (Centre) and one Technical Member (State).

Regional Benches are particularly relevant for businesses with disputes that proceed beyond the first appellate stage and fall within the jurisdiction allocated to the relevant Bench.

Member typeWhy it is relevant
Judicial MemberProvides judicial expertise in interpretation, procedure and adjudication.
Technical Member (Centre)Provides central tax administration expertise.
Technical Member (State)Provides State tax administration expertise.

7. State Bench

Section 109 establishes the State Bench within the Tribunal structure. The Act provides for the State Bench and Area Benches, with their jurisdiction and functioning governed by the statutory framework and applicable notifications.

For a taxpayer, the practical point is that “which State?” and “which Bench?” should be determined from the current Tribunal jurisdiction rather than assumed from the taxpayer's head office alone.

Example

A company has GST registrations in Telangana, Maharashtra and Karnataka. A dispute may relate to one particular registration and the order passed by the relevant authority. The tax team should identify the correct appellate jurisdiction for that specific matter rather than automatically filing based on the company's corporate headquarters.

8. Area Benches

Area Benches operate within the State-level structure to provide territorial access to the Tribunal. The exact location and jurisdiction of an Area Bench should be verified from the current notifications and Tribunal arrangements applicable at the time of filing.

Do not rely on outdated bench lists. Tribunal constitution and functioning have developed over time. Always verify the currently notified Bench and filing instructions before submitting an appeal.

9. Jurisdiction and the appeal route

Section 109 establishes the Tribunal, while Section 112 contains the substantive provision governing appeals to the Tribunal. Therefore, when a taxpayer receives a Section 107 or Section 108 order, the next step is not simply “file before GSTAT”. The team should first determine whether Section 112 permits an appeal and identify the appropriate Bench.

StepQuestion
1What order has been received?
2Was it passed under Section 107 or Section 108?
3Is the order appealable under Section 112?
4What is the limitation period?
5What pre-deposit/fee requirements apply?
6Which Tribunal Bench has jurisdiction?
7What documents and evidence are required?

10. Why the composition of Members matters

The Tribunal structure deliberately includes judicial and technical expertise. GST disputes frequently involve both legal interpretation and technical tax administration questions.

For example, an ITC dispute may require analysis of statutory conditions, invoice records, supplier data, accounting entries and departmental verification. A classification dispute may require understanding the product's technical characteristics as well as tariff/legal principles.

For finance professionals, this is a reminder that Tribunal preparation should be evidence-heavy. A legal argument becomes much stronger when the underlying commercial and accounting facts are clearly demonstrated.

12. Preparing a Tribunal case file

A GSTAT matter should have a dedicated digital and physical case file.

  1. Impugned appellate/revisional order.
  2. Communication proof and limitation calculation.
  3. Previous show-cause notice and reply.
  4. Original adjudication order.
  5. Section 107 appeal and order, where applicable.
  6. Section 108 revision order, where applicable.
  7. Statement of facts and grounds proposed for Tribunal appeal.
  8. Contracts, invoices and technical evidence.
  9. ITC/GSTR-2B reconciliations where relevant.
  10. Tax, interest, penalty and pre-deposit calculations.
  11. Hearing submissions and final order.
Best practice: Create an “issue matrix” showing each disputed point, the department's finding, taxpayer's position, evidence, legal provision, financial amount and status.

13. Practical examples

Example 1 — Large ITC dispute

A company loses a first appeal on ITC relating to hundreds of invoices. The Tribunal strategy should not be based on a general statement that “all invoices are genuine”.

Better approach: Build an invoice-level evidence matrix showing supplier GSTIN, invoice number/date, taxable value, tax, GSTR-2B availability, receipt of goods/services, accounting entry, payment evidence and the exact disputed condition.

Example 2 — Classification dispute

A manufacturer disputes the tax classification adopted by the department.

Better approach: Combine legal submissions with product literature, technical specifications, manufacturing process, sample invoices and the commercial use of the product.

Example 3 — Multiple GST registrations

A group has several GSTINs and similar disputes arise in different States.

Better approach: Maintain a GSTIN-wise litigation register. Do not assume that the same factual evidence or appellate jurisdiction applies identically to every registration.

Example 4 — Project-based dispute

An infrastructure company faces a dispute involving one major project.

Better approach: Prepare a project evidence file instead of relying only on corporate-level accounting summaries.

16. Common mistakes

  1. Assuming Section 109 itself creates the right of appeal. The appeal mechanism is principally dealt with in Section 112.
  2. Using an old Bench location. Verify current jurisdiction and Tribunal arrangements.
  3. Ignoring limitation. Tribunal deadlines should be calculated immediately after receipt of the appealable order.
  4. Preparing only legal arguments. Technical and documentary evidence can be critical.
  5. Failing to reconcile the demand. The Tribunal file should show the exact disputed amount.
  6. Not preserving lower-authority records. The Tribunal case should contain the procedural history and earlier findings.
  7. Applying one GSTIN's case strategy to another GSTIN. Analyse each order and factual matrix separately.

19. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GST Appellate Tribunal?

GSTAT is the specialist appellate tribunal established under the GST framework for hearing appeals against specified orders of the Appellate Authority and Revisional Authority.

What does Section 109 mainly deal with?

It deals with constitution of the Appellate Tribunal and its Benches, including the National Bench, Regional Benches, State Bench and Area Benches.

Does Section 109 itself provide the time limit for filing a Tribunal appeal?

No. Section 109 primarily establishes the Tribunal and Bench structure. The substantive appeal route and limitation are dealt with principally under Section 112 and the applicable rules.

Why are there technical members?

GST disputes can involve detailed tax administration and technical issues as well as legal questions. Section 109 provides a structure combining judicial and technical expertise.

Can a company choose any GSTAT Bench it wants?

No. The correct Bench depends on the statutory jurisdiction and current notifications/Tribunal arrangements. Verify the applicable jurisdiction before filing.

Should finance teams be involved in a Tribunal appeal?

Yes. Finance teams often control the ledgers, invoices, reconciliations, tax payments, pre-deposit calculations and accounting impact that form an important part of the evidence and financial strategy.

Are old GSTAT Bench lists reliable?

Not necessarily. Tribunal constitution and operational arrangements can change. Always verify the current notified Bench, jurisdiction and filing procedure before filing.

21. Key takeaways

  • Section 109 establishes the Goods and Services Tax Appellate Tribunal and its Benches.
  • The structure includes the National Bench, Regional Benches, State Bench and Area Benches.
  • The National Bench is situated at New Delhi and has the statutory composition specified in Section 109.
  • Regional Benches have judicial and technical member representation as provided by the section.
  • Section 109 establishes the forum; Section 112 deals with the substantive appeal to the Tribunal.
  • Businesses should verify current Bench jurisdiction and filing arrangements rather than relying on old information.
  • Finance and tax teams should prepare a complete, issue-wise Tribunal file linking legal grounds to contracts, invoices, reconciliations and accounting records.
