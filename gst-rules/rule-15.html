CGST Rule 15 – Extension of Registration for Casual & Non-Resident Taxable Persons

A practical guide to extending the GST registration period of a registered casual taxable person or non-resident taxable person, including FORM GST REG-11, advance payment, timing, validity and compliance controls.

1. What Current Rule 15 Means

Rule 15 provides the procedure for extending the period of registration of a registered casual taxable person (CTP) or non-resident taxable person (NRTP). It becomes relevant when the business needs to continue taxable activities beyond the validity period originally granted in its registration.

The extension is not automatic. The registered person must apply electronically in the prescribed form FORM GST REG-11 before the existing registration expires and make the payment required under Section 27(2).

Practical takeaway: If a temporary GST registration is approaching expiry and the business still has taxable supplies to make, do not wait until the last day. Prepare the extension application and additional estimated tax payment in advance.

2. Who Does Rule 15 Apply To?

Rule 15 specifically covers two special categories:

  • Casual Taxable Person (CTP): a person who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services in a State or Union territory where the person has no fixed place of business.
  • Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP): a person who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services, whether as principal or agent or in another capacity, but has no fixed place of business or residence in India.

These taxpayers receive a registration for a limited period rather than the ordinary continuing registration applicable to most resident businesses.

3. Rule 15(1) – Extension Application

Where a registered CTP or NRTP wants to extend the period of registration stated in the original application, the person must submit an application in FORM GST REG-11 electronically through the common portal.

The application may be submitted directly or through a notified Facilitation Centre. The critical timing condition is that the application must be made before the end of the validity of the registration already granted.

Do not treat extension as post-expiry regularisation. Rule 15 is designed for an extension requested while the special registration is still valid. If the registration has already expired, the business should not continue taxable supplies on the assumption that a late REG-11 filing automatically revives the registration.

4. FORM GST REG-11 – What the Application Captures

FORM GST REG-11 is specifically designed for extension of the registration period of a casual or non-resident taxable person. The form captures information such as:

  • GSTIN and legal/trade name;
  • address;
  • original registration validity period;
  • period for which extension is requested;
  • estimated turnover for the extended period;
  • estimated net tax liability for the extended period; and
  • payment details for the advance tax amount.

The application also contains a declaration by the authorised signatory. The prescribed form therefore links the requested extension directly with the additional estimated tax liability for the extended period.

5. Rule 15(2) – Payment Before Acknowledgement

Rule 15(2) provides that the extension application is acknowledged only on payment of the amount specified in Section 27(2) of the CGST Act.

Section 27(2) requires a CTP or NRTP seeking registration to make an advance deposit of tax equivalent to the estimated tax liability for the period for which registration is sought. Where an extension is requested, the additional estimated tax liability for the extended period must be deposited.

Important: This is an advance tax deposit mechanism. It is not the same thing as a separate registration fee. The deposited amount is credited to the taxpayer's electronic cash ledger and is used in accordance with the GST payment provisions.

6. How to Calculate the Additional Advance Tax

The finance team should estimate the taxable supplies expected during the extension period and calculate the expected GST liability using the applicable rates and eligible tax treatment.

Estimated additional advance tax = Estimated GST liability for the requested extension period

Example: A casual taxable person has registration valid until 31 October. The business expects another ₹40 lakh of taxable supplies during November and estimates GST of ₹7.20 lakh for that period. The extension application should be supported by the required additional advance tax deposit based on the estimated liability.

The estimate should be reasonable and supported by sales orders, contracts, project schedules, expected invoices and other business records.

Keep the registration certificate, original REG-01/REG-09 details, contracts, estimated turnover workings and payment evidence together in the compliance file.

8. Practical Example – Casual Taxable Person

A Hyderabad-based manufacturer participates in a trade exhibition in Bengaluru and obtains a casual registration for taxable supplies connected with the temporary activity. The original registration is valid until 30 November.

The exhibition is extended and the business expects additional taxable sales in December. Since the existing registration is still valid, the business should estimate the December liability, make the required additional advance tax payment and submit REG-11 before 30 November.

Management lesson: The extension should be planned as soon as the commercial activity is extended. Waiting until after expiry creates avoidable compliance risk.

9. Practical Example – Non-Resident Taxable Person

A foreign business enters India temporarily to make taxable supplies and obtains NRTP registration. Its original registration period is approaching expiry, but the Indian project requires additional taxable supplies for another period.

The foreign business should review the expected Indian supplies and tax liability, arrange the additional advance tax deposit and submit REG-11 before the existing registration validity ends.

The Indian authorised signatory and finance team should maintain evidence of the extension filing and payment because the foreign head office may not directly manage the Indian GST portal process.

11. What Happens If the Registration Expires?

A CTP or NRTP should not continue taxable supplies after the registration validity expires merely because the business intends to seek an extension.

The finance team should treat the expiry date as a hard compliance deadline and verify that:

  • sales orders and billing schedules are reviewed;
  • GST invoices are not generated after expiry without valid registration;
  • the portal shows the correct extended validity before continuing activity;
  • tax collections and returns are aligned with the valid registration period; and
  • the contractual team knows whether further supplies can legally be made.
Do not confuse commercial continuation with GST registration continuation. A project, exhibition, event or contract may continue commercially, but the special GST registration must remain valid for taxable supplies covered by it.

12. Finance & ERP Controls

Businesses using ERP, billing software or project accounting systems should build the Rule 15 deadline into their compliance calendar.

  • Store the GST registration expiry date as a master-data field.
  • Create alerts 15–30 days before expiry.
  • Link the extension request to the relevant project/event/customer.
  • Maintain the REG-11 ARN and payment reference.
  • Block or flag invoices after registration expiry until the extension is verified.
  • Reconcile the estimated tax deposited with the eventual GST liability.

For temporary construction, exhibition, event, project or foreign-supplier activities, this control is particularly important because the commercial activity may outlast the original registration period.

13. Common Mistakes Under Rule 15

  1. Applying for extension after the registration has already expired.
  2. Filing REG-11 without arranging the required advance tax payment.
  3. Using the original estimated tax instead of recalculating liability for the extension period.
  4. Failing to monitor the registration expiry date in the ERP/compliance calendar.
  5. Continuing invoicing while the extension status is still unresolved.
  6. Not retaining payment evidence and ARN.
  7. Assuming Rule 15 is a general extension facility for ordinary GST registrations.

14. GST Audit / Departmental Notice Relevance

Rule 15 can become important during departmental verification where taxable supplies are found after the validity period of a CTP/NRTP registration.

An officer may examine the registration certificate, original validity, REG-11 filing, advance tax payment, invoices, returns and electronic cash ledger. A mismatch between the registration validity and the period in which supplies were reported can therefore become a significant compliance issue.

Recommended audit file: registration certificate, original application details, REG-11, ARN, payment challan/CIN/BRN, estimated turnover working, contracts, invoices and return reconciliation.

15. Management Decision Checklist

  • Is the taxpayer a CTP or NRTP?
  • What is the exact expiry date of the current registration?
  • Will taxable supplies continue beyond that date?
  • What turnover is expected during the extension period?
  • What is the estimated GST liability for that period?
  • Has the additional advance tax been deposited?
  • Has REG-11 been filed before expiry?
  • Has the ARN/status been tracked?
  • Has the ERP/billing team been informed of the new validity?

16. Rule 15 – Practical Interpretation

Rule 15 should be understood as a continuity mechanism for a temporary GST registration, not as a general relaxation of registration validity. The taxpayer must proactively demonstrate that the temporary business activity is continuing, estimate the additional tax liability and complete the prescribed extension process before the existing registration expires.

The safest approach is to begin the review well before expiry. This gives the finance team enough time to validate the tax estimate, arrange funds, file REG-11 and ensure the billing system reflects the correct validity.

17. Frequently Asked Questions

Can REG-11 be filed after expiry?

Rule 15 requires the extension application to be submitted before the end of the existing validity period. A business should not rely on a post-expiry filing to authorise taxable supplies made during the gap.

Is advance tax payment required for extension?

Yes. Rule 15(2) links acknowledgement of the extension application to payment of the amount specified under Section 27(2), which covers the additional estimated tax liability for the extension period.

Which form is used for extension?

FORM GST REG-11 is the prescribed application for extension of the registration period of a casual taxable person or non-resident taxable person.

Does Rule 15 apply to every GST registration?

No. It is specifically designed for the special registration categories of casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons.

Where can I check the original validity?

Review the GST registration certificate and the registration particulars maintained for the CTP/NRTP. The extension should be planned against the actual validity date.

20. Practical Takeaway

Rule 15 is fundamentally a deadline-and-payment rule. If a casual taxable person or non-resident taxable person needs more time, the taxpayer should identify the expiry date early, estimate the additional GST liability, deposit the required advance tax, file REG-11 electronically before expiry and preserve the complete compliance trail.
