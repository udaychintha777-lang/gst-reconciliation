Section 163 of the CGST Act, 2017

Levy of fee. Section 163 deals with the fee payable when a person applies for a copy of an order or other document that is to be provided under the GST law.

3. Statutory Text of Section 163

163. Levy of fee.—

Wherever a copy of any order or document is to be provided to any person on an application made by him for that purpose, there shall be paid such fee as may be prescribed.
Important: Section 163 establishes the statutory authority for the fee. The section itself does not specify a fixed rupee amount.

4. Meaning in Simple Language

GST records can include assessment orders, adjudication orders, certificates and other documents. In situations where a person applies for a copy of an order or document that is to be provided, Section 163 provides that a prescribed fee is payable.

The wording has two important parts: application for the copy and fee as may be prescribed. Therefore, the exact amount cannot be inferred merely from reading Section 163; the applicable prescribed provision or process must be checked.

Example: A taxpayer needs a certified or official copy of an older GST order for an internal record or litigation file. If the applicable procedure requires an application and prescribed fee, the request should be made using that procedure and the applicable fee paid.

6. What Does “Such Fee as May Be Prescribed” Mean?

Section 163 deliberately does not write a fixed rupee amount into the section. It uses the expression “such fee as may be prescribed.” This means the amount and operational details need to be checked in the applicable prescribed framework.

Do not assume a fixed amountThe Act's Section 163 text itself does not state a specific rupee fee.
Check the applicable rule/processBefore making payment, verify the current prescribed fee and application method applicable to the requested copy.
Keep proofRetain the application, payment evidence, acknowledgement and copy received in the tax file.
Practical caution: Fee requirements can depend on the applicable rules and administrative process. Do not publish a historical fee amount as though it were automatically the current fee without verifying the relevant legal instrument.

7. What Kind of Orders or Documents Can Be Relevant?

Section 163 uses broad wording: “any order or document” where a copy is to be provided on an application made for that purpose. The exact availability of a copy, format, certification and application procedure can depend on the relevant GST provision and prescribed process.

Document typePossible business need
Adjudication / demand orderAppeal file, audit documentation, management review or litigation record.
Assessment-related documentTax computation and statutory record.

Other GST documentEvidence or record required for a statutory or business purpose.
Important distinction: Section 163 is about the levy of fee when the statutory situation described in the section exists. It should not be read as saying that every GST portal download automatically attracts a Section 163 fee.

8. Practical Application Workflow

  1. Identify the document: record the document type, order number, GSTIN, tax period and date.
  2. Check availability: determine whether the copy is to be provided under the relevant GST provision/process.
  3. Make the application: use the applicable prescribed or departmental method.
  4. Verify the fee: check the current prescribed fee before payment.
  5. Pay and retain evidence: preserve payment details and acknowledgement.
  6. Collect the copy: verify that the copy corresponds to the requested document.
  7. Check completeness: compare pages, annexures, schedules and attachments where relevant.
  8. Update litigation/audit file: store the copy with the original communication and related proceedings.
For companies with multiple GSTINs: tag every requested copy by GSTIN, legal entity, state registration, tax period, document number and case reference to prevent mixing documents.

9. Practical Examples

Example 1 – Copy of an adjudication order

A taxpayer requires a copy of an order for its litigation file. If the applicable process requires an application and prescribed fee, Section 163 provides the statutory basis for the fee.

Example 2 – Older document

A finance team needs an older GST document that is not readily available in its internal records. The team identifies the document and follows the applicable copy-request process.

Example 3 – Appeal preparation

An order is required to prepare an appeal file. The taxpayer should retain the original communication, application for copy where relevant, fee/payment evidence and copy received.

Example 4 – Audit evidence

An internal audit team requires a formal copy of a GST order. The company should verify whether a formal application and prescribed fee apply to the requested copy.

Example 5 – Multiple GSTINs

A company has registrations in several States. A copy request should clearly identify the GSTIN and document to avoid receiving or filing the wrong order.

Example 6 – Portal download vs formal copy

A document available as a routine portal download should not automatically be assumed to attract a Section 163 fee. The statutory and administrative process applicable to the requested copy must be checked.

Example 7 – Certified copy requirement

Where a formal or certified copy is required for a particular proceeding, the taxpayer should follow the applicable procedure rather than relying only on an informal screenshot or internal printout.

Example 8 – Fee amount

A taxpayer knows Section 163 applies but cannot find the current fee. The correct approach is to verify the prescribed rule/process rather than assuming an old amount.

Example 9 – Missing annexure

A copy received does not include an annexure referenced in the order. The taxpayer should check the request and applicable process and seek the missing component if required.

Example 10 – Litigation tracker

A litigation team records the document request date, payment, acknowledgement, copy received and case deadline so the requested document does not delay the next procedural step.

Example 11 – Departmental record

An officer or authority may have a document in its records that the taxpayer needs for a proceeding. Section 163 should be read with the specific provision governing the document and its supply.

Example 12 – No application made

Section 163 specifically refers to a copy provided on an application made for that purpose. A taxpayer should therefore distinguish routine statutory communications from a separately requested copy.

11. Common Mistakes

  • Assuming Section 163 fixes a specific rupee fee directly in the Act.
  • Paying a historical fee amount without checking the current prescribed process.
  • Failing to identify the exact GSTIN and document number.
  • Confusing a routine GST portal download with a separately requested formal copy.
  • Not retaining payment and acknowledgement evidence.
  • Requesting a document without checking whether annexures or schedules are also required.
  • Allowing a copy request to delay an independent appeal or response deadline.
  • Mixing documents of different GST registrations in a group-company tax file.

13. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 163 of the CGST Act?

It provides that where a copy of an order or document is to be provided to a person on an application made for that purpose, a prescribed fee shall be paid.

2. Does Section 163 state a fixed fee?

No. The section says “such fee as may be prescribed”; it does not itself state a fixed rupee amount.

3. Does every GST document download require a fee?

No automatic conclusion should be drawn. Section 163 concerns a copy provided on an application for that purpose. The applicable procedure should be checked for the specific document.

4. What does “application made by him for that purpose” mean?

It refers to an application by the person seeking the copy for that purpose, subject to the applicable GST procedure.

5. What is the fee under Section 163?

The Act section does not specify the amount. The applicable prescribed rule or administrative process should be verified for the current fee.

6. Can I request a copy of a GST order?

Where the relevant GST law/process provides for the copy to be provided on application, Section 163 addresses the fee payable for that copy.

7. Is Section 163 about rectification?

No. Rectification of errors is dealt with by Section 161. Section 163 concerns the levy of fee for providing a requested copy.

8. Is Section 163 connected with service of notices?

It is distinct from Section 169, which deals with service of notice in specified circumstances.

9. Should I keep proof of fee payment?

Yes. For audit, litigation and document-control purposes, retain the application, payment evidence, acknowledgement and copy received.

10. Does Section 163 apply to every order?

The wording refers to a copy of any order or document where it is to be provided on application. The specific availability and process should be checked under the applicable legal framework.

11. Can a company request a copy for appeal?

Where the applicable process permits it, a company can seek the required copy for its records or proceedings, while ensuring that appeal limitation is tracked separately.

12. What if the prescribed fee is unclear?

Verify the current applicable rule, notification or administrative process rather than relying on an old fee amount.

13. Does Section 163 create a taxpayer right to every GST record?

The section addresses the levy of fee where a copy is to be provided on application. It should not be read in isolation as a general disclosure or records-access provision.

14. What if I need a certified copy?

Follow the applicable process for the required type of copy and preserve the request and acknowledgement.

15. Does Section 163 affect GST appeal deadlines?

No automatic extension should be assumed. Track the applicable appeal deadline independently while obtaining the copy.

16. Does Section 163 apply to electronic documents?

The statutory text uses “order or document” and does not itself prescribe a general electronic-document fee. The specific procedure applicable to the requested copy should be checked.

17. What should a finance team record?

GSTIN, document number, tax period, date, issuing authority, application date, prescribed fee, payment reference, acknowledgement and date of receipt.

18. Is this page legal advice?

No. It is an educational explanation. Verify the current rules and applicable administrative procedure before making a copy request or relying on the document for litigation.

14. Quick Revision – Section 163

  • Subject: Levy of fee.
  • Trigger: a person applies for a copy of an order or document that is to be provided.
  • Fee: such fee as may be prescribed.
  • Fixed amount in Section 163: none.
  • Practical control: verify the current prescribed fee and application process.
  • Records: retain application, payment evidence, acknowledgement and copy received.
  • Do not confuse: Section 163 with Section 161 rectification or Section 169 service of notices.
  • Deadline: obtaining a copy should not be allowed to cause an independent appeal or response deadline to be missed.
