Rule 23 – Revocation of Cancellation of Registration

1. Rule 23 at a glance

Rule 23 of the CGST Rules, 2017 deals with revocation of cancellation of registration. It is the mechanism through which an eligible registered person can seek restoration of a GST registration that was cancelled by the proper officer on the officer's own motion.

Important distinction: Rule 23 is not the normal route for a registration cancelled voluntarily by the taxpayer through an application under Rule 20. Rule 23 specifically addresses cancellation by the proper officer on his own motion, subject to the statutory conditions.

The process broadly works as: REG-19 cancellation → REG-21 application → officer examines compliance → REG-21 order revoking or REG-22 notice → taxpayer reply in REG-23 → final decision.

2. When is revocation available?

A registered person whose registration has been cancelled by the proper officer on his own motion may apply for revocation under Rule 23, subject to the statutory time limit and conditions.

SituationRule 23 relevanceAction
Officer cancelled registration on his own motionGenerally the situation addressed by Rule 23Consider REG-21 within the applicable period.
Cancellation because returns were not furnishedAdditional compliance condition appliesFirst furnish required returns and pay tax, interest, penalty and late fee as applicable.
Cancellation was voluntary under Rule 20Rule 23 is not the ordinary routeReview the facts and applicable statutory remedy rather than assuming revocation is available.

3. Return filing and payment conditions

Where registration was cancelled for failure to furnish returns, Rule 23 restricts filing of the revocation application until the required pending returns have been furnished and the tax due under those returns has been paid together with applicable interest, penalty and late fee.

This is one of the most important practical conditions. Filing REG-21 without first resolving the return default can result in the application being procedurally defective or incapable of being accepted.

Finance-team approach: Prepare a GSTIN-wise return matrix showing every pending period, taxable turnover, output tax, ITC, net tax payable, interest, late fee and payment reference before applying for revocation.

4. Application in FORM GST REG-21

The revocation application is filed electronically in FORM GST REG-21 with the proper officer. The normal period specified in Rule 23 is thirty days from the date of service of the cancellation order, subject to the extension mechanism available under Section 30.

The application should not be treated as a simple request to “restore GST”. It should explain why the cancellation should be revoked and demonstrate that the underlying default or cancellation issue has been addressed.

Recommended REG-21 preparation

  1. Read the cancellation order and identify the exact ground.
  2. Determine the effective date of cancellation.
  3. Complete all return-filing and payment conditions applicable to the case.
  4. Prepare a reconciliation showing the corrective action.
  5. Collect supporting documents and payment evidence.
  6. Submit the REG-21 application within the applicable time.
  7. Track the application and any subsequent REG-22 communication.

5. Officer's decision — revocation order

Where the proper officer is satisfied, for reasons to be recorded in writing, that sufficient grounds exist for revocation, the officer revokes the cancellation by order in FORM GST REG-21 within the period prescribed by Rule 23 and communicates the order to the applicant.

The important point is that revocation is a reasoned statutory decision. The taxpayer should therefore provide enough factual and documentary material for the officer to conclude that the grounds for cancellation have been satisfactorily addressed.

Practical result: Revocation restores the registration subject to the statutory consequences and pending compliance created by the cancellation period.

6. If the officer proposes to reject the revocation application

If the proper officer proposes to reject the application, Rule 23 requires a notice in FORM GST REG-22 giving the applicant an opportunity to show cause why the application should not be rejected.

The applicant is required to furnish the reply in FORM GST REG-23 within seven working days from the date of service of the REG-22 notice.

After receiving the clarification or information in REG-23, the officer may dispose of the application in accordance with Rule 23 within the period prescribed by the rule.

Do not ignore REG-22. This is a second opportunity to establish why revocation should be granted. The reply should directly answer the officer's proposed reason for rejection.

REG-22 response format

  • State the notice reference and cancellation order details.
  • Address each proposed reason for rejection separately.
  • Attach proof of return filing and tax/interest/late-fee payment where relevant.
  • Explain any remaining difference with documentary support.
  • Request revocation and continuation of the registration.

7. Returns between cancellation and revocation

Rule 23 contains additional return-filing requirements for the period between cancellation and revocation. The rule provides that returns falling due from the date of the cancellation order until the date of the revocation order are to be furnished within the prescribed period after revocation.

Where registration has been cancelled with retrospective effect, the rule also contains a specific requirement to furnish returns for the period from the effective cancellation date to the date of the revocation order within the prescribed period after revocation.

Important: Revocation does not mean that the intervening period can simply be ignored. The taxpayer should prepare for the return, tax and documentation consequences immediately after the revocation order.

8. Retrospective cancellation and revocation

A cancellation order can in appropriate cases operate from an earlier effective date. Where revocation is subsequently granted, Rule 23 specifically addresses returns relating to the period covered by the retrospective cancellation.

For finance and accounting teams, the effective date in the cancellation order should therefore be captured separately from the date on which the cancellation order was served and the date on which revocation is eventually granted.

9. Practical business examples

Example A — Contractor's registration cancelled for non-filing

A construction company has not filed several GST returns and its registration is cancelled by the officer. Before applying for revocation, the finance team prepares a period-wise return schedule, files the pending returns, pays the applicable tax, interest, penalty and late fee, and preserves all evidence. It then files REG-21 with a factual explanation and supporting documents.

Example B — Cancellation based on a disputed compliance issue

A business's registration is cancelled after the officer concludes that a specified cancellation ground exists. The taxpayer believes the conclusion is factually incorrect. The REG-21 application should directly address the cancellation ground, explain the facts and provide evidence rather than merely stating that the business is active.

Example C — Multiple GST registrations

A company has registrations in several States and one GSTIN is cancelled. The team should treat that GSTIN separately, because return filing, tax payment, registration status and revocation timelines are GSTIN-specific. The accounting system should prevent the cancelled GSTIN from being incorrectly used for another State's transactions.

12. Common mistakes

  • Assuming every cancelled GST registration can automatically be restored.
  • Filing REG-21 without first resolving the return-default condition where it applies.
  • Missing the applicable revocation filing period.
  • Ignoring the effective date of retrospective cancellation.
  • Failing to maintain proof of tax, interest, penalty and late-fee payments.
  • Submitting a generic REG-21 explanation without addressing the cancellation ground.
  • Ignoring REG-22 or failing to answer the proposed rejection point-by-point.
  • Forgetting the additional return requirements after revocation.

13. FAQs

Can I revoke a GST registration cancelled by the proper officer?

Yes, where the cancellation was by the proper officer on his own motion and the statutory conditions for revocation are satisfied.

What form is used for revocation?

The application is made in FORM GST REG-21.

What is the normal time limit?

Rule 23 provides a normal period of thirty days from service of the cancellation order, subject to the extension mechanism available under Section 30.

Can I apply for revocation if cancellation was due to non-filing?

Yes, but Rule 23 requires the specified pending returns to be furnished and the applicable tax, interest, penalty and late fee to be paid before the revocation application can be filed.

What is REG-22?

REG-22 is the notice issued where the proper officer proposes to reject the revocation application.

What is REG-23?

REG-23 is the form used by the applicant to furnish the reply to the REG-22 notice. Rule 23 provides seven working days from service of the notice for the reply.

Does revocation automatically remove all pending GST work?

No. Rule 23 contains specific requirements for returns due during the cancellation-to-revocation period, including the prescribed treatment where cancellation operated retrospectively.

Is Rule 23 the same as filing a fresh GST registration?

No. Revocation seeks restoration of the cancelled registration. A fresh registration is a different process and should not be assumed to be an equivalent substitute.

14. Compliance checklist

  • ☐ Obtain and preserve the REG-19 cancellation order.
  • ☐ Record cancellation effective date, order date and service date.
  • ☐ Identify the exact cancellation ground.
  • ☐ Check the Rule 23 filing period and any legally available extension.
  • ☐ File all required pending returns.
  • ☐ Pay applicable tax, interest, penalty and late fee.
  • ☐ Prepare REG-21 with a reasoned explanation and evidence.
  • ☐ Monitor for REG-22.
  • ☐ If REG-22 is issued, submit REG-23 within seven working days.
  • ☐ After revocation, complete the prescribed intervening-period returns and reconcile books/returns.
