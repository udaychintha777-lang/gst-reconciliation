CGST Rule 13 – Grant of Registration to Non-Resident Taxable Person

A practical guide to GST registration for a non-resident taxable person, covering FORM GST REG-09, the five-day advance application requirement, passport/TIN documents, advance tax deposit, authorised signatory requirements and the registration workflow.

1. What Rule 13 Is About

Rule 13 prescribes the registration procedure for a Non-Resident Taxable Person (NRTP). A non-resident business entering India to make taxable supplies cannot simply use the ordinary resident-registration process without considering the special NRTP provisions.

Simple meaning: Rule 13 creates a special registration route for a non-resident taxable person. The applicant uses FORM GST REG-09, applies at least five days before commencing business, and must make the required advance tax deposit before the registration acknowledgement is issued.

The special procedure is designed around the temporary/limited nature of NRTP activity and the statutory safeguard of advance tax payment.

2. Who Is a Non-Resident Taxable Person?

The CGST Act defines a non-resident taxable person as a person who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services or both, whether as principal, agent or in any other capacity, but who has no fixed place of business or residence in India.

This definition is important because “foreign company” and “non-resident taxable person” are not automatically interchangeable expressions. The actual business presence and activity in India must be examined.

Practical test: Ask three questions — Is the person making taxable supplies in India? Is the activity occasional/temporary in nature? Does the person lack a fixed place of business or residence in India? The statutory definition and the complete facts should be considered together.

3. Rule 13 – Clause-by-Clause Explanation

Rule 13(1) – Apply in FORM GST REG-09

A non-resident taxable person electronically submits an application for registration in FORM GST REG-09 at least five days before commencement of business.

The application is submitted on the common portal either directly or through a Facilitation Centre notified by the Commissioner.

Passport requirement

The application is accompanied by a self-attested copy of a valid passport.

Foreign business entity – TIN / unique number / PAN

Where the applicant is a business entity incorporated or established outside India, the application is also submitted with the tax identification number or unique number by which the entity is identified by the government of its country, or its PAN if available.

Rule 13(2) – Temporary reference number and advance tax deposit

The applicant receives a temporary reference number through the common portal for making the advance deposit required under Section 27.

The acknowledgement is issued electronically only after the required deposit is credited to the applicant's electronic cash ledger.

Very important: The advance deposit is not an arbitrary security amount. It is linked to the person's estimated GST liability for the period for which registration is sought.

Rule 13(3) – Rules 9 and 10 apply

The verification and grant provisions in Rules 9 and 10 apply, with necessary modifications, to an NRTP registration application.

Therefore, although the application is special, the applicant should still expect a verification/approval process before the registration certificate is issued.

Rule 13(4) – Resident authorised signatory

The application is signed or electronically verified by an authorised signatory who is a person resident in India having a valid PAN.

Business implication: A foreign business planning an Indian taxable-supply activity should identify its India-resident authorised signatory before starting the registration process.

4. Complete NRTP Registration Flow

1Confirm NRTP status
2File REG-09 ≥ 5 days before business
3Receive temporary reference + deposit estimated tax
4Verification under Rules 9/10
5REG-06 + GSTIN

The sequence matters. An applicant should not treat the submission of REG-09 alone as permission to begin taxable Indian operations.

5. Advance Tax Deposit – Practical Understanding

Section 27 provides the underlying statutory requirement for a non-resident taxable person to make an advance deposit of tax equivalent to the estimated tax liability for the period for which registration is sought.

The deposited amount is credited to the electronic cash ledger and can be used in accordance with the GST payment framework.

StepWhat happens
1NRTP estimates taxable supplies and corresponding GST liability for the requested registration period.
2Temporary reference number is provided for the deposit process.
3Estimated tax is deposited as required.
4Amount is credited to the electronic cash ledger.
5Registration acknowledgement can be issued after the required deposit.
Illustration: A foreign company plans a temporary taxable exhibition-related business activity in India and estimates GST liability of ₹18 lakh for the relevant registration period. The finance team should plan the required advance tax deposit as part of the registration cash-flow requirement, rather than treating registration as a zero-cash administrative step.

6. Why the Five-Day Rule Matters

The application must be made at least five days prior to commencement of business. This is especially important for foreign exhibitors, event participants, temporary project activities and other businesses entering India for a limited taxable activity.

Do not plan backwards from the first invoice. Passport/document preparation, authorised signatory arrangements, estimated tax computation, advance deposit and portal verification all require time.

7. Practical Examples

Example 1 – Foreign exhibitor

A foreign company with no fixed place of business in India participates in a taxable trade exhibition and makes taxable supplies in India. The company should examine NRTP registration before commencing the taxable activity, rather than waiting until the exhibition begins.

Example 2 – Foreign supplier temporarily supplying goods

A foreign business plans to supply goods in India during a short commercial event. It should evaluate whether it falls within the NRTP definition and, if so, plan REG-09, passport/entity documentation, Indian resident authorised signatory and advance tax deposit before the activity starts.

Example 3 – Foreign entity with Indian fixed establishment

A foreign company has a continuing fixed place of business in India. It should not automatically assume that Rule 13 applies merely because its parent entity is incorporated overseas. The definition of NRTP and the actual Indian business structure must be examined.

Example 4 – Last-minute registration

A foreign participant arrives in India and only then asks its consultant to obtain GST registration. The five-day requirement and advance deposit can create a practical timing problem. The compliance process should therefore begin before the taxable business activity starts.

9. Registration Validity – Do Not Confuse Rule 13 With Extension

The validity period of an NRTP registration is governed principally by Section 27 of the CGST Act. The certificate is generally valid for the period specified in the application or 90 days from the effective date of registration, whichever is earlier, subject to the statutory framework.

The proper officer may, on sufficient cause being shown, extend the 90-day period by a further period not exceeding 90 days. The additional period requires the corresponding additional advance tax deposit based on the estimated liability for the extension period.

Important navigation point: The procedure for extension is dealt with separately in Rule 15. Rule 13 primarily establishes the initial NRTP registration process.

11. Audit & Departmental Verification Relevance

NRTP registration records are particularly important because the GST administration may need to verify the identity of a foreign person, the Indian authorised signatory, the nature and period of the Indian taxable activity and the tax deposited in advance.

Maintain a dedicated file containing:

  • REG-09 application;
  • passport and foreign entity identification documents;
  • resident authorised signatory documents;
  • temporary reference number and advance tax deposit proof;
  • REG-06 certificate;
  • sales invoices and contracts;
  • GST returns and tax-payment workings; and
  • extension/closure documentation where applicable.

12. Common Mistakes

  • Applying only after the taxable business activity has started.
  • Ignoring the five-day advance application requirement.
  • Failing to arrange an India-resident authorised signatory with valid PAN.
  • Submitting incomplete passport or foreign entity identification documents.
  • Underestimating the tax liability used for the advance deposit without a defensible working.
  • Assuming every foreign company automatically qualifies as an NRTP.
  • Confusing NRTP registration with ordinary resident GST registration.
  • Failing to monitor the limited validity period.
  • Starting taxable invoicing before confirming the registration certificate and GSTIN.
  • Not reconciling the advance deposit with final tax liability.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

What form is used for NRTP registration?

FORM GST REG-09 is the application prescribed under Rule 13 for registration of a non-resident taxable person.

How many days before starting business should an NRTP apply?

The application is required to be submitted at least five days before commencement of business.

Does an NRTP have to deposit tax in advance?

Yes. Section 27 requires an advance deposit equivalent to the estimated tax liability for the period for which registration is sought, and Rule 13 provides the portal procedure for making that deposit.

Is a PAN mandatory for a foreign business entity?

For an entity incorporated or established outside India, Rule 13 permits the application to be supported by its foreign tax identification/unique number or its PAN if available. The exact document set should be matched to the applicant's facts.

Does an NRTP need an Indian authorised signatory?

Yes. Rule 13 requires the authorised signatory to be a person resident in India having a valid PAN.

How long is NRTP registration valid?

Section 27 provides that registration is generally valid for the period specified in the application or 90 days from the effective date, whichever is earlier, subject to the statutory provisions and possible extension by up to a further 90 days on sufficient cause.

Can NRTP registration be extended?

Yes, subject to the statutory conditions and the extension procedure under Rule 15, including the additional advance tax deposit where applicable.

What is the next Rule?

Rule 14 deals with registration of certain overseas suppliers, including online information and database access or retrieval services and, under the current framework, online money gaming.

17. Practical Takeaway

Rule 13 should be treated as a pre-entry GST compliance process, not a registration formalisation after business starts. A foreign business should determine NRTP status early, appoint an India-resident authorised signatory, prepare REG-09, estimate the GST liability, make the required advance deposit and obtain the registration certificate before commencing taxable Indian business.
