GST ACT — SECTION 71

Section 71 CGST Act — Access to Business Premises for GST Audit, Scrutiny & Verification

Section 71 allows an officer authorised by the proper officer not below the rank of Joint Commissioner to access the place of business of a registered person for inspecting books, documents, computers and other things, and to verify transactions, stocks, returns and tax liability. This guide explains the provision in simple language and distinguishes ordinary access under Section 71 from search under Section 67.

1. Why Section 71 matters

Searches such as “Section 71 CGST Act”, “Section 71 GST”, “GST officer access to business premises”, “GST audit premises”, “GST verification”, “GST officer inspecting books”, “GST access to computers” and “GST audit rights” relate directly to this provision.

Section 71 is an important but often misunderstood provision. It gives authorised officers access to a registered person's business premises for inspection of books, documents, computers and other relevant things and for verification of transactions and tax liability.

Simple distinction: Section 71 is primarily an access-and-verification provision. It should not automatically be treated as the search-and-seizure power under Section 67.

2. Section 71 in very simple language

Suppose GST officers are conducting an audit or verification of a company. They need to see the accounting records, purchase invoices, sales records, stock details or computerised books at the company's business premises.

Section 71 provides the statutory route for authorised access to those premises.

Authorisation → access to business premises → inspect books/documents/computers → verify transactions/stock/tax → record findings

The purpose is to allow the department to examine information relevant to GST compliance and protect the interest of revenue.

3. What Section 71 actually says

Section 71(1) provides that any officer under the Act, authorised by the proper officer not below the rank of Joint Commissioner, shall have access to any place of business of a registered person to inspect books of account, documents, computers and computer programs, if available, and such other things as he may require and which may be available at such place, for the purposes specified in the section.

Those purposes include:

  • carrying out any audit, scrutiny, verification and checks as may be necessary to safeguard the interest of revenue;
  • verifying turnover declared;
  • verifying tax paid;
  • verifying ITC availed; and
  • verifying the tax payable.

Section 71(2) also provides a specific access framework for a place of business of a person other than the registered person where books of account, documents, computers or computer programs relating to the registered person are kept.

4. Who can authorise access?

The authorisation chain matters.

PersonRole
Proper officer not below Joint CommissionerProvides the statutory authorisation.
Authorised officerExercises access to the business premises for the purposes specified by Section 71.
Registered personProvides lawful access to relevant books/documents/computers and cooperates with the verification.
The statutory requirement of authorisation is one of the important differences between ordinary Section 71 access and an informal request to enter premises.

5. Why can access be taken?

Section 71 is aimed at verification and revenue protection. Typical matters include:

  • turnover reported in GST returns;
  • tax paid;
  • ITC claimed;
  • tax payable;
  • stock records;
  • purchase and sales records;
  • books of account;
  • computerised accounting systems;
  • transactions requiring verification; and
  • other checks connected with GST compliance.

The section is therefore broader than simply checking whether a single invoice exists.

7. What is a place of business?

Section 71(1) refers to the place of business of a registered person. This can include the premises identified in the GST registration and relevant places where the business actually maintains records or carries out business activities, subject to the statutory framework.

For businesses with multiple offices, warehouses and project locations, maintaining a clear registration and records structure is particularly important.

Principal place
Core registered business location.
Additional place
Registered additional premises such as warehouses/offices.
Project location
Construction/site records may be maintained depending on the business model and registration facts.
Third-party premises
Section 71(2) addresses places of another person where the registered person's records are kept.

8. Computers and electronic records

Section 71 expressly recognises modern computerised accounting by referring to computers and computer programs.

Businesses should therefore be able to explain:

  • which ERP/accounting system is used;
  • where the books are stored;
  • how GST reports are generated;
  • how the audit trail works;
  • who has access;
  • how backups are maintained; and
  • how electronic records reconcile with GST returns.
Practical point: Do not alter or delete accounting data during an audit or verification. Maintain appropriate backups and preserve the integrity of the records.

9. Section 71 and GST audit

Section 71 can support access required for audit and verification. Section 65 is the specific provision dealing with departmental audit of registered persons.

10. Section 71 and scrutiny/verification

Section 61 deals with scrutiny of returns, while Section 71 provides a physical access mechanism where authorised access is required for audit, scrutiny, verification and checks.

A mismatch identified during scrutiny can therefore lead to a request or statutory process requiring examination of underlying books and documents.

Return data → discrepancy → underlying records → verification → explanation/correction or further proceedings

14. Unregistered locations and records

Section 71(2) is especially important for businesses that keep records at a location that is not the registered person's own place of business.

It provides access to a place of business of a person other than the registered person where the registered person's books, documents, computers or computer programs are kept.

For example, accounting records may be maintained by a service provider, group-company office or another permitted records location. The statutory provision addresses access to such records.

15. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Turnover verification

The department wants to verify whether reported turnover agrees with the company's sales ledger, invoices and bank records. Authorised access under Section 71 can facilitate this verification.

Example 2 — ITC verification

The department examines purchase invoices, GRNs, 2B reconciliation and accounting records to verify ITC claimed.

Example 3 — Stock verification

Physical stock is compared with stock registers and purchase/dispatch records.

Example 4 — ERP records

The business maintains accounts entirely in ERP. Officers may inspect relevant computer records and programs within the statutory purpose.

Example 5 — Outsourced accounting

The company's books are maintained at an outsourced accounting service provider. Section 71(2) can become relevant to access records kept at another person's place of business.

Example 6 — Multiple offices

A company has a registered principal office and several additional places. The relevant records should be organised so that each location's role is clear.

Example 7 — Construction project

Site records are checked against head-office books, purchase invoices, GRNs and project stock.

Example 8 — Bank reconciliation

Officers compare sales ledger, bank receipts and GSTR-1/GSTR-3B to verify declared turnover.

Example 9 — Related-party transaction

Agreements, invoices and valuation workings are examined to understand the GST treatment.

Example 10 — Scrutiny follow-up

A return mismatch cannot be resolved from the return alone. The department examines underlying books and documents to verify the explanation.

18. Practical rights and safeguards

  • Verify the officer's identity and authorisation details.
  • Understand the purpose of the visit and records being examined.
  • Provide reasonable access to records within the statutory scope.
  • Maintain an internal record of documents/data examined or provided.
  • Preserve original records and electronic backups.
  • Do not alter accounting records during the verification.
  • Ask for appropriate acknowledgement of documents physically handed over where applicable.
  • Escalate material disputes to tax/legal professionals.
Important distinction: Section 71 access should not automatically be treated as a Section 67 search. If a search/seizure is being conducted, the separate Section 67 authorisation and safeguards become relevant.

21. Common mistakes

  • Confusing Section 71 access with Section 67 search.
  • Not maintaining books at the locations where they are actually kept.
  • Failing to preserve electronic backups.
  • Not reconciling physical stock with accounting records.
  • Giving inconsistent explanations from head office and project sites.
  • Not maintaining a record of documents provided during verification.
  • Changing or deleting ERP records during an ongoing verification.
  • Assuming a physical verification visit automatically means an offence has been established.

23. Section 71 GST FAQs

What is Section 71 of the CGST Act?

Section 71 gives authorised GST officers access to a registered person's place of business to inspect books, documents, computers, computer programs and other relevant things for audit, scrutiny, verification and checks to safeguard revenue.

Who can authorise access under Section 71?

The proper officer authorising the access must not be below the rank of Joint Commissioner.

Can GST officers inspect computers?

Yes. Section 71 expressly covers computers and computer programs where they are available at the relevant place of business.

Can GST officers inspect books of account?

Yes. Inspection of books of account and documents is specifically contemplated by Section 71.

Is Section 71 the same as a GST search?

No. Section 67 contains the separate inspection/search/seizure framework. Section 71 primarily provides access for audit, scrutiny, verification and checks.

Can GST officers access records kept at another person's premises?

Section 71(2) specifically addresses a place of business of another person where the registered person's books, documents, computers or computer programs are kept.

Can Section 71 be used for audit?

Yes. The statutory purpose expressly includes carrying out audit, scrutiny, verification and checks necessary to safeguard the interest of revenue.

What is the difference between Section 70 and Section 71?

Section 70 is the summons power for attendance/evidence/documents. Section 71 provides access to business premises and records for specified audit and verification purposes.

What is the difference between Section 71 and Section 69?

Section 71 concerns access to premises and records. Section 69 concerns arrest in specified GST offence situations.

What records should a company keep ready?

Books, invoices, contracts, stock records, GST returns, ITC reconciliation, bank records, ERP data, e-way bills and other transaction evidence should be organised and readily retrievable.

Can a company refuse access?

A validly authorised officer exercising the statutory power should be provided the required access. If the business disputes the scope or legality of the action, it should document the issue and obtain professional legal advice rather than obstructing lawful proceedings.

Does a Section 71 visit mean tax evasion has been established?

No. Access may be for audit, scrutiny, verification or checks. Findings must be evaluated through the applicable statutory process.

25. Key takeaways

  • Section 71 provides access to business premises for GST audit, scrutiny, verification and checks.
  • The authorising proper officer must not be below the rank of Joint Commissioner.
  • Books, documents, computers and computer programs can fall within the scope of inspection.
  • Section 71(2) addresses records kept at another person's business premises.
  • Section 71 is different from Section 67 search and seizure.
  • Section 71 is also different from Section 70 summons and Section 69 arrest.
  • Electronic accounting records are expressly relevant because the section recognises computers and computer programs.
  • Businesses should maintain strong records, stock reconciliation, ITC workings and electronic backups.
  • A Section 71 visit does not itself establish tax evasion or create a final tax demand.
  • For a material verification, maintain a complete record of authorisation, documents examined, submissions and explanations.
