GST ACT — SECTION 28

Amendment of Registration

Section 28 provides the statutory framework for keeping a GST registration accurate after it has been granted. It requires registered persons and UIN holders to report changes in registration particulars, gives the proper officer a verification and approval/rejection role for prescribed amendments, and links Central GST and corresponding State/UT GST amendment decisions.

1. What Section 28 does

Registration does not become permanently correct merely because a GSTIN has been issued. When the underlying facts change, the registration particulars must be updated.

Section 28 covers both ordinary registered persons and persons holding a Unique Identity Number. The provision requires changes in the information furnished at registration or subsequently to be reported to the proper officer in the prescribed form, manner and period.

Core principle: GST registration should continuously reflect the legal and operational reality of the business — name, address, additional places, responsible persons and other prescribed particulars.

CBIC's published statutory text states that every registered person and UIN holder must inform the proper officer of changes in registration information, and that the officer may approve or reject prescribed amendments. citeturn0search0

The current statutory architecture is therefore simple: report → verify where required → approve/reject → coordinate the corresponding GST registration. citeturn0search0

3. What is a registration amendment?

A registration amendment is a change to information that forms part of the GST registration record. It is different from cancellation, fresh registration and merely changing an internal accounting record.

Registration amendment
Address, legal name, responsible persons and other prescribed particulars.
Fresh registration
May be required where the legal entity/PAN changes.
Cancellation
Applies where registration should cease.
Internal change only
Not every operational change automatically requires a GST registration amendment.
Important: The correct treatment depends on the nature of the change. A change in constitution that results in a new PAN is generally not handled merely as an amendment; the Rules require fresh registration.

4. Time limit for amendment

The detailed timeline is prescribed by Rule 19. The registration Rules require the registered person to submit the amendment application within the prescribed period after the change.

The commonly stated Rule 19 framework requires application within 15 days of the change. This deadline should be treated as a compliance control rather than a target to be ignored until an annual GST review.

Control: Record the actual date on which the underlying event occurred — for example, the date a new partner was appointed or the premises changed — and calculate the amendment deadline from that date.

The GST Council's original registration-rule design also used a 15-day amendment application period. citeturn0search2

6. Non-core fields

Other prescribed registration particulars do not ordinarily require the same prior officer approval. The Rules provide that the certificate can stand amended upon submission through the common portal for the prescribed non-core changes.

This distinction exists to prevent routine administrative changes from being delayed by a manual approval process.

TypeGeneral treatment
Core particularsOfficer verification/approval generally required.
Other prescribed particularsCertificate can stand amended upon portal submission, subject to prescribed verification.
Mobile/e-mail of authorised signatorySubject to online verification mechanism.
Always use the current GST portal classification of the field. The legal distinction comes from Section 28 and Rule 19, but the portal interface can evolve.

7. Legal name and trade name

A change in the legal name of a business is a registration amendment where the underlying legal person/PAN remains appropriate for amendment treatment.

Trade-name changes and legal-name changes should not be treated as interchangeable. The supporting document must establish what has actually changed.

Important: If the change in constitution results in a change of PAN, Rule 19 requires fresh registration rather than merely amending the old GSTIN.

The GST Council's registration-rule framework specifically provided that a constitution change resulting in a PAN change requires a fresh REG-01 application. citeturn0search2

8. Principal and additional places of business

Address changes are among the most important GST registration amendments because the place of business determines jurisdiction, notices, physical verification and the State/registration structure.

Principal place

A shift of the principal place of business within the same State can generally be handled through amendment if the legal registration remains valid.

Additional place

Adding or deleting a warehouse, branch, factory, project office or other additional place should be reflected in the registration where the place qualifies under the GST registration rules.

Construction example: A contractor opens a project office/warehouse that qualifies as an additional place of business. The finance team should review the GST registration master before the site begins issuing invoices or receiving taxable supplies through that location.

9. Partners, directors and responsible persons

Changes in partners, directors, Karta, Managing Committee, Board of Trustees, Chief Executive Officer or equivalent persons responsible for day-to-day affairs can require amendment.

These changes are important because GST registration identifies the people responsible for the taxable person's compliance.

ChangeAction
New partner admittedAmend registration within prescribed period.
Partner retiresDelete/update the person and supporting records.
Director changesUpdate the prescribed registration particulars.
Responsible officer changesUpdate authorised/responsible-person details.

10. Constitution and PAN changes

This is one of the most important boundaries in Section 28.

If the legal constitution changes but the same PAN continues and the change is eligible for amendment under the Rules, an amendment route may be available. However, where the change results in a new PAN, the existing registration cannot simply be amended into the new legal person.

Example: A proprietorship is converted into a company and a new PAN is obtained. The company should obtain fresh GST registration rather than attempting to replace the proprietor's PAN through an ordinary amendment.

This distinction follows the registration Rules' specific treatment of constitution changes resulting in PAN change. citeturn0search2turn0search12

11. Effective date of amendment

For prescribed core amendments approved by the proper officer, the Rules provide that the amendment takes effect from the date of occurrence of the event warranting amendment.

This is important for historical invoices, notices, jurisdiction and return-period analysis.

Example: A partner retires on 1 September and the amendment application is filed within the prescribed period. Where the amendment is approved under the Rule 19 core-field process, the amendment can take effect from the event date rather than merely from the date of portal approval.

CBIC's published Rule 19 text expressly describes this effective-date principle for the core changes covered by the rule. citeturn0search3

12. Approval and rejection

Section 28(2) gives the proper officer power to approve or reject prescribed amendments based on information furnished by the taxpayer or information otherwise ascertained by the officer.

The officer's role is therefore not limited to mechanically accepting every change. Where verification is required, supporting documents can be examined.

OutcomeMeaning
ApprovedRegistration particulars are amended according to the prescribed process.
Deficiency / clarificationApplicant may need to provide documents or explanation.
RejectedAmendment does not take effect through that application; the statutory hearing requirement applies.

13. Opportunity of being heard

The second proviso to Section 28(2) contains an important natural-justice safeguard: the proper officer shall not reject an amendment application without giving the person an opportunity of being heard.

Practical implication: A rejection should not be treated as a silent portal event. The taxpayer should review the notice/reason for proposed rejection and use the prescribed response/hearing mechanism.

This safeguard is part of the statutory text itself. citeturn0search0

14. CGST–SGST/UTGST coordination

Section 28(3) provides that approval or rejection of amendments under the State GST Act or Union Territory GST Act is deemed to be approval or rejection under the CGST Act.

This is the amendment counterpart to the coordination mechanism in Section 26.

Registration eventCoordination provision
Grant/rejection of registrationSection 26
Approval/rejection of amendmentSection 28(3)
CancellationSection 29 and corresponding GST law
The objective is consistency: the same registration particulars should not ordinarily have contradictory amendment outcomes under the Central and corresponding State/UT GST laws.

15. UIN amendments

Section 28 expressly covers a person to whom a Unique Identity Number has been assigned. Therefore, UIN holders also have an obligation to update prescribed changes in their registration information.

This is relevant for specified international organisations, embassies, consulates and other notified entities that hold UINs for GST purposes.

Important: UIN is not an ordinary GSTIN, but Section 28 still applies its amendment discipline to the information furnished for the UIN.

16. Rule 19 procedure

Rule 19 supplies the operational machinery for Section 28. It distinguishes core amendments from other amendments and sets out the officer's verification and notice process.

Rule 19 areaPractical treatment
ApplicationAmendment application is filed electronically in the prescribed form through the common portal.
Core fieldsOfficer verification and approval/rejection process.
Other fieldsCertificate can stand amended upon submission, subject to the Rule.
DeficiencyOfficer can issue notice seeking explanation/documents.
ResponseTaxpayer must respond within prescribed time.

CBIC's published Registration Rules contain the Rule 19 amendment framework, while GST Council's original registration-rule material shows the same core-field/non-core-field architecture. citeturn0search3turn0search2

17. Documentation checklist

  • Board/partner/management resolution where relevant.
  • Certificate of incorporation/name-change evidence.
  • Rent/lease/ownership proof for changed premises.
  • Consent/NOC and address documents where applicable.
  • Partner/director appointment or retirement documents.
  • Amended partnership deed or constitutional documents.
  • PAN documents where constitution/legal identity changes.
  • SEZ/project/warehouse documents where relevant.
  • Proof of event date causing the change.
  • Amendment application and ARN.
  • Officer notices and replies.
  • Approval/rejection order and updated registration certificate.
The most important document is often not the amendment application itself but the evidence establishing when the underlying event occurred. That date can determine the effective date of the amendment.

18. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — office shifted

A company moves its principal office from one Hyderabad address to another. The change should be reflected through the prescribed registration-amendment process, with address proof and event-date evidence.

Example 2 — new warehouse

A contractor starts operating a qualifying warehouse as an additional place of business. The GST registration master should be amended before the place is used as an operational GST location.

Example 3 — partner retirement

A partner retires on 1 September. The firm should update the registration within the prescribed period and preserve the retirement deed/resolution.

Example 4 — new director

A company appoints a new director responsible for day-to-day affairs. The prescribed registration particulars should be updated.

Example 5 — legal name change

A company changes its legal name while the legal entity/PAN remains the same. The change can be handled through the prescribed amendment route, subject to verification.

Example 6 — constitution changes PAN

A proprietorship becomes a company and receives a new PAN. Fresh registration is required; an ordinary amendment cannot substitute the new PAN into the old GST registration.

Example 7 — non-core detail

A prescribed non-core registration field changes. Where Rule 19 permits self-amendment, the certificate can stand amended upon portal submission without waiting for separate core-field approval.

Example 8 — rejection

The officer proposes to reject a core amendment because supporting documents are incomplete. The taxpayer should respond and use the opportunity of being heard before rejection.

Example 9 — State/Central coordination

An amendment approved/rejected under the corresponding State GST law receives the deemed approval/rejection consequence under Section 28(3).

Example 10 — construction project office

A construction company opens a project office that qualifies as an additional place of business. The finance team should verify whether the location is required to be added to the GST registration and update the registration accordingly.

23. GST Council background

Section 28 was part of the original GST registration architecture considered during the GST Council's formulation of the registration Rules.

Original design
Registration information had to remain current.
Core-field verification
Important identity/location changes were designed for officer review.
Non-core self-amendment
Routine changes could be handled more efficiently through the portal.
Central-State coordination
Section 28(3) avoids contradictory amendment outcomes.

The 14th GST Council agenda specifically set out the proposed Rule 19 amendment process, including legal name, principal/additional place and partner/director changes, and the requirement for fresh registration where a constitution change results in a PAN change. citeturn0search2

24. Common mistakes

  • Not reporting changes within the prescribed period.
  • Assuming every field needs officer approval.
  • Assuming no approval means no amendment is required.
  • Using amendment where a new PAN requires fresh registration.
  • Failing to record the actual event date.
  • Adding a new project office/warehouse without checking additional-place requirements.
  • Ignoring partner/director changes.
  • Not responding to an amendment clarification notice.
  • Failing to reconcile updated GST registration details with ERP and invoices.
  • Assuming a CGST amendment and State amendment can remain inconsistent.
  • Confusing Section 28 amendment with Section 29 cancellation.

26. Key takeaways

  • Section 28 requires registered persons and UIN holders to keep registration information current.
  • The detailed amendment deadline is prescribed by Rule 19; the standard framework is 15 days from the change.
  • Core fields such as legal name, principal/additional places and specified responsible persons generally require officer verification.
  • Prescribed non-core fields can be amended through the portal without separate officer approval.
  • A change resulting in a new PAN generally requires fresh registration rather than ordinary amendment.
  • For core changes, the amendment can take effect from the date of the underlying event under the Rules.
  • An amendment application cannot be rejected without an opportunity of being heard.
  • Section 28(3) coordinates approval/rejection between CGST and the corresponding State/UT GST law.
  • Maintain evidence of the event date and supporting documents for every material amendment.
