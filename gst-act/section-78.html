GST ACT — SECTION 78

Section 78 CGST Act — Payment of GST Demand, Interest & Penalty

Section 78 answers a very practical question after a GST demand becomes payable: how much time does the taxpayer get to pay the amount determined under the Act? It establishes the statutory payment period after an order, permits a limited extension by the Commissioner in appropriate cases, and connects non-payment with the recovery machinery under Section 79. This detailed guide explains the provision in simple language and shows how finance and tax teams should manage a GST demand from order to payment or recovery.

1. Why Section 78 matters

Searches such as “Section 78 CGST Act”, “Section 78 GST”, “GST demand payment time limit”, “how long to pay GST demand after order”, “GST demand payment within 3 months”, “Section 78(1) GST”, “GST DRC-07 payment deadline” and “GST recovery after demand order” all relate to this provision.

Sections 73, 74, 74A and other provisions determine the tax, interest and penalty liability in the relevant proceeding. Section 78 deals with the next practical stage: payment of the amount that has become payable pursuant to an order.

For a business, this is not merely a legal provision. Once a demand order is received, the tax team needs to know the exact payment deadline, whether an appeal is being filed, whether a stay is available or required, whether an instalment request is possible, and when recovery exposure begins.

Simple idea: Section 78 gives the taxpayer a statutory window to pay the amount determined under the GST law after the order is served. If the amount remains unpaid after the applicable period, the recovery provisions can be used.

2. Section 78 in very simple language

Suppose a GST officer passes an order creating a demand of:

  • Tax: ₹20 lakh
  • Interest: ₹3 lakh
  • Penalty: ₹2 lakh
  • Total: ₹25 lakh

Section 78 does not itself decide whether ₹25 lakh is correct. That question belongs to the underlying adjudication and appeal provisions.

Instead, Section 78 tells the taxpayer when the amount determined by the order has to be paid.

GST demand order served → statutory payment period → pay / obtain applicable relief → otherwise recovery machinery

3. What Section 78 actually does

Section 78 is a payment-timing provision. It applies to amounts payable pursuant to an order under the Act.

The provision is important because GST law separates three different stages:

Stage 1 — Determination
The department determines tax, interest or penalty through the relevant statutory provision.
Stage 2 — Payment
Section 78 provides the statutory period for payment after the order.
Stage 3 — Recovery
If the amount is not paid within the applicable period, Section 79 recovery provisions can become relevant.
Stage 4 — Appeal
The taxpayer may challenge the order under the applicable appellate framework, subject to pre-deposit and stay requirements.

Keeping these stages separate helps finance teams avoid the common mistake of treating the demand order, payment deadline and recovery action as if they were the same event.

4. Clause-wise statutory explanation

ProvisionCore meaning
Section 78Any amount payable pursuant to an order under the Act must be paid within the prescribed statutory period, subject to the Commissioner's power to allow a further period in the manner and circumstances permitted by the provision.

Section 78 is intentionally short. Its importance comes from how it connects the adjudication provisions with the recovery provisions.

The practical payment period under the current statutory framework is generally expressed as three months from the date of service of the order, subject to the proviso permitting a shorter or longer period in specified circumstances.

Because Section 78 is short, it should always be read together with the actual demand order, Section 79, Section 80, the appeal provisions and the relevant Rules.

5. The three-month payment rule

The main rule under Section 78 is that the amount payable pursuant to an order is to be paid within three months from the date of service of the order.

This is the normal statutory window.

The important point is that the period is linked to service of the order, not simply to the date printed on the order.

Normal payment deadline = Date of service of GST order + 3 months

The exact computation should be documented in the demand tracker, especially where the service date and order date are different.

6. Why service of order matters

For Section 78, the date of service is practically significant because it starts the statutory payment period.

A tax team should therefore preserve:

  • the date appearing on the order;
  • the date the order became available/was communicated through the prescribed electronic mechanism;
  • the date of actual receipt where relevant;
  • the DRC-07 or equivalent electronic summary;
  • email/SMS or portal communication evidence where available; and
  • the date entered into the internal demand tracker.
Control point: Never calculate the Section 78 deadline from memory or from the date of a hearing. Preserve evidence of the actual service/communication of the order.

7. How to calculate the payment deadline

Assume a demand order is served on 15 September 2026.

The normal three-month statutory payment period should be tracked to 15 December 2026, subject to the exact legal computation and any applicable order, stay or statutory modification.

Finance teams should record the deadline in a calendar and begin the payment/appeal decision process well before the final date.

8. Extension by Commissioner

The proviso to Section 78 allows the Commissioner, in appropriate circumstances and for reasons to be recorded in writing, to require payment within a period shorter than three months or to allow payment within a period longer than three months.

This is an important qualification to the normal three-month rule.

Why could the period be shorter?

The statutory proviso recognises that certain cases may require earlier payment because of the circumstances of the case.

Why could a longer period be allowed?

A longer period may be allowed where the circumstances justify additional time, subject to the statutory conditions and the Commissioner's order.

A taxpayer should not assume that the three-month period can always be shortened or extended automatically. Any deviation should be traceable to a valid order or statutory basis.

9. Conditions for extension

Where the Commissioner exercises the power under the proviso to Section 78, the decision should be read carefully to identify:

  • the exact payment period specified;
  • the reason recorded for the special period;
  • whether the period is shorter or longer than three months;
  • whether the order contains conditions; and
  • the date from which the special period is calculated.

For internal control purposes, the special period specified by the Commissioner should override the normal deadline in the demand tracker once the legally valid order is verified.

10. Interest continues separately

Section 78 should not be misunderstood as an interest waiver provision.

Interest arising under the applicable GST provisions remains a separate liability. The taxpayer must examine the underlying order and the applicable interest provision, particularly Section 50 and the relevant demand section.

Where the tax remains unpaid after the order, additional consequences may arise depending on the nature of the liability and applicable law.

Important: The three-month Section 78 payment period should not be read as “interest-free three months” in every situation. Interest must be calculated under the applicable substantive provision.

11. Penalty and other amounts

An order may create several components of liability:

ComponentWhat the finance team should check
TaxPrincipal demand confirmed by the order.
InterestRate, period and calculation under the relevant provision.
PenaltyStatutory section under which imposed and amount confirmed.
Fee/other amountNature and legal basis of the amount.
Pre-depositAmount already paid for appeal, if applicable.
ReliefAny reduction already granted in the order or later appellate decision.

Section 78 determines the payment window for the amount payable pursuant to the order; it does not replace the provisions that determine each component.

12. Section 78 and appeal

Receiving a demand order does not mean that the taxpayer has lost the right to challenge it. The first appeal mechanism under Section 107 and subsequent appellate provisions may be available subject to statutory conditions.

However, a crucial distinction must be made:

  • Right to appeal is one question.
  • Whether recovery is stayed is another question.
  • Whether statutory pre-deposit has been made is another question.
  • Whether the demand remains recoverable depends on the applicable law and actual relief/stay.
Do not assume that filing an appeal by itself automatically means that every recovery consequence disappears. The taxpayer should verify the applicable stay and pre-deposit framework.

13. Section 78 and stay of demand

If a competent appellate authority or court grants a stay or other legally effective protection affecting recovery, the taxpayer should preserve that order and communicate it through the prescribed mechanism.

The demand tracker should contain:

✓ Appeal filing date
✓ Appeal acknowledgement
✓ Pre-deposit details
✓ Stay application status
✓ Stay order, if granted
✓ Conditions of stay
✓ Next hearing date
✓ Outstanding balance after relief

A stay should never be assumed from the mere filing of an appeal.

14. What happens after the deadline

If the amount payable under the order remains unpaid after the applicable Section 78 payment period, the matter can move into the recovery framework.

Section 79 contains the principal recovery machinery. Depending on the circumstances and the applicable Rules, recovery may involve measures such as:

  • deduction from money owed to the defaulter;
  • detention and sale of goods;
  • proceedings against third parties in appropriate circumstances;
  • attachment and sale of property;
  • recovery through a prescribed authority; and
  • other statutory recovery mechanisms.
Practical message: Section 78 should be treated as the last controlled payment window before the recovery provisions become increasingly important.

15. Section 78 vs Section 79

Section 78Section 79
Deals primarily with the time allowed for payment of amounts payable pursuant to an order.Deals with recovery of amounts that remain recoverable under the Act.
Normally provides a three-month payment period from service of order.Provides the statutory recovery methods.
May be modified by a valid Commissioner order in the circumstances permitted.Operates when an amount is recoverable and remains unpaid.

In simple terms: Section 78 gives the payment window; Section 79 provides the recovery tools.

16. DRC-07 and electronic demand

GST demand orders are accompanied by electronic summaries in the prescribed forms. Rule 142 deals with notices and orders for amounts payable under the Act and provides for electronic demand documentation.

DRC-07 is particularly important for finance teams because it represents the electronic summary of the demand/order in the GST system.

CBIC's Rules framework also uses DRC-03 for specified voluntary payments and DRC-04 as acknowledgement, while DRC-05 can conclude certain proceedings where the statutory conditions for payment before order are satisfied. citeturn0search17turn0search19

Do not treat the DRC form as a substitute for reading the complete adjudication order. The order determines the legal basis; the electronic summary helps implement and track the liability.

17. Electronic liability register

The GST payment framework maintains an electronic tax liability register in FORM GST PMT-01. Amounts payable through returns, orders and other statutory mechanisms are reflected in the electronic liability framework according to the Rules.

CBIC's payment rules explain that demand amounts are recorded in the electronic tax liability register and are reduced to the extent of relief granted by an appellate authority, Appellate Tribunal or court. citeturn0search2

For a finance team, this means the accounting ledger should be reconciled with:

  • the adjudication order;
  • DRC-07;
  • electronic liability register;
  • payments made; and
  • appeal relief, if any.

18. Instalment facility under Section 80

Section 78 should also be read with Section 80, which permits the Commissioner, subject to prescribed conditions, to allow payment of tax or other amounts due in instalments or grant additional time.

Rule 158 prescribes the application and order process. The Rules provide for an electronic application in FORM GST DRC-20, followed where approved by an order in FORM GST DRC-21. The Rules also prescribe conditions under which instalment facility is not available. citeturn0search21

This is different from the ordinary Section 78 three-month period. A business facing genuine cash-flow constraints should examine the Section 80 route early rather than waiting until recovery action starts.

19. Partial payment

A taxpayer may make payment of a demand in circumstances where the liability is partly undisputed, an appeal is being pursued for the balance, or an instalment arrangement has been authorised.

However, finance teams should not simply reduce the demand in their books because a partial payment was made.

Prepare a reconciliation showing:

ParticularAmount
Total order demandOriginal confirmed liability.
Amount paidActual payment made.
Appeal pre-depositAmount paid for appellate compliance, where applicable.
Relief grantedReduction under appellate order, if any.
Balance recoverableAmount remaining after valid adjustments.

20. Demand and refund adjustment

A taxpayer may have both a refund receivable and an outstanding GST demand. The existence of a refund claim does not automatically mean the demand disappears.

GST refund rules permit adjustment of sanctioned refund against outstanding demand in the circumstances prescribed by law. CBIC's refund guidance explains that partial or complete adjustment of a sanctioned refund against outstanding demand can be made through the prescribed refund order mechanism. citeturn0search20

Maintain a separate reconciliation of refund claims and outstanding demands. Never net them informally in the accounting records without confirming the statutory adjustment.

22. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Ordinary three-month period

A demand order is served on 15 September. The normal Section 78 payment period runs for three months from service. The finance team should record the corresponding deadline and complete the appeal/payment decision before the deadline.

Example 2 — Order dated earlier than service

An order is dated 10 September but is served on 15 September. Section 78 tracking should focus on the legally relevant service date rather than simply using the date printed on the order.

Example 3 — Commissioner specifies shorter period

The circumstances of a particular case justify a shorter payment period and the Commissioner issues a valid order under the proviso. The taxpayer should follow the special period specified in that order.

Example 4 — Commissioner allows longer period

A taxpayer faces circumstances justifying additional time and the Commissioner allows a longer period in accordance with Section 78. The demand tracker should be updated to the authorised deadline.

Example 5 — Appeal filed

A taxpayer files a first appeal against a ₹50 lakh demand. The tax team must separately track the appeal, statutory pre-deposit and any stay/recovery position. Filing the appeal should not be treated as an automatic cancellation of the demand.

Example 6 — Partial payment

A taxpayer pays ₹20 lakh against a ₹30 lakh demand. The remaining ₹10 lakh continues to require legal and recovery tracking unless valid relief or stay applies.

Example 7 — Instalment request

A taxpayer cannot arrange the entire amount immediately. The team examines Section 80 and Rule 158, prepares DRC-20 and seeks the applicable instalment facility rather than simply allowing the Section 78 deadline to expire.

Example 8 — Demand reduced on appeal

A ₹40 lakh order is reduced to ₹15 lakh by the appellate authority. The electronic liability should reflect the applicable relief and the finance team should reconcile the post-appeal balance.

Example 9 — Refund and demand coexist

A taxpayer has a ₹12 lakh sanctioned refund and a ₹7 lakh outstanding demand. The refund should not simply be netted in the books; the statutory adjustment mechanism and actual order should be checked.

Example 10 — Construction company demand

A contractor receives a demand relating to ITC on a project. The company should immediately create a demand file containing the SCN, reply, order, DRC-07, service evidence, Section 78 deadline, appeal papers and payment reconciliation.

Example 11 — Multi-GSTIN group

A group has 10 GST registrations and receives a demand under one GSTIN. The payment should be made and accounted for against the correct registration. Group-level netting without GSTIN-wise reconciliation can create further compliance problems.

Example 12 — Demand under Section 76

An order under Section 76 determines tax collected but not paid. Section 78 governs the payment window following the order, while the underlying liability remains governed by Section 76 and related provisions.

23. Construction and infrastructure examples

RA bill ITC demand

If a GST demand arises from ITC claimed on subcontractor RA bills, preserve the contract, work order, invoice, GSTR-2B/IMS evidence, payment proof, work certification and reconciliation used during adjudication.

Mobilisation advance dispute

If a demand concerns GST on mobilisation advances, maintain the advance register, receipt evidence, tax invoice, adjustment against RA bills and GSTR-1/GSTR-3B reconciliation.

Retention money

Where the dispute involves retention, maintain the contract clause, RA bills, certification dates, receipt dates and the tax treatment adopted.

Large project demand

For a large infrastructure project, the demand should be analysed GSTIN-wise and project-wise. A central demand tracker should identify which project, invoice population and tax issue generated the demand.

Multiple State projects

Where the business operates across States, ensure that payment is made from the correct GST registration and that the demand is not incorrectly settled from another State registration.

27. Common mistakes

  • Calculating the payment period from the order date instead of checking the service date.
  • Assuming every GST order automatically gives exactly three months regardless of a valid special order.
  • Assuming filing an appeal automatically stays recovery.
  • Ignoring the pre-deposit requirement for appeal.
  • Waiting until the Section 78 deadline before deciding whether to appeal.
  • Failing to reconcile DRC-07 with PMT-01.
  • Paying the demand from the wrong GSTIN.
  • Ignoring Section 80 instalment possibilities where genuinely relevant.
  • Informally netting refunds against demands.
  • Not updating the demand tracker after appellate relief.

29. Section 78 GST FAQs

What is Section 78 of the CGST Act?

Section 78 specifies the period within which an amount payable pursuant to a GST order must be paid and connects non-payment with the recovery framework.

How long do I get to pay a GST demand order?

The normal statutory period under Section 78 is three months from the date of service of the order, subject to the proviso allowing a different period in the circumstances permitted by law.

Does the three months start from the order date?

The statutory wording refers to the date of service of the order. Therefore, the service/communication date should be documented carefully.

Can the Commissioner reduce the three-month period?

Yes, the proviso to Section 78 permits the Commissioner, for reasons to be recorded in writing and in the circumstances permitted by the provision, to require payment within a shorter period.

Can the Commissioner give more than three months?

Yes, the proviso also permits a longer period in the circumstances and manner allowed by Section 78.

What happens if GST demand is not paid within three months?

The amount can become subject to the recovery provisions of Section 79, subject to any valid appeal relief, stay, extension, instalment order or other legal protection.

Does filing an appeal automatically stop recovery?

A taxpayer should not assume that merely filing an appeal automatically stops every recovery consequence. The applicable pre-deposit and stay framework should be checked.

What is DRC-07?

DRC-07 is the electronic summary used for specified GST demand orders. It should be reconciled with the complete adjudication order.

What is PMT-01?

PMT-01 is the electronic tax liability register under the GST payment rules. Demand and other liabilities are reflected in the electronic liability framework.

Can GST demand be paid in instalments?

Section 80 and Rule 158 provide a mechanism under which the Commissioner may allow additional time or instalments subject to prescribed conditions.

What form is used for an instalment application?

Rule 158 provides for FORM GST DRC-20 for the application and DRC-21 for the order allowing payment in instalments or additional time, subject to the Rule's conditions.

Does Section 78 itself waive interest for three months?

No. Section 78 is a payment-period provision. Interest must be examined under the applicable substantive provisions.

What is the difference between Section 78 and Section 79?

Section 78 primarily gives the payment period after an order; Section 79 contains the recovery machinery for amounts that remain recoverable and unpaid.

Can a refund be adjusted against a GST demand?

Where the statutory conditions are satisfied, sanctioned refunds can be adjusted against outstanding demands through the prescribed refund mechanism. The taxpayer should not make an informal accounting net-off.

Does Section 78 apply to Section 76 orders?

Yes, Section 78 governs the payment period for amounts payable pursuant to an order under the Act, while the underlying liability is determined under the relevant substantive provision.

Does Section 78 apply to Section 74A?

Section 78 is a general payment provision and operates alongside the applicable determination framework, including the current Section 74A framework for relevant tax periods.

What should a finance team do immediately after receiving a GST demand order?

Capture the service date, identify the demand period and section, reconcile tax/interest/penalty, calculate the Section 78 deadline, assess appeal and stay options, and decide payment or other lawful action well before the deadline.

31. Key takeaways

  • Section 78 is the payment-timing provision after a GST demand order.
  • The normal statutory payment period is three months from service of the order.
  • The Commissioner can prescribe a shorter or longer period in the circumstances permitted by the proviso.
  • The service date should be captured carefully because it drives the normal Section 78 deadline.
  • Section 78 does not itself determine whether the demand is legally correct.
  • Filing an appeal should not automatically be treated as an unconditional stay of recovery.
  • Section 79 provides the main recovery framework once recoverable amounts remain unpaid.
  • Section 80 may provide an instalment/additional-time route subject to conditions.
  • DRC-07 and PMT-01 should be reconciled with the actual demand order and books.
  • Refunds and demands should not be informally netted without checking the statutory adjustment mechanism.
  • A GST demand tracker should capture service date, payment deadline, appeal, stay, payment and balance.
