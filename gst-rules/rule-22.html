1. Rule 22 at a glance

Rule 22 of the CGST Rules, 2017 prescribes the procedure for cancellation of GST registration by the proper officer. It is different from Rule 20, which deals with an application for cancellation by the registered person, and Rule 21A, which deals with suspension of registration.

Where the proper officer has reasons to believe that a registration is liable to be cancelled under Section 29, Rule 22 provides the procedural path: issue of show-cause notice, taxpayer's reply, examination of the reply, and either cancellation of registration or dropping of proceedings.

Simple sequence: Rule 21 / Section 29 cancellation ground → Rule 21A suspension where applicable → Rule 22 notice and proceedings → REG-19 cancellation or REG-20 proceedings dropped.

2. Show-cause notice — FORM GST REG-17

Under Rule 22(1), where the proper officer has reasons to believe that the registration is liable to be cancelled under Section 29, the officer shall issue a notice in FORM GST REG-17.

The notice requires the registered person to show cause as to why the registration should not be cancelled. The current Rule 22 provides a period of seven working days from the date of service of the notice for the taxpayer to respond.

What should be checked immediately?

  • Date and mode of service of REG-17.
  • Exact reason or reasons stated in the notice.
  • Relevant tax period and GSTIN.
  • Whether the notice is connected with Rule 21 or Rule 21A.
  • Documents, returns, payments or registrations referred to in the notice.
  • Reply deadline calculated using the prescribed working-day period.
Do not ignore REG-17. Failure to submit a proper response can allow the cancellation proceedings to move forward. A response should address the actual allegation rather than merely requesting more time or stating that the business is compliant.

3. Reply to the show-cause notice

Rule 22(2) provides that the reply to the notice issued under sub-rule (1) is to be furnished in the prescribed GST reply form within the period specified in the notice.

A strong reply should establish a clear connection between the allegation and the supporting evidence. For example, if the allegation concerns non-functioning of the declared place of business, the reply should contain appropriate evidence of the actual place of business and the relevant registration records.

Recommended reply structure

  1. Reference: quote the GSTIN, notice number/date and tax period.
  2. Issue: reproduce the substance of the allegation in your own words.
  3. Facts: state what actually happened, with dates.
  4. Reconciliation: provide books/returns/data comparison wherever the issue is numerical.
  5. Evidence: identify each document attached and explain its relevance.
  6. Corrective action: state what has already been corrected, if anything.
  7. Request: request dropping of cancellation proceedings where the facts and evidence support it.
Best practice: Use an “allegation → facts → evidence → conclusion” format. This makes the officer's review easier and reduces the risk that a genuine explanation is buried inside a long narrative.

4. Cancellation order — FORM GST REG-19

Under Rule 22(3), where the person who applied for cancellation is no longer liable to be registered or the registration is otherwise liable to be cancelled, the proper officer shall issue an order in FORM GST REG-19.

The order is issued within the period prescribed by Rule 22(3), calculated from the relevant cancellation application or the reply to the show-cause notice, including the specific route linked to Rule 21A(2A) where applicable.

The cancellation order determines the effective date of cancellation and notifies the taxable person. The order can also direct payment of arrears of tax, interest or penalty, including the amount liable under Section 29(5).

Why the effective date matters

The effective date affects return obligations, tax invoices, ITC reversal/adjustment consequences, stock and capital-goods treatment, final return requirements and post-cancellation compliance. Finance teams should therefore record the exact effective date in the GST master and accounting records.

Important: Cancellation of registration does not erase earlier tax liabilities or statutory obligations. A business should reconcile all liabilities and pending compliance rather than treating the cancellation date as a complete closure of GST exposure.

5. When cancellation proceedings are dropped — FORM GST REG-20

Where the reply furnished by the taxpayer is found to be satisfactory, Rule 22(4) requires the proper officer to drop the proceedings and pass an order in FORM GST REG-20.

This is the practical objective of a well-supported response where the taxpayer has a valid explanation and evidence establishing that cancellation is not warranted.

For a business that has received a cancellation notice because of an apparent data mismatch, the response should therefore focus on proving the reason for the mismatch and showing that there is no continuing contravention requiring cancellation.

6. Special route for pending returns and payment

Rule 22 contains a specific proviso for proceedings arising from contravention of the relevant cancellation grounds under Section 29(2)(b) or Section 29(2)(c). Instead of replying to the notice under Rule 22(1), where the registered person furnishes all pending returns and makes full payment of the tax dues together with applicable interest and late fee, the proper officer is required to drop the proceedings and pass an order in FORM GST REG-20, subject to the conditions in the rule.

Practical implication: Where the notice is fundamentally linked to return non-filing or the specified default, immediately calculate the complete outstanding return and payment position. Do not assume that filing one missing return automatically resolves the entire cancellation proceeding.

7. Cancellation where the proprietor has died

Rule 22(5) provides that the provisions of Rule 22(3) apply, with necessary changes, to the legal heirs of a deceased proprietor as if the cancellation application had been submitted by the proprietor himself.

This is relevant where a proprietorship business needs to be closed following the proprietor's death. The legal heirs should coordinate the cancellation application, pending return and tax-liability position, and documentary requirements rather than simply abandoning the GST registration.

8. Connection between Rule 21A and Rule 22

Rule 21A and Rule 22 should be read together when a registration has been suspended. Rule 21A can suspend the registration pending completion of cancellation proceedings under Rule 22.

SituationRelevant provisionPractical action
Registration appears liable to cancellationSection 29 / Rule 21Identify and correct the underlying issue.
Registration suspended pending proceedingsRule 21ARespect suspension restrictions and prepare the required explanation/compliance.
Formal cancellation proceedingsRule 22Respond to REG-17 and provide evidence.
Reply acceptedRule 22(4)Proceedings dropped through REG-20.
Cancellation orderedRule 22(3)Comply with cancellation-related obligations and consider Rule 23 where revocation is legally available.
Key distinction: A suspension status is not itself the final cancellation. The Rule 22 proceedings determine whether the registration is ultimately cancelled or the proceedings are dropped.

9. Practical business examples

Example 1 — Non-filing of returns

A company has several pending GST returns and receives a cancellation notice. The finance team should prepare a complete return-wise schedule, file the pending returns, calculate tax/interest/late fee, make the required payments and preserve the evidence. Where the specific statutory conditions are satisfied, Rule 22 provides a route for dropping the proceedings.

Example 2 — GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B difference

A construction company has ₹5 crore of outward supplies in GSTR-1 but ₹4.4 crore reported in GSTR-3B. Before replying, the team should reconcile invoices, credit notes, amendments, advances, timing differences and classification issues. A bare statement that “the difference is due to an error” is weaker than an invoice-level reconciliation with corrective evidence.

Example 3 — Declared premises issue

An officer believes the taxpayer is not conducting business from the declared place. The taxpayer should establish the factual position using relevant registration records, ownership/lease documents, utility evidence, site records and business activity evidence, depending on the facts.

Example 4 — Construction project at multiple sites

A contractor operates multiple project locations. A project site being temporary or mobile does not by itself answer the registration question. The taxpayer should review the exact registration structure, declared places, contractual arrangements and actual business operations before responding to a cancellation allegation.

12. Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the seven-working-day response period in REG-17.
  • Submitting a generic reply without addressing the exact cancellation ground.
  • Giving explanations without supporting documentary evidence.
  • Failing to reconcile GSTR-1, GSTR-1A where applicable, GSTR-3B and books.
  • Not checking the GST registration effective date and historical amendments.
  • Assuming that payment of one amount automatically closes the cancellation proceeding.
  • Failing to preserve the final REG-19 or REG-20 order.
  • Treating cancellation as extinguishing earlier tax liabilities.

13. FAQs

What is REG-17?

FORM GST REG-17 is the show-cause notice issued under Rule 22 when the proper officer has reasons to believe that registration is liable to cancellation under Section 29.

How much time is available to reply to REG-17?

Rule 22(1) provides seven working days from the date of service of the notice.

What is REG-19?

FORM GST REG-19 is the order used for cancellation of registration under Rule 22(3).

What is REG-20?

FORM GST REG-20 is the order used for dropping cancellation proceedings where the taxpayer's reply is found satisfactory, including the specific statutory route for certain return/payment defaults.

Can cancellation proceedings be dropped after filing pending returns?

For the specific statutory situation covered by the Rule 22 proviso, the rule provides for dropping proceedings when the required pending returns are furnished and the applicable tax dues, interest and late fee are fully paid.

Does cancellation remove old GST liabilities?

No. Cancellation does not by itself erase liabilities or obligations arising for periods before cancellation. The effective cancellation date and outstanding compliance must be reviewed carefully.

What should I do if registration is suspended and REG-17 is also received?

Treat them as connected but distinct procedural events: comply with the suspension restrictions under Rule 21A, then respond fully to the Rule 22 cancellation proceedings within the prescribed time.

Can a cancelled registration be restored?

In eligible cases, revocation of cancellation is governed by Rule 23. The applicable eligibility, timelines and compliance requirements should be checked against the reason for cancellation.

14. Compliance checklist

  • ☐ Record the REG-17 service date.
  • ☐ Calculate the seven-working-day response deadline.
  • ☐ Identify every allegation and applicable GST provision.
  • ☐ Reconcile books with GST returns and payment records.
  • ☐ Check pending returns and tax/interest/late-fee exposure.
  • ☐ Prepare evidence for each allegation.
  • ☐ Submit a point-by-point response and retain acknowledgement.
  • ☐ Track whether proceedings are dropped through REG-20 or cancellation is ordered through REG-19.
  • ☐ If cancellation occurs, record the effective date and evaluate further compliance and Rule 23 options where applicable.
