1. Why Section 44 matters

Searches such as “Section 44 CGST Act”, “GST annual return”, “GSTR-9”, “GSTR-9C”, “GST annual return due date”, “who has to file GSTR-9” and “GST annual reconciliation” all lead to the annual-compliance framework created by Section 44 and the Rules.

Section 44 is the statutory bridge between the transactions recorded during the financial year and the annual GST position reported after the year closes. It is not merely a form-filing provision. A good annual return requires reconciliation of books, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B, electronic ledgers, credit/debit notes, advances, amendments and tax payments.

Core idea: Section 44 requires the applicable registered person to furnish an annual return containing the prescribed details of supplies made/received during the financial year, subject to the exclusions, forms, thresholds and conditions prescribed under the Act, Rules and notifications.

3. Current statutory framework

Section 44 requires every registered person, subject to the prescribed exceptions and conditions, to furnish an annual return for every financial year electronically in the prescribed form and manner within the prescribed time.

The exact annual-return filing population and form requirements must be read together with Rule 80, the relevant notifications and the instructions applicable to the particular financial year.

Do not use an old GSTR-9 article blindly: GST annual-return rules have changed several times. Exemptions, GSTR-9C requirements, reporting tables and due dates have differed by financial year.

4. What is a GST annual return?

A GST annual return is a consolidated year-level statement of the taxpayer's GST activity. It brings together information that has already been reported through periodic returns and related statements.

The annual return should therefore be viewed as a reconciliation exercise, not as an opportunity to reconstruct the books for the first time.

Sales
Reconcile books with GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
Purchases
Reconcile purchase register and ITC data.
ITC
Reconcile GSTR-2B, GSTR-3B and eligible ITC.
Tax
Reconcile tax liability, payment and ledgers.

5. GSTR-9

Rule 80 provides the framework for furnishing the annual return electronically in FORM GSTR-9 for applicable registered persons. The prescribed form captures financial-year information relating to outward and inward supplies, tax payable/paid, ITC and related adjustments. citeturn0search4turn0search5

The annual return is therefore best prepared from a controlled reconciliation workbook or ERP rather than by simply downloading figures from one GST return.

6. Who is excluded?

The annual-return framework has specific exclusions. Rule 80 has historically excluded persons such as:

  • Input Service Distributors;
  • persons paying tax under Section 51 (TDS);
  • persons paying tax under Section 52 (TCS);
  • casual taxable persons; and
  • non-resident taxable persons.

The exact filing requirement for composition taxpayers and other special categories must be checked for the relevant financial year because the prescribed form and exemptions have changed over time.

Practical rule: determine annual-return applicability financial-year by financial-year. Do not rely on a generic statement such as “every GST registered person must file GSTR-9”.

7. Annual-return due date

Section 44 provides the statutory annual-return framework, while the Rules prescribe the operational form and timing. The standard statutory framework has historically placed the annual return due date on 31 December following the end of the financial year, subject to extensions or special notifications.

For example, for FY 2025-26, the normal statutory calendar would point to 31 December 2026, unless the Government changes or extends the applicable due date.

Historical GST annual returns have frequently received extensions. CBIC records, for example, show extensions for FY 2017-18, FY 2018-19 and FY 2019-20. citeturn0search6turn0search2

8. GSTR-9C and reconciliation

GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement rather than simply another version of GSTR-9. It reconciles the annual GST figures with the taxpayer's financial statements and provides explanations for significant differences.

The requirement, threshold and certification framework for GSTR-9C have changed over time. Therefore the applicable year's Rule 80 and notification must be checked.

Important current-law distinction: the historical requirement for a CA/CMA “GST audit” under Section 35(5) is no longer the current statutory audit mechanism because Section 35(5) was omitted with effect from 1 August 2021. Do not describe GSTR-9C as the old Section 35(5) audit requirement.

12. GSTR-9 table architecture

The prescribed GSTR-9 form is designed around broad categories of annual GST information. The form historically includes sections for basic details, outward/inward supplies, ITC, tax paid and other information/adjustments. 13. Turnover reconciliation

Turnover is one of the most important annual-return controls. The finance team should reconcile:

Books turnover
Revenue as per audited/management financial statements.
GST turnover
Taxable, exempt, zero-rated and other GST reporting categories.
Credit/debit notes
Year-level adjustments.
Non-GST items
Items included in books but outside GST turnover treatment.

Differences can arise from timing, advances, deemed supplies, branch/distinct-person transactions, exports, SEZ supplies, credit notes, year-end accruals and accounting classification.

14. Outward-supply reconciliation

A robust annual return should start from the sales ledger and reconcile every GST registration separately.

SourceCheck
Sales registerInvoice population and taxable value.
GSTR-1Invoice-level outward reporting.
GSTR-3BTax liability actually declared.
Credit/debit notesAdjustments after original invoices.
E-invoice dataIndependent invoice-level validation where applicable.

15. ITC reconciliation

Annual ITC reconciliation should bridge three numbers:

ITC available/eligible → ITC claimed in GSTR-3B → ITC requiring reversal or adjustment.

The reconciliation should separately identify:

  • GSTR-2B matched ITC;
  • current-year invoices appearing later;
  • prior-year invoices claimed in the current year;
  • credit notes;
  • Rule 37/37A reversals;
  • Section 17 reversals;
  • RCM ITC;
  • ISD credit;
  • imports; and
  • ineligible or blocked credit.

16. Tax-paid reconciliation

Annual return preparation should reconcile tax liability with actual payment across IGST, CGST, SGST/UTGST and cess.

Compare the annual tax liability from GSTR-3B with the electronic cash and credit ledgers and investigate differences caused by amendments, reverse charge, interest, late fees, DRC-03 payments or other adjustments.

17. Credit/debit notes

Credit and debit notes are frequent sources of annual-return differences because the accounting entry, GST reporting date and original invoice year may differ.

The annual return team should maintain an invoice-to-note mapping and determine the financial-year treatment according to the applicable Section 34 and reporting rules.

18. Previous-year adjustments

GST annual returns can contain information that relates to transactions originating in an earlier financial year but reported or adjusted in the current year.

For every such adjustment, maintain a bridge showing:

  • original financial year;
  • original invoice/return reference;
  • current-year reporting;
  • tax impact;
  • ITC impact; and
  • reason for the timing difference.

19. Books vs GST returns

The annual return should not be prepared by forcing the books to equal GST returns. Differences should be identified, quantified, explained and, where legally required, corrected.

DifferencePossible cause
Books sales higherNon-GST income, timing or unreported supply.
GSTR-1 higherDuplicate invoice, amendment or accounting omission.
GSTR-3B tax differsReturn-period timing or tax-head error.
ITC books higherIneligible/missing/documentation issue.
GSTR-2B higherUnbooked purchases, supplier timing or wrong GSTIN.

20. Construction and infrastructure examples

Annual-return preparation is particularly important for construction, mining, roads, irrigation and infrastructure businesses because project accounting, RA bills, advances, retention, subcontractors and multiple GST registrations can create significant timing differences.

21. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — sales reconciliation

Books show ₹100 crore revenue while GST returns show ₹98 crore taxable turnover. The annual-return team investigates ₹2 crore of exempt/non-GST revenue and timing differences before finalising the return.

Example 2 — GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B

GSTR-1 contains a taxable invoice omitted from the corresponding GSTR-3B. The annual reconciliation identifies the short-paid liability and determines the appropriate correction.

Example 3 — ITC mismatch

Purchase register shows ₹8 crore ITC while GSTR-2B shows ₹7.5 crore. The ₹50 lakh difference is classified into timing, supplier reporting, ineligible and documentation categories.

Example 4 — prior-year invoice

An FY 2024-25 invoice becomes eligible/claimed in FY 2025-26. The annual return working separately tracks the prior-year origin and current-year claim.

Example 5 — credit note

A supplier issues a credit note in the following financial year. The company links it to the original invoice and records the corresponding ITC adjustment according to the applicable law.

Example 6 — RCM

RCM tax paid through GSTR-3B is separately reconciled with the RCM register and subsequent ITC claim.

Example 7 — import IGST

Books show imported goods while the customs/ICEGATE data and GSTR-2B import data show a different amount. The difference is investigated before annual-return finalisation.

Example 8 — multiple GSTINs

A company has registrations in several States. Annual-return workbooks are prepared GSTIN-wise and then reviewed at entity level.

Example 9 — GSTR-9C

Where the current financial year's threshold makes GSTR-9C applicable, the reconciliation starts from audited financial statements and bridges turnover and tax differences to GST returns.

Example 10 — DRC-03 payment

A tax difference identified after periodic returns is paid through DRC-03. The annual-return working separately documents the reason, period and payment so that the annual reconciliation remains auditable.

23. GST Council background

The GST Council and the Government have repeatedly modified the annual-return framework to balance detailed reconciliation with compliance burden. The history includes changes to forms, thresholds, certification requirements, filing dates and exemptions.

This is why annual-return research should always identify the financial year before applying a threshold or form requirement.

27. Common mistakes

  • Assuming every registered person automatically has the same GSTR-9 requirement.
  • Using an old year's GSTR-9C threshold for a current financial year.
  • Preparing GSTR-9 directly from GSTR-3B without books reconciliation.
  • Ignoring GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B differences.
  • Ignoring credit notes reported after the original invoice year.
  • Not reconciling GSTR-2B and ITC claimed.
  • Confusing Section 35(5) historical GST audit with the current GSTR-9C framework.
  • Consolidating multiple GSTINs without GSTIN-wise reconciliation.
  • Failing to document explanations for material differences.
  • Assuming an extension granted for one financial year automatically applies to another.

28. Section 44 GST FAQs

What is Section 44 of the CGST Act?

Section 44 is the statutory provision governing the GST annual return for applicable registered persons.

What is the GST annual return form?

For applicable regular taxpayers, the prescribed annual return is generally FORM GSTR-9 under Rule 80, subject to the exemptions and financial-year-specific notifications.

Who has to file GSTR-9?

Applicability must be determined under Section 44, Rule 80 and the notification applicable to the relevant financial year. Certain categories such as ISD, TDS/TCS persons, casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons are excluded under the prescribed framework.

What is the due date for GSTR-9?

The standard statutory annual-return framework provides for filing by 31 December following the end of the financial year, subject to any Government extension or special notification.

What is GSTR-9C?

GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement that reconciles the GST annual-return figures with the financial statements where the applicable turnover threshold and rules require it.

Is GSTR-9C the same as GST audit?

No. The old Section 35(5) GST-audit provision was omitted from 1 August 2021. Current GSTR-9C requirements should be determined under the operative Section 44/Rule 80 framework and applicable notifications.

What turnover is relevant for GSTR-9C?

The threshold has changed over time. It must be checked for the specific financial year under Rule 80 and the applicable notification.

Can GSTR-9 figures differ from GSTR-3B?

Yes. GSTR-9 is an annual consolidation and can contain year-level adjustments and prescribed reporting differences. Material differences should be reconciled and explained.

How is GSTR-9 reconciled with books?

Prepare a books-to-GSTR-1 turnover bridge, GSTR-1-to-GSTR-3B tax bridge, purchase-register-to-GSTR-2B ITC bridge and tax-ledger reconciliation.

Should GSTR-9 be prepared GSTIN-wise?

Yes. GST returns are registration-specific, so a multi-State entity should prepare and review the annual return separately for each applicable GSTIN.

What documents are needed for GSTR-9?

Typically sales and purchase registers, financial statements, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B, credit/debit notes, ledgers, RCM workings, import data and reconciliation schedules.

Can previous-year transactions appear in current-year GSTR-9?

Yes, depending on the reporting and adjustment provisions applicable to the relevant financial years. Such items should be separately tracked and documented.

Does Section 44 require an audit?

Section 44 itself establishes the annual-return framework. The current GSTR-9C/reconciliation requirements must be checked separately under the operative Rule 80 and applicable notifications. The historical Section 35(5) GST-audit provision is no longer operative.

30. Key takeaways

  • Section 44 is the statutory foundation for GST annual-return compliance.
  • GSTR-9 is the principal annual-return form for applicable regular taxpayers, subject to the relevant financial-year rules and exemptions.
  • Annual-return applicability should be checked financial-year by financial-year.
  • The standard statutory annual-return due date is 31 December following the financial year, subject to extensions.
  • GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement and its threshold has changed over time.
  • The historical Section 35(5) GST-audit requirement was omitted from 1 August 2021.
  • Annual return preparation should reconcile books, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B and electronic ledgers.
  • Multi-GSTIN businesses should maintain GSTIN-wise annual-return workings.
  • Do not carry forward old GSTR-9/GSTR-9C thresholds or reporting rules without checking the relevant financial year.
