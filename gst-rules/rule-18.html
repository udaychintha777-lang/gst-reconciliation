Rule 18 – Display of Registration Certificate and GSTIN

A practical guide to the GST requirement for displaying the registration certificate and GSTIN at the principal and additional places of business. Understand exactly what must be displayed, where it must be displayed, practical controls for offices and project sites, audit relevance and common mistakes.

1. What is Rule 18?

Rule 18 of the CGST Rules deals with the physical display of the GST registration certificate and the GSTIN at the registered places of business.

The rule has only two sub-rules, but the practical importance is high because it creates a visible compliance requirement for registered businesses.

Simple meaning: If a business is GST registered, it should display its registration certificate prominently at its principal place of business and at every additional place of business. The GSTIN must also be displayed on the name board at the entry of each such place.

2. Statutory text – Rule 18

ProvisionRequirement
Rule 18(1)Every registered person shall display the certificate of registration in a prominent location at the principal place of business and at every additional place or places of business.
Rule 18(2)Every registered person shall display the GSTIN on the name board exhibited at the entry of the principal place of business and at every additional place or places of business.

Therefore, the rule creates two separate but connected requirements:

  1. display the registration certificate; and
  2. display the GSTIN on the entry name board.

3. What exactly must be displayed?

A. Registration certificate

The certificate of registration should be displayed in a prominent location. The practical objective is that the registration status is visibly available at the registered business premises.

B. GSTIN

The GSTIN must be displayed on the name board exhibited at the entry of the principal place and every additional place of business.

Best practical approach: Keep a current printed copy of the registration certificate at the reception/entry area or another clearly visible location, and print the correct GSTIN on the business name board.

4. Principal place of business vs additional place of business

LocationCertificate displayGSTIN on name board
Principal place of businessYesYes
Additional place of businessYesYes

The requirement follows the places recorded in the registration framework. A business with multiple registered locations should therefore maintain a location-wise compliance register rather than assuming that displaying the certificate only at the head office is sufficient.

5. Example – Construction company with project offices

Consider a construction company with a registered head office and several project offices that have been declared as additional places of business under the relevant GST registration.

The finance/compliance team should ensure that:

  • the current registration certificate is available at the principal place;
  • the current certificate is displayed at each additional registered place;
  • the correct GSTIN is displayed on the name board at each location;
  • location-specific GSTINs are not accidentally interchanged; and
  • old certificates are removed when registration particulars are amended.
Important: A construction company may have many physical project locations, but the Rule 18 requirement should be mapped to the locations that are actually registered/declared as places of business. Do not automatically treat every temporary worksite as an additional place without first examining the GST registration position.

6. Example – Multiple GST registrations in different States

A company operates in Telangana, Karnataka and Maharashtra and has separate GST registrations in each State.

The GSTIN displayed at each registered location should correspond to the GST registration applicable to that location. A common mistake is to use the corporate head-office GSTIN on every signboard even where the local premises are covered by a different GSTIN.

LocationCorrect control
Telangana registered premisesDisplay the Telangana GSTIN applicable to that registration.
Karnataka registered premisesDisplay the Karnataka GSTIN applicable to that registration.
Maharashtra registered premisesDisplay the Maharashtra GSTIN applicable to that registration.

7. Why is the name-board requirement important?

The GSTIN on the name board provides a simple public-facing identification of the registered business at its premises.

It can help customers, vendors, departmental officers and other stakeholders identify the GST registration associated with the premises.

It also reduces the risk of staff, vendors or customers using an incorrect GSTIN in invoices and other documentation.

8. What does “prominent location” practically mean?

The rule uses the expression prominent location for the registration certificate. The safest compliance practice is to place it somewhere easily visible and accessible at the registered premises rather than storing it only in an internal file.

For a conventional office, this could be the reception area, accounts/compliance area or another clearly visible location. For a factory, warehouse or project office, the business should choose a practical visible location that is appropriate to the premises and can be produced during an inspection.

Practical principle: The objective is visibility and easy verification—not merely possession of a PDF somewhere on a computer.

9. What if the registration certificate is amended?

Registration particulars can change during the life of a business—for example, a change in address, legal name or other registration details.

When the registration certificate is amended, the business should update the displayed copy and ensure that the GSTIN/name-board information remains correct.

This is particularly important after:

  • change in principal place of business;
  • addition or deletion of an additional place;
  • change in legal name;
  • amendment of other registration particulars; or
  • any event resulting in a new registration or cancellation.

10. Rule 18 and GST registration amendments

Rule 18 should be read together with the provisions dealing with amendment of registration. When a registered person's details change, the compliance team should not stop at submitting the amendment application.

After the amendment is approved, the business should also complete the physical compliance step:

  1. download/obtain the current certificate;
  2. verify the registration details;
  3. replace the old displayed certificate;
  4. verify the GSTIN on the name board; and
  5. update the location compliance checklist.

11. Digital copy vs physical display

A business may maintain the registration certificate digitally for internal records and operational convenience. However, Rule 18 specifically requires display of the certificate at the registered place of business.

Therefore: Keeping only a PDF in the accounting department's computer is not the same thing as completing the physical display requirement.

A sensible system is to maintain both:

  • a controlled digital copy in the GST compliance folder; and
  • a current displayed copy at the relevant registered premises.

12. Audit, inspection and departmental relevance

Rule 18 is a straightforward compliance requirement, but it can become relevant during a departmental visit or physical verification of business premises.

A department officer may compare:

  • the physical premises;
  • the name board;
  • GSTIN displayed at the premises;
  • registration certificate;
  • address recorded in GST registration;
  • books and invoices; and
  • other evidence establishing that business is actually being conducted from the declared place.
Important: A display issue by itself should not be confused with the separate legal question of whether the premises qualifies as a place of business or whether registration is otherwise valid. Those are different compliance questions.

13. Physical verification – do not confuse Rule 18 with Rule 25

Rule 18 is about display. It is not the rule that creates the general physical-verification procedure.

Businesses sometimes see older GST material referring to physical verification under a different rule number. The current numbering should be checked before quoting a rule in a notice reply, legal opinion or compliance document.

Rule-number accuracy matters: Rule 18 = display of certificate and GSTIN. Physical verification provisions are dealt with separately under the current rules.

14. Common mistakes under Rule 18

  1. Displaying the certificate only at the head office when additional registered places also exist.
  2. Displaying an outdated registration certificate.
  3. Printing an incorrect GSTIN on the name board.
  4. Using one State's GSTIN at another State's registered premises.
  5. Keeping the certificate only in digital form without physical display.
  6. Removing the name board during renovation and not restoring the GSTIN display.
  7. Failing to update displays after registration amendments.
  8. Assuming a temporary worksite automatically requires a separate GSTIN or that every worksite is automatically an additional place of business.

16. ERP and finance-team controls

For businesses with many branches, factories, warehouses or project offices, Rule 18 can be incorporated into the GST master-data process.

  • Maintain a GST registration master for every State registration.
  • Maintain a place-of-business master linked to the relevant GSTIN.
  • Store the latest REG-06 certificate electronically.
  • Trigger a compliance task whenever an address or registration amendment is approved.
  • Require site/admin confirmation that the certificate and GSTIN are displayed.
  • Perform periodic photograph-based verification for large multi-location organisations.

17. Practical example – amendment of registered address

A company shifts its principal place of business and the GST registration is amended accordingly.

The finance team completes the GST amendment but forgets to replace the old certificate at the office and leaves the old GSTIN/name-board information in place.

The better process is:

  1. approve the amendment;
  2. download the updated certificate;
  3. replace the old certificate at the premises;
  4. verify the name board; and
  5. close the location-level compliance task.

  • ☐ GSTIN displayed on the entry name board at principal place.

20. Frequently Asked Questions

Where must the GST registration certificate be displayed?

At a prominent location at the principal place of business and at every additional place or places of business.

Where must GSTIN be displayed?

On the name board exhibited at the entry of the principal place of business and every additional place of business.

Is displaying GSTIN only on invoices enough?

No. Invoice disclosure and the physical name-board requirement are separate compliance requirements.

Can we keep only a digital copy of the certificate?

A digital copy is useful for internal records, but Rule 18 specifically requires the certificate to be displayed at the registered place of business.

What if the company has several additional places of business?

The certificate display and GSTIN name-board requirements apply at each additional place covered by the rule.

What happens if the certificate has been amended?

The business should replace the displayed copy with the current certificate and ensure the name-board GSTIN/details remain accurate.

Does Rule 18 itself decide whether a project site is an additional place of business?

No. The classification of a location as a place of business is a separate question under the GST registration framework. Rule 18 then applies to the registered principal/additional places.

22. Practical takeaway

Rule 18 is simple but operationally important: display the current GST registration certificate prominently at the principal and every additional place of business, and display the correct GSTIN on the entry name board at each such location. Multi-location businesses should treat this as a location-wise compliance control rather than a one-time head-office activity.
