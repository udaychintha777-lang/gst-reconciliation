CGST ACT • CHAPTER II — ADMINISTRATION

Section 3 — Officers under this Act

Section 3 establishes the statutory framework for the classes of Central Tax officers who administer the CGST Act. It is the starting point for understanding who may be appointed as Central Tax officers and how the administrative structure of the CGST law is constituted.

Section 3 at a Glance

ProvisionSection 3
ChapterChapter II — Administration
SubjectOfficers under the Act

Section 3 is an administrative provision. It does not itself determine a taxpayer's tax rate, ITC eligibility or liability. Instead, it creates the statutory framework under which the Government appoints the classes of officers responsible for administering the CGST Act.

Simple way to understand it: Section 3 answers “which classes of Central Tax officers exist under the Act”; Sections 4 and 5 then deal with appointment and powers in greater operational detail.

1. What is the purpose of Section 3?

A tax law needs an administrative structure through which registrations, assessments, audits, investigations, adjudication, refunds and other statutory functions can be performed. Section 3 provides the basic statutory recognition of the classes of Central Tax officers.

The section authorises the Government to appoint officers by notification. It therefore works together with the appointment notifications issued under the CGST Act and with the subsequent provisions dealing with appointment, powers, delegation and jurisdiction.

Importantly, merely identifying a designation under Section 3 does not mean that every officer can exercise every power under the Act in every case. The particular power, the officer to whom it is assigned, jurisdiction, delegation and any conditions or limitations must be examined from the Act and the relevant orders or notifications.

2. Classes of Officers Covered by Section 3

ClauseClass of officerBroad administrative role
(a)Principal Chief Commissioners of Central Tax / Principal Directors General of Central TaxSenior-most levels of the Central Tax administrative structure.
(b)Chief Commissioners of Central Tax / Directors General of Central TaxSenior administrative and specialised functions within Central Tax.
(c)Principal Commissioners of Central Tax / Principal Additional Directors General of Central TaxPrincipal-level field or specialised administration.
(d)Commissioners of Central Tax / Additional Directors General of Central TaxCommissioner-level administration and functions assigned under the Act.
(e)Additional Commissioners / Additional Directors of Central TaxSenior operational and supervisory functions.
(f)Joint Commissioners / Joint Directors of Central TaxSupervisory and statutory functions as assigned.
(g)Deputy Commissioners / Deputy Directors of Central TaxOperational and adjudication/compliance functions as assigned.
(h)Assistant Commissioners / Assistant Directors of Central TaxOperational, assessment, scrutiny and other functions as assigned.
(i)Any other class of officers as the Government may deem fitProvides flexibility to create or recognise additional classes when required for administration.

The section therefore creates a hierarchy but does not mean that the hierarchy alone determines the jurisdiction of an officer. Jurisdiction and assignment of specific statutory functions must be traced to the relevant notification or order.

3. Appointment by Notification

The operative words in Section 3 are that the Government shall, by notification, appoint the specified classes of officers for the purposes of the Act.

This is important because the statutory provision creates the framework, while the notification puts the administrative structure into operation. The notification can identify the officers, their jurisdiction and the powers/functions that are vested in them, subject to the Act.

Practical compliance point: When challenging or examining an action taken by a tax officer, do not stop at the officer's designation. Check the statutory provision under which the power is exercised and the applicable appointment/jurisdiction/function notification.

4. Original Commencement and the First Officer Appointment Notification

Section 3 was brought into force from 22 June 2017 through Notification No. 01/2017-Central Tax dated 19 June 2017. The same commencement notification brought several administrative provisions into force before the main GST charging and compliance provisions commenced from 1 July 2017.

Immediately after this, Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax dated 19 June 2017 was issued under Section 3 read with Section 5 of the CGST Act and Section 3 of the IGST Act. It appointed the specified classes of Central Tax officers and vested them with powers under the CGST and IGST Acts and rules in relation to the jurisdictions specified in its tables.

Why Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax matters

This was not merely a list of designations. It established the initial jurisdictional framework for Central Tax administration. The notification also covered specialised designations such as Central Tax Audit and Central Tax Appeals officers and the officers subordinate to them.

The notification was subsequently amended over time to reflect changes in administrative jurisdiction and officer structure. Therefore, the original 2017 notification should not be treated as the final jurisdiction notification for every later period.

5. Were the Words of Section 3 Subsequently Amended?

The important point for Section 3 is that the substantive wording of the section has remained substantially unchanged since its commencement. The current statutory text continues to contain the same classes of Central Tax officers and the deeming proviso concerning officers appointed under the Central Excise Act, 1944.

That does not mean the administration under Section 3 remained static. The practical appointment, jurisdiction and assignment of functions have been modified through notifications and orders. These administrative instruments should be distinguished from an amendment to the text of Section 3 itself.

Important distinction: A notification amending the jurisdiction of a Commissionerate or assigning a function to a particular class of officer is not automatically an “amendment to Section 3”. For legal research, identify whether the change altered the Act, the appointment notification, the jurisdiction notification, or merely an internal assignment.

6. GST Council / Legislative Background

Section 3 was part of the original GST administrative architecture. During the development of the GST legislation, the draft provision evolved before enactment. In the 12th GST Council meeting materials, the wording of Section 3 was changed from the draft expression “specify” to “appoint”, aligning the provision with the statutory concept of appointment.

The enacted provision consequently uses the expression that the Government shall, by notification, appoint the specified classes of officers.

This is a useful illustration of the difference between legislative development and later amendment: discussion in Council materials may explain how the drafting evolved, but the enacted Act and operative notifications determine the legal position.

7. Circulars and Administrative Clarifications

Circular No. 3/3/2017-GST dated 5 July 2017

This circular is particularly relevant to the practical administration of the CGST Act. It assigned specified functions as “proper officer” to particular classes/designations of Central Tax officers for provisions other than registration and composition, using the framework of Section 2(91), Section 5 and the relevant provisions of the CGST Act.

The circular demonstrates an important practical point: the designation of an officer under Section 3 and the assignment of a particular statutory function are related but distinct questions.

Jurisdiction notifications and later amendments

Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax and subsequent amendments have been used to define and modify Central Tax jurisdiction. These instruments become particularly important when a taxpayer needs to identify the correct Commissionerate, Division or Range or examine which officer has jurisdiction over a particular registered person.

8. Section 3 vs. “Proper Officer”

One of the most important practical distinctions is between an officer under Section 3 and a proper officer.

Section 3 establishes classes of Central Tax officers. The term “proper officer” is separately defined in Section 2(91) and the specific statutory function can be assigned to an officer by the Board or Government through the relevant legal instrument, depending upon the provision.

QuestionWhat to check
Is this person a Central Tax officer under the Act?Section 3 and the applicable appointment notification.
Does this officer have jurisdiction over the taxpayer?Current jurisdiction notification/order.
Can this officer exercise the particular statutory power?The relevant section plus assignment/authorisation of that function.
Can the officer exercise the power through delegation?Section 5, relevant delegation order and statutory limitations.
Is the officer acting as “proper officer”?Section 2(91) and the applicable assignment of the function.

9. Practical Examples

Example 1 — GST notice received from an officer

A company receives a notice from an Assistant Commissioner. The company should not conclude that the notice is valid merely because the designation appears in Section 3. The legal review should identify the section under which the notice was issued, whether that function was assigned to the officer, and whether the officer had jurisdiction over the taxpayer.

Example 2 — Wrong Commissionerate concern

A business changes its principal place of business from one jurisdiction to another. A dispute then arises about which Central Tax office should handle a proceeding. Section 3 alone does not answer the question. The current jurisdiction notification and the registered person's GST jurisdiction have to be checked.

Example 3 — Audit proceedings

A registered person receives a GST audit communication. The finance team should identify the officer/designation, the legal authority for the audit, the jurisdiction and the relevant appointment/assignment framework. A generic statement such as “GST officer has asked for records” is not enough for a strong compliance file.

Example 4 — Appeal vs adjudication

An officer involved in adjudication and an officer handling an appeal do not automatically exercise the same powers. The officer structure, the specific statutory provision and the relevant assignment/jurisdiction notification should be checked separately.

Example 5 — Old 2017 matter

Suppose a taxpayer is defending a proceeding relating to July 2017. The officer structure and jurisdiction applicable to the historical period may need to be established from the notifications then in force. Current administrative arrangements should not automatically be projected backward.

Example 6 — Delegated power

A senior officer delegates a statutory function to a subordinate officer. The taxpayer should verify the delegation against Section 5 and the relevant order, rather than assuming that the subordinate officer can exercise every power available to the superior officer.

11. Statutory Text of Section 3

The provision is reproduced below for ease of reference:

3. Officers under this Act.—

The Government shall, by notification, appoint the following classes of officers for the
purposes of this Act, namely:—

(a) Principal Chief Commissioners of Central Tax or Principal Directors General of Central Tax,

(b) Chief Commissioners of Central Tax or Directors General of Central Tax,

(c) Principal Commissioners of Central Tax or Principal Additional Directors General of Central Tax,

(d) Commissioners of Central Tax or Additional Directors General of Central Tax,

(e) Additional Commissioners of Central Tax or Additional Directors of Central Tax,

(f) Joint Commissioners of Central Tax or Joint Directors of Central Tax,

(g) Deputy Commissioners of Central Tax or Deputy Directors of Central Tax,

(h) Assistant Commissioners of Central Tax or Assistant Directors of Central Tax, and

(i) any other class of officers as it may deem fit:

Provided that the officers appointed under the Central Excise Act, 1944 (1 of 1944) shall be
deemed to be the officers appointed under the provisions of this Act.

For a particular proceeding, always verify the version of the statute, applicable notifications, orders and jurisdiction in force for the relevant date.

12. Key Takeaways

  • Section 3 establishes the classes of Central Tax officers under the CGST Act.
  • The Government appoints the classes of officers by notification.
  • Section 3 should be read together with Sections 4 and 5.
  • Being an officer under Section 3 does not by itself mean that every statutory power can be exercised by that officer.
  • Jurisdiction and assignment of particular functions must be checked from the applicable notifications and orders.
  • Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax was the key original appointment/jurisdiction notification.
  • Circular No. 3/3/2017-GST is important for understanding assignment of “proper officer” functions.
  • The wording of Section 3 itself has remained substantially unchanged, while the administrative framework around it has evolved.
  • For historical disputes, use the notifications and jurisdiction applicable to the relevant period rather than relying only on today's structure.
