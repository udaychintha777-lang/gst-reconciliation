Section 146 of the CGST Act, 2017

Common Portal. Understand the statutory foundation for the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal and the digital functions through which GST registration, payment, returns, IGST settlement, e-way bill and other prescribed functions are facilitated.

1. Statutory Text of Section 146

146. Common Portal.—

The Government may, on the recommendations of the Council, notify the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal for facilitating registration, payment of tax, furnishing of returns, computation and settlement of integrated tax, electronic way bill and for carrying out such other functions and for such purposes as may be prescribed.
Current-law position: Section 146 is currently in force. The current statutory text continues to describe the Government's power to notify the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal on the recommendations of the GST Council.

2. Section 146 Explained in Simple Language

GST is designed as an electronic tax system. Taxpayers interact with the GST system through online facilities rather than relying only on physical forms and departmental offices.

Section 146 provides the statutory foundation for notifying the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal. The section itself is short, but its practical importance is large because many day-to-day GST activities are facilitated through the electronic portal ecosystem.

Think of Section 146 as the legal gateway for GST's common digital infrastructure. It does not itself contain the detailed procedure for every return, payment or registration. Those details come from other provisions, rules, notifications, forms and portal processes.

Why was a common portal necessary?

  • GST operates across Central and State tax administrations.
  • Taxpayers may have multiple registrations and recurring filing obligations.
  • Returns and payments require standardised electronic processes.
  • Inter-State supplies require IGST computation and settlement mechanisms.
  • Goods movement requires electronic compliance mechanisms such as the e-way bill system.
  • A common digital infrastructure helps different GST functions operate through an integrated ecosystem.

3. Who Can Notify the Common Portal?

The section says that the Government may, on the recommendations of the Council, notify the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal.

ExpressionPractical meaning
GovernmentThe statutory authority empowered to notify the portal.
Recommendations of the CouncilThe GST Council's recommendation forms part of the statutory framework for notification.
NotifyFormally designate the electronic portal for the functions contemplated by the Act.
Important: Section 146 is an enabling provision. It should not be read as saying that every technical feature appearing on a GST website is independently created by Section 146. Detailed functions are supported by the Act, rules, notifications and other legal instruments.

4. Registration Through the GST Electronic Portal

Registration is one of the functions expressly mentioned in Section 146. In practical terms, the GST electronic portal provides the digital interface through which applicants submit registration information and interact with the registration process established under the GST law.

However, Section 146 itself does not prescribe the eligibility threshold, documents, approval procedure, amendment procedure or cancellation procedure. Those matters arise under other provisions, particularly the registration provisions and the CGST Rules.

Example:

A new business becomes liable to register. The business uses the GST electronic portal to submit the prescribed application and supporting information. The portal is the electronic channel; the legal eligibility and registration requirements come from the relevant GST provisions and rules.

5. Payment of Tax

Section 146 expressly mentions payment of tax. GST taxpayers use electronic mechanisms to generate challans, make payments and reflect payment-related information in the GST system.

The detailed legal rules concerning electronic ledgers, challans, payment order and utilisation of balances are found elsewhere in the CGST Act and Rules. Section 146 provides the statutory basis for the portal facilitating this function.

Example – monthly tax payment:

A registered person finalises its GST liability, generates the relevant payment instrument through the electronic system and pays the amount through the prescribed channel. The portal facilitates the transaction and records the relevant information.

6. Furnishing of GST Returns

Return filing is another core function expressly named in Section 146. GST returns and related statements are generally furnished electronically through the GST system in accordance with the applicable provisions, rules, forms and due dates.

ActivityTypical portal interaction
Return preparationEnter/import relevant data and prepare the prescribed return.
ValidationSystem checks may identify errors or missing fields.
SubmissionAuthorised person submits the return using the prescribed authentication mechanism.
AcknowledgementFiling generates the relevant acknowledgement/reference.
Post-filing reconciliationFinance team reconciles books, returns, payments and portal data.
Practical point: The portal is the filing channel; it does not replace the taxpayer's responsibility to maintain accurate books, reconcile figures and comply with substantive GST law.

7. Computation and Settlement of Integrated Tax

Section 146 specifically mentions computation and settlement of integrated tax. This is important because IGST is used for inter-State supplies and related transactions within the GST framework.

The section does not itself prescribe the rate of IGST or the detailed settlement formula. Those matters are governed by the IGST Act, CGST/SGST framework and related rules and procedures.

Example – inter-State sale:

A supplier in Telangana makes a taxable inter-State supply to a recipient in Karnataka. The transaction is reported through the GST electronic system, and the applicable IGST information becomes part of the electronic tax ecosystem. Section 146 is one of the statutory foundations for that electronic infrastructure.

8. Electronic Way Bill

The electronic way bill is expressly listed in Section 146. The e-way bill system facilitates prescribed compliance relating to movement of goods.

The detailed legal requirements for when an e-way bill is required, who must generate it, its validity, documentation and related procedures are found principally in the relevant provisions and rules, including the e-way bill provisions under the CGST Rules.

Example – movement of goods:

A company dispatches goods from its warehouse to a customer. Where an e-way bill is required, the relevant electronic document is generated and managed through the prescribed e-way bill system.

Do not confuse: Section 146 provides for the portal function; it does not by itself specify every e-way bill condition or threshold.

9. Other Functions and Prescribed Purposes

The final part of Section 146 is deliberately broad. It allows the Common Portal to carry out such other functions and for such purposes as may be prescribed.

This gives the GST electronic infrastructure flexibility to support functions beyond the five specifically listed in the section, where the relevant law or prescribed framework provides for them.

Expressly mentionedBroader statutory wording
RegistrationOther functions and purposes as may be prescribed.
Payment of tax
Furnishing of returns
Computation and settlement of IGST
Electronic way bill

The broad wording is important because a modern GST system requires electronic processes that cannot necessarily be reduced to a short list of five activities.

10. What the Common Portal Means in Practice

The phrase “common portal” is also defined in Section 2(26) of the CGST Act by reference to the common goods and services tax electronic portal referred to in Section 146.

In practical taxpayer usage, GST portal services provide a central electronic interface for numerous compliance activities. The GST Council records also identify the statutory basis for notifying the common portal and note the portal's role in the GST system.

Taxpayer sideRegistration, return filing, payments and other electronic compliance activities.
Government sideReceipt, processing and electronic administration of GST information and transactions.
System sideCommon infrastructure connecting multiple GST functions and participants.

11. Business Compliance Workflow Around the Common Portal

  1. Maintain master data: GSTIN, legal name, registration state, authorised signatories and portal credentials.
  2. Prepare books: ensure sales, purchases, tax ledgers and adjustments are updated before filing.
  3. Download portal data: retain relevant GSTR-2B, GSTR-1, return acknowledgements and other reports.
  4. Reconcile: compare books with portal-generated data before final submission.
  5. Prepare return: use the applicable electronic return process.
  6. Review: have a maker-checker process for material tax figures.
  7. File and authenticate: complete the prescribed electronic filing process.
  8. Pay tax: ensure payment and ledger entries reconcile with the return.
  9. Archive evidence: save filed return, ARN/acknowledgement, challan and supporting workings.
  10. Monitor notices: regularly check the portal for communications and notices.
For a finance team: portal compliance should never be treated as a last-minute data-entry exercise. The portal should be the final electronic step after books, reconciliations and review are substantially complete.

12. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – New GST registration

A company crossing the applicable registration threshold uses the electronic portal to apply for registration. Section 146 supports the portal infrastructure; eligibility and procedure come from the registration provisions.

Example 2 – GSTR-1 filing

The accounts team prepares outward-supply data and furnishes the prescribed return electronically. The portal facilitates the filing process.

Example 3 – GSTR-3B filing and payment

The company finalises liability, files the prescribed return and pays the tax electronically through the prescribed system.

Example 4 – GSTR-2B download

The purchase team downloads the portal-generated statement used for ITC reconciliation. The file is retained with the month's reconciliation working.

Example 5 – Inter-State transaction

An inter-State supply is reported with IGST. The GST electronic ecosystem supports computation and settlement of integrated tax.

Example 6 – E-way bill

Goods are transported in circumstances requiring an e-way bill. The prescribed electronic system is used to generate and manage the document.

Example 7 – Multiple GST registrations

A company has registrations in several States. The finance team maintains separate GSTIN-wise books and portal records and reconciles each registration separately.

Example 8 – Return correction

A return contains an error that requires correction through the mechanism permitted by GST law. The portal facilitates the relevant electronic process, but the legal correction rules determine what can actually be amended.

Example 9 – Portal acknowledgement

After filing, the team saves the acknowledgement/reference with the final working papers. This provides evidence of the electronic filing event.

Example 10 – Portal outage

If a taxpayer faces a technical issue, the appropriate remedy depends on the nature of the issue and any extension/relaxation or technical advisory applicable at that time. Section 146 itself does not grant an automatic extension of every statutory due date.

Example 11 – Electronic notice

A communication appears on the GST portal. The taxpayer should review it promptly because portal-based communication can trigger statutory response timelines under the relevant provision.

Example 12 – Compliance evidence

A company maintains a monthly GST folder containing portal downloads, return acknowledgements, payment challans, reconciliation reports and approval evidence.

13. What Section 146 Does Not Do

It does not create every GST compliance obligation

The detailed obligations come from the substantive provisions of the CGST Act, IGST Act, rules, notifications, circulars and other applicable legal instruments.

It does not itself set return due dates

Due dates and filing requirements arise under the relevant provisions and rules. Section 146 only provides for the portal to facilitate furnishing of returns.

It does not itself determine tax rates

Rates are governed by the applicable GST rate framework and notifications, not Section 146.

It does not guarantee uninterrupted portal availability

The statutory enabling provision does not itself provide that every technical problem automatically excuses a statutory obligation.

It does not replace books of account

Portal data is not a substitute for accurate accounting records and reconciliations.

It does not make every portal field legally conclusive

A portal entry is part of an electronic compliance system. The underlying transaction, return provisions and applicable law remain important.

15. Common Mistakes

“Section 146 itself tells me how to file every GST return.”

No. It provides the portal foundation. Detailed filing rules are found elsewhere.

“GST portal filing means books do not matter.”

Wrong. Portal filing should be based on accurate accounting records and reconciliations.

“A portal error automatically extends the due date.”

Not automatically. Any extension or relaxation depends on the applicable notification, order, advisory or other legal mechanism.

“Section 146 is only about gst.gov.in.”

The statutory concept is the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal. Specific electronic services can operate through designated components or related systems under the GST framework.

“Section 146 itself creates the e-way bill rules.”

No. It identifies electronic way bill as one of the functions the common portal may facilitate. Detailed requirements come from the relevant law and rules.

“Portal data and books must always be identical at every instant.”

Different systems can have timing differences. The finance team should investigate and reconcile differences rather than assume that either system is automatically correct.

17. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 146 of the CGST Act?

Section 146 is titled “Common Portal” and empowers the Government, on the recommendations of the GST Council, to notify the Common Goods and Services Tax Electronic Portal for specified GST functions.

2. What is the GST Common Portal?

It is the common electronic portal referred to in the CGST Act for facilitating major GST functions. The statutory definition in Section 2(26) refers to the portal under Section 146.

3. Does Section 146 cover registration?

Yes. Registration is expressly listed as a function to be facilitated by the Common Portal.

4. Does Section 146 cover tax payment?

Yes. Payment of tax is expressly listed.

5. Does Section 146 cover GST returns?

Yes. Furnishing of returns is expressly listed.

6. Does Section 146 cover IGST?

Yes. The section specifically refers to computation and settlement of integrated tax.

7. Does Section 146 cover e-way bills?

Yes. Electronic way bill is expressly mentioned.

8. Can the portal perform other GST functions?

Yes. The section also allows such other functions and purposes as may be prescribed.

9. Does Section 146 itself prescribe return due dates?

No. Due dates and detailed filing requirements arise under other provisions, rules and applicable notifications.

10. Does Section 146 itself determine GST rates?

No. GST rates are governed by the applicable rate provisions and notifications.

11. What happens if the GST portal has a technical problem?

The legal consequence depends on the applicable circumstances and any notification, extension, technical advisory or other relief issued at that time. Section 146 does not itself provide an automatic extension for every technical problem.

12. Is Section 146 still in force?

Yes. Current consolidated statutory sources show Section 146 as an in-force provision of the CGST Act.

13. What is the difference between the common portal and an e-way bill system?

The common portal is the broader statutory electronic portal framework. The e-way bill is one of the functions expressly identified in Section 146 and operates through the prescribed electronic system.

14. Is Section 146 important for accountants?

Yes. Although short, it underpins the electronic infrastructure through which accountants and finance teams perform many recurring GST compliance activities.

15. Does portal filing remove the need for reconciliation?

No. A good compliance process reconciles books, portal data, returns and payments before and after filing.
