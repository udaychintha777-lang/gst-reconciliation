CGST Rule 9 – Verification of the Application and Approval of Registration

A practical guide to what happens after a GST registration application is filed: officer scrutiny, clarification notices, replies, approval or rejection, deemed approval and the controls a business should maintain to avoid registration delays.

1. What Rule 9 Does

Rule 8 deals with making the registration application. Rule 9 governs what happens after that application reaches the tax administration. The proper officer examines the application and supporting documents. Depending on the facts and the applicable verification route, the application may be approved, clarification may be sought, further verification may take place, or the application may be rejected for recorded reasons.

Simple meaning: Filing REG-01 does not by itself complete registration. Rule 9 is the legal verification and decision stage before the GSTIN and registration certificate are issued under Rule 10.

2. Statutory Framework – Explained Practically

Rule 9 should be read together with Rule 8, Section 25 of the CGST Act and the registration forms. The operative framework can be understood through the following stages.

Rule 9(1) – Examination and approval

The registration application and accompanying documents are forwarded to the proper officer. Where the application is complete, consistent and otherwise in order, the officer may approve registration within the period prescribed by the Rule.

Current registration verification also interacts with Aadhaar authentication and other verification requirements prescribed under Rule 8. Accordingly, the practical approval timeline can depend on the verification route applicable to the applicant.

Rule 9(2) – Deficiency or clarification: FORM GST REG-03

If information or documents are deficient, inconsistent, or the officer needs clarification, a notice may be issued electronically in FORM GST REG-03. The applicant responds electronically in FORM GST REG-04 within the prescribed time.

Important: A REG-03 notice is not the same as rejection. It is an opportunity to explain, correct permitted particulars and furnish supporting evidence before the officer takes a final decision.

Rule 9(3) – Approval after clarification

When the applicant submits REG-04 and the officer is satisfied with the clarification, information and documents, registration may be approved within the prescribed period.

Rule 9(4) – Rejection: FORM GST REG-05

If the applicant does not reply within the allowed period, or the reply and supporting evidence do not satisfactorily resolve the officer's concerns, the application may be rejected. Reasons are required to be recorded and the applicant is informed electronically in FORM GST REG-05.

Rule 9(5) – Deemed approval

Rule 9 contains a deemed-approval mechanism where the proper officer does not act within the applicable statutory time limit. However, businesses should not treat this as permission to ignore portal status, verification requirements or communications. The actual registration record and certificate should be checked on the portal before operational decisions are made.

5. How to Handle a REG-03 Clarification Notice

A weak REG-04 response is one of the most avoidable causes of registration difficulty. Do not upload a generic letter merely stating that all details are correct.

  1. Identify the exact objection. Separate address, constitution, authorised signatory, nature of possession, business activity and identity-related queries.
  2. Reply point-by-point. Mirror the sequence of the REG-03 notice so the officer can easily verify each response.
  3. Reconcile names and addresses. PAN records, incorporation documents, rent/lease documents, electricity or property records and application particulars should not contradict each other.
  4. Explain unusual facts. Shared premises, newly incorporated entities, virtual or co-working arrangements, group-company premises and sites without conventional utility records may need a clear factual explanation and proper supporting documents.
  5. Upload readable evidence. Scanned documents should be complete, legible and relevant to the particular objection.
  6. Keep proof of filing. Save the notice, reply, attachments and acknowledgement as part of the company's permanent GST registration file.
Do not wait until the last day. A technical issue, wrong attachment or incomplete response close to the deadline can turn a correctable registration query into a rejection risk.

6. Aadhaar, Biometric and Physical Verification – Why It Matters

The registration framework now uses Aadhaar authentication and, in specified cases, additional identity/document or physical verification processes. These requirements arise from the registration rules read together, particularly Rule 8 and Rule 9.

From a business perspective, this means two applicants filing similar REG-01 applications may not necessarily experience an identical verification path or processing time. Risk-based verification, identity authentication and physical verification requirements can affect when the application becomes ready for approval.

Example: A newly incorporated company applies for GST registration for a leased office. If the application is selected for additional verification, the business should keep the premises identifiable, maintain the lease and ownership/supporting documents, ensure the authorised person is reachable and keep the application particulars consistent with the actual premises.

7. Practical Examples

Example 1 – Address document mismatch

ABC Pvt Ltd files REG-01 using “Plot 18, Industrial Area” as its principal place of business, but the supporting utility document shows a different door/plot description. The officer seeks clarification in REG-03. The company should not merely say both addresses are the same. It should provide a clear reconciliation, supporting premises document and any evidence that establishes the identity of the location.

Example 2 – Rented premises

A trader operates from rented premises. The registration file should ordinarily contain the relevant occupancy evidence and supporting premises documentation appropriate to the facts. If the owner's name or address details differ across documents, the applicant should explain the connection rather than leaving the officer to infer it.

Example 3 – No response to REG-03

An applicant receives REG-03 but does not monitor the portal/e-mail and misses the response period. The officer may reject the application through REG-05. This is a compliance-control failure, not merely a portal problem.

Example 4 – New project/site registration

A construction company takes registration in another State for a new project. Before filing, management should confirm the contractual/project address, possession documents, authorised signatory, nature of business activities and supporting site evidence. A rushed application with inconsistent site details can delay mobilisation, vendor onboarding and tax invoicing.

9. Common Mistakes That Create Registration Problems

  • Using an address in REG-01 that is not exactly reconcilable with supporting records.
  • Uploading incomplete rent/lease or premises documents.
  • Incorrect constitution of business or promoter/director particulars.
  • Using an authorised signatory who is not properly supported by company records.
  • Ignoring portal notices because no one is assigned to monitor the application.
  • Submitting REG-04 without answering each objection separately.
  • Uploading blurred, password-protected, cropped or irrelevant evidence.
  • Assuming that an ARN automatically means GST registration has been granted.
  • Starting ERP/invoicing configuration without checking the final GSTIN and certificate.

10. Audit, Investigation and Departmental Relevance

Rule 9 is primarily a registration-stage provision, but the documents and declarations made during registration can remain important later. During departmental proceedings, officers may compare the registered principal/additional places of business, constitution, authorised persons and nature of business with actual operations.

Therefore, a business should maintain a permanent registration dossier containing the original application, ARN, REG-03/REG-04 correspondence if any, supporting documents, approval details and REG-06 certificate.

Practical control: Whenever there is a later change in core registration particulars, do not merely update internal ERP records. Examine whether a GST registration amendment is also required under the applicable rules.

12. Frequently Asked Questions

Is REG-03 a rejection of GST registration?

No. REG-03 is a notice seeking clarification, information or documents. The applicant gets an opportunity to respond in REG-04 within the prescribed period.

What happens if REG-04 is not filed?

Failure to respond within the permitted period can result in rejection of the registration application through REG-05, subject to Rule 9.

Can details be corrected through the clarification process?

Rule 9 permits clarification to include correction/modification of certain registration particulars, but specified Part A particulars such as PAN and other protected fields are not simply correctable through this route. The exact procedural position should be checked against the application facts.

Does an ARN mean the GSTIN is active?

No. ARN confirms the application workflow. The business should verify that registration has actually been approved and the GSTIN/REG-06 certificate has been issued.

Can registration be approved automatically if the officer does not act?

Rule 9 contains deemed-approval provisions for failure to act within the applicable statutory period. In practice, always verify the portal status and registration certificate before treating the registration as operational.

What is the next Rule after Rule 9?

Rule 10 deals with issue of the registration certificate after approval.

14. Source & Verification Note

This guide is prepared from the CGST registration framework, including Rule 9, the related registration forms and connected provisions of the CGST Act and Rules. Registration procedures have been amended over time, particularly in relation to Aadhaar authentication and verification. Businesses should therefore apply the rule to the facts and procedural requirements prevailing for their application date.

This page is an educational and practical interpretation intended to help taxpayers understand the compliance workflow. It is not a substitute for professional advice in a disputed or fact-specific registration matter.

15. Practical Takeaway

Rule 9 is the quality-control gate between filing a GST registration application and receiving the GSTIN. The safest approach is to file only after the legal identity, premises, authorised signatory and supporting records are internally reconciled. If REG-03 is issued, respond quickly, point-by-point and with evidence. Treat the registration file as a permanent compliance record, not a one-time portal exercise.
