CGST ACT • CHAPTER II — ADMINISTRATION

Section 6 — Authorisation of Officers of State Tax or Union Territory Tax as Proper Officer in Certain Circumstances

Section 6 creates the cross-empowerment framework under which officers appointed under a State GST Act or the Union Territory GST Act can act as proper officers for purposes of the CGST Act, subject to the statutory conditions and notifications issued on the recommendations of the GST Council.

Section 6 at a Glance

ProvisionSection 6
ChapterChapter II — Administration
SubjectCross-empowerment & Proper Officer

GST is administered through both Central and State/Union Territory tax authorities. Section 6 provides the statutory mechanism for allowing State/UT officers to act for CGST purposes in specified circumstances. It also prevents parallel proceedings on the same subject matter and creates a separation between original proceedings and rectification, appeal and revision.

Core principle: Cross-empowerment does not mean that every State or UT officer automatically has unlimited CGST powers. The statutory framework, applicable notification, conditions and the particular function must be examined.

1. Why Was Section 6 Needed?

GST replaced several separate indirect tax systems with a dual administration. CGST is administered by the Central Government, while SGST is administered by the respective State. For taxpayers, however, the same transaction can create both Central and State tax consequences.

A practical GST administration therefore needs a mechanism under which officers of one tax administration can perform specified functions for the other administration. Section 6 provides that mechanism for the CGST Act.

This becomes particularly relevant for refunds, enforcement, adjudication and other proceedings where the taxpayer may otherwise face multiple authorities dealing with overlapping facts.

2. Section 6(1) — State/UT Officers as CGST Proper Officers

Section 6(1) provides that officers appointed under the State GST Act or the Union Territory GST Act are authorised to be proper officers for purposes of the CGST Act, subject to such conditions as the Government specifies by notification on the recommendations of the GST Council.

The important elements are:

  • the officer must be appointed under the relevant State GST or UTGST law;
  • the officer is being used as a proper officer for CGST purposes;
  • conditions can be specified by notification;
  • the notification is issued on the recommendations of the GST Council.
Practical point: Always identify the exact notification and its conditions before concluding that a State Tax officer can exercise a particular CGST function.

3. Section 6(2)(a) — One Order for CGST and State/UT GST

Section 6(2)(a) provides that where a proper officer issues an order under the CGST Act, the officer shall also issue an order under the corresponding State GST or UTGST law, as authorised by that law, with intimation to the jurisdictional officer of State Tax or Union Territory Tax.

The provision is designed to avoid forcing the taxpayer to undergo separate but substantially identical proceedings merely because the tax is divided into Central and State/UT components.

Practical example: Suppose a properly authorised officer determines a tax liability arising from a transaction that gives rise to both CGST and the corresponding State tax. Where the statutory conditions are satisfied, the officer's order framework can cover the corresponding tax components instead of requiring an entirely separate parallel determination.

4. Section 6(2)(b) — No Parallel Proceedings on the Same Subject Matter

This is one of the most important safeguards in Section 6. If a proper officer under the State GST or UTGST law has already initiated proceedings on a subject matter, a proper officer under the CGST Act cannot initiate proceedings on the same subject matter.

The objective is to prevent two tax administrations from simultaneously conducting duplicate proceedings against the same taxpayer on the same issue.

Practical example: A State Tax officer initiates proceedings concerning a specific classification issue for a registered person. A Central Tax officer should not independently initiate another proceeding on the same subject matter merely because the Central and State taxes arise from the same GST framework.
Important: “Same subject matter” requires factual and legal analysis. It should not automatically be assumed that two proceedings are identical merely because they relate to the same taxpayer or the same financial year.

5. Section 6(3) — Appeal, Revision and Rectification

Section 6(3) provides that proceedings for rectification, appeal and revision, wherever applicable, of an order passed by an officer appointed under the CGST Act shall not lie before an officer appointed under the State GST or UTGST law.

This maintains the separation between the Central and State administrative hierarchies. Cross- empowerment for specified original functions does not mean that every appellate or revisional function can be transferred across administrations.

Practical example: If an order has been passed by a Central Tax officer, a taxpayer cannot assume that the appeal against that order can be filed before a State Tax officer merely because State Tax officers may have cross-empowerment for some original functions.

7. GST Council Background — How Cross-Empowerment Developed

The cross-empowerment concept was part of the GST administrative design from the beginning. The 9th GST Council meeting held on 16 January 2017 discussed intelligence-based enforcement and the need for both Central and State tax administrations to have appropriate powers in their respective domains. citeturn0search0

The 22nd GST Council meeting on 6 October 2017 specifically considered the practical problem that taxpayers could face where enforcement action was initiated by one administration and consequential actions such as adjudication, rectification, revision or appeal had to be coordinated. The Council materials referred expressly to Section 6 and its cross-empowerment framework. citeturn0search0turn0search2

The Council also considered model notifications for cross-empowerment. This is important because a model notification or Council recommendation is not itself the final operative legal authority. The actual notification issued under Section 6 and its conditions determine the enforceable scope.

8. Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax — The Key Initial Cross-Empowerment Notification

The most important early notification under Section 6 was Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax dated 13 October 2017. It cross-empowered specified State GST and Union Territory GST officers to act as proper officers for sanction of refunds under Sections 54 and 55 of the CGST Act, subject to the conditions specified in the notification. citeturn1search0turn1search3

This notification is significant because it demonstrates the limited and conditional nature of cross-empowerment. It did not simply say that every State officer could perform every CGST function. It specified a particular area — refund sanction under Sections 54 and 55 — and attached conditions.

9. Notification No. 10/2018-Central Tax — Amendment to the Refund Cross-Empowerment

Notification No. 10/2018-Central Tax dated 23 January 2018 amended Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax. The amendment replaced the broad wording “except rule 96” with a narrower exclusion covering sub-rules (1) to (8) and sub-rule (10) of Rule 96. citeturn1search4turn1search5

This is a good example of why a legal research file should preserve the original notification together with later amending notifications. The operative position may change without the statutory section itself being rewritten.

10. Amendments to Section 6 — What Changed?

The important legislative and administrative developments around Section 6 should be distinguished carefully.

2017 — Original cross-empowerment framework

Section 6 formed part of the original CGST administrative architecture. Its mechanism allowed State/UT officers to be authorised as proper officers for CGST purposes subject to notified conditions.

2017–2018 — Refund cross-empowerment

Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax operationalised cross-empowerment for specified refund functions, and Notification No. 10/2018-Central Tax amended the Rule 96 exclusion.

Later years — Administrative and judicial development

A major practical issue became the distinction between a taxpayer being assigned to one administration and a separate administration having statutory authority to initiate proceedings. The GST Council records, departmental material and court decisions have therefore become important in understanding the limits of cross-empowerment.

Important: Do not describe every change to a Section 6 notification as an amendment to Section 6 itself. Notification No. 10/2018-Central Tax amended Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax; it did not rewrite the statutory text of Section 6.

11. Circulars and Administrative Clarifications

Circular No. 1/1/2017-GST — Division of Taxpayer Base

The early GST administrative framework contemplated division of the taxpayer base between Central and State administrations so that taxpayers could generally have a single interface for administration. The circular is relevant background when studying why cross-empowerment mechanisms were developed.

Refund administration

The cross-empowerment notifications under Section 6 were particularly relevant to refund processing. CBIC's notification records identify Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax as the instrument for cross-empowering State Tax officers for processing and sanction of refunds. citeturn1search0turn0search13

Important distinction from proper-officer circulars

Circulars such as Circular No. 1/1/2017-GST and later proper-officer circulars deal with administrative assignment and implementation. They should be read together with the actual Section 6 notification applicable to the function being examined.

12. Litigation and Jurisdiction — Why Section 6 Became Important

Cross-empowerment has generated significant litigation, particularly where Central and State officers initiated proceedings against the same taxpayer or concerning overlapping subject matter.

The key statutory question is whether the officer had the required authority under Section 6 and the applicable notification. A taxpayer should not assume that the existence of a general concept of “cross-empowerment” automatically validates every proceeding by a counterpart authority.

Legal-research lesson: A draft/model notification discussed by the GST Council does not itself confer jurisdiction. The final notification actually issued under the statutory power must be identified. The GST Council's 47th meeting agenda expressly discussed this issue in the context of cross-empowerment and recurring show-cause notices. citeturn0search0

13. Practical Examples

Example 1 — State Tax officer processes a CGST refund

A registered person applies for a refund and the application falls within the specified cross-empowerment framework. A State Tax officer can act as the CGST proper officer for the specified refund function where Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax and its conditions apply.

Example 2 — State officer starts proceedings after Central officer has already acted

A Central Tax officer has already initiated proceedings on a particular subject matter. A State Tax officer should not start a second proceeding on the same subject matter where Section 6(2)(b) applies. The two proceedings must be compared factually and legally to determine whether they truly concern the same subject matter.

Example 3 — Appeal against a Central Tax order

A Central Tax officer passes an order. The taxpayer cannot simply approach a State Tax appellate officer because State officers may have cross-empowerment for certain original functions. Section 6(3) preserves the relevant appellate/revisional hierarchy.

Example 4 — Refund Rule 96 issue

A State Tax officer may be cross-empowered for specified CGST refund functions, but the exact exclusions in Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax, as amended, matter. The tax team should check the notification instead of assuming that every Rule 96 refund function is covered.

Example 5 — Same taxpayer, different issue

A taxpayer has one proceeding before the State Tax authority relating to classification and another Central Tax proceeding concerning a completely different statutory issue. The fact that both involve the same taxpayer does not automatically mean Section 6(2)(b) prohibits the second proceeding. The “same subject matter” requirement must be examined.

Example 6 — Historical refund

If an auditor reviews a refund sanctioned in 2017 or 2018, the file should contain the Section 6 notification and any amendment applicable on the date. Notification No. 10/2018-Central Tax changed the wording of the original refund cross-empowerment notification from 23 January 2018.

14. Professional Checklist for Management, Tax Team and Auditors

  1. Identify whether the officer is Central Tax, State Tax or UT Tax.
  2. Identify the exact CGST/SGST/UTGST provision under which the officer is acting.
  3. Check whether a Section 6 notification exists for the particular function.
  4. Read the conditions in that notification carefully.
  5. Check whether another administration has already initiated proceedings on the same subject matter.
  6. For an order, identify which administration's appellate/revisional hierarchy applies.
  7. For historical matters, verify the notification and amendment applicable on the relevant date.
  8. Keep the original notification and all relevant amending notifications in the audit/legal file.
  9. Do not rely on a Council agenda or draft model notification as if it were an operative notification.

15. Statutory Text of Section 6

The provision is reproduced below for ease of reference:

6. Authorisation of officers of State tax or Union territory tax as proper officer
in certain circumstances.—

(1) Without prejudice to the provisions of this Act, the officers appointed under the
State Goods and Services Tax Act or the Union Territory Goods and Services Tax Act are
authorised to be the proper officers for the purposes of this Act, subject to such
conditions as the Government shall, on the recommendations of the Council, by notification,
specify.

(2) Subject to the conditions specified in the notification issued under sub-section (1),—

(a) where any proper officer issues an order under this Act, he shall also issue an order
under the State Goods and Services Tax Act or the Union Territory Goods and Services Tax Act,
as authorised by the said Act under intimation to the jurisdictional officer of State tax or
Union territory tax;

(b) where a proper officer under the State Goods and Services Tax Act or the Union Territory
Goods and Services Tax Act has initiated any proceedings on a subject matter, no proceedings
shall be initiated by the proper officer under this Act on the same subject matter.

(3) Any proceedings for rectification, appeal and revision, wherever applicable, of any order
passed by an officer appointed under this Act shall not lie before an officer appointed under
the State Goods and Services Tax Act or the Union Territory Goods and Services Tax Act.

For any dispute, verify the statutory version and the exact Section 6 notification and conditions applicable to the relevant date and function.

16. Key Takeaways

  • Section 6 provides the statutory framework for cross-empowerment of State/UT officers for CGST purposes.
  • Cross-empowerment is subject to conditions specified through notification on GST Council recommendations.
  • Section 6(2)(b) is designed to prevent parallel proceedings on the same subject matter.
  • Section 6(2)(a) provides a mechanism for corresponding CGST and State/UT orders where the statutory conditions are satisfied.
  • Section 6(3) preserves the appellate, revision and rectification hierarchy.
  • Notification No. 39/2017-Central Tax dated 13 October 2017 is the key early notification for cross-empowerment of State/UT officers for specified CGST refund functions.
  • Notification No. 10/2018-Central Tax dated 23 January 2018 amended the refund cross-empowerment notification.
  • A GST Council recommendation or draft model notification is not by itself an operative source of jurisdiction.
  • For any cross-empowerment issue, check the exact notification, conditions, subject matter and date.
