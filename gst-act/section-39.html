1. Why Section 39 matters

Searches such as “Section 39 CGST Act”, “GSTR-3B”, “GSTR-3B due date”, “GST monthly return”, “GST quarterly return”, “how to file GSTR-3B”, “GSTR-3B ITC” and “three year GST return filing rule” all point to the compliance framework created by Section 39.

Section 39 is the core return-filing provision for regular GST taxpayers. It connects the taxpayer's outward-supply liability, inward supplies liable to reverse charge, input tax credit and tax payment into the summary return mechanism.

Simple way to understand it: Section 37 supplies detailed outward-supply data, Section 38 supplies recipient-side ITC information, and Section 39 brings the taxpayer's summary tax liability and eligible ITC together for the return and payment process.

For most regular taxpayers, the practical form associated with Section 39 is FORM GSTR-3B, furnished electronically under Rule 61.

3. Complete statutory structure

Section 39(1) requires every registered person, other than specified excluded categories, to furnish electronically a return for each calendar month or part thereof containing details of outward and inward supplies, ITC availed, tax payable and tax paid, in the prescribed form and manner.

The section also creates separate return provisions for TDS deductors, ISDs and non-resident taxable persons. The exact return form and frequency are therefore determined by reading Section 39 with the applicable Rules.

Section 39(6) allows the Commissioner, on the recommendations of the Council, to extend the due date for furnishing a return for a specified period in specified circumstances. Recent extensions illustrate that the power is used through notifications for affected taxpayers/periods. citeturn0news1

5. Section 39 and GSTR-3B

GSTR-3B is the principal summary return used by regular taxpayers under Section 39.

It brings together the major tax-return components:

Outward supplies
Taxable, zero-rated, exempt/nil-rated and other outward supplies.
Reverse charge
Inward supplies on which the recipient is liable to pay tax.
ITC
Eligible credit, reversals and other adjustments.
Tax payment
Net liability discharged through the electronic ledgers.

Rule 61 prescribes FORM GSTR-3B and the electronic filing mechanism. The Rules also prescribe monthly and quarterly filing dates.

Important: Always check whether a specific notification has extended the due date for the relevant tax period. Section 39(6) is the statutory basis for such extensions.

7. QRMP and quarterly returns

The Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme permits eligible taxpayers to furnish returns quarterly while paying tax monthly according to the prescribed mechanism.

For a quarterly GSTR-3B filer, the first two months of the quarter involve monthly tax payment/working and the final return is furnished for the quarter. The applicable payment method and invoice-reporting mechanism should be considered separately.

The QRMP scheme should therefore not be interpreted as “no GST compliance for the first two months”. Tax payment and invoice reporting obligations can continue during the quarter.

8. What GSTR-3B contains

AreaTypical information

The exact portal table structure can be updated by the Government/GSTN. The taxpayer should use the form and instructions applicable to the relevant tax period.

9. Tax payment through GSTR-3B

Section 39(7) requires tax, interest, penalty, fees and other amounts payable to be paid within the prescribed time, generally through the electronic cash ledger and/or electronic credit ledger to the extent legally permitted.

GSTR-3B therefore has two connected functions:

  • declare the summary liability and eligible ITC; and
  • facilitate discharge of the resulting amount through the electronic ledgers.

A return should not be treated as complete merely because the form has been submitted. The liability must also be correctly discharged.

10. ITC in GSTR-3B

GSTR-3B ITC is the final reporting outcome after the finance team considers:

  • GSTR-2B and other GST data;
  • purchase register and invoice documents;
  • Section 16 conditions;
  • Section 17 blocked credit/apportionment;
  • credit-note reversals;
  • Rule 37/37A reversals;
  • RCM and import credits; and
  • prior-period adjustments.
Important: GSTR-2B is not a substitute for the Section 16/17 ITC analysis. The amount appearing in the GST system still requires a legal eligibility review.

11. Reverse charge

GSTR-3B separately captures inward supplies liable to reverse charge. The recipient may be responsible for paying the tax even though the supplier does not charge the recipient in the normal manner.

A proper RCM process should reconcile:

  • contracts and purchase register;
  • RCM notifications;
  • invoice/recipient-created document where applicable;
  • tax payment in GSTR-3B;
  • cash-ledger payment where required; and
  • eligible ITC after payment and other conditions.

12. Nil GSTR-3B

A taxpayer's return obligation does not automatically disappear merely because no tax is payable. Section 39(8) establishes the return-filing obligation subject to the applicable provisions.

Where the taxpayer qualifies for a nil return, the GST system permits the prescribed nil-filing process. The nil return is still a statutory compliance event and should be documented.

13. Errors and corrections

Section 39(9) allows errors or omissions in a return to be rectified in the prescribed manner, subject to the statutory restrictions. This is different from simply reopening and editing a filed GSTR-3B.

For an error, the taxpayer should identify:

  1. tax period of the original error;
  2. whether tax was short-paid or excess-paid;
  3. whether ITC was wrongly claimed/reversed;
  4. whether interest arises;
  5. whether the correction can be reflected in a subsequent return under the prescribed mechanism; and
  6. whether the statutory time limit has expired.

14. No revised GSTR-3B

GSTR-3B should not be treated like an income-tax return that can simply be revised after filing. The GST framework provides correction mechanisms through subsequent returns and prescribed adjustments rather than a general “revised GSTR-3B” process.

From July 2025, the portal's GSTR-3B filing process was also tightened so that a filed GSTR-3B cannot simply be edited after filing. This makes pre-filing review particularly important. citeturn0news0

Practical control: once the authorised person files GSTR-3B, retain the filed return, payment/challan evidence and reconciliation working as a locked compliance record.

15. Late filing

Failure to furnish a Section 39 return by the prescribed due date can trigger late fee and interest consequences and may create downstream restrictions on later-period filing.

Late filing can also affect:

  • vendor/customer reconciliations;
  • ITC availability and timing;
  • Rule 59 filing restrictions;
  • cash-flow planning;
  • annual-return reconciliation; and
  • departmental compliance risk.

16. Interest and Section 50

Section 39 must be read with Section 50 for interest. Interest can arise where tax remains unpaid beyond the prescribed time, subject to the specific statutory rules applicable to the nature of liability.

For finance teams, interest should be calculated separately for:

  • delayed output-tax payment;
  • wrong ITC utilisation where interest is legally attracted;
  • RCM tax paid late; and
  • other tax-payment defaults.

Do not assume that every numerical difference in GSTR-3B automatically produces the same interest result; the applicable provision and nature of the default must be identified.

17. Late fee

Late fee is governed by Section 47 and the applicable notifications/rules, while Section 39 creates the underlying return obligation.

Where relief or special late-fee caps are notified for particular periods, the transaction-period notification should be checked rather than applying a historical amount automatically.

18. Three-year filing restriction

Section 39(11) provides that a registered person cannot furnish a return for a tax period after the expiry of three years from the due date of furnishing that return, subject to the Government's power to prescribe exceptions/conditions within the statutory framework.

This is a major change for old pending returns. It means that “we can file it later” is no longer an unlimited assumption.

IssueMeaning

The three-year restriction was implemented across specified GST return forms from 1 July 2025. citeturn0news0

19. Portal filing controls

GSTR-3B filing is connected to several system controls. A taxpayer can face filing restrictions because of:

  • non-filing of preceding returns;
  • Rule 59 restrictions on subsequent GSTR-1;
  • unresolved mismatch intimations where the Rules prescribe a block;
  • three-year filing restriction;
  • technical/portal validations; or
  • other notified compliance conditions.

Therefore, the return calendar should include a pre-filing blocker review at least a few days before the statutory due date.

20. Section 37/GSTR-1 reconciliation

GSTR-1 is the detailed outward-supply statement under Section 37. GSTR-3B is the summary liability return under Section 39.

GSTR-1GSTR-3B
Invoice/detail level outward supplies.Summary outward tax liability.
Credit/debit notes and amendments.Net tax payable after adjustments.
Recipient-facing transaction data.Tax-payment return.

Before filing GSTR-3B, the finance team should reconcile the relevant GSTR-1 tax values with the liability reported in GSTR-3B.

21. Section 38/GSTR-2B reconciliation

The ITC portion of GSTR-3B should be reconciled with GSTR-2B and the purchase register.

Purchase register
Accounting evidence.
GSTR-2B
GST-system supplier/other-source data.
Eligibility review
Sections 16 and 17.
GSTR-3B
Final ITC reported after adjustments.

The reconciliation should explain every material difference instead of forcing GSTR-3B to equal GSTR-2B automatically.

22. Electronic cash and credit ledger

Tax payable under GSTR-3B is discharged through the electronic ledgers subject to the legal restrictions governing utilisation of credit.

The finance team should reconcile:

  • opening cash ledger;
  • cash deposits;
  • eligible credit ledger;
  • credit utilisation;
  • cash payment;
  • interest/late fee/other amounts; and
  • closing balances.

Ledger reconciliation is particularly important where multiple GST registrations and large monthly tax payments are involved.

23. Tax liability and offset

A good GSTR-3B working should show how the final liability is derived rather than only showing the amount paid.

StageWorking
1Determine outward and RCM tax liability.
2Determine eligible ITC.
3Apply required reversals.
4Determine net tax payable.
5Utilise credit according to permitted tax-head rules.
6Pay balance through cash ledger.
7Reconcile filed return with ledgers and books.

25. 2025 filing developments

Two practical developments materially changed the way finance teams should approach GSTR-3B:

Filed return should be treated as final for the period

From July 2025, the portal stopped allowing post-filing editing of GSTR-3B. The practical consequence is that tax, ITC and reconciliation checks should be completed before the authorised filing. citeturn0news0

Three-year old-return restriction

GSTN implemented the statutory three-year filing restriction for specified returns from July 2025. Old pending GSTR-3B periods therefore require immediate review rather than indefinite carry-forward. citeturn0news0

26. Current-date compliance note

As of September 2026, the Section 39 framework should be applied with the Rules, current GST Portal functionality and period-specific notifications.

For example, due dates can be extended for particular taxpayer groups by notification under Section 39(6). A 2026 notification extended the March 2026 GSTR-3B deadline for specified taxpayers, illustrating why a static “20th every month” statement is not sufficient for every tax period. citeturn0news1

Professional approach: use the ordinary Rule 61 due date as the starting point, then check whether a notification, special taxpayer category or portal instruction changes the date for the particular period.

27. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — normal monthly filer

A regular taxpayer calculates September output tax of ₹20 lakh and eligible ITC of ₹14 lakh. The net liability is reconciled to the books and discharged through the appropriate electronic ledger before filing GSTR-3B.

Example 2 — GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B difference

GSTR-1 shows taxable output tax of ₹10 lakh while GSTR-3B working shows ₹9 lakh. The ₹1 lakh difference is investigated before filing rather than accepting the mismatch.

Example 3 — 2B vs ITC

GSTR-2B contains ₹8 lakh ITC, but ₹50,000 relates to blocked credit. Only the legally eligible amount is considered for GSTR-3B.

Example 4 — RCM

A recipient has ₹2 lakh of RCM liability. It reports and pays the tax in GSTR-3B and separately evaluates the resulting ITC according to the applicable conditions.

Example 5 — nil return

A registered person has no taxable transactions or tax liability for a month. The taxpayer still reviews whether a nil GSTR-3B is required and completes the filing.

Example 6 — late filing

A taxpayer misses the due date. Before filing, the team calculates tax, applicable interest and late fee and ensures sufficient cash balance.

Example 7 — error after filing

An output-tax error is identified after GSTR-3B filing. The taxpayer does not attempt to “revise” the filed return; the correction is evaluated under the prescribed subsequent-return mechanism and applicable time limits.

Example 8 — quarterly filer

A QRMP taxpayer pays monthly tax for the first two months and files the quarterly GSTR-3B in the prescribed quarter-end return cycle.

Example 9 — old pending return

A company has an old GSTR-3B pending for more than three years from its due date. The Section 39(11) restriction is checked immediately because the normal filing window may have expired.

Example 10 — due-date extension

A taxpayer falls within a class covered by a period-specific extension notification. The extended date is followed rather than the ordinary Rule 61 date.

28. Construction and infrastructure examples

For construction, mining, irrigation, road, pipeline and infrastructure businesses, GSTR-3B should be prepared from a controlled tax working rather than directly from the accounting trial balance.

AreaControl

Mobilisation advanceTrack tax timing and subsequent adjustment separately.
RetentionReconcile contractual retention with GST timing and invoice records.
Subcontractor ITCReconcile purchase register/GSTR-2B and apply Sections 16/17.
RCMMaintain a separate project-wise RCM register.
Credit notesLink commercial adjustments to GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B impact.

A robust monthly process is: project billing register → sales register → GSTR-1 → GSTR-3B liability → purchase/2B ITC → RCM → tax payment → ledger reconciliation.

30. GST Council background

Original return architecture

The GST Council's early return design contemplated a detailed outward-supply return, inward-supply return and summary return. The operational system subsequently moved towards GSTR-1, GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B.

Return-reform period

The Council's return-reform recommendations progressively aligned the statutory framework with the electronic GST data model and reduced dependence on the original two-way GSTR-2 process.

Three-year restriction

The statutory three-year restriction was introduced as part of the broader return-compliance tightening and later became an important portal control for old pending returns.

34. Common mistakes

  • Filing GSTR-3B directly from the trial balance without tax reconciliation.
  • Assuming GSTR-2B equals eligible ITC.
  • Ignoring RCM because it is not a normal supplier invoice.
  • Not reconciling GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
  • Forgetting credit-note effects.
  • Attempting to treat GSTR-3B as a revisable return.
  • Ignoring interest on delayed tax payment.
  • Ignoring late fee.
  • Missing a period-specific due-date extension or relief notification.
  • Leaving old returns pending until the three-year restriction applies.
  • Not preserving the filed return and tax-payment evidence.
  • Claiming ITC without applying Sections 16 and 17.

35. Section 39 GST FAQs

What is Section 39 of the CGST Act?

Section 39 deals with furnishing GST returns, including the principal summary-return framework for registered persons and special return provisions for specified categories.

What is GSTR-3B under Section 39?

GSTR-3B is the principal summary return used by regular taxpayers to report tax liability, eligible ITC and tax payment information.

What is the GSTR-3B due date?

The ordinary monthly due date is the 20th of the succeeding month. Quarterly QRMP taxpayers generally follow the 22nd or 24th depending on the prescribed state/UT group, subject to extensions.

Is GSTR-3B a monthly return?

For ordinary regular taxpayers it is generally monthly. Eligible QRMP taxpayers can furnish it quarterly under the prescribed scheme.

Can GSTR-3B be revised?

There is no general revised-GSTR-3B mechanism. Errors are dealt with through the prescribed subsequent-return/correction framework and applicable statutory time limits.

What is Section 39(9)?

Section 39(9) provides the statutory framework for rectifying errors or omissions in a return in the prescribed manner, subject to restrictions.

What is Section 39(11)?

Section 39(11) restricts furnishing specified GST returns after three years from their due date, subject to the statutory framework and prescribed exceptions.

What happens if GSTR-3B is filed late?

Late filing can result in late fee and interest consequences and can also create restrictions on subsequent return filing.

Does GSTR-3B include ITC?

Yes. Eligible ITC and prescribed reversals are reported in GSTR-3B.

Does GSTR-3B include RCM?

Yes. Inward supplies liable to reverse charge are separately reported and the corresponding tax is discharged according to the applicable provisions.

What is Rule 61 GST?

Rule 61 prescribes the form and manner for furnishing GSTR-3B and the ordinary monthly/quarterly filing framework.

Can the government extend the GSTR-3B due date?

Yes. Section 39(6) provides the statutory power for specified extensions, and notifications may extend the date for particular taxpayers or periods.

What is the three-year GST return rule?

Section 39(11) prevents furnishing specified returns after three years from their due date, subject to the prescribed statutory exceptions and conditions.

Does GSTR-1 have to match GSTR-3B?

The detailed outward-supply reporting and summary tax liability should be reconciled. Material differences should be identified and explained before filing.

Does GSTR-2B have to match GSTR-3B ITC?

Not necessarily line-for-line. GSTR-2B is a major input into the ITC working, but eligible ITC can differ after applying Sections 16/17, reversals, RCM, imports, ISD and other adjustments.

What should be checked before filing GSTR-3B?

At minimum: GSTR-1, GSTR-2B, purchase register, sales register, RCM, credit/debit notes, ITC reversals, electronic ledgers, interest, late fee, prior-period adjustments and current due-date/portal restrictions.

37. Key takeaways

  • Section 39 is the core GST return-filing provision for regular taxpayers.
  • GSTR-3B is the principal summary return operating under Section 39.
  • GSTR-1 and GSTR-2B should be reconciled before finalising GSTR-3B.
  • Eligible ITC must be determined after applying Sections 16 and 17, not merely from GSTR-2B.
  • RCM liability should be independently tracked and reported.
  • GSTR-3B does not have a general revised-return mechanism.
  • Section 39(6) permits specified due-date extensions.
  • Section 39(9) provides the statutory correction framework for errors/omissions.
  • Section 39(11) creates a three-year restriction on furnishing specified old returns.
  • From 2025, stronger portal controls make pre-filing reconciliation even more important.
  • Construction and infrastructure businesses should prepare GSTR-3B from controlled project-wise tax workings.
