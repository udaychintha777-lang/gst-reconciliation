Section 151 of the CGST Act, 2017

Power to call for information. Understand when the Commissioner or an authorised officer can direct a person to furnish information relating to a matter dealt with in connection with the CGST Act.

1. Current Statutory Text

151. Power to call for information.— The Commissioner or an officer authorised by him may, by an order, direct any person to furnish information relating to any matter dealt with in connection with this Act, within such time, in such form, and in such manner, as may be specified therein.
Current-law point: This is the post-1 January 2022 version of Section 151. The section was substituted by the Finance Act changes made in 2021 with effect from 1 January 2022. The current provision is no longer the earlier “power to collect statistics” provision.

2. Important 2022 Change – Do Not Use the Old Section 151 Text

Older versions of GST material may show Section 151 with the heading “Power to collect statistics”. That was the earlier form of the provision.

With effect from 1 January 2022, Section 151 was substituted and became “Power to call for information.” The current section empowers the Commissioner or an authorised officer to direct any person to furnish information connected with the Act, within the time, form and manner specified in the order.

Earlier Section 151Current Section 151
Power to collect statistics.Power to call for information.
Commissioner could direct collection of statistics by notification.Commissioner or authorised officer may, by an order, direct any person to furnish information.
Focused on statistics and related returns.Focused on information connected with matters dealt with under the Act.
Old Section 124 expressly referred to failure to furnish information/return under Section 151.Current Section 151 should be read with the post-2022 statutory framework rather than relying on old descriptions.
Important for legal-library content: Do not publish the old “Power to collect statistics” wording as the current CGST Act Section 151. Many older websites and PDFs still reproduce the pre-2022 text.

3. Section 151 Explained in Simple Language

Section 151 is an information-gathering power. It allows the GST administration to obtain information relevant to the administration of the CGST Act from a person who is directed to provide it.

The provision has four core elements:

  1. Authority: Commissioner or an officer authorised by the Commissioner.
  2. Method: an order.
  3. Subject: information relating to any matter dealt with in connection with the CGST Act.
  4. Compliance format: within the time, in the form and in the manner specified in the order.
Simple example: If the GST administration needs specified information about a transaction, business activity, record or other GST-related matter, the competent authority can issue an order under Section 151 directing a person to furnish that information in the specified manner.

4. Who Can Exercise the Power?

The statute names two levels of authority:

AuthorityRole
CommissionerThe Commissioner may exercise the power directly by issuing an order.
Officer authorised by the CommissionerAn officer can exercise the power where the Commissioner has authorised that officer.

The wording is important because Section 151 does not say that every GST officer automatically has this power merely because the officer is a tax officer. The officer must fall within the statutory authority described in the section.

5. What Does “Any Person” Mean?

The current Section 151 uses the broad expression “any person.” Therefore, the provision is not drafted only for registered taxpayers.

Depending on the information required and the circumstances, an order can potentially be directed to a person who possesses relevant information connected with the CGST Act.

Possible information holderIllustrative information
Registered businessBooks, transaction details, reconciliation data or other GST-related records.
Customer or supplierTransaction-related information relevant to a GST matter.
Intermediary/service providerRecords relevant to a transaction or business arrangement.
Record-maintaining organisationInformation connected with a GST-related matter, where lawfully required.
Do not overread “any person.” The information must relate to a matter dealt with in connection with the CGST Act, and the demand is made through the statutory order specifying how the information is to be furnished.

6. What Information Can Be Called For?

The section deliberately does not provide a closed list of data fields. Instead, it uses the phrase “information relating to any matter dealt with in connection with this Act.”

Depending on the specific order and GST matter, information could relate to areas such as:

  • Supply of goods or services.
  • Invoices and transaction records.
  • Accounting records relevant to GST.
  • Input tax credit transactions.
  • Output tax transactions.
  • Payments connected with taxable supplies.
  • Contracts or commercial arrangements relevant to a GST issue.
  • Registration-related information.
  • Reconciliation information.
  • Other information connected with administration of the CGST Act.
The order matters. A generic list of “documents normally asked under Section 151” should not replace reading the actual order. The exact information required depends on the subject matter specified by the competent authority.

7. Order, Time, Form and Manner

Section 151 specifically allows the order to prescribe three operational requirements:

TimeThe order can specify when the information must be furnished.
FormThe order can specify the format in which information must be provided.
MannerThe order can specify how the information is to be furnished.

This is important for businesses because receiving an information order is not simply a request to “send whatever you have.” The recipient should read the order carefully and identify every required field, document, period and submission method.

Example of a controlled response

  1. Read the order and identify the legal subject.
  2. Mark the information period.
  3. Create a list of every requested data field.
  4. Map each field to the source ledger/system/document.
  5. Validate totals and identifiers.
  6. Prepare the requested form/file.
  7. Obtain internal review and approval.
  8. Submit within the specified time and preserve evidence.

8. Section 151 Order vs a Normal GST Notice

Section 151 is specifically a power to call for information. It should not automatically be described as a demand notice, show-cause notice or assessment order.

Section 151 information orderDemand / adjudication proceeding
Primary purpose is to obtain specified information.May determine tax, interest, penalty or another legal consequence under the applicable provision.
Specifies information, time, form and manner.Operates under the relevant adjudication/recovery provision.
Receipt does not by itself establish tax liability.Legal liability depends on the applicable substantive/procedural provision and facts.
Requires careful factual response.May require a formal legal defence and hearing depending on the provision.
Practical point: An information request can become important evidence in later proceedings, but the Section 151 order itself should not automatically be described as a finding of tax liability.

9. Section 151 vs Section 150

Section 150Section 151
Obligation to furnish information return.Power to call for information.
Targets specified categories of persons/record-holders under the statutory information-return framework.Allows the Commissioner or authorised officer to direct any person to furnish information.
Operational details such as period/form/manner are prescribed.The order itself can specify time, form and manner.
Has a specific defective-return and notice mechanism under Section 150(2)–(3).Is framed as an order directing information to be furnished.

The two provisions can work alongside each other in a data-driven tax administration, but they should not be treated as interchangeable.

10. Section 151 and Confidentiality – Section 152

Section 152 places statutory restrictions on disclosure and use of information furnished for the purposes of Sections 150 and 151. The current Section 152 also contains a hearing safeguard before such information is used for proceedings under the Act in the circumstances covered by the provision.

Section 151 therefore should not be understood as creating an unrestricted public-disclosure power. The information-gathering power and the statutory rules governing disclosure are separate but connected parts of the framework.

Business implication: When responding to a Section 151 order, businesses should identify confidential commercial material and provide it in accordance with the order and applicable law, while maintaining a clear record of what was furnished and when.

11. What If the Information Is Not Furnished?

Section 151 itself is primarily an enabling power to call for information; it does not contain a separate penalty amount within its text.

If a person receives an order and fails to comply, the consequences must be examined under the applicable provisions of the CGST Act and the facts of the case. A compliance team should therefore not assume that every Section 151 order automatically carries a fixed penalty stated inside Section 151 itself.

Important historical distinction: Older versions of Section 124 referred to failure to furnish information or return under the old Section 151 statistics framework. Since Section 151 was substituted with effect from 1 January 2022, old articles quoting that provision should not be used as the sole basis for describing the current law.

12. Information Is Not Automatically Proof of Tax Liability

A Section 151 information order can seek facts and records. The existence of information does not automatically establish every legal conclusion that might later be drawn from it.

For example, a transaction record may establish that an entry exists, but further questions may remain:

  • Was the transaction actually a supply?
  • What was the contractual arrangement?
  • When did supply take place?
  • Which GSTIN made or received the supply?
  • Was tax charged correctly?
  • Was consideration paid?
  • Was ITC legally available?
  • Are there credit notes, amendments or cancellations?

These questions may require additional documents and analysis under the relevant substantive provisions.

13. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – Sales information

A company receives an order requiring specified sales information for a defined period. The company extracts the requested data from its sales register and GST records, validates it and furnishes it in the form and manner specified.

Example 2 – Purchase information

An officer seeks information relating to purchases connected with a GST matter. The business maps the request to its purchase register, invoices, ledger and reconciliation records.

Example 3 – Multiple GSTINs

A company operates several GST registrations. The order covers a particular GST-related matter. The company identifies which GSTINs and periods fall within the request instead of combining unrelated data.

Example 4 – ERP extraction

The requested information is maintained in an ERP. The finance team exports the relevant report, checks the date range and reconciles totals before submission.

Example 5 – Contract records

The information requested concerns a commercial arrangement. The business prepares the contract, amendments, invoices and payment evidence relevant to the specified transaction.

Example 6 – Data mismatch

A third-party record differs from the company's books. The company should not alter books merely to make them match. It should investigate the underlying transaction and explain the difference with evidence.

Example 7 – Information from a non-registered person

Because the current section says “any person,” a Section 151 order is not limited on its face to registered taxpayers. The recipient should read the order and determine why the information is being requested.

Example 8 – Specified file format

The order requires information in a particular format. The recipient should follow the specified format rather than sending an unrelated spreadsheet that omits required fields.

Example 9 – Deadline

An order specifies a date for furnishing information. The compliance team should treat that date as a statutory task deadline and maintain proof of submission.

Example 10 – Partial records

Some requested records are unavailable because of a system migration. The business should identify the gap, reconstruct information from reliable source records where possible and document the limitation rather than silently omitting the item.

Example 11 – Information vs demand

An information order asks for transaction details. It should not be described internally as a tax demand merely because the information could later be examined for tax implications.

Example 12 – Legal review

Where the request is complex, the finance team can prepare the factual response while tax/legal personnel review scope, relevance, confidentiality and the wording of the submission.

15. Business Records to Keep Ready

Businesses can reduce response time by maintaining a structured GST evidence repository.

Transaction layerSales register, purchase register, invoices, debit/credit notes and e-invoice/e-way bill records where applicable.
Accounting layerLedgers, trial balance, journal entries, bank records and reconciliation workings.
GST layerGSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B, annual returns, payment challans and electronic-ledger records.
  • Keep GSTIN-wise folders.
  • Separate financial-year and tax-period records.
  • Preserve original source files and final submitted reports.
  • Maintain a notice/order tracker.
  • Keep a reconciliation trail showing how reported numbers were derived.
  • Do not overwrite source evidence after a request is received.

16. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 – Using the old Section 151

The old “Power to collect statistics” text was replaced with effect from 1 January 2022.

Mistake 2 – Treating every information request as a tax demand

Section 151 is an information-gathering provision.

Mistake 3 – Ignoring the order's format

The order can specify the form and manner of furnishing information.

Mistake 4 – Sending unverified ERP exports

Raw system data can contain duplicates, cancelled entries, wrong GSTINs or date-range issues. Validate before submission.

Mistake 5 – Combining different GST registrations

Where the order is registration-specific, keep the information GSTIN-wise.

Mistake 6 – Missing the response deadline

Enter the deadline into the compliance tracker immediately.

Mistake 7 – Providing unrelated information

Focus on the information specified in the order and obtain advice where the scope is unclear.

Mistake 8 – Assuming information proves liability

Information may require further factual and legal analysis.

Mistake 9 – Forgetting confidentiality controls

Section 152 should be considered for information furnished under Section 151.

19. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 151 of the CGST Act?

It is the power to call for information. The Commissioner or an officer authorised by the Commissioner may, by an order, direct any person to furnish information relating to any matter dealt with in connection with the Act.

2. Is Section 151 still called “Power to collect statistics”?

No. That was the earlier version. With effect from 1 January 2022, Section 151 was substituted and is titled “Power to call for information.”

3. Who can issue an order under Section 151?

The Commissioner or an officer authorised by the Commissioner.

4. Can the order be issued to a person who is not registered under GST?

The current text says “any person.” Therefore, Section 151 is not drafted only for registered taxpayers. The relevance of the information to a matter connected with the Act and the actual order should be examined.

5. What information can be requested?

The section refers broadly to information relating to any matter dealt with in connection with the CGST Act. The actual order determines the information required.

6. Does Section 151 prescribe a fixed response period?

No fixed number of days appears in the section. The order specifies the time within which the information must be furnished.

7. Can the officer prescribe a particular format?

Yes. Section 151 expressly permits the order to specify the form and manner in which information is to be furnished.

8. Is a Section 151 order the same as a show-cause notice?

No. Section 151 is an information-gathering power. Whether a later show-cause notice or other proceeding arises depends on the facts and applicable provisions.

9. Does receiving a Section 151 order mean tax is payable?

No. The order itself is not automatically a tax demand.

10. Can information obtained under Section 151 be relevant to proceedings?

Information obtained by tax administration can be relevant to later proceedings subject to the Act, evidence and the safeguards applicable to its use and disclosure.

11. What is the connection with Section 152?

Section 152 contains restrictions and safeguards concerning disclosure and use of information furnished for Sections 150 and 151.

12. What is the connection with Section 150?

Section 150 creates an information-return obligation for specified persons and record-holders, while Section 151 is a direct power to call for information from any person through an order.

13. What is the connection with Section 70?

Section 70 deals with summons for giving evidence or producing documents. It is a separate statutory power from Section 151.

14. What should a company do after receiving a Section 151 order?

Register the order, identify the scope and deadline, gather source records, reconcile the information, review the response and submit it in the specified form and manner.

15. Should ERP data be sent without checking?

No. ERP exports should be validated against books, GST returns and source documents before submission.

16. Does Section 151 itself prescribe a penalty amount?

No. Section 151 itself contains the power to call for information; it does not state a standalone penalty amount in its text.
