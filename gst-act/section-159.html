Section 159 of the CGST Act, 2017

Publication of information in respect of persons in certain cases. Understand when the Commissioner or an authorised officer can publish a person's name and particulars relating to GST proceedings or prosecution, the protection available before penalty-related publication, and the special rule for firms, companies and associations.

1. Section 159 at a Glance

Core powerThe Commissioner or an officer authorised by him may publish the name and other particulars of a person where publication is considered necessary or expedient in the public interest.
What can be published?The provision covers the person's name and other particulars relating to proceedings or prosecution under the CGST Act.
Penalty safeguardPublication relating to a penalty is restricted until the Section 107 appeal period has expired without appeal, or the appeal has been disposed of.
In simple language: Section 159 gives the GST administration a specific statutory power to publish the name and relevant particulars of a person in certain cases when the authorised authority considers publication necessary or expedient in the public interest. It is not a general power to publish every taxpayer's GST information.

3. Statutory Text of Section 159

159. Publication of information in respect of persons in certain cases.—

(1) If the Commissioner, or any other officer authorised by him in this behalf, is of the opinion that it is necessary or expedient in the public interest to publish the name of any person and any other particulars relating to any proceedings or prosecution under this Act in respect of such person, it may cause to be published such name and particulars in such manner as it thinks fit.

(2) No publication under this section shall be made in relation to any penalty imposed under this Act until the time for presenting an appeal to the Appellate Authority under section 107 has expired without an appeal having been presented or the appeal, if presented, has been disposed of.

Explanation.— In the case of firm, company or other association of persons, the names of the partners of the firm, directors, managing agents, secretaries and treasurers or managers of the company, or the members of the association, as the case may be, may also be published if, in the opinion of the Commissioner, or any other officer authorised by him in this behalf, circumstances of the case justify it.
Important: Section 159 is different from Section 158. Section 158 establishes a general confidentiality framework with specified exceptions, while Section 159 specifically provides a power to publish a person's name and particulars in certain public-interest circumstances.

4. Section 159 in Simple Language

GST proceedings can involve significant information about taxpayers, businesses, transactions, demands and prosecutions. Section 159 creates a specific statutory mechanism for publication of information concerning persons where the authorised authority considers publication necessary or expedient in the public interest.

The provision has two important safeguards/features:

  • The publication decision must come from the Commissioner or an officer authorised by him.
  • The authority must form an opinion that publication is necessary or expedient in the public interest.

For penalty matters, there is an additional statutory timing safeguard under Section 159(2). Publication cannot be made in relation to a penalty until the Section 107 appeal period has expired without an appeal, or an appeal already filed has been disposed of.

Simple example: If a penalty order has just been passed and the taxpayer is still within the statutory period to appeal, Section 159(2) prevents publication of that penalty under Section 159 during that pending appeal window.

5. Who Can Authorise Publication?

PersonRole under Section 159Important condition
CommissionerCan form the required opinion and cause publication.Must consider publication necessary or expedient in the public interest.
Other officer authorised by the CommissionerCan exercise the power when properly authorised.Same public-interest requirement applies.

The section does not say that every GST officer can independently publish taxpayer particulars. The authority must be the Commissioner or an officer authorised by the Commissioner for this purpose.

Why authorisation matters

Publication of a person's name and GST-related particulars can have significant consequences. Section 159 therefore places the power at a specified level of authority instead of making it an ordinary operational power of every field officer.

6. Public Interest – The Key Test

The expression “necessary or expedient in the public interest” is central to Section 159(1).

The provision does not simply say that publication can occur whenever a proceeding exists. The Commissioner or authorised officer must form the statutory opinion that publication is necessary or expedient in the public interest.

QuestionWhy it matters
Is there a GST proceeding or prosecution?The particulars must relate to proceedings or prosecution under the CGST Act in respect of the person.
Who is making the publication decision?The section identifies the Commissioner or an officer authorised by him.
Has the public-interest opinion been formed?This is an express statutory condition.
Is the matter a penalty?Section 159(2) imposes a separate timing restriction linked to the Section 107 appeal process.
Is the person an entity with connected individuals?The Explanation permits specified names of partners/directors/other persons to also be published where circumstances justify it.
Do not equate “public interest” with “any information the department considers interesting.” Section 159 contains a specific statutory test and should be applied according to the Act and the facts of the case.

7. What Information Can Be Published?

Section 159(1) refers to:

  • the name of any person; and
  • any other particulars relating to any proceedings or prosecution under the Act in respect of that person.

The wording therefore goes beyond the person's name alone. However, the particulars must be connected with the relevant proceedings or prosecution, and the statutory public-interest test must be satisfied.

Potential categoryHow to understand it
Name of personThe section expressly permits publication of the person's name.
Particulars of proceedingsRelevant information about proceedings under the CGST Act can fall within the provision.
Particulars of prosecutionInformation relating to prosecution under the Act may be covered.
Connected persons in entitiesThe Explanation permits certain names to be published where circumstances justify it.

Section 159 does not itself prescribe a single mandatory website, newspaper or database. It says publication may occur “in such manner as it thinks fit”, subject to the statutory conditions.

8. Special Protection for Penalty Cases

Section 159(2) is particularly important for taxpayers because it creates a timing restriction specifically for publication relating to a penalty imposed under the Act.

No publication under Section 159 can be made in relation to that penalty until either:

  1. the time for presenting an appeal to the Appellate Authority under Section 107 has expired without an appeal being presented; or
  2. an appeal has been presented and that appeal has been disposed of.
Practical meaning: A penalty order does not automatically mean that publication can immediately take place under Section 159. The statutory appeal stage mentioned in Section 159(2) must first be dealt with.

9. Relationship With Section 107 Appeal

Section 107 provides the first appellate mechanism against specified orders under GST law. Section 159(2) specifically refers to the time for presenting an appeal to the Appellate Authority under Section 107.

SituationSection 159(2) position
Penalty imposed; Section 107 appeal period still openPublication relating to that penalty cannot be made under Section 159 during that period.
Appeal period expires without appealThe statutory condition in Section 159(2) is satisfied.
Appeal filed within timePublication relating to the penalty must wait until the appeal is disposed of.
Appeal disposedThe specific waiting condition in Section 159(2) is satisfied, subject to the other requirements of Section 159.
Important distinction: Section 159(2) does not itself decide whether the penalty is legally correct. It establishes a timing safeguard for publication.

10. Firms, Companies and Associations

The Explanation to Section 159 specifically addresses entities such as firms, companies and other associations of persons.

Where the circumstances of the case justify it, the names of specified persons connected with the entity may also be published:

EntityPersons specifically mentioned
FirmPartners
CompanyDirectors, managing agents, secretaries and treasurers or managers
Association of personsMembers

The Explanation does not mean that every director, partner, manager or member is automatically published whenever a company, firm or association becomes subject to GST proceedings. The wording requires the circumstances of the case to justify such publication and the opinion of the Commissioner or authorised officer.

11. Section 159 and Conviction / Prosecution

Section 159 expressly covers particulars relating to proceedings or prosecution under the CGST Act. This means the provision is not limited to penalty orders.

CBIC has also issued administrative instructions concerning publication of names of persons convicted under the Act, directing that Section 159 should be invoked in deserving cases in respect of persons convicted under the Act. This is an administrative instruction and should be distinguished from the wording of Section 159 itself. citeturn0search39

Legal reading: Always distinguish between (1) the statutory power in Section 159, (2) departmental instructions about how the power should be used, and (3) the facts of the individual case.

Memory aid: Section 158 asks, “When may protected GST information be disclosed?” Section 159 asks, “When may the name and particulars of a person connected with GST proceedings or prosecution be published in the public interest?”

13. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – Ordinary GST proceeding

A taxpayer is undergoing a GST proceeding. The existence of the proceeding alone does not mean that Section 159 automatically requires publication. The statutory public-interest opinion must be formed by the competent authority.

Example 2 – Penalty order just issued

A penalty is imposed today. If the Section 107 appeal period is still running, Section 159(2) prevents publication in relation to that penalty during the statutory waiting period.

Example 3 – Penalty appealed

The taxpayer files an appeal under Section 107. Publication relating to the penalty must wait until the appeal is disposed of.

Example 4 – Appeal period expires without appeal

If the appeal period expires without an appeal, the specific Section 159(2) waiting condition is satisfied, subject to the other requirements of Section 159.

Example 5 – Company proceeding

A company is involved in a GST proceeding and the competent authority considers publication necessary or expedient in the public interest. Section 159 can potentially cover the company's name and relevant particulars.

Example 6 – Directors' names

The circumstances may justify publication of directors' names under the Explanation. Such publication is not automatic merely because they are directors.

Example 7 – Partnership firm

Where a firm is involved in relevant proceedings and the statutory circumstances justify it, names of partners may also be published.

Example 8 – Association of persons

The Explanation allows names of members to be published where the circumstances justify it and the competent authority forms the required opinion.

Example 9 – Prosecution

Particulars relating to prosecution under the CGST Act can fall within Section 159 where the public-interest test is satisfied.

Example 10 – Unauthorised publication

A field officer independently uploads a taxpayer's particulars without the authority contemplated by Section 159. The question of statutory authority and compliance with the provision would need to be examined.

Example 11 – Section 158 confidentiality

A business asks why all GST information is not publicly available. Section 158 provides the general confidentiality framework, while Section 159 creates a specific publication power in qualifying circumstances.

Example 12 – Publication after appeal disposal

A penalty appeal is filed and later disposed of. If publication is still considered necessary or expedient in the public interest, the Section 159(2) waiting condition has been addressed, but the other requirements of Section 159 still need to be satisfied.

15. What to Do If Publication Is a Concern

  1. Identify the exact proceeding: obtain the notice, order, prosecution document or other relevant record.
  2. Identify the authority: determine whether the decision comes from the Commissioner or an officer authorised for the purpose.
  3. Check the public-interest basis: review the stated reasons and factual circumstances.
  4. Determine whether it concerns a penalty: if yes, immediately check the Section 107 appeal period and status.
  5. File the appropriate appeal/remedy: do not miss statutory deadlines merely because a publication issue is being considered.
  6. Preserve the record: maintain service proof, order copies, appeal acknowledgement and correspondence.
  7. Obtain professional advice: publication issues can involve statutory interpretation and procedural questions that should be assessed on the actual facts.
Do not assume that publication itself determines the tax liability. The publication power and the merits of the underlying GST proceeding are separate legal questions.

16. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 – “Every GST defaulter's name can be published.”

Section 159 requires the competent authority to form the specified public-interest opinion.

Mistake 2 – “A penalty can be published immediately.”

Section 159(2) imposes a waiting condition linked to the Section 107 appeal process.

Mistake 3 – “Once an appeal is filed, publication is permanently prohibited.”

The section says publication must wait until the appeal is disposed of. It does not create a permanent prohibition.

Mistake 4 – “All company directors are automatically named.”

The Explanation permits specified names to be published where the circumstances justify it.

Mistake 5 – “Section 159 and Section 158 are the same.”

Section 158 is principally a confidentiality/disclosure provision; Section 159 is a publication power in specified circumstances.

Mistake 6 – “Section 159 itself proves guilt.”

Publication under a statutory power does not replace the underlying adjudication, appeal or prosecution process.

Mistake 7 – Ignoring appeal deadlines

Section 159(2) specifically links penalty publication to the Section 107 appeal process, making deadline tracking important.

Mistake 8 – Treating old departmental guidance as the statutory text

Administrative instructions can explain departmental practice, but the current Act remains the primary statutory reference.

18. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 159 of the CGST Act?

It allows the Commissioner or an authorised officer, where considered necessary or expedient in the public interest, to publish a person's name and other particulars relating to proceedings or prosecution under the CGST Act.

2. Can the GST department publish every taxpayer's name?

No. Section 159 contains a specific public-interest test and applies to names and particulars relating to proceedings or prosecution under the Act.

3. Who can exercise the power?

The Commissioner or another officer authorised by the Commissioner for this purpose.

4. What does “necessary or expedient in the public interest” mean?

It is the statutory opinion that the competent authority must form before exercising the publication power. Whether that requirement is satisfied depends on the facts and applicable law.

5. Can penalty information be published immediately after a penalty order?

No. Section 159(2) prevents publication relating to the penalty until the Section 107 appeal period expires without appeal or an appeal already filed is disposed of.

6. What if the taxpayer files an appeal?

Publication relating to the penalty must wait until that appeal is disposed of.

7. What if no appeal is filed?

Once the Section 107 appeal period has expired without an appeal, the specific waiting condition in Section 159(2) is satisfied, subject to the remaining requirements of Section 159.

8. Can directors' names be published?

Yes, the Explanation allows specified names such as directors' names to also be published where the circumstances of the case justify it and the competent authority forms the required opinion.

9. Can partners' names be published?

Yes, the Explanation specifically refers to partners of a firm, subject to the conditions in Section 159.

10. Can names of association members be published?

Yes, the Explanation refers to members of an association of persons where the circumstances justify publication.

11. What particulars can be published?

The section refers to the person's name and other particulars relating to proceedings or prosecution under the Act in respect of that person.

12. Does Section 159 itself create a tax demand?

No. It is a publication provision. The underlying tax, penalty, adjudication or prosecution follows its own statutory framework.

13. Is Section 159 the same as Section 158?

No. Section 158 establishes confidentiality and disclosure rules for specified GST information, while Section 159 provides a specific publication power in qualifying circumstances.

14. Is publication under Section 159 automatic after conviction?

The section still requires the competent authority to exercise the publication power according to its statutory conditions. CBIC has separately issued instructions concerning publication in deserving conviction cases.

15. Can publication happen before a penalty appeal is decided?

Section 159(2) says no publication under the section in relation to that penalty until the appeal is disposed of.

16. Does Section 159 prevent a taxpayer from appealing?

No. The taxpayer's statutory appeal rights remain governed by the applicable GST provisions. In fact, Section 159(2) specifically refers to the Section 107 appeal process.

17. Where can publication occur?

Section 159 says the name and particulars may be published “in such manner as it thinks fit”, subject to the statutory requirements.

18. Is this page legal advice?

No. It is an educational explanation. Verify the current CGST Act, rules, notifications, circulars, departmental instructions and judicial interpretation applicable to the relevant date and facts.

19. Quick Revision – Section 159

  • Section 159 = Publication of information in respect of persons in certain cases.
  • Power rests with the Commissioner or an officer authorised by him.
  • Publication requires an opinion that it is necessary or expedient in the public interest.
  • Publication can concern a person's name and particulars relating to GST proceedings or prosecution.
  • For penalties, Section 159(2) prevents publication until the Section 107 appeal period expires without appeal or the appeal is disposed of.
  • The Explanation covers possible publication of specified names connected with firms, companies and associations where circumstances justify it.
  • Section 159 is different from Section 158's general confidentiality/disclosure framework.
  • Publication does not itself determine the merits of the underlying tax dispute.
  • CBIC has separately issued administrative instructions concerning publication in deserving conviction cases.
