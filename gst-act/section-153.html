Section 153 of the CGST Act, 2017

Taking assistance from an expert. Understand when a GST officer can obtain expert assistance in scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or other proceedings, why the provision exists and how expert involvement can work in complex tax matters.

CGST Act Section 153Expert AssistanceGST InvestigationScrutinyTechnical Expertise

Section 153 at a Glance

Who can use it?Any officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.
When?At any stage of scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or any other proceedings before the officer.
Why?Having regard to the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue.
In simple language: Section 153 allows a sufficiently senior GST officer to take assistance from an expert when a case requires specialised knowledge. The section recognises that some GST matters can involve accounting, valuation, information technology, engineering, industry-specific processes, legal or other technical issues that an investigating officer may need expert help to understand.

1. Statutory Text of Section 153

153. Taking assistance from an expert.—

Any officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner may, having regard to the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue, take assistance of any expert at any stage of scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or any other proceedings before him.
Current-law position: Section 153 is in force from 1 July 2017 and the current text continues to provide for expert assistance by an officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner. Current consolidated sources do not show a separate Section 153 rule, form, notification, circular or instruction prescribing a special procedure under this section.

2. Section 153 Explained in Simple Language

GST cases can sometimes go beyond routine return checking. A dispute may involve complex accounting, software systems, valuation, manufacturing processes, engineering measurements, industry practices or other technical questions.

Section 153 recognises this practical reality. It permits an officer of at least the rank of Assistant Commissioner to obtain assistance from an expert when the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue justify it.

Not every caseThe section is framed around cases where the relevant circumstances justify expert assistance.
Not one specific professionThe Act does not list a closed category of experts in Section 153.
Any stageThe provision can operate during scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or other proceedings before the officer.

3. Who Can Take Expert Assistance?

The statutory threshold is important: the officer must be not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.

OfficerSection 153 wordingPractical implication
CommissionerAbove the minimum rank.Falls within the “not below” threshold.
Additional/Joint/Deputy CommissionerAbove the minimum rank where applicable.Falls within the statutory threshold.
Assistant CommissionerMinimum rank expressly specified.Can take expert assistance under Section 153.
Officer below Assistant CommissionerBelow the stated threshold.Section 153 itself does not confer this power on such an officer.
Do not confuse officer hierarchy with authorisation for a particular proceeding. Section 153 states who may take assistance under this provision; other provisions may separately govern jurisdiction, delegation or procedural authority.

4. Conditions in Section 153

Section 153 contains three important qualifiers.

1. Nature and complexity of the case

The officer must have regard to the nature and complexity of the case. The provision is therefore particularly relevant where specialised knowledge may help understand the matter.

2. Interest of revenue

The officer must also have regard to the interest of revenue. This phrase connects expert assistance with the tax-administration purpose of the proceeding.

3. Stage of proceeding

The assistance may be taken at any stage of scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or any other proceedings before the officer.

Easy formula: Qualified officer + nature/complexity of case + interest of revenue + relevant proceeding = Section 153 expert assistance.

5. Who Is an “Expert”?

Section 153 does not provide an exhaustive statutory list of professions or qualifications for the word expert. The appropriate expert would depend on the technical subject involved in the case.

Depending on the facts, specialised assistance could potentially be relevant in areas such as:

AreaIllustrative expertise
AccountingComplex accounting treatment, cost allocation, revenue recognition or financial records.
Tax / lawSpecialised legal or tax analysis where appropriate.
Information technologyERP architecture, database records, digital evidence, software-generated reports or data integrity.
EngineeringTechnical characteristics of machinery, construction, manufacturing or infrastructure activities.
ValuationTechnical valuation issues where specialist knowledge is relevant.
Industry processesSpecialised manufacturing, mining, chemical, pharmaceutical or other technical processes.
Data analyticsLarge datasets, transaction patterns or technical data analysis.
Important: These are examples of areas in which expert knowledge might be useful. Section 153 itself does not say that every person holding one of these qualifications is automatically an “expert” for every GST case.

6. At What Stage Can Expert Assistance Be Taken?

The provision expressly covers:

  • Scrutiny
  • Inquiry
  • Investigation
  • Any other proceedings before the officer
StageIllustrative situation
ScrutinyComplex reconciliation, valuation or accounting treatment requires technical analysis.
InquiryThe officer needs specialised understanding of a transaction or business process.
InvestigationDigital records, manufacturing processes or technical evidence require expert assistance.
Other proceedingsThe wording is broad enough to cover other proceedings before the officer where the statutory conditions are met.

7. “Nature and Complexity of the Case”

This phrase is central to Section 153. Not every factual question is technically complex.

Examples of potentially complex matters include:

  • Multi-location accounting structures.
  • Large ERP datasets requiring technical extraction or validation.
  • Specialised manufacturing processes relevant to classification or valuation.
  • Engineering measurements in works contracts.
  • Technical characteristics of machinery or equipment.
  • Complex financial arrangements.
  • Industry-specific business models.
  • Digital records requiring specialist examination.
Example:

A large infrastructure contractor has thousands of project transactions, equipment movements and complex cost allocations. If a GST proceeding requires understanding a technical project process, an officer may consider expert assistance under Section 153.

8. “Interest of Revenue”

The section also requires the officer to have regard to the interest of revenue. This does not mean that every expert engagement must result in additional tax demand.

Expert assistance can help the administration understand facts accurately, which may support either a revenue-related finding or clarification that a proposed tax position is not supported by the technical facts.

Important distinction: “Interest of revenue” is a statutory consideration for taking expert assistance. It is not itself a conclusion that tax has been evaded or that additional tax is payable.

9. What Can an Expert Help With?

The Act does not prescribe a detailed job description. The assistance should be connected with the case before the officer.

IssuePossible expert contribution
ERP / digital recordsExplain database structure, extraction methodology, system-generated reports and technical data relationships.
Engineering workExplain quantities, measurements, technical specifications or project processes.
ManufacturingExplain production stages, inputs, outputs, wastage and technical characteristics.
ValuationAssist with technical valuation analysis where the case requires it.
AccountingExplain complex books, accounting entries, cost structures or financial data.
Data analysisAnalyse large datasets and identify patterns or relationships relevant to the proceeding.

10. Expert Assistance Is Not Automatic Delegation

Section 153 says an officer may take assistance of an expert. The wording does not say that the expert becomes the statutory decision-maker under the CGST Act.

The officer remains the officer conducting the relevant proceeding. Expert assistance is intended to provide specialised knowledge to help understand the case.

Practical distinction: “Expert assistance” should not automatically be described as “transfer of the GST case to an expert.” The statutory provision does not say that the expert replaces the officer.

Why this matters

  • The expert's role should be understood in the context of the assignment.
  • The statutory proceeding remains before the competent officer.
  • Expert analysis does not automatically decide tax liability.
  • The underlying legal decision remains governed by the CGST Act and applicable procedure.

11. What It Means for a Taxpayer

A taxpayer may encounter expert assistance where the department is dealing with a technically complex matter. This does not by itself establish wrongdoing.

For a taxpayer, the practical response is to ensure that its own technical and accounting evidence is organised.

Prepare the factual foundation

  • Contracts and work orders.
  • Invoices and debit/credit notes.
  • Ledgers and trial balance.
  • GST returns and reconciliation workings.
  • Technical specifications where relevant.
  • Project measurement records where relevant.
  • ERP reports and source data.
  • Bank/payment evidence.
  • Internal approvals and correspondence.

12. Expert Opinion vs Evidence

Expert assistance and documentary evidence are not necessarily the same thing.

ConceptMeaning
Expert assistanceSpecialised help to understand or analyse a technically complex matter.
Documentary evidenceContracts, invoices, ledgers, reports, records and other documents relevant to the facts.
Taxpayer explanationThe person's factual and legal response to the issue.
Officer's findingThe conclusion reached under the applicable GST provision and procedure.

A technical opinion does not automatically replace the underlying source records. Similarly, a taxpayer should not rely only on a verbal explanation where the issue can be supported with contemporaneous documents.

13. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – ERP investigation

An investigation involves millions of ERP entries. A technical expert may help explain database structures, extraction logic and relationships between transaction tables.

Example 2 – Construction project

A works-contract matter involves technical quantities and project measurements. An engineering expert may help explain the technical records relevant to the GST issue.

Example 3 – Manufacturing process

A classification or valuation question depends on the technical manufacturing process. Specialist industry knowledge may assist the officer in understanding the process.

Example 4 – Mining operation

A mining business's records involve extraction, processing, transportation and multiple contractors. Technical assistance may help explain the operational chain relevant to the tax question.

Example 5 – Complex accounting

A company uses complicated project accounting and cost allocations. An accounting expert may assist in understanding how the books were constructed.

Example 6 – Digital evidence

The case concerns electronic records and system logs. An IT expert may help interpret how the system generated, stored or exported the records.

Example 7 – Valuation

A transaction requires specialised valuation knowledge. An appropriate valuation expert may assist the officer in understanding technical valuation inputs.

Example 8 – Large data analysis

The officer needs to compare a very large number of transactions. Data-analysis assistance may help identify patterns requiring further verification.

Example 9 – Expert does not decide tax

An expert provides technical analysis. The tax conclusion still has to be made under the applicable GST law by the competent authority.

Example 10 – Taxpayer disagrees with technical interpretation

The taxpayer can present its own technical documents and explanation through the applicable proceeding. A disagreement should be addressed with evidence rather than simply asserting that an expert's view is wrong.

Example 11 – Expert assistance during scrutiny

A complex reconciliation issue is identified during scrutiny. Section 153 permits expert assistance even though the matter has not reached a full investigation stage.

Example 12 – Other proceedings

The section is not limited to the three listed stages. It also covers “any other proceedings” before the officer, subject to the statutory conditions.

17. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 – Assuming Section 153 applies only to tax experts

The Act does not define a closed list of expert professions. The relevant expertise depends on the case.

Mistake 2 – Thinking the expert replaces the officer

Section 153 permits assistance; it does not say that the expert becomes the statutory adjudicating officer.

Mistake 3 – Assuming expert involvement proves wrongdoing

The provision is triggered by the nature and complexity of the case and interest of revenue. Expert involvement itself is not a finding of fraud.

Mistake 4 – Ignoring technical records

Businesses sometimes focus only on invoices and returns even where the tax issue depends on an underlying technical process.

Mistake 5 – Sending unexplained ERP extracts

Large data files should be accompanied by a clear description of fields, period, source and reconciliation methodology.

Mistake 6 – Confusing expert assistance with summons

Section 153 concerns the officer taking assistance; Section 70 is a separate power concerning summons.

Mistake 7 – Treating an expert's view as the final tax order

The legal consequence depends on the competent authority and the applicable statutory proceeding.

19. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 153 of the CGST Act?

Section 153 allows an officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner to take assistance of an expert, having regard to the nature and complexity of the case and the interest of revenue.

2. Who can take expert assistance?

Any officer not below the rank of Assistant Commissioner may take assistance under Section 153.

3. At what stage can expert assistance be taken?

At any stage of scrutiny, inquiry, investigation or any other proceedings before the officer.

4. Does the Act define “expert”?

Section 153 does not provide an exhaustive definition or closed list of expert professions. The appropriate expertise depends on the nature and complexity of the case.

5. Can an IT expert be relevant?

Potentially, where the case involves technical IT systems, databases, digital records or other specialist issues. The actual relevance depends on the case.

6. Can an engineer be relevant?

Potentially, where technical engineering knowledge is relevant to the GST issue, such as certain project, manufacturing or machinery matters.

7. Can a Chartered Accountant be an expert?

Accounting or financial expertise may be relevant in an appropriate case, but Section 153 does not say that a CA is automatically an expert for every GST matter.

8. Does expert assistance mean the case is transferred to the expert?

No. The section says the officer may take assistance of an expert. It does not itself make the expert the statutory decision-maker.

9. Does expert involvement mean the taxpayer has committed an offence?

No. Expert assistance is a procedural facility for complex matters and is not by itself a finding of wrongdoing.

10. What does “interest of revenue” mean?

It is one of the statutory considerations the officer must have regard to when deciding to take expert assistance. It does not itself establish additional tax liability.

11. Is there a special Rule or Form under Section 153?

Current consolidated reference material identifies no specific Section 153 rule, form, circular or instruction prescribing a separate procedure. The section itself contains the substantive power.

12. Is Section 153 currently in force?

Yes. It has been in force from 1 July 2017 and current legal sources continue to reproduce the provision.

13. How is Section 153 different from Section 151?

Section 151 is a power to call for information. Section 153 concerns an officer obtaining expert assistance in a proceeding.

14. How is Section 153 different from Section 70?

Section 70 concerns summons to give evidence or produce documents. Section 153 concerns expert assistance to the officer.

15. Can expert assistance be taken during scrutiny?

Yes. Scrutiny is expressly included in Section 153.

16. Can expert assistance be taken during investigation?

Yes. Investigation is expressly included.

17. Can expert assistance be taken in other proceedings?

Yes. The section also covers “any other proceedings” before the officer.

18. What should a taxpayer do if an expert is involved?

Maintain clear factual, accounting and technical records and respond through the applicable proceeding. Where the matter is complex, obtain appropriate professional assistance for the taxpayer's own response.

  • Current reference material does not identify a separate Section 153 rule/form/circular prescribing a special procedure.
