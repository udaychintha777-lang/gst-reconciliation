1. Why Section 48 matters

Searches such as “Section 48 CGST Act”, “GST Practitioner”, “GSTP eligibility”, “GST practitioner functions”, “how to become GST practitioner”, “GSTP authorisation”, “PCT-05” and “Rule 83 GST” all point to the legal framework governing professionals who are authorised to perform prescribed GST functions for registered persons.

Section 48 does not make a GST Practitioner a substitute for the taxpayer. Instead, it creates a regulated mechanism through which an approved GST Practitioner can perform prescribed GST filing and related functions on behalf of an authorised registered person.

Core principle: A GSTP can perform authorised functions, but Section 48(3) expressly keeps responsibility for the correctness of the particulars furnished with the registered person on whose behalf the details or return are filed.

2. What is a GST Practitioner?

A Goods and Services Tax Practitioner, commonly called a GSTP, is a person approved/enrolled under the GST framework to perform prescribed functions for registered persons.

The GSTP framework is designed to allow taxpayers to delegate specified compliance activities without transferring the taxpayer's statutory responsibility for the accuracy of the information furnished.

PartyRole
Registered personOwns the tax compliance and remains responsible for correctness.
GST PractitionerPerforms authorised functions within the prescribed framework.
Government/Proper officerApproves, regulates and can remove/disqualify GSTPs according to the Rules.

3. Section 48 statutory structure

Section 48 contains three important principles.

Sub-sectionMeaning
48(1)Approval, eligibility, duties, obligations, removal and other GSTP conditions are prescribed through the Rules.
48(2)A registered person may authorise an approved GSTP to furnish specified details/returns and perform other prescribed functions.
48(3)Correctness of particulars furnished by the GSTP continues to remain the responsibility of the registered person.

The current Section 48 text includes outward supplies under Section 37 and returns under Sections 39, 44 and 45, along with other prescribed functions. The reference to inward supplies under Section 38 was omitted with effect from 1 October 2022. citeturn0search0turn0search7

4. GSTP eligibility

The detailed eligibility conditions are prescribed in Rule 83. The Rules specify categories of persons who may qualify for enrolment as a GST Practitioner, subject to the conditions prescribed at the relevant time.

The eligibility framework has historically covered specified professional/accounting/taxation backgrounds and persons satisfying prescribed educational and examination conditions. Because eligibility rules can be amended, a person applying today should use the current Rule 83 requirements rather than relying on an old list copied from an earlier GST article.

Important: Section 48 itself does not contain the complete eligibility list. Section 48 delegates those details to the Rules.

5. Approval and enrolment

Rule 83 provides the procedural framework for enrolment as a GST Practitioner. The applicant submits the prescribed application and the proper officer may undertake verification before accepting or rejecting the application.

The GSTP framework includes:

  • application for enrolment;
  • verification of eligibility;
  • approval or rejection;
  • examination requirements where applicable;
  • deemed enrolment in specified circumstances;
  • duties and confirmation requirements; and
  • disqualification/removal procedure.

The Rules currently contain the GSTP application and enrolment procedure in Rule 83 and the related prescribed forms.

6. GSTP examination

Examination requirements are part of the Rules rather than Section 48 itself. Rule 83 and Rule 83A contain the relevant examination framework.

For a person planning to become a GSTP, the practical sequence is:

  1. Confirm eligibility under the current Rule 83.
  2. Apply through the prescribed GSTP enrolment process.
  3. Complete examination requirements where applicable.
  4. Obtain approved/enrolled GSTP status.
  5. Accept taxpayer authorisations before performing authorised functions.

7. Taxpayer authorisation

Approval as a GSTP does not automatically authorise the practitioner to file returns for every registered person. The taxpayer must authorise the GSTP through the prescribed GST Portal process.

Section 48(2) is therefore best understood as a two-level framework:

Level 1 — GSTP approval
The person is approved/enrolled as a GST Practitioner.
Level 2 — Taxpayer authorisation
A specific registered person authorises the GSTP to perform permitted functions.

8. Functions a GSTP can perform

The precise functions are prescribed through the Rules. Rule 83 includes the framework for activities that an authorised GSTP may perform on behalf of a registered person.

Depending on the applicable Rules and authorisation, GSTP functions can include activities connected with:

  • furnishing outward-supply details;
  • furnishing specified GST returns;
  • annual return/final-return compliance;
  • registration-related applications;
  • amendment/cancellation-related applications;
  • refund applications;
  • other prescribed GST Portal functions; and
  • related compliance submissions permitted under the Rules.

Section 48 should always be read with the current Rule 83 list because the Act provides the authority while the Rules define the detailed operational scope.

10. Taxpayer confirmation and consent

The GSTP Rules contain safeguards intended to ensure that the registered person knows what is being furnished on their behalf. Rule 83 includes a mechanism under which the GSTP obtains confirmation/consent from the registered person before furnishing specified returns/details.

This is an important internal control because GSTP authorisation should not be treated as an unlimited standing instruction to submit any figure without taxpayer review.

Best practice: maintain a documented maker-checker process: GSTP prepares → taxpayer reviews → taxpayer confirms → GSTP files → acknowledgement is archived.

11. Taxpayer remains responsible

Section 48(3) is the most important risk-allocation provision in this section. Even when an approved GSTP files the return or details, the registered person remains responsible for correctness.

SituationResponsibility principle
GSTP enters wrong turnoverRegistered person's statutory responsibility continues.
GSTP claims ineligible ITCTaxpayer must ensure the return is correct.
Taxpayer supplies incorrect dataAuthorisation does not transfer the underlying responsibility to GSTP.
GSTP files after taxpayer confirmationTaxpayer still remains responsible for correctness.

12. GSTP duties

Rule 83 prescribes duties and obligations for GST Practitioners. The GSTP should:

  • act only within the authorised scope;
  • maintain confidentiality and proper records;
  • obtain required taxpayer confirmation;
  • avoid unauthorised filings;
  • maintain accurate working papers;
  • cooperate with prescribed examination/verification requirements; and
  • comply with the GSTP Rules and portal procedures.

The exact operative duties should be read from the current Rule 83 rather than relying on a historical checklist.

13. Removal/disqualification

A GST Practitioner can face removal/disqualification where the conditions prescribed in Rule 83 are breached, including specified misconduct or failure to satisfy the continuing requirements.

The Rules provide a procedure involving notice and opportunity as prescribed before disqualification in the covered circumstances.

Important: GSTP enrolment is not an unrestricted professional licence. The approval remains subject to the statutory and Rules-based conditions.

14. Appeal against disqualification

Rule 83 provides an appeal mechanism against an order of disqualification in the prescribed circumstances. The Rules specify the time and procedure for filing the appeal.

A GSTP facing disqualification should preserve the original order, grounds, supporting documents and evidence of compliance before filing the prescribed appeal.

15. Deemed enrolment

The GSTP Rules contain a deemed-enrolment mechanism in specified circumstances. This is a Rule-based concept and should not be confused with automatic qualification of every person who satisfies a professional category.

Historical transitional provisions also dealt with persons who were already practising as tax return preparers under earlier indirect-tax laws. Such historical enrolment rules should be distinguished from the current GSTP enrolment framework.

16. GSTP and digital filing

Section 48 is fundamentally a digital-compliance provision. The GSTP does not merely sign a paper return; the practitioner uses the GST Portal/common portal to perform authorised functions electronically.

The practical control chain should therefore include:

  1. GSTP login/authorised access;
  2. taxpayer authorisation;
  3. data preparation;
  4. taxpayer confirmation;
  5. electronic filing;
  6. ARN/acknowledgement capture; and
  7. working-paper retention.

17. GSTP vs tax consultant, CA or accountant

“GST Practitioner” is a specific statutory status under the GST framework. A person may be a CA, tax consultant, accountant or other professional and separately qualify/enrol as a GSTP if the statutory conditions are met.

ConceptMeaning
GST PractitionerStatutory GSTP status governed by Section 48 and Rule 83.
CA/tax professionalProfessional qualification/status under a separate legal framework.
AccountantBusiness role; does not by itself establish GSTP status.
GSTP authorisationSpecific taxpayer authorisation to perform prescribed GST functions.

18. Compliance governance

For businesses using GSTPs, authorisation should be incorporated into the tax-control framework.

  • Maintain a list of approved GSTPs.
  • Map each GSTP to authorised GSTINs.
  • Use written engagement terms.
  • Define who prepares and who approves returns.
  • Require taxpayer confirmation before filing.
  • Retain return workings and acknowledgements.
  • Review access when the engagement ends.

19. Construction and infrastructure examples

Large construction and infrastructure businesses often operate multiple GSTINs, project sites and high-volume invoice populations. A GSTP can assist with routine filings, but Section 48(3) makes management review critical.

20. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — GSTP files GSTR-1

The company authorises a GSTP. The GSTP prepares and files GSTR-1 after receiving taxpayer confirmation. The company remains responsible for the correctness of the reported invoices.

Example 2 — wrong ITC in GSTR-3B

A GSTP accidentally claims ineligible ITC. The taxpayer cannot simply argue that the GSTP filed the return; Section 48(3) keeps responsibility with the registered person.

Example 3 — multiple GSTINs

A company has five GSTINs but authorises the GSTP for only three. The GSTP should not treat the authorisation as covering the remaining registrations.

Example 4 — GSTP engagement ends

The taxpayer terminates the GSTP relationship. The company should update the relevant portal authorisation/access controls and preserve the completed return records.

Example 5 — annual return

A GSTP is authorised to prepare the annual return. Management reviews reconciliation and confirms the figures before filing.

Example 6 — final return

A cancelled GSTIN authorises a GSTP to file GSTR-10. The GSTP prepares the final return, but the taxpayer remains responsible for the closing stock/capital-goods information and correctness.

Example 7 — refund application

A GSTP performs a permitted refund-related function on behalf of the taxpayer. The underlying refund claim remains the taxpayer's claim and must be supported by appropriate records.

Example 8 — construction business

A GSTP receives site-level sales data from the company's finance team. The company's tax manager reviews RA bills, RCM and ITC before authorising filing.

Example 9 — incorrect taxpayer data

The taxpayer provides an incorrect invoice register. The GSTP files it after the prescribed confirmation. The company remains responsible for correcting the statutory reporting and addressing resulting liability.

Example 10 — GSTP disqualification

A GSTP receives a disqualification order. The practitioner reviews the grounds and uses the prescribed appeal mechanism if legally appropriate.

22. GST Council background

The GST Practitioner framework was part of the original GST return-compliance architecture. The policy objective was to allow taxpayers to use trained and approved intermediaries for routine GST compliance while retaining taxpayer accountability.

GST Council and Law Committee work around the original Model GST Law included the structure for authorised intermediaries and GST return filing. Subsequent amendments refined the functions that GSTPs could perform.

The current operative framework should always be read from Section 48 together with Rule 83 because Section 48 deliberately leaves eligibility, duties, functions and removal details to the Rules. 26. Common mistakes

  • Assuming every accountant is automatically a GST Practitioner.
  • Assuming GSTP approval automatically authorises filing for every taxpayer.
  • Giving a GSTP unlimited authority without reviewing the permitted functions.
  • Failing to obtain taxpayer confirmation before filing.
  • Assuming GSTP filing transfers statutory responsibility away from the registered person.
  • Not reviewing GSTR-1/GSTR-2B/GSTR-3B reconciliation before authorising filing.
  • Leaving former GSTP access active after termination.
  • Relying on an old eligibility list instead of current Rule 83.
  • Not retaining working papers and filing acknowledgements.
  • Confusing GSTP status with a separate professional qualification.

27. Section 48 GST FAQs

What is Section 48 of the CGST Act?

Section 48 deals with Goods and Services Tax Practitioners and establishes the framework for their approval, authorisation, functions and responsibility.

What is a GST Practitioner?

A GST Practitioner is an approved/enrolled person who can perform prescribed GST functions for a registered person after the required authorisation.

Who can become a GST Practitioner?

Eligibility is prescribed in Rule 83 and related provisions. The current Rule 83 should be checked for the applicable professional, educational and examination requirements.

Can a CA become a GST Practitioner?

A CA may qualify for GSTP enrolment if the current statutory and Rules-based eligibility conditions are satisfied. Professional qualification alone should not be treated as a substitute for GSTP enrolment.

Can an accountant become a GST Practitioner?

An accountant can become a GSTP only if the person satisfies the prescribed eligibility and enrolment requirements.

Can a GST Practitioner file GSTR-3B?

An approved and authorised GSTP can perform functions prescribed under the Rules, including applicable return-filing functions, subject to the current Rules and taxpayer authorisation.

Can a GST Practitioner file GSTR-1?

Yes, where the GSTP is approved, authorised and performing the function permitted by the Rules.

Can a GST Practitioner file GSTR-9?

Section 48(2) expressly refers to returns under Section 44, subject to the prescribed manner and taxpayer authorisation.

Can a GST Practitioner file GSTR-10?

Section 48(2) expressly refers to returns under Section 45, subject to the prescribed Rules and authorisation.

Who is responsible if a GST Practitioner makes a mistake?

Section 48(3) expressly states that responsibility for correctness of particulars furnished by the GSTP continues to rest with the registered person.

Is GSTP authorisation permanent?

Authorisation should be treated according to the current GST Portal/Rules framework. Businesses should review and revoke or modify authorisations when the professional relationship changes.

What is PCT-05?

GST PCT-05 is the prescribed authorisation mechanism through which a registered person authorises a GST Practitioner for permitted GST functions.

Can GSTP enrolment be cancelled?

Yes. The Rules contain removal/disqualification provisions and a prescribed process for specified misconduct or non-compliance.

Is a GST Practitioner the same as a CA?

No. GST Practitioner is a specific GST statutory status; CA is a professional qualification/status governed by separate legislation.

29. Key takeaways

  • Section 48 is the statutory framework for Goods and Services Tax Practitioners.
  • Eligibility, duties, functions and removal are largely prescribed through Rule 83.
  • An approved GSTP still requires taxpayer authorisation to perform functions for a registered person.
  • Section 48(2) covers outward-supply details and returns under Sections 39, 44 and 45, along with other prescribed functions.
  • The Section 38 inward-supply reference was omitted from Section 48(2) with effect from 1 October 2022.
  • Section 48(3) keeps responsibility for correctness with the registered person.
  • Businesses should use maker-checker controls even when a GSTP handles filing.
  • GSTP status should not be confused with CA or accountant status.
