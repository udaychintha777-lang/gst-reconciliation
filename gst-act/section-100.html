1. Why Section 100 matters

Section 98 deals with the original AAR ruling. Section 100 provides the route for challenging that ruling before the Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling.

An advance ruling can influence the GST treatment of a transaction, classification, exemption, valuation, tax liability, registration or input tax credit issue. If an eligible person disagrees with the ruling, Section 100 provides the statutory appeal mechanism.

Simple memory rule: Section 98 = AAR ruling. Section 99 = Appellate Authority. Section 100 = Appeal. Section 101 = Appellate order.

3. Section 100 in simple language

Imagine a business applies for an advance ruling and the AAR gives a ruling that the business believes is incorrect.

The business does not have to simply accept the AAR decision. If it is an applicant covered by Section 100 and is aggrieved by the ruling, it may appeal to the Appellate Authority.

The same appeal mechanism is also available to the concerned officer and jurisdictional officer where they are aggrieved by the advance ruling.

AAR ruling → Aggrieved party → Check 30 days → File prescribed appeal → AAAR hearing/order

4. Complete statutory structure

ProvisionWhat it does
Section 98Provides the AAR procedure and original advance ruling.
Section 99Identifies the Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling.
Section 100(1)Identifies who may appeal against an advance ruling.
Section 100(2)Provides the 30-day appeal period and a further period of up to 30 days where sufficient cause is accepted.
Section 100(3)Requires the appeal to be in the prescribed form, with prescribed fee and verification.
Section 101Deals with the order of the Appellate Authority.
Section 102Provides for rectification of an advance ruling in specified circumstances.

Section 100 is therefore the central provision governing the filing of an appeal against an advance ruling.

5. Who can appeal?

Section 100(1) identifies three categories of persons who may appeal against an advance ruling, subject to the statutory requirement that they are aggrieved:

Applicant
The person who sought the advance ruling and is adversely affected by the ruling.
Concerned officer
The officer concerned with the advance-ruling matter may appeal if aggrieved.
Jurisdictional officer
The jurisdictional officer may also appeal where the statutory conditions are satisfied.
Key point
The appeal right is not limited only to the taxpayer/applicant.

This is important because the department may also disagree with an AAR ruling and may use the appellate route provided by Section 100.

6. Applicant's appeal

Suppose a company asks the AAR whether a particular service is eligible for a concessional GST rate. The AAR concludes that the concession is not available.

If the applicant is aggrieved by the ruling, it can consider an appeal under Section 100.

Before filing, the applicant should ask:

✓ What exact question was asked?
✓ What exact answer did AAR give?
✓ Which findings are disputed?
✓ What facts did AAR rely upon?
✓ Is the actual transaction identical to those facts?
✓ What statutory provision supports the appeal?
✓ When was the ruling communicated?
✓ Has the limitation period started?

7. Officer's appeal

Section 100 is not only a taxpayer appeal provision.

The concerned officer or jurisdictional officer may also appeal where they are aggrieved by an advance ruling.

This is particularly relevant where the AAR's conclusion differs from the department's interpretation of classification, taxability, exemption, valuation or ITC.

Practical lesson: When an AAR ruling is favourable to the applicant, the finance team should still preserve the complete ruling and monitor whether an eligible departmental appeal is filed.

8. 30-day limitation period

Section 100(2) provides that every appeal under Section 100 is to be filed within 30 days from the date on which the ruling sought to be appealed against is communicated to the concerned officer, jurisdictional officer and the applicant.

The communication date is therefore extremely important for limitation tracking.

Step 1 — Ruling: AAR pronounces the advance ruling.
Step 2 — Communication: Ruling is communicated to the applicant, concerned officer and jurisdictional officer.
Step 3 — Limitation: The statutory 30-day appeal period is tracked from the communication framework specified in Section 100(2).
Step 4 — Filing: Appeal is filed in the prescribed form with required fee and verification.
Do not calculate the deadline casually. Preserve the actual communication/service evidence and obtain professional advice where the last date is disputed or unclear.

9. Further 30 days — sufficient cause

Section 100 contains a proviso allowing the Appellate Authority to permit an appeal to be presented within a further period not exceeding 30 days where it is satisfied that the appellant was prevented by sufficient cause from filing within the original 30-day period.

This should not be understood as an automatic 60-day appeal period.

Normal period
30 days.
Additional period
Up to a further 30 days, only where the statutory condition regarding sufficient cause is satisfied.
Important: A taxpayer should not intentionally delay filing merely because a further period may be available. The safest approach is to file within the original limitation period wherever possible.

10. ARA-02 and ARA-03

Rule 106 of the CGST Rules prescribes the forms and manner for appeals to the Appellate Authority for Advance Ruling.

FormWho uses it?Purpose
FORM GST ARA-02ApplicantAppeal against the advance ruling.
FORM GST ARA-03Concerned officer / jurisdictional officerAppeal against the advance ruling by the officer.

The prescribed appeal form, verification, supporting documents and filing method should be checked against the current Rules and GST portal process before filing.

Easy memory: ARA-02 = Applicant appeal and ARA-03 = Officer appeal.

11. Appeal fee

Rule 106 prescribes the fee for an applicant's appeal and the corresponding filing mechanism.

AppellantFormFee
ApplicantARA-02₹10,000 as prescribed under Rule 106(1).

The fee should be deposited in the manner specified under the Rules. Always verify the current filing workflow on the GST portal before submission.

13. How to prepare grounds of appeal

A strong appeal should not merely say that the AAR ruling is wrong. It should identify the exact finding being challenged and explain why.

A practical structure

PartWhat to include
IssueIdentify the exact question decided by AAR.
FindingSummarise the AAR's conclusion.
FactsExplain the actual transaction and supporting documents.
Legal positionIdentify the relevant Act, Rules, notification and other legal material.
Why AAR is disputedExplain the factual or legal error issue-wise.
Relief soughtState the precise conclusion requested from AAAR.
Professional tip: Make every ground traceable to a specific paragraph/finding in the AAR order. This makes the appeal easier to review internally and during the hearing.

15. Practical GST examples

Example 1 — GST classification

A manufacturer asks AAR to classify a specialised product. AAR adopts a classification that results in a higher GST rate. The applicant may appeal under Section 100 if aggrieved.

Example 2 — Exemption notification

A contractor believes a service qualifies for a GST exemption. AAR rejects the claim. The applicant reviews the ruling and files ARA-02 within the applicable limitation period.

Example 3 — Input tax credit

A business seeks a ruling on ITC for a specific inward supply. AAR concludes that ITC is unavailable. The applicant can challenge the ruling through the prescribed appellate mechanism.

Example 4 — Valuation

A company asks AAR about the value of a supply involving multiple contractual components. If the ruling adversely affects the company's tax liability, the company can evaluate an appeal.

Example 5 — Departmental appeal

AAR gives a ruling favourable to the taxpayer, but the jurisdictional officer believes the ruling is incorrect. The officer may examine the Section 100 appeal route.

16. Construction and infrastructure examples

Government works contract

A contractor receives an AAR ruling on the GST rate applicable to a government works contract. The finance team should preserve the tender, agreement, BOQ, scope of work and tax treatment before deciding whether to appeal.

HAM road project

An infrastructure company receives an adverse ruling involving a hybrid annuity arrangement. The appeal should address the exact concession agreement, payment structure, milestones and consideration mechanism.

Mining contract

A mining contractor disputes the classification of its service. The appeal should clearly explain the actual activity, contract obligations and why the AAR's classification should be reconsidered.

Subcontractor

A subcontractor receives an adverse AAR ruling on taxability or rate. Its appeal file should include the main contract link, subcontract/work order, invoices and actual nature of work.

17. Detailed practical cases

Case 1 — Filed within 30 days

AAR ruling is communicated and the applicant completes ARA-02 within the original limitation period. This is the safest filing approach.

Case 2 — Delay due to genuine circumstances

An applicant cannot file within the original 30 days and seeks admission during the further statutory period. The applicant must establish sufficient cause to the satisfaction of the Appellate Authority.

Case 3 — Applicant waits beyond the further period

An applicant assumes that every delayed appeal can be accepted. That is unsafe. The proviso permits only a further period not exceeding 30 days subject to sufficient cause.

Case 4 — Favourable AAR ruling

The applicant should still preserve the order and monitor whether an eligible officer files an appeal.

Case 5 — Wrong form assumption

An applicant should not use the officer's appeal form. Rule 106 distinguishes ARA-02 for an applicant from ARA-03 for the concerned/jurisdictional officer.

Case 6 — Appeal grounds too general

An appeal that simply states “AAR ruling is wrong” is weaker than an issue-wise challenge supported by facts and law.

Case 7 — Changed facts

If the commercial arrangement changes after the AAR ruling, the company should separately analyse whether the ruling continues to address the changed facts.

Case 8 — Internal control

Finance maintains an AAR/AAAR register with order date, communication date, limitation date, appeal form, filing date and outcome.

18. What happens after filing?

Section 100 is primarily the filing provision. The next major provision is Section 101, which governs the order of the Appellate Authority.

StageProvision / action
AAR rulingSection 98
Appellate Authority identifiedSection 99
Appeal filedSection 100
Hearing and appellate orderSection 101
Rectification, where applicableSection 102

Under Section 101, the Appellate Authority may, after giving the parties an opportunity of being heard, pass an order confirming or modifying the ruling appealed against or referred to.

19. Common mistakes

  • Assuming Section 100 is a general GST appeal provision for all GST orders.
  • Confusing AAR appeal with the ordinary Section 107 departmental appeal route.
  • Missing the original 30-day period.
  • Assuming the further 30 days is an automatic extension.
  • Failing to preserve the actual communication date/evidence.
  • Using the wrong prescribed form.
  • Ignoring the applicable appeal fee for an applicant.
  • Preparing generic grounds without challenging specific AAR findings.
  • Failing to attach relevant factual and contractual evidence.
  • Waiting until the limitation deadline before starting legal review.
  • Assuming a favourable AAR ruling can never be challenged by the department.

20. Section 100 vs Section 107

These sections are frequently confused because both deal with GST appeals, but they apply to different situations.

PointSection 100Section 107
Main subjectAppeal against an advance ruling.Appeal against a decision/order of an adjudicating authority.
Appellate forumAppellate Authority for Advance Ruling.Appellate Authority under the general GST appeal framework.
Normal taxpayer limitation30 days, subject to further period allowed under Section 100(2).Generally 3 months under Section 107(1).
FormsARA-02 / ARA-03 under Rule 106.Different forms under the general appeal rules.
Important: First identify whether the order is an advance ruling or an ordinary adjudication order. Choosing the wrong appellate provision can create serious limitation and maintainability issues.

22. Section 100 GST FAQs

What is Section 100 of the CGST Act?

Section 100 provides the appeal mechanism against an advance ruling pronounced by the Authority for Advance Ruling.

Who can appeal under Section 100?

The applicant, concerned officer or jurisdictional officer may appeal where the person is aggrieved by the advance ruling.

What is the normal time limit?

The appeal is generally required to be filed within 30 days from the date specified in Section 100(2) for communication of the ruling.

Can the 30-day period be extended?

The Appellate Authority may allow a further period not exceeding 30 days where it is satisfied that sufficient cause prevented filing within the original period.

Is the extra 30 days automatic?

No. It is subject to sufficient cause and the statutory discretion of the Appellate Authority.

Which form does an applicant use?

Rule 106 prescribes FORM GST ARA-02 for an applicant's appeal.

Which form does an officer use?

Rule 106 prescribes FORM GST ARA-03 for an appeal by the concerned or jurisdictional officer.

What is the applicant's appeal fee?

Rule 106(1) prescribes a fee of ₹10,000 for an applicant's appeal.

Does an officer pay appeal fee?

Rule 106(2) provides that no fee is payable by the concerned or jurisdictional officer for filing the appeal.

What happens after the appeal?

Section 101 governs the Appellate Authority's order after providing the parties an opportunity of being heard.

Is Section 100 applicable to ordinary GST demand orders?

No. Section 100 specifically concerns appeals against advance rulings. Ordinary adjudication appeals are governed by the applicable general GST appeal provisions.

What should a company do after receiving an adverse AAR ruling?

Immediately preserve the complete ruling and communication evidence, calculate the limitation period, review the disputed findings, prepare the grounds and complete the prescribed appeal process within time.

23. Key takeaways

  • Section 100 provides the appeal mechanism against an advance ruling.
  • The applicant, concerned officer and jurisdictional officer may appeal when aggrieved.
  • The normal appeal period is 30 days under Section 100(2).
  • A further period not exceeding 30 days may be allowed where sufficient cause is established.
  • The additional 30 days is not an automatic extension.
  • Applicant appeals use FORM GST ARA-02; officer appeals use FORM GST ARA-03 under Rule 106.
  • The applicant appeal fee prescribed under Rule 106 is ₹10,000; no fee is payable by the concerned/jurisdictional officer.
  • Section 100 is different from the general GST appeal provision under Section 107.
  • After Section 100, Section 101 governs the appellate order.
  • Finance teams should maintain a clear AAR/AAAR limitation tracker.
