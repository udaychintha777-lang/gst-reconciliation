Why Rule 4 matters to a business

Rule 4 of the CGST Rules, 2017 answers a very practical question: from which date does the composition levy actually become effective?

This date matters. It controls the point from which the taxpayer must align invoicing, tax collection, accounting, GST returns and the treatment of supplies with composition status.

A common mistake is to treat the date on which a taxpayer submits a form as automatically being the date from which composition applies. Rule 4 shows that the answer depends on which composition-intimation route was used.

For management and finance teams, Rule 4 is therefore a cut-off rule. Once the effective date is established, transactions immediately before and after that date should be reviewed separately.

Rule 4 — statutory text

4. Effective date for composition levy.—

(1) The option to pay tax under section 10 shall be effective from the beginning of the financial year, where the intimation is filed under sub-rule (3) of rule 3, and from the appointed day where the intimation is filed under sub-rule (1) of rule 3.

(2) The intimation under sub-rule (2) of rule 3 shall be considered only after the grant of registration to the applicant and the option to pay tax under section 10 shall be effective from the date fixed under the applicable registration provisions.

Core principle: Rule 4 links the effective date to the route through which the taxpayer opted for composition. Always identify the applicable Rule 3 sub-rule first; only then determine the effective date under Rule 4.

Rule 4 in simple language

Rule 4 can be reduced to one practical question:

Sub-rule 1 — beginning of financial year vs appointed day

Rule 4(1) contains two different dates because Rule 3 contains different composition routes.

Case A — Rule 3(3): existing registered taxpayer

If an existing registered person opts for composition under Rule 3(3), the option becomes effective from the beginning of the financial year for which the option is exercised.

For a normal financial year in India, this means the transition point is ordinarily 1 April. The taxpayer should therefore complete the pre-year-end checks before the financial year starts rather than treating composition as something that can be switched on after a few months of the year.

Business example: A regular GST taxpayer wants composition for FY 2027-28. The finance team should plan the transition before 1 April 2027, complete the applicable intimation and ITC-related work, and ensure the billing system is ready for the new status from the applicable effective date.

Case B — Rule 3(1): provisional-registration route

For the specified provisional-registration situation covered by Rule 3(1), Rule 4 links the effective date to the appointed day. This is a historical/transitional route associated with the commencement of GST.

This distinction is important when analysing old GST records or historical assessments. A historical dispute should be examined using the law and dates applicable at that time, rather than automatically applying the current-day registration workflow.

Sub-rule 2 — new registration and composition

Where the composition option is given through the registration application under Rule 3(2), Rule 4(2) states that the option is considered only after registration is granted.

The effective date then follows the date fixed under the applicable registration provisions. This creates an important distinction between:

  • the date on which the applicant submits the registration application;
  • the date on which registration is actually granted/effective; and
  • the date from which the composition treatment can legally operate.
Do not back-date the accounting merely because the registration application was submitted earlier. The finance team should establish the actual registration-effective date and then apply the composition treatment from the legally applicable date.

Real business examples

Example 1 — Existing trader wants composition from 1 April

A registered trader operating under regular GST decides in February that composition will be commercially better from the next financial year. The correct approach is to plan the transition before the new financial year, file the prescribed intimation within the applicable time and prepare the ITC transition working. The business should not wait until June and assume composition can simply be switched on from that month under Rule 3(3).

Example 2 — Registration application filed in March

A new business applies for GST registration and selects the composition option through the registration application. The accounts team should not treat the application-submission date as the composition-effective date. Rule 4(2) connects the option to the grant of registration and the applicable registration-effective date.

Example 3 — Invoice issued near the cut-off

A taxpayer's composition effective date is 1 April. An invoice relates to a supply made on 31 March but is issued later. The team should examine the applicable time-of-supply and invoicing provisions rather than simply applying the tax status based on the invoice-printing date. The effective date must be analysed together with the substantive GST provisions governing the transaction.

Example 4 — ERP not changed on time

A business becomes a composition taxpayer from 1 April, but the ERP continues generating normal GST tax invoices for April. Even if the composition intimation was correctly filed, the billing-system failure can create a serious reconciliation problem. A controlled cut-over checklist should be completed before the effective date.

Example 5 — Historical GST assessment

A department examines the taxpayer's composition status for FY 2017-18. The finance team should not use today's workflow as the only evidence. It should establish the Rule 3 route, the Rule 4 effective date and the notifications/amendments applicable during the historical period.

Rule 4 and the “no mid-year switch” principle

For an existing registered person opting under Rule 3(3), Rule 4 makes the composition option effective from the beginning of the financial year. This is why the composition decision should normally be taken before the relevant financial year begins.

Management implication: If a business is considering composition during the middle of the year, do not assume that filing CMP-02 immediately will convert the taxpayer from that date. First identify the legally available route and effective date.

This is particularly important for businesses preparing monthly MIS, GST reconciliations and tax forecasts. A mid-year assumption can distort both GST reporting and management accounts.

Effective-date checklist for finance teams

  1. Identify the exact Rule 3 sub-rule under which composition was chosen.
  2. Identify the applicable Rule 4 effective-date rule.
  3. Confirm the registration-effective date where Rule 3(2) applies.
  4. For an existing taxpayer, check the beginning of the relevant financial year.
  5. Document the effective date in the GST compliance file.
  6. Review sales invoices around the transition date.
  7. Review input-tax-credit balances and transition requirements.
  8. Update ERP tax codes and invoice formats.
  9. Reconcile GST returns around the cut-off.
  10. Retain the acknowledgement and supporting working papers.

Common mistakes under Rule 4

  • Confusing filing date with effective date. They are not automatically the same.
  • Ignoring Rule 3. Rule 4 cannot be applied correctly until the composition route is identified.
  • Assuming an existing taxpayer can switch in the middle of a financial year. The Rule 3(3) route is tied to the beginning of the financial year.
  • Using the application date as the registration-effective date. For new applicants, Rule 4(2) links effectiveness to the applicable registration date.
  • Changing the ERP after the effective date. System readiness should be completed before the cut-off.
  • Ignoring transactions around the cut-off. Sales and purchases near the transition need careful review.
  • Applying current law to a historical period. Historical assessments require period-specific verification.

Audit and GST notice relevance

Rule 4 can be decisive when a department questions whether composition treatment was correctly applied from a particular date.

A strong audit file should demonstrate:

  • the taxpayer's composition option and applicable Rule 3 route;
  • the date on which the intimation was filed;
  • the registration-effective date, where relevant;
  • the Rule 4 provision determining the effective date;
  • the first invoice/bill issued under composition;
  • the last relevant transaction under the previous GST status;
  • ITC transition workings; and
  • GST returns reconciled to the transition date.
Notice-response strategy: If a notice disputes the composition effective date, prepare a chronological “date bridge” showing the application/intimation date → registration date where applicable → Rule 4 provision → legal effective date → first composition transaction. This is usually clearer than giving a general explanation of the composition scheme.

Frequently asked questions about CGST Rule 4

What is Rule 4 of the CGST Rules?

Rule 4 determines the effective date from which the option to pay tax under the composition levy becomes effective. The date depends on the route through which the taxpayer opted for composition.

When does composition become effective for an existing registered taxpayer?

Where the option is exercised under Rule 3(3), Rule 4 provides that the composition option is effective from the beginning of the financial year for which the option is exercised.

When does composition become effective for a new registration applicant?

Where the option is given through the registration application under Rule 3(2), Rule 4 provides that the option is considered after registration is granted and becomes effective from the applicable registration-effective date.

Is the composition effective date always the date of filing CMP-02?

No. For an existing registered person using Rule 3(3), the effective date is tied to the beginning of the financial year, not simply the date on which CMP-02 is filed.

Why is Rule 4 important for GST invoices?

The effective date creates an important cut-off for tax treatment. Billing and accounting systems must be aligned with the taxpayer's actual GST status from the applicable date.

Can composition normally be started from the middle of a financial year?

For an existing registered taxpayer opting through Rule 3(3), Rule 4 ties the option to the beginning of the financial year. A business considering a mid-year change should examine whether another legally applicable route exists rather than assuming a mid-year switch is available.

What should be checked for a historical GST dispute?

Check the Rule 3 route, Rule 4 wording, relevant registration date, applicable notifications/amendments and the transaction facts for the specific historical period.

Practical management decision framework

Before approving composition, management should ask:

  1. Are we legally eligible under Section 10 and the applicable provisions?
  2. Which Rule 3 route applies?
  3. What is the Rule 4 effective date?
  4. What happens to ITC at the transition?
  5. Are our customers affected commercially?
  6. Are all GSTINs under the PAN considered?
  7. Is the ERP ready from the exact cut-off?
  8. Have sales, purchase and stock teams been informed?

Practical takeaway

Remember Rule 4 like this: Rule 3 tells you how composition is chosen; Rule 4 tells you when it starts. Never decide the accounting or invoicing cut-off merely from the date a form was filed.

For business decisions, establish the effective date first, document it, and then build the accounting, ERP, invoice and GST-return cut-off around that date. This simple control can prevent significant reconciliation and departmental-notice problems.
