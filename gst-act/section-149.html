1. Statutory Text of Section 149

149. Goods and services tax compliance rating.—

(1) Every registered person may be assigned a goods and services tax compliance rating score by the Government based on his record of compliance with the provisions of this Act.

(2) The goods and services tax compliance rating score may be determined on the basis of such parameters as may be prescribed.

(3) The goods and services tax compliance rating score may be updated at periodic intervals and intimated to the registered person and also placed in the public domain in such manner as may be prescribed.
Current statutory point: The current consolidated CGST Act continues to contain Section 149 with these three subsections. The section creates the framework but leaves the detailed parameters and manner of updating/public disclosure to be prescribed.

2. Section 149 Explained in Simple Language

Section 149 is one of the GST Act's provisions intended to connect a taxpayer's compliance record with a formal compliance-rating concept.

In principle, the Government can assign a GST compliance rating score to a registered person based on that person's compliance record. The statute then contemplates prescribed parameters, periodic updating, intimation to the taxpayer and public-domain disclosure.

Important distinction: Section 149 is the legal framework. It does not itself say that the rating is a particular number such as 10/10, does not specify a fixed grading scale, and does not list every factor that must be used. Those details would need to come from the prescribed framework.

Why was a compliance rating contemplated?

  • To create a structured way of representing a registered person's compliance record.
  • To potentially provide taxpayers with information about their compliance standing.
  • To support transparency within the GST ecosystem.
  • To allow compliance information to be updated periodically rather than being treated as a one-time assessment.

3. Section 149(1) – Who May Be Rated?

The section begins with the words “Every registered person may be assigned” a GST compliance rating score by the Government.

This means the provision is not restricted to a particular industry or business size on its face. The statutory framework contemplates registered persons generally.

PointExplanation
Registered personThe rating concept is linked to GST registration.
EveryThe provision is drafted broadly rather than limiting the concept to large businesses.
May be assignedThe wording creates an enabling framework; it is not itself a declaration that every registered person necessarily has a currently displayed rating.
BasisThe person's record of compliance with the Act.
Practical caution: Do not state that every GSTIN currently has an official public compliance score merely because Section 149 exists.

4. Section 149(2) – How Is the Rating Determined?

Section 149(2) says the score may be determined on the basis of such parameters as may be prescribed.

This is deliberately open-ended at the Act level. The Act itself does not provide a numerical formula such as “one point for each return” or a fixed 0-to-10 scale.

Why does this matter?

Compliance is multidimensional. A rating system, if prescribed, could require consideration of different aspects of GST compliance. But the exact legally operative parameters must be identified from the applicable prescribed framework rather than assumed from articles, software websites or informal explanations.

For website content: avoid publishing an unsupported “official GST score formula.” If a third-party source describes a 0–10 score or a particular weighting, clearly identify it as a non-statutory description unless an official prescribed source establishes it.

5. Section 149(3) – Updating and Public Disclosure

The third subsection contemplates three related actions:

  1. Periodic updating: the compliance rating score may be updated at periodic intervals.
  2. Intimation: the registered person may be informed of the score.
  3. Public domain: the score may also be placed in the public domain in the prescribed manner.
ConceptSimple meaning
Periodic updateThe rating is designed as a changing compliance indicator rather than a permanent one-time label.
IntimationThe taxpayer is contemplated as being informed of its rating.
Public domainThe prescribed framework can provide for public access to the rating.
Do not confuse the statutory possibility with current portal visibility. Section 149 says what the framework may provide. Actual portal functionality and public availability depend on implementation and prescribed procedures.

6. Practical Status of GST Compliance Rating

The GSTN portal has historically included compliance-rating information in its taxpayer-search functionality. In a 2021 GST portal functionality compilation, GSTN stated that additional information available in Search Taxpayer included “Compliance Rating” along with other taxpayer information.

However, the existence of a portal functionality or a statutory provision should not be converted into a claim that every taxpayer currently has a publicly visible, continuously updated numerical score. The exact availability, display and operational scope should be verified against the current GST portal and official instructions applicable at the time of use.

Website-writing rule: For 2026 content, describe Section 149 as a statutory compliance-rating framework. Do not promise users that they can definitely see a live official score unless the current portal actually provides it to them.

7. GST Portal and Compliance Rating

The GST portal is the principal electronic environment through which taxpayers access many GST services. Section 149 contemplates intimation and public-domain placement of the rating in the prescribed manner, so an electronic portal is naturally relevant to implementation.

But Section 149 itself does not specify a particular portal screen, menu, API or dashboard. Portal functionality can change independently of the wording of the Act.

Example:

A taxpayer sees “Compliance Rating” as a field in a taxpayer-search system. That does not by itself establish what numerical methodology was used. To understand the legal basis, the taxpayer should distinguish the portal display from the statutory wording and any prescribed parameters.

8. What Could a Compliance Record Include?

The statutory phrase is broad: “record of compliance with the provisions of this Act.” It therefore points to compliance with GST law generally rather than one isolated filing.

Possible compliance records can include areas such as:

  • Whether required GST returns/statements are furnished.
  • Whether tax obligations are discharged as required.
  • Whether registration particulars are maintained correctly.
  • Whether statutory records are maintained.
  • Whether required information is furnished to the department.
  • Whether prescribed procedures are followed.
These are compliance areas, not a statutory scoring formula. Section 149(2) says the actual rating is based on parameters as may be prescribed. Do not convert general compliance obligations into an invented numerical weighting.

9. What Section 149 Does Not Say

It does not prescribe a fixed score range

The section does not say that the official rating must be 0–10, 0–100 or any other particular range.

It does not state a fixed formula

The parameters are left to the prescribed framework.

It does not itself create a penalty

A compliance rating is conceptually different from a penalty imposed for a statutory contravention.

It does not automatically deny ITC

Input tax credit eligibility is governed by the relevant ITC provisions and rules. A rating should not be described as an independent ITC-denial mechanism unless another applicable legal provision says so.

It does not automatically increase audit frequency

Do not state that a particular rating necessarily causes an audit unless an official rule or instruction establishes such a consequence.

It does not mean a supplier with a lower rating is automatically fraudulent

A compliance indicator, if operational, should not be confused with a finding of fraud or criminal liability.

10. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – Timely compliance record

A registered company consistently completes its GST obligations. Its compliance history could form part of the record considered under any prescribed rating framework.

Example 2 – Late returns

A taxpayer repeatedly files required returns late. If the prescribed rating parameters include filing timeliness, that record could affect the score.

Example 3 – Tax payment compliance

A registered person has repeated unpaid statutory dues. If payment compliance is included in the prescribed parameters, the record could be relevant.

Example 4 – Correct registration particulars

A company keeps its GST registration details updated when changes occur. This is part of its broader compliance record, although the exact scoring treatment depends on prescribed parameters.

Example 5 – Multiple GSTINs

A company has GST registrations in several States. Compliance information may need to be analysed registration-wise because each GSTIN is a separate registration record.

Example 6 – Supplier due diligence

A purchaser checks publicly available GST information about a supplier. A compliance rating, if currently made publicly available in the prescribed manner, could be one information source. It should not be treated as the only due-diligence evidence.

Example 7 – Rating dispute

A taxpayer believes the displayed compliance information is inaccurate. The taxpayer should first identify the source of the underlying data and use the correction/grievance mechanism applicable at that time.

Example 8 – Portal display

A compliance-rating field appears on a taxpayer-search screen. The presence of a field does not by itself explain the underlying scoring parameters.

Example 9 – Historical rating information

A business finds an old article describing a 0–10 GST score. It should not present that scale as the current statutory formula without an official source establishing it.

Example 10 – Compliance vs penalty

A taxpayer receives a penalty under another section. The penalty and any compliance rating are separate legal concepts; one should not automatically be described as the other.

Example 11 – ITC decision

A purchaser uses GST registration and filing information as part of vendor due diligence. It should still independently verify invoices, supply receipt, tax payment conditions and other ITC requirements.

Example 12 – Management dashboard

A company creates an internal GST compliance dashboard tracking return filing, tax payment, reconciliations and notices. This is an internal control tool, not an official Section 149 rating unless expressly based on the prescribed system.

12. Supplier Due Diligence and Compliance Rating

The statutory provision's public-domain concept can be relevant to commercial due diligence if and when a rating is made publicly available in the prescribed manner.

However, a purchaser should not rely on a single compliance indicator to decide whether a supplier is genuine or whether ITC is available.

A practical vendor GST check

  1. Verify GSTIN status.
  2. Check legal name and registration details.
  3. Review invoice details.
  4. Confirm goods/services were actually received.
  5. Reconcile relevant ITC records.
  6. Review payment evidence.
  7. Check unusual changes in registration or banking information.
  8. Review available portal information.
  9. If an official compliance rating is available, treat it as one due-diligence input rather than conclusive proof of every transaction.
Important: Section 149 is not a substitute for the recipient's statutory ITC conditions.

13. Important Limitations

A compliance score, if assigned, is an administrative indicator. It should be interpreted carefully.

  • A score cannot by itself establish whether a particular invoice is genuine.
  • A score cannot by itself establish whether a supply was actually received.
  • A score cannot by itself establish that tax was or was not paid on a particular invoice.
  • A score cannot by itself determine ITC eligibility.
  • A score should not be treated as a substitute for legal analysis of a specific transaction.
  • Historical portal descriptions may not reflect the current implementation.
For management: Use an internal compliance dashboard with transaction-level evidence. Do not rely solely on a single external rating indicator.

15. Common Misunderstandings

“GST compliance rating is officially 0 to 10.”

Do not present that as the statutory formula. Section 149 itself does not specify a numerical scale.

“Every GSTIN currently has a public rating.”

The Act contemplates public disclosure in the prescribed manner, but current availability and implementation must be verified from the official system.

“A low rating proves the supplier is fake.”

No. A compliance indicator is not the same as a finding of fraud or invalid registration.

“A high rating guarantees ITC.”

No. ITC depends on the statutory conditions applicable to the recipient and supply.

“Compliance rating replaces GST reconciliation.”

No. Invoice-level reconciliation remains important for accounting and ITC controls.

“The rating is the same as a GST certificate.”

Section 149 creates a rating concept. It does not itself prescribe a certificate.

“The rating can never change.”

Section 149(3) expressly contemplates periodic updating.

17. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 149 of the CGST Act?

Section 149 provides for a GST compliance rating score that may be assigned to registered persons based on their compliance record, using prescribed parameters.

2. Who can receive a GST compliance rating?

The section says every registered person may be assigned a GST compliance rating score by the Government.

3. What is the rating based on?

The statutory basis is the registered person's record of compliance with the CGST Act, with the detailed parameters to be prescribed.

4. Does Section 149 specify a 0–10 score?

No. The section itself does not prescribe a numerical range or scoring formula.

5. Can the rating be updated?

Yes. Section 149(3) says the score may be updated at periodic intervals.

6. Will the taxpayer be informed?

The section contemplates that the score may be intimated to the registered person in the prescribed manner.

7. Can the rating be made public?

Yes. Section 149(3) contemplates placement in the public domain in the prescribed manner.

8. Is GST compliance rating currently visible for every taxpayer?

The statute provides the framework, while actual functionality depends on implementation. GSTN has historically included compliance-rating information among taxpayer-search information, but current visibility should be checked on the official portal rather than assumed.

9. Does a compliance rating determine ITC?

No. ITC eligibility is governed by the relevant provisions and conditions of GST law.

10. Does a low rating mean a supplier is fraudulent?

No. A compliance rating, if assigned, is not by itself a finding of fraud.

11. Does a high rating guarantee that all invoices are genuine?

No. Invoice authenticity and actual receipt of supplies must be established based on the transaction and applicable law.

12. Does Section 149 impose a penalty?

No. Section 149 establishes a compliance-rating framework. Penalties are imposed under the applicable penalty provisions when their conditions are satisfied.

13. Is the rating the same as a GST registration status?

No. Registration status indicates whether a GST registration is active/cancelled/etc.; compliance rating is a separate concept.

14. Can a company have multiple ratings?

Because GST registrations are maintained by GSTIN, compliance information can be relevant registration-wise. The exact implementation should be checked in the official system.

15. What should businesses do about Section 149?

Maintain strong GST compliance controls: timely filing, accurate payments, reconciliations, proper records, prompt notice responses and reliable evidence.

16. Is Section 149 still in force?

Yes. Current consolidated CGST Act sources continue to contain Section 149 with subsections (1) to (3).

18. Quick Revision – Section 149

  • Section 149 = GST Compliance Rating.
  • Every registered person may be assigned a compliance rating score.
  • The score is based on the person's record of compliance with the Act.
  • Detailed parameters are to be prescribed.
  • The score may be updated periodically.
  • The registered person may be intimated.
  • The score may be placed in the public domain in the prescribed manner.
  • Section 149 itself does not specify a numerical score range.
  • Section 149 itself does not prescribe a fixed scoring formula.
  • Compliance rating is not the same as penalty, fraud finding or ITC eligibility.
  • Actual portal implementation and current visibility should be verified rather than assumed from historical descriptions.
