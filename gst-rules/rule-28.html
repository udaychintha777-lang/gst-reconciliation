GST RULES • RULE 28

Rule 28 – Value of Supply Between Distinct or Related Persons

A practical and detailed guide to valuation of goods or services supplied between distinct persons and related persons, including the full-ITC proviso, the 90% option and corporate guarantee valuation.

Core provisionSection 15 read with Rule 28
Main valuationOpen Market Value first

1. Rule 28 at a Glance

Rule 28 provides the valuation mechanism when goods or services are supplied between distinct persons or between related persons, other than a supply made through an agent.

The provision is particularly important for companies having multiple GST registrations, head office and branch registrations, common-service arrangements, group companies and other related-party transactions.

Key point: Rule 28 does not mean that every inter-branch transaction must automatically be valued at cost plus 10%. The prescribed hierarchy begins with open market value, followed by like kind and quality and then Rule 30/31. Where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit, the value declared in the invoice is deemed to be the open market value under the second proviso to Rule 28(1).

Before applying Rule 28, first establish that there is a taxable supply. Schedule I can make specified supplies between related or distinct persons taxable even when made without consideration.

3. Rule 28(1) – Valuation Hierarchy

The normal sequence is:

1. Open Market Value → 2. Value of like kind and quality → 3. Rule 30 → 4. Rule 31

Step 1 – Open Market Value

The first benchmark is the open market value of the supply. A reliable comparable external or internal market price can therefore be highly important in defending the declared value.

Step 2 – Like Kind and Quality

If open market value is not available, the value of supply of goods or services of like kind and quality is considered.

Step 3 – Rule 30

If value cannot be determined under the first two methods, Rule 30 provides a cost-based method. Broadly, the taxable value is 110% of the cost of production, manufacture, acquisition or provision of the relevant goods or services, as applicable.

Step 4 – Rule 31

Where the earlier methods cannot determine value, the residual method under Rule 31 is used, subject to the rule's requirements.

4. First Proviso – 90% Option for Further Supply

Where the goods are intended for further supply as such by the recipient, the supplier has an option to value the supply at an amount equivalent to 90% of the price charged for the supply of goods of like kind and quality by the recipient to its customer who is not a related person.

Example: Company A supplies goods to its related distributor Company B. Company B subsequently sells the same goods to an unrelated customer for ₹10,00,000. If the statutory conditions for the 90% option are satisfied, Company A may opt for a value of ₹9,00,000 under this proviso.

This is an option of the supplier; it is not a mandatory 90% valuation in every related-party transaction.

5. Second Proviso – Recipient Eligible for Full ITC

This is one of the most practically important parts of Rule 28. Where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit, the value declared in the invoice is deemed to be the open market value of the goods or services.

Practical consequence: If a branch receiving an inter-branch service is eligible for full ITC, the supplier's invoice value can be treated as the open market value. The valuation dispute is therefore substantially simplified, subject to the recipient actually satisfying the full-ITC condition.

Nil-value illustration

CBIC has clarified that, in specified cases where full ITC is available and the supplier has not issued a tax invoice for a particular service, the value may be deemed to have been declared as Nil, which can operate as the open market value under the second proviso. This should not be confused with a general exemption from invoicing or GST compliance.

For head-office and branch arrangements, the documentation and ITC eligibility of the recipient should therefore be checked before relying on this proviso.

6. Head Office and Branch – Practical Treatment

Different GST registrations of the same legal entity in different States are treated as distinct persons. Services provided by one registration to another can therefore constitute taxable supplies even without a conventional third-party commercial customer.

7. Rule 28(2) – Corporate Guarantee

Rule 28 contains a separate valuation mechanism for a corporate guarantee supplied by a supplier to a related person located in India on behalf of that recipient.

Under Rule 28(2), the value is deemed to be 1% per annum of the amount of the guarantee offered, or the actual consideration, whichever is higher. The proviso provides that where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit, the value declared in the invoice is deemed to be the value of the supply.

Do not mix the two provisions: Rule 28(1) is the general valuation framework for distinct/related-person supplies. Rule 28(2) is a specific deeming provision for qualifying corporate-guarantee services and must be analysed separately.

The current framework also reflects the 2024 amendment clarifying the 1% amount as an annual measure. Recent judicial discussion has also examined the interaction between the corporate-guarantee deeming rule and the full-ITC proviso. citeturn1search5

8. Construction Company Examples

Example 1 – Inter-state project office

A construction company has a Telangana GST registration and a Karnataka GST registration. The Telangana office provides project-management support to the Karnataka registration. If Karnataka is eligible for full ITC, the invoice value can be deemed OMV under the second proviso to Rule 28(1).

Example 2 – Common employee/service cost

Head Office incurs common administrative costs and provides support to branch registrations. The company should identify the nature of the supply, determine the recipient's ITC eligibility and maintain a defensible basis for the invoice value.

Example 3 – Related group company

A parent company provides taxable management services to a related subsidiary. If a comparable market price exists, OMV should be considered first. If not, move through the Rule 28 valuation hierarchy.

Example 4 – Corporate guarantee

A holding company gives a corporate guarantee to a bank for a related Indian subsidiary. The transaction must be tested under Rule 28(2), rather than simply applying the ordinary cost-plus methodology of Rule 30.

10. Accounting & ERP Controls

  • Maintain a master of all GST registrations and identify distinct-person transactions.
  • Flag related-party vendors/customers and inter-branch transactions automatically.
  • Capture the valuation method used for each Rule 28 transaction.
  • Record whether the recipient is eligible for full ITC at the time of valuation.
  • Retain comparable market-price evidence where OMV is used.
  • For the 90% option, retain evidence of the recipient's onward sale to an unrelated customer.
  • Separate corporate-guarantee transactions from ordinary inter-company services.
  • Reconcile Rule 28 invoices with GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and recipient ITC records.
  • Maintain agreements, service descriptions, cost workings and management approvals.

12. FAQs

Is Rule 28 applicable only when there is no consideration?

No. It applies to specified supplies between distinct or related persons regardless of whether consideration is present. Schedule I can separately make certain supplies taxable without consideration.

Can I use invoice value when the recipient has full ITC?

Yes. The second proviso to Rule 28(1) deems the invoice value to be the open market value where the recipient is eligible for full ITC.

Does full ITC mean the recipient must merely be registered?

No. Registration alone is not the test. The relevant condition is eligibility for full input tax credit.

Is Rule 28(2) the same as normal related-party valuation?

No. Corporate guarantees covered by Rule 28(2) have a specific valuation mechanism.

What if there is no comparable market value?

Move through the prescribed hierarchy and consider Rule 30 and Rule 31 in the order required by the Rules.

13. Compliance Checklist

  1. Identify whether supplier and recipient are distinct or related persons.
  2. Confirm that the transaction is a supply under GST.
  3. Determine whether Rule 28(1) or the special corporate-guarantee Rule 28(2) applies.
  4. Check open market value.
  5. If OMV is unavailable, assess like kind and quality.
  6. If required, move to Rule 30 and Rule 31.
  7. Check full-ITC eligibility before using the second proviso.
  8. Check the 90% option where goods are intended for further supply as such.
  9. Document the valuation basis and supporting evidence.
  10. Reconcile tax invoice, return reporting and recipient ITC.
