1. Understanding the Hybrid Annuity Model

HAM is not simply an “annuity receipt” transaction. The GST analysis begins with the concession agreement and the actual contractual supply. In the NHAI HAM structure, the concessionaire undertakes construction and long-term operation and maintenance, while consideration is spread across the construction and deferred-payment periods.

Construction obligation
Design/construction activities create the core infrastructure supply.
Deferred consideration
A portion of consideration is paid later through scheduled instalments/annuity.
O&M obligation
The concessionaire remains contractually responsible for operation and maintenance.
Project finance
Private financing and government payments create accounting entries that must not be confused with GST tax points.
Key legal point: Circular 221/15/2024-GST treats the NHAI HAM arrangement as a single contract for construction and O&M and addresses its treatment as a continuous supply of services for time-of-supply purposes.

3. Why HAM road construction is generally analysed as a works contract

Section 2(119) covers contracts involving building, construction, fabrication, completion, erection, installation, fitting out, improvement, modification, repair, maintenance, renovation, alteration or commissioning of immovable property where transfer of property in goods is involved in execution.

Road construction normally involves civil works and transfer/use of materials in execution of an immovable infrastructure asset. Therefore, a qualifying road construction contract is commonly analysed as a works-contract service.

Important: “Road project” is not itself the legal definition. The contract must be tested against Section 2(119), the actual scope, transfer of goods, immovable-property character and the recipient's contractual rights.

4. GST rate framework for road works

Notification No. 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended, contains the construction-services rate framework under Heading 9954. The road/bridge/tunnel public-use works-contract entry has historically carried 6% CGST plus the corresponding State/UT tax for an intra-State supply, giving an effective 12% rate where the entry and conditions apply.

QuestionWhat to verify
Is it a works contract?Section 2(119).
Is it road/bridge/tunnel construction/repair etc.?Actual BOQ and contract scope.
For general public?Project documents and intended public use.
Which tax period?Rate notification and amendments applicable to that period.
Was there a change-in-law clause?Contract escalation/change-in-law mechanism.
Rate-control rule: Do not hard-code a rate in the ERP solely from the project name. Store the tax period, notification entry, amendment history and legal basis for each contract.

5. The critical annuity vs toll distinction

Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate) originally contained Entry 23 for access to a road/bridge on payment of toll and Entry 23A for access on payment of annuity. Entry 23A was omitted with effect from 1 January 2023 by Notification No. 15/2022-Central Tax (Rate).

CBIC Circular 150/06/2021-GST had earlier clarified the treatment of road access on annuity under Entry 23A. That clarification must now be read with the later omission of Entry 23A and the later HAM-specific Circular 221/15/2024-GST.

Do not write “HAM annuity is exempt” as a blanket conclusion. The historical Entry 23A position and the post-1 January 2023 legal framework must be distinguished by tax period and by the actual supply under the concession agreement.

6. Time of supply under Circular 221/15/2024-GST

Circular 221/15/2024-GST dated 26 June 2024 is central to current HAM time-of-supply analysis. It was issued under Section 168(1) to clarify the time of supply for construction and maintenance services under NHAI HAM projects where part of the consideration is received during construction and the balance is paid through deferred annuity instalments.

Why HAM is treated as continuous supply

The circular explains that the HAM concession is a single contract covering construction and O&M, with staggered payments linked to specified periods/events. The contract should be considered holistically rather than artificially divided into separate construction and O&M contracts solely because payment schedules differ.

SituationSectional frameworkPractical outcome
Invoice issued within prescribed periodSection 13(2) read with Section 31(5)Time of supply generally follows the earlier of invoice/payment under the applicable service-time rule.
Invoice not issued within prescribed periodSection 13(2)(b)Analyse date of provision of service/payment, whichever is earlier, subject to the statutory wording.
Periodic/event-linked paymentSection 31(5)Invoice is linked to specified date or completion of event.
Practical control: Maintain a HAM tax-point register with contract milestone/event, contractual due date, invoice date, payment date, taxable value, GST and return period.

7. Annuity value, interest and other consideration

Section 15 provides the valuation framework. Section 15(2)(d) requires interest, late fee or penalty for delayed payment of consideration to be included in value to the extent provided by the law. Circular 221/15/2024-GST specifically addresses the interest component included in HAM instalments.

8. Construction period: invoice, RA bills and milestones

Road projects generate RA bills, engineer certifications, measurement books, milestone certificates, advances, retention, variation orders and debit/credit notes. Each document should be linked to the underlying contractual event.

ContractBOQMeasurementEngineer certificateRA billTax invoiceGSTR-1GSTR-3B

RA bill is not merely an accounting document

An RA bill may evidence the amount becoming due under the contract. For a continuous supply, Section 31(5) makes the contractual periodic/event-linked billing mechanism important to invoice timing. The tax team should therefore preserve the contract clause that determines when payment becomes due or when the specified event is completed.

Construction-period checklist

  1. Identify milestone/event.
  2. Confirm measurement/engineer certification.
  3. Determine contractual due date.
  4. Issue invoice within the statutory period.
  5. Compute taxable value and GST.
  6. Post accounting entry to project code.
  7. Report in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
  8. Reconcile with customer records/TDS.

12. ITC during HAM project execution

ITC must be tested invoice by invoice under Sections 16 and 17. The fact that an expense is capitalised in project accounts does not by itself determine GST eligibility.

Inward expenseStarting analysis
Subcontract works contractSection 17(5)(c), including further-supply exception where applicable.
Engineering consultancySection 16 business-use test and Section 17 restrictions.
Machinery rentalBusiness-use ITC subject to normal conditions.
Own office constructionSection 17(5)(d) risk.
Plant and machineryCheck statutory definition and exclusions.
Professional feesSection 16 documentation and business purpose.

Section 17(5)(c) vs Section 17(5)(d)

Do not combine them. Section 17(5)(c) deals with inward works-contract services used for construction of immovable property, subject to the further-supply exception. Section 17(5)(d) addresses goods/services received for construction of immovable property on the taxpayer's own account. The ownership, contractual supply and use facts can change the answer.

13. Retention, liquidated damages, penalties and variations

HAM contracts commonly contain retention, performance security, LD, availability deductions, change-in-law compensation and price variation. Each must be classified independently.

ItemDo not assume
RetentionIt is automatically a separate taxable supply.
Performance securityIt is consideration merely because money is withheld.
Liquidated damagesEvery contractual deduction is consideration for tolerating an act.
Price escalationIt is outside taxable value because it arose later.
Variation orderIt can be ignored because the original contract already exists.

Use the contract clause, commercial substance, Section 7, Section 15 and Section 34 framework together. A separate legal analysis is preferable for material deductions or compensation.

15. Project accounting and tax workpaper

For a large HAM project, the GST file should be capable of reconstructing the tax position from contract clause to return. A useful project tax register should contain:

Billing data

Legal data

ITC data

Subcontractor works in a State different from its GSTIN.
Practical treatment: Verify registration, place of supply and invoice tax type.
110. Temporary project office
Project office operates for several years.
Practical treatment: Review registration and place-of-supply implications based on the actual establishment and business activity.
111. Works contract vs pure goods
Supplier delivers road barriers without installation.
Practical treatment: If there is no works-contract service, classify the supply based on actual goods/service terms.
112. Composite supply
One contract includes construction, installation and maintenance.
Practical treatment: Determine principal supply and statutory classification; do not split merely for convenience.
113. Mixed supply risk
Unrelated services are bundled for one price.
Practical treatment: Test composite versus mixed supply under Sections 2(30) and 2(74).
114. Subcontractor material supplied free

Practical treatment: Read the subcontract and determine whether consideration/valuation consequences arise; document free-issue material.
115. Subcontractor buys material
Subcontractor procures its own materials.
Practical treatment: Retain purchase invoices and assess ITC against its outward taxable works-contract supply.
116. Wastage
Subcontractor has normal material wastage.
Practical treatment: Contractual wastage does not automatically create a separate supply; analyse scrap transfer/disposal separately.
117. Scrap sale
Project generates scrap metal and sells it.
Practical treatment: Treat the scrap disposal as a separate outward supply of goods and account for GST as applicable.
118. Damaged material
Material is damaged at site.
Practical treatment: Determine whether it is written off, sold as scrap, recovered from contractor or otherwise disposed.
119. Free issue recovery
Contractor recovers value of material from subcontractor.

121. Canteen at project site

Practical treatment: Employee expense/blocked-credit issues should be analysed separately from the road works contract.

19. FAQs

Is every HAM annuity exempt from GST?

No blanket conclusion should be used. Entry 23A of Notification 12/2017 was omitted from 1 January 2023. Current HAM tax treatment must be determined from the applicable period, contract and supply, together with Circular 221/15/2024-GST.

Is HAM a continuous supply of services?

Circular 221/15/2024-GST specifically explains the NHAI HAM contract as a single contract covering construction and O&M and applies the continuous-supply framework for time of supply.

When should the invoice be raised?

For continuous supplies with periodic/event-linked payments, Section 31(5) is central. The contractual due date or completion of the specified event should be mapped to the statutory invoice rule.

Does deferred payment mean deferred GST?

Not automatically. GST time of supply follows the statutory time-of-supply provisions, not simply the date on which the full commercial consideration is finally collected.

Is interest in HAM annuity taxable?

Where the interest forms part of the consideration covered by the applicable HAM arrangement, Section 15(2)(d) and Circular 221/15/2024-GST are relevant to its inclusion in taxable value.

Can a road contractor claim ITC on subcontractor works?

Potentially, subject to Section 16 and the specific Section 17(5) analysis. Where works-contract service is used for further supply of works-contract service, the statutory exception in Section 17(5)(c) is important.

Does government recipient status make road construction exempt?

No. The exact notification entry and conditions must be tested.

What is the biggest practical HAM GST risk?

For large projects, one of the biggest control risks is treating contract accounting, invoice timing and GST timing as if they were automatically identical. They should be reconciled but independently tested.
