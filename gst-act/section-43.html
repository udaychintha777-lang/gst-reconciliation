1. Why Section 43 matters

Searches such as “Section 43 CGST Act”, “Section 43 GST”, “GST credit note matching”, “credit note ITC reversal”, “Section 43 omitted” and “credit note reconciliation” need one critical clarification: Section 43 is not currently operative.

Section 43 was originally titled “Matching, reversal and reclaim of reduction in output tax liability.” It created a supplier-side matching mechanism for GST credit notes. The supplier's reduction in output tax liability was matched with the recipient's corresponding reduction in ITC.

Current position: Section 43 has been omitted with effect from 1 October 2022. Current credit-note adjustment is primarily governed by Section 34 and the current ITC provisions, not by the old Section 43 matching process.

3. What original Section 43 did

The original Section 43 was the supplier-side counterpart to the recipient ITC matching architecture under Section 42.

When a supplier issued a credit note and reduced its output tax liability, the law contemplated matching that reduction with the corresponding reduction in the recipient's ITC.

Supplier
Reports credit note and reduces output tax liability.
Recipient
Reduces corresponding ITC claim.
Matching
GST system compares the two positions.
Reversal / reclaim
Unresolved differences could trigger reversal, while later correction could permit reclaim.

4. Original statutory architecture

The original Section 43 contained a structured matching process.

Sub-sectionHistorical subject
43(1)Matching of credit-note details furnished by the supplier with the corresponding reduction in the recipient's ITC claim, and checking for duplicate claims for reduction in output tax liability.
43(2)Final acceptance and communication where the supplier's reduction matched the recipient's corresponding ITC reduction.
43(3)Communication of discrepancies where the supplier's reduction did not match the recipient's ITC reduction.
43(4)Reversal/addition to supplier output tax liability where the discrepancy remained unresolved.
43(5)Interest consequences on the amount added to output tax liability.
43(6)Reclaim by the supplier after the recipient rectified the discrepancy and the position matched.
43(7)Interest treatment connected with the reclaim/reversal process.
43(8)Refund of certain interest in specified circumstances after subsequent correction.
43(9)Rules concerning discrepancy resolution and statutory conditions.

This is a historical explanation. The old matching mechanism must not be presented as current GST filing procedure.

5. Credit-note matching

The original process can be simplified as:

  1. Supplier issues a GST credit note.
  2. Supplier reports the credit note and reduces output tax liability.
  3. Recipient reduces the corresponding ITC.
  4. GST system compares the two positions.
  5. If they match, the supplier's reduction is finally accepted.
  6. If they do not match, the discrepancy is communicated.

The original system was therefore intended to protect the Government from a situation where the supplier reduced tax but the recipient continued retaining the corresponding ITC.

6. Duplicate reduction claims

Section 43(1)(b) also addressed duplication of claims for reduction in output tax liability.

For example, if the same GST credit note were accidentally reported twice by the supplier and each entry reduced output tax, the original matching architecture contained a mechanism for identifying duplicate reduction claims.

The underlying accounting lesson remains relevant: modern GST systems should detect duplicate credit notes using credit-note number, supplier GSTIN, original invoice reference, date, taxable value and tax amount.

7. Mismatch and communication

A mismatch could occur where the supplier reduced output tax through a credit note but the recipient did not reduce the corresponding ITC by the same amount.

Historical Rule 73 prescribed the mechanism for matching credit-note details and communicating discrepancies.

8. Reversal of supplier reduction

Where a supplier's reduction in output tax liability remained unmatched, the original Section 43 framework could add the amount of the discrepancy to the supplier's output tax liability in the prescribed manner.

This was an important legal safeguard: the supplier could not indefinitely retain the benefit of reducing output tax if the recipient had not correspondingly reduced the ITC.

Historical only: do not use the old Section 43 reversal procedure for current credit-note compliance. Current law must be determined under Section 34 and the applicable ITC provisions.

9. Reclaim after recipient correction

The original Section 43 also provided a route for the supplier to reclaim the reduction after the recipient corrected its return and reduced the corresponding ITC, subject to the prescribed process.

The historical cycle was therefore:

Credit note → supplier output-tax reduction → recipient ITC reduction → matching → final acceptance.

Or, where unmatched:

Credit note → mismatch → supplier reduction reversed → recipient correction → subsequent matching → supplier reclaim.

10. Interest under historical Section 43

The original Section 43 contained interest consequences where an unmatched reduction in output tax liability was added back to the supplier's liability. It also contained a historical mechanism concerning interest when the supplier subsequently reclaimed the reduction.

These provisions should be distinguished from current interest rules under Section 50.

11. Historical Rules 73–75

The original Section 43 matching architecture was supported by Rules 73 to 75.

Historical RuleBroad function
Rule 73Manner of matching credit-note details and communication of discrepancies.
Rule 74Communication of discrepancy and related supplier/recipient action.
Rule 75Reversal/reclaim consequences associated with the historical credit-note matching process.

These historical Rules are useful when researching old GST records but should not be presented as current return-filing instructions.

12. Historical return architecture

The original GST return architecture contemplated supplier-side GSTR-1 and recipient-side GSTR-2 information. Section 43 was designed to work within that electronic matching model.

The conceptual flow was:

Supplier GSTR-1 credit note → recipient GSTR-2 ITC reduction → Section 43 matching → acceptance/reversal/reclaim.

The operational GST return system subsequently moved away from this model.

13. Why Section 43 was omitted

The original credit-note matching provisions were designed for a return system that relied on detailed two-way matching between supplier and recipient returns. That was not the permanent operational GST architecture.

As the GST system evolved toward GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and system-generated ITC information, the original Sections 42 and 43 matching mechanisms became obsolete as operative statutory procedures.

Key point: Omission of Section 43 did not eliminate the need to reconcile credit notes. It eliminated the old statutory matching mechanism.

14. 2022 return-system restructuring

Section 107 of the Finance Act, 2022 omitted Section 43, and the amendment became effective from 1 October 2022. The Government brought the relevant Finance Act provisions into force through Notification 18/2022-Central Tax dated 28 September 2022. citeturn0search0turn0search1

The same restructuring omitted Sections 42 and 43A. This is why current GST legal research must distinguish pre-October-2022 material from the present law.

15. Section 34 today

Current credit-note compliance is principally governed by Section 34.

Current Section 34 issuePractical meaning
Credit noteSupplier can issue a credit note in specified circumstances, including excess taxable value/tax, returns/deficiency and applicable discount situations.
ReportingCredit-note details are declared in the return for the relevant period, subject to the statutory time limit.
Output tax reductionSupplier's reduction is subject to the conditions in the current Section 34 framework.
Recipient ITCRecipient must evaluate the corresponding ITC reduction where required.

The old Section 43 matching procedure should therefore not be copied into a current Section 34 SOP.

16. Recipient ITC reversal today

When a supplier issues a credit note that reduces tax, the recipient should identify the corresponding ITC and determine the required reduction under the current law.

For a finance team, the safest control is to maintain a credit-note-to-ITC mapping:

  • original supplier invoice;
  • credit-note number/date;
  • taxable value reduced;
  • tax reduced;
  • ITC originally claimed;
  • ITC reversed;
  • return period of reversal; and
  • reconciliation status.

17. GSTR-2B and credit notes

Supplier credit notes can affect the recipient's ITC reconciliation. Current GSTR-2B data can therefore be used as an important control to identify supplier-issued credit notes and ensure corresponding recipient-side ITC adjustments are considered.

Important: GSTR-2B is a reconciliation source, not a replacement for the legal analysis of Section 34, Section 16 and Section 17.

18. Credit-note reconciliation

StepCheck
1Identify supplier credit notes in purchase register/GSTR-2B.
2Link each credit note to the original invoice.
3Confirm supplier has reported the credit note correctly.
4Determine the ITC originally claimed.
5Calculate corresponding ITC reduction.
6Record the reversal in the appropriate return period.
7Reconcile supplier output-tax adjustment with recipient ITC adjustment.

This modern process achieves the same commercial objective that the historical Section 43 matching system attempted to achieve, but through today's operative provisions and GST Portal data.

20. Practical examples

Example 1 — historical matching

A supplier issues a credit note reducing GST by ₹18,000. Recipient reduces ITC by ₹18,000. Under the original Section 43 framework, the two reductions matched.

Example 2 — historical mismatch

Supplier reduces output tax by ₹18,000 but recipient reduces ITC by only ₹10,000. The historical system would identify a discrepancy.

Example 3 — historical supplier reversal

The recipient does not correct the mismatch. The supplier's reduction could be added back to output tax liability under the historical mechanism.

Example 4 — historical reclaim

The recipient subsequently corrects its return and reduces the corresponding ITC. The supplier could pursue reclaim under the historical Section 43 mechanism.

Example 5 — current credit note

A supplier issues a credit note in 2026. The supplier and recipient follow current Section 34 and ITC provisions; Section 43 is not applied.

Example 6 — current GSTR-2B

The credit note appears in the recipient's GSTR-2B. The recipient links it to the original invoice and calculates the required ITC adjustment.

Example 7 — partial credit note

A supplier reduces taxable value by ₹2 lakh against a ₹20 lakh invoice. The recipient identifies the corresponding proportionate ITC impact and records the adjustment.

Example 8 — duplicate credit note

The supplier accidentally reports the same credit note twice. The recipient's reconciliation system flags the duplicate and prevents duplicate ITC reversal.

Example 9 — credit note after payment

A supplier issues a post-supply credit note. The finance team checks the legal basis, reporting deadline and recipient ITC effect before closing the return.

Example 10 — large contractor

A contractor receives hundreds of subcontractor credit notes. Its ERP maps every credit note to the original invoice and tracks the recipient-side ITC adjustment.

23. GST Council background

The original GST return architecture was designed around electronic matching between supplier and recipient data. Credit notes were included in that matching architecture because a supplier's output-tax reduction could otherwise occur without a corresponding reduction in recipient ITC.

As the GST return system evolved away from the original GSTR-2 matching model, the statutory matching provisions became unnecessary for the operative system. The Finance Act 2022 therefore omitted Sections 42, 43 and 43A.

For current compliance, historical Council discussions should be used to understand the evolution of the system, not as authority for an operative Section 43 obligation.

27. Common mistakes

  • Publishing Section 43 as if it is still operative.
  • Applying historical Section 43 matching to current credit notes.
  • Confusing Section 43 with current Section 34.
  • Failing to reverse recipient ITC when a valid supplier credit note reduces tax.
  • Relying only on accounting credit notes without checking GST treatment.
  • Not linking credit notes to original invoices.
  • Missing supplier credit notes appearing in GSTR-2B.
  • Applying old Rules 73–75 to current periods.
  • Failing to distinguish historical and current tax periods.

28. Section 43 GST FAQs

What is Section 43 of the CGST Act?

Section 43 was the original GST provision for matching a supplier's credit-note-based reduction in output tax liability with the corresponding reduction in the recipient's ITC, including reversal and reclaim.

Is Section 43 currently applicable?

No. Section 43 is omitted from the CGST Act with effect from 1 October 2022.

When was Section 43 omitted?

Finance Act 2022 omitted Section 43, with the amendment effective from 1 October 2022.

What did Section 43 match?

It matched credit-note details furnished by the supplier with the corresponding reduction in the recipient's ITC and also addressed duplicate reduction claims.

What happened if supplier credit note and recipient ITC reduction did not match?

Under the historical framework, the discrepancy could be communicated and, if unresolved, the supplier's output-tax reduction could be reversed/added to liability, with later reclaim possible after correction subject to the prescribed conditions.

What replaced Section 43?

There is no single replacement section. Current credit-note treatment is primarily under Section 34, while recipient ITC is governed by Sections 16, 17 and the current return/ITC framework.

Is GSTR-2B based on Section 43?

No. Current GSTR-2B is part of the Section 38/Rule 60 framework.

Does Section 43 apply to current credit notes?

No. Current credit notes should be analysed under Section 34 and the current applicable Rules.

Does the recipient need to reverse ITC for a supplier credit note?

Where the credit note reduces the tax/value corresponding to a supply for which ITC was claimed, the recipient should determine the required ITC adjustment under the current applicable provisions.

Why is Section 43 still relevant?

It remains relevant for historical GST periods, legacy records, departmental communications, old return architecture and litigation involving the period when Section 43 was operative.

What were Rules 73–75?

They supported the historical Section 43 credit-note matching, discrepancy and reversal/reclaim architecture.

How should a company reconcile credit notes today?

Match supplier credit notes to original invoices, GSTR-2B and purchase records, determine the Section 34 treatment and record the corresponding recipient ITC adjustment in the appropriate return period.

30. Key takeaways

  • Section 43 is currently omitted.
  • It originally dealt with matching, reversal and reclaim of reduction in output tax liability caused by credit notes.
  • The original system matched supplier credit notes with recipient ITC reductions.
  • Section 43 was omitted with effect from 1 October 2022.
  • Current credit-note compliance is primarily governed by Section 34 and current ITC provisions.
  • Do not describe current GSTR-2B credit-note reconciliation as Section 43 matching.
  • Credit notes should still be reconciled invoice-by-invoice and supplier-GSTIN-wise.
  • Historical Section 43 remains relevant for legacy periods and litigation.
