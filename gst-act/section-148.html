Section 148 of the CGST Act, 2017

Special Procedure for Certain Processes. Understand how the Government can prescribe special GST procedures for specified classes of registered persons, including special rules relating to registration, returns, tax payment and administration.

CGST Act Section 148Special ProcedureGST NotificationsReturnsTax PaymentAdministration

Section 148 at a Glance

Who can be covered?Specified classes of registered persons notified by the Government.
Who notifies?The Government acts on the recommendations of the GST Council.
What can change?A notified class may have a special procedure concerning registration, returns, tax payment and administration.
In simple language: Section 148 allows the GST system to create a tailored compliance procedure for a particular class of registered persons when the ordinary procedure is not suitable for that process. It is an enabling provision; the actual procedure comes from the specific notification issued under Section 148.

1. Statutory Text of Section 148

148. Special procedure for certain processes.—

The Government may, on the recommendations of the Council, and subject to such conditions and safeguards as may be prescribed, notify certain classes of registered persons, and the special procedures to be followed by such persons including those with regard to registration, furnishing of return, payment of tax and administration of such persons.
Current-law position: Section 148 remains an enabling provision allowing the Government, on GST Council recommendations and subject to prescribed conditions and safeguards, to notify classes of registered persons and special procedures for them. Current CBIC material also records later special procedures issued under Section 148, including Notification No. 22/2024-Central Tax for a rectification process.

2. Section 148 Explained in Simple Language

GST normally uses common procedures for registration, returns, payment and administration. But some situations are unusual enough that applying the ordinary process exactly as it stands may create practical or legal difficulties.

Section 148 provides flexibility. It allows the Government to identify a specific class of registered persons and prescribe a special procedure for them.

Think of Section 148 as a “special procedure switch.” It does not automatically change the GST procedure for everyone. A specific notification must identify the class of persons and the special procedure applicable to that situation.

Why is this useful?

  • Different industries and processes can have different compliance characteristics.
  • Temporary or exceptional legal changes may require a tailored procedure.
  • A special procedure can simplify or structure a process for a defined class.
  • It can provide a mechanism for implementing specific policy decisions recommended by the GST Council.

3. Who Can Notify a Special Procedure?

RequirementMeaning
GovernmentThe statutory power to notify is with the Government.
GST Council recommendationThe notification power operates on the recommendations of the Council.
Conditions and safeguardsThe special procedure is subject to conditions and safeguards as may be prescribed.
Specific classThe notification should identify the class of registered persons to whom the special procedure applies.
Do not assume a special procedure applies to everyone. Always read the specific Section 148 notification, its effective date, eligible persons, conditions, forms and any subsequent amendments or circulars.

4. What Is a “Class of Registered Persons”?

The expression is important. Section 148 does not simply say that the Government can create a special procedure for every taxpayer whenever it wants. It refers to certain classes of registered persons.

A class may be defined by the characteristics specified in the notification. Depending on the particular notification, the relevant distinction can relate to turnover, type of transaction, a particular legal issue, filing status, industry/process or another defined criterion.

Example:

If a notification says that a special procedure applies to registered persons against whom a specified type of order was passed in a specified circumstance, only persons meeting that description should use the procedure. A taxpayer outside that class should not use the procedure merely because it appears convenient.

5. What Can a Special Procedure Cover?

Section 148 expressly gives examples of the areas in which a special procedure may be prescribed.

AreaPossible subject matter
RegistrationSpecial registration-related requirements or processes for the notified class.
Furnishing of returnSpecial return filing requirements, forms, timing or reporting process.
Payment of taxSpecial payment procedure or treatment for the notified class.
AdministrationSpecial administrative procedure connected with the notified class or process.

The wording is inclusive rather than exhaustive. A notification can establish the detailed procedure within the statutory framework.

6. Special Registration Procedures

Section 148 expressly allows special procedures relating to registration. This does not mean that the basic registration provisions disappear. Rather, a notification can prescribe a particular procedure for a defined class.

Example:

A notification identifies a particular class of registered persons and prescribes a special registration-related process. The taxpayer should follow the notified process rather than assume that the ordinary registration workflow is sufficient.

Before acting, check:

  • Whether the person falls within the notified class.
  • The effective date of the notification.
  • Whether the procedure is mandatory or optional, if the notification distinguishes them.
  • Required forms and declarations.
  • Time limits.
  • Conditions and safeguards.

7. Special Return Procedures

Return filing is one of the four areas expressly mentioned in Section 148. A special procedure may alter the reporting mechanism for the notified class.

Historical example – annual return:

Notification No. 47/2019-Central Tax was issued under Section 148 to provide a special procedure for specified registered persons with aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore who had not furnished the annual return for the relevant financial year. CBIC later clarified the optional filing framework.

Do not treat historical notifications as permanently applicable. A special procedure is tied to its notification, financial year/period and conditions. Always verify the notification applicable to the relevant period.

8. Special Procedures for Payment of Tax

Section 148 expressly includes payment of tax. This gives the Government flexibility to prescribe a special payment process for a notified class where the legal framework requires it.

The special procedure may interact with the electronic cash/credit ledger system, payment timing, reporting and other statutory requirements depending on the specific notification.

Finance-team rule: Do not create an accounting treatment merely from the phrase “special procedure.” Read the exact notification to determine whether the special procedure changes the timing, method, form or documentation of payment.

9. Special Administrative Procedures

Section 148 also expressly refers to administration. This can be important when a special process requires a defined administrative route rather than the ordinary taxpayer workflow.

Administrative provisions can include who processes the application, what information is required, what verification is performed and how the result is communicated, depending on the notification.

Example:

If a Section 148 notification creates a special rectification process for a particular category of orders, the taxpayer must follow the procedure, form and verification mechanism specified by that notification and related instructions.

10. Important Section 148 Notifications and Use Cases

Section 148 has been used for several different purposes over the life of GST. The examples below demonstrate why it is important to read the individual notification rather than trying to infer the procedure from Section 148 alone.

ExampleWhat it demonstrates
Notification No. 47/2019-Central TaxSpecial procedure concerning annual return filing for specified registered persons for the relevant period.
Notification No. 22/2024-Central TaxSpecial procedure for rectification of certain orders affected by the retrospective changes to Section 16(4), as recommended by the GST Council and later implemented.
Lesson: Section 148 is a framework provision. Its practical meaning comes from the notification issued under it.

11. 2024 Special Rectification Procedure – An Important Modern Example

The 2024 GST Council recommended a special procedure under Section 148 for rectification of certain orders where input tax credit had been denied solely because of the then-existing Section 16(4) time limit, but the retrospective insertion of Section 16(5) or Section 16(6) could make that ITC available.

CBIC subsequently referred to the special procedure notified under Section 148 by Notification No. 22/2024-Central Tax dated 8 October 2024. Circular No. 238/32/2024-GST provided implementation clarifications, including circumstances in which an application was not required because the benefit was already available through the relevant proceedings.

Why this example matters

It shows how Section 148 can be used as a targeted procedural mechanism rather than as a permanent change to the entire GST system.

Period-specific caution: The 2024 rectification procedure is an example of Section 148 in operation. It should not be assumed to be an open-ended procedure for every ITC dispute in 2026.

12. Annual Return Example – Why Historical Context Matters

Notification No. 47/2019-Central Tax is a useful historical example of Section 148. It provided a special procedure for specified registered persons with aggregate turnover not exceeding ₹2 crore who had not furnished the annual return under Section 44(1) for the relevant period. CBIC issued a clarification regarding the optional nature of filing under that notification.

This illustrates an important legal-research principle: a Section 148 notification may apply only to a particular period and class. A website article should therefore distinguish historical examples from current procedures.

13. Detailed Practical Examples

Example 1 – Special annual-return procedure

A specified class of registered persons receives a special annual-return procedure under a Section 148 notification. The taxpayer checks the notification's period and eligibility before deciding whether and how to file.

Example 2 – Special rectification

A taxpayer has an order affected solely by a legal change for which a Section 148 rectification procedure has been notified. The taxpayer follows the special form and timeline rather than using an unrelated general application.

Example 3 – Wrong class

A taxpayer sees a special notification online but does not satisfy its eligibility conditions. It should not use the special procedure merely because the procedure appears easier.

Example 4 – Notification amended

A notification initially provides one procedure and is later amended. The finance team must use the applicable version for the relevant date.

Example 5 – Expired period

A historical Section 148 notification applied to FY 2018-19. A taxpayer in a later year should not assume the same procedure continues unless a current legal instrument extends or recreates it.

Example 6 – Special payment process

A notified class is given a special tax-payment mechanism. The accounts team follows the notification while separately recording the transaction in the books.

Example 7 – Special return format

A class is required to furnish a special return or statement. The taxpayer should not replace it with an ordinary return if the notification makes the special procedure applicable.

Example 8 – Multiple notifications

A taxpayer finds two Section 148 notifications covering related matters. The team checks effective dates, amendments and whether one supersedes or complements the other.

Example 9 – Portal mismatch

A special procedure requires a particular portal form, but the portal display differs. The taxpayer should check the notification, circular and current portal advisory rather than inventing a manual workaround.

Example 10 – Audit documentation

An auditor asks why the company followed a non-standard GST process. The company produces the Section 148 notification, eligibility working and evidence of compliance with its conditions.

Example 11 – Group company confusion

One GSTIN qualifies for a special procedure while another GSTIN of the same legal entity does not. Eligibility should be tested registration-wise where the notification requires it.

Example 12 – Period-specific benefit

A special procedure is available only for a specified period. The company maintains a separate tracker to prevent the procedure from being used in later periods without authority.

14. Finance & Compliance Workflow for Section 148

  1. Identify the issue: determine why a special procedure may be relevant.
  2. Search the legal instrument: locate the specific Section 148 notification.
  3. Check effective date: confirm the relevant financial year/date.
  4. Check eligible class: verify that the GSTIN/person falls within the notified class.
  5. Read conditions: list every condition and safeguard.
  6. Check forms: identify the prescribed form, portal route or declaration.
  7. Check deadline: record the statutory/notification deadline.
  8. Maker-checker review: have a second person verify eligibility.
  9. Execute: follow the special procedure exactly.
  10. Archive: save notification, working, filing acknowledgement and supporting evidence.

15. What Section 148 Does Not Do

It does not automatically create a special procedure for every taxpayer

A specific notification must identify the class and procedure.

It does not override the entire CGST Act

The special procedure operates within the statutory framework and subject to its conditions and safeguards.

It does not make historical notifications permanently applicable

Many special procedures are tied to a particular period, event or class.

It does not remove the need to verify conditions

Eligibility is not established merely because a taxpayer's facts look similar to an example.

It does not mean every portal problem can be solved under Section 148

A Section 148 procedure exists only where the Government has actually notified one.

It does not itself determine tax rates

Section 148 concerns special procedures, not a general power to change tax rates.

17. Common Mistakes

“Section 148 itself tells me the special procedure.”

No. It is the enabling provision. The actual procedure is in the notification and related legal instruments.

“A Section 148 notification applies to all taxpayers.”

No. It applies to the class and conditions identified in that notification.

“Old Section 148 notifications remain valid forever.”

No. Check the period, amendments, supersession and current status.

“If the notification helps me, I can use it.”

Eligibility must be established objectively. A convenient procedure cannot be selected without satisfying its conditions.

“Section 148 changes tax rates.”

Not generally. Its statutory subject is special procedure, not a general rate-setting power.

“Portal availability means the legal procedure is automatically satisfied.”

No. The taxpayer must meet the legal conditions even when the portal permits a submission.

19. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 148 of the CGST Act?

It empowers the Government, on GST Council recommendations and subject to prescribed conditions and safeguards, to notify classes of registered persons and special procedures for them.

2. Why is Section 148 called a special procedure provision?

Because it allows a procedure different from the ordinary process for a specifically identified class of registered persons.

3. Does Section 148 apply to everyone?

No. The special procedure applies only to the class identified by the relevant notification.

4. What areas can a Section 148 procedure cover?

The section expressly mentions registration, furnishing of returns, payment of tax and administration.

5. Who issues the Section 148 notification?

The Government, on the recommendations of the GST Council.

6. Are Section 148 procedures subject to safeguards?

Yes. The section expressly refers to conditions and safeguards as may be prescribed.

7. Is Notification No. 22/2024-Central Tax a Section 148 notification?

Yes. It notified a special procedure for rectification of specified orders affected by the retrospective Section 16 changes. CBIC's implementation circular refers to this special procedure.

8. What was Notification No. 47/2019-Central Tax?

It provided a special procedure for specified registered persons concerning annual return filing for the relevant period. CBIC later issued clarification regarding its optional filing framework.

9. Can Section 148 prescribe a special return?

Yes. Furnishing of return is expressly included in the section.

10. Can Section 148 prescribe a special tax-payment process?

Yes. Payment of tax is expressly included.

11. Does a Section 148 notification override the GST Act?

It operates within the authority granted by Section 148 and its conditions/safeguards. It should not be treated as a general power to disregard unrelated statutory provisions.

12. Can a taxpayer choose a special procedure voluntarily?

Only if the notification permits it and the taxpayer satisfies the applicable eligibility and conditions. A taxpayer cannot create its own Section 148 procedure.

13. How should a company document Section 148 compliance?

Keep the notification, eligibility working, effective-date analysis, forms/declarations, filing acknowledgement, payment evidence and reviewer approval.

14. Are Section 148 notifications permanent?

Not necessarily. Some are period-specific, event-specific or subject to amendment/supersession.

15. Is Section 148 still relevant in 2026?

Yes. It remains part of the CGST Act and has continued to be used as a legal mechanism for targeted special procedures, including a 2024 rectification procedure.

16. What is the safest way to use a Section 148 notification?

Identify the exact notification, verify the GSTIN/person falls within its class, check the relevant date, follow every condition and retain evidence of compliance.
