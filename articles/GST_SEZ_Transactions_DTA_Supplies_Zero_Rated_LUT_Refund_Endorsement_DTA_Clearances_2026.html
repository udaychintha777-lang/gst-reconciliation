GST on SEZ Transactions – DTA Supplies, Zero-Rated Benefits, LUT, Refunds, Endorsement & SEZ-to-DTA Clearances

1. Executive summary – do not treat every SEZ transaction alike

The GST result depends on the direction of supply, the recipient's status, whether the supply is for authorised operations, and the documents proving receipt into or use within the SEZ.

DTA supplier → SEZ unit/developer

Inter-State and potentially zero-rated. The zero-rated benefit is restricted to supplies for authorised operations. The supplier follows the applicable LUT/IGST and refund route.

SEZ unit/developer → DTA buyer

Goods cleared into the Domestic Tariff Area are treated under the import/customs framework. The DTA buyer must examine Bill of Entry and customs/IGST payment, rather than treating it as an ordinary domestic GST invoice.

DTA supplier → SEZ for non-authorised use

Do not automatically claim zero-rating. Determine the normal tax treatment and whether the transaction meets the statutory authorised-operations condition.

SEZ unit and DTA unit of same legal entity

Separate GST registrations may be required. Transfers between registrations can be supplies between distinct persons even without consideration, subject to Schedule I and valuation rules.

Critical principle From 1 October 2023, the statutory definition of zero-rated supply was amended to link supplies to SEZ units/developers with authorised operations. A valid SEZ GSTIN alone is not enough to establish zero-rating.

3. When does a DTA-to-SEZ supply qualify as zero-rated?

Section 16 of the IGST Act includes supplies of goods or services or both to an SEZ developer or SEZ unit for authorised operations within zero-rated supplies. Section 7(5)(b) treats supplies to an SEZ developer or SEZ unit as inter-State supplies.

Supplier's basic eligibility test

  1. Recipient is an SEZ unit or SEZ developer and the correct SEZ GSTIN is used.
  2. The goods or services are actually supplied to that SEZ recipient.
  3. The supply is for authorised operations of the unit/developer.
  4. The supplier has the required evidence of receipt/admission and authorised use, including the prescribed endorsement.
  5. The supplier complies with invoice, return, LUT/IGST and refund requirements.
Zero-rated treatment If all statutory conditions are met, the DTA supplier may use the zero-rated supply procedure under Section 16 and claim the eligible refund through the route permitted by the current law and rules.
Do not claim zero-rating automatically If the supply is for personal use, non-authorised operations, or the supplier cannot establish the statutory conditions, the supplier should not rely only on the recipient's SEZ status.

4. Authorised operations – the most important practical test

Authorised operations are the operations authorised for the SEZ unit or developer under the SEZ framework and the relevant approval/Letter of Approval. The supplier should obtain enough information to connect the invoice to those operations.

Supply examplePotential authorised-operation linkEvidence to obtain
Production machinery for approved manufacturing activityUsually strong if covered by the unit's approved operations and actually received.Purchase order, asset details, delivery proof, goods receipt and SEZ endorsement.
Raw materials / components for approved productionStrong where consumed in the approved manufacturing process.Material receipt, inward register, production issue records and endorsement.
IT software or professional services for unit operationsCan qualify where used for authorised operations.Agreement, scope, service completion evidence, user/location details and endorsement.
Hotel stay or event service for visiting employeesNot automatically eligible; factual nexus and authorised-operations evidence are essential.Business purpose, attendee details, approval and specified officer's endorsement.
Personal goods or services for employees/directorsNormally not an authorised operational procurement merely because the recipient is an SEZ entity.Review purpose and recipient; do not claim zero-rating without support.
Do not use a blanket SEZ declaration. Obtain the unit's Letter of Approval / approved activity details or a suitable confirmation of authorised operations, and retain the endorsement for each relevant supply or prescribed consolidated evidence.

5. LUT without IGST or payment of IGST – which route should be selected?

Section 16(3) and the applicable rules provide the zero-rated supply/refund framework. A registered supplier may generally make eligible zero-rated supplies under a bond or Letter of Undertaking without payment of IGST and claim refund of eligible unutilised ITC, subject to the statutory conditions and restrictions. The alternative route of payment of IGST and refund must be checked against the current statutory text and notifications applicable to the supplier and supply; do not assume it is universally available.

PointUnder LUT / bondOn payment of IGST
Cash-flowNo output IGST paid at invoice stage; working capital is tied up in eligible input credits until refund.IGST is paid first; refund of tax paid is claimed if the route is legally available.
DocumentValid LUT/bond and invoice declaration as prescribed.Tax invoice charging IGST and payment evidence.
Refund basisRefund of eligible unutilised ITC under the prescribed formula.Refund of IGST paid, subject to statutory conditions.
RiskMissing authorised-operations endorsement or ITC mismatch can delay/reject refund.Wrongly assuming payment route is available, or missing SEZ receipt evidence, can create refund risk.
Before choosing the route: Check the current version of Section 16(3) of the IGST Act, Rule 89, Rule 96A and the latest notifications restricting or permitting refund of IGST paid. The statutory position and refund portal process have changed over time.

6. Invoice, e-way bill and movement documents

For goods

  • Tax invoice with recipient's SEZ name, address and correct SEZ GSTIN.
  • Clear description, HSN, quantity, value, rate and zero-rated/LUT declaration as applicable.
  • Delivery challan where appropriate, transport document and e-way bill if required under the rules.
  • Vehicle details, gate pass, weighment slip, packing list and proof of dispatch.
  • SEZ inward/gate entry and goods receipt evidence.
  • Endorsement by the specified/authorised officer in the form accepted under the applicable rules.

For services

  • Agreement/work order and scope of service.
  • Invoice addressed to the SEZ unit/developer with correct GSTIN.
  • Service delivery evidence, completion certificate, timesheets, system access or other relevant proof.
  • Evidence that the service was received for authorised operations.
  • Endorsement/evidence from the SEZ officer as prescribed for refund purposes.
  • Bank receipt and reconciliation with invoice and return data.
Goods and services have different proof points. For goods, establish that the goods were admitted in full into the SEZ. For services, establish receipt of the services for authorised operations. Do not use a goods gate-entry document as the only proof for a service refund.

7. SEZ endorsement – why it matters and how to control it

Rule 89(1) contains a specific requirement for supplies to SEZ units/developers. In the case of goods, the supplier's refund application is linked to goods being admitted in full into the SEZ for authorised operations, as endorsed by the specified officer. In the case of services, the supplier must furnish evidence of receipt of services for authorised operations as endorsed by the specified officer.

Where an endorsement is delayed, incomplete or refers to a different invoice/value/quantity, follow up with the SEZ recipient before filing the refund claim. Do not wait until the refund application is due to identify missing evidence.

8. Who files the refund and how should it be reconciled?

For DTA-to-SEZ supplies, the refund claim is generally made by the supplier, not the SEZ recipient, under the applicable zero-rated refund provisions. The supplier must use the appropriate refund category and prescribed form/procedure, supported by invoice-level details and SEZ endorsement evidence.

Section 7(5)(b) treats supplies to SEZ units/developers as inter-State supplies. The zero-rated status and refund eligibility must nevertheless be tested separately under Section 16 and Rule 89.

10. SEZ-to-DTA clearance – buyer's customs and GST treatment

Goods cleared from an SEZ to the Domestic Tariff Area are treated as imports into India for the applicable customs framework. The DTA buyer should not process the transaction as though it were an ordinary domestic GST sale by a supplier charging CGST/SGST or IGST on a regular tax invoice.

Documents and checks for the DTA buyer

  • Confirm the SEZ unit's clearance approval and applicable customs procedure.
  • Obtain the Bill of Entry or other prescribed customs clearance document.
  • Verify assessable value, classification, customs duty, IGST and any applicable exemption/concession.
  • Match the Bill of Entry to goods received, purchase order and stock records.
  • Evaluate ITC of import IGST under Section 16 and other conditions using the prescribed customs document.
  • Reconcile import IGST with the relevant GST portal/ICEGATE records and books.
Do not book import IGST as ordinary supplier GST. The customs document and import clearance trail are central to the tax and ITC treatment. Check the current customs law, SEZ Rules and applicable exemption notifications for the particular clearance.

11. Separate GST registration for SEZ and DTA units

An SEZ unit located in a State is treated as a business vertical distinct from other units located outside the SEZ, and separate GST registration is required under the registration provisions. A company operating both DTA and SEZ units should maintain separate GSTIN-level books and tax controls.

SituationPractical GST control
Same legal entity has DTA GSTIN and SEZ GSTIN in same StateMaintain separate registrations and invoice/return ledgers. Evaluate inter-registration supplies under Schedule I and valuation rules.
DTA head office incurs common expenses for SEZ unitReview whether input services must be distributed through ISD under the current provisions or whether a taxable cross-charge is required based on the facts.
Goods transferred from DTA registration to SEZ registrationCheck whether the movement is a supply to the SEZ unit for authorised operations and maintain the zero-rated documentation.
SEZ registration used for procurement but goods delivered to DTAHigh risk. Correct the recipient, delivery and tax treatment; SEZ GSTIN alone does not make it zero-rated.
Related-party and distinct-person rule: Separate registrations of the same legal entity can be distinct persons under Section 25. Supplies between them may be taxable even without consideration under Schedule I, subject to the applicable valuation rules.

12. RCM and special situations

SEZ receives a service liable to reverse charge

Do not assume that zero-rating automatically removes a reverse-charge liability. Where the supply is notified under Section 9(3) of the CGST Act / Section 5(3) of the IGST Act, determine who is liable to pay tax under the reverse-charge entry and the recipient's status. CBIC's GST FAQ explains that where a supplier is not liable because the supply is under RCM, the SEZ recipient is treated as the deemed supplier for this purpose and the SEZ has to pay the applicable tax. Apply the exact notification and facts.

Unregistered supplier to SEZ

Do not rely on old FAQs without checking the current inter-State registration and reverse-charge provisions. A supply to an SEZ is treated as inter-State; examine the supplier's registration obligation and the specific RCM notification, if any, applicable to the transaction.

Common services and head-office expenses

Where the DTA head office procures services and uses them for both DTA and SEZ units, determine the correct recipient, place of supply, ISD distribution and cross-charge consequences. Do not automatically treat the full invoice as a zero-rated supply to SEZ.

Example B – DTA supplier provides consultancy to SEZ for non-authorised employee recreation event

Do not assume zero-rating. The supplier should obtain evidence of the purpose and test authorised operations. If the service is not for authorised operations, the zero-rated benefit is not available merely because the recipient has an SEZ GSTIN. Determine normal tax treatment and invoice accordingly.

Example C – SEZ unit clears imported machinery into DTA

The DTA buyer follows the applicable customs clearance process. It obtains the Bill of Entry, pays applicable customs duties/IGST and evaluates import ITC based on the customs document and statutory eligibility. It does not treat the transaction as a normal domestic GST invoice.

Example D – DTA head office transfers goods to its SEZ registration

Confirm that the SEZ registration is the actual recipient, the goods are for authorised operations and the required invoice, transport and SEZ admission endorsement are obtained. Evaluate the transfer under the distinct-person rules and zero-rated provisions.

Example E – DTA supplier provides software services to SEZ unit

Use the SEZ GSTIN and service agreement, retain evidence of delivery and use for authorised operations, obtain the required endorsement and reconcile the invoice with GSTR-1 and the refund claim.

15. Common reasons for refund delay or rejection

  • Invoice is raised to the wrong GSTIN or to the DTA unit instead of the SEZ unit.
  • Supplier cannot prove that goods/services were for authorised operations.
  • Goods were not admitted in full into the SEZ or the endorsed quantity is short.
  • Service receipt endorsement or supporting service evidence is missing.
  • Endorsement refers to a different invoice number, date, value or recipient.
  • GSTR-1 invoice data does not match the refund statement or books.
  • Supplier claims refund before obtaining the required evidence.
  • LUT is not valid for the relevant period or invoice declaration is defective.
  • Ineligible or blocked ITC is included in the refund computation.
  • Refund route is selected without checking current Section 16(3) and relevant notifications.
  • Payment/realisation evidence for services is missing where required.
  • Same invoice is claimed twice or included in another refund category.
Best control Do not mark a SEZ invoice as “refund ready” in the ERP until the SEZ inward/service endorsement, GSTR-1 match and ITC eligibility check are complete.

16. Monthly compliance checklist for DTA suppliers and SEZ recipients

DTA supplier – monthly close

  1. Verify recipient SEZ GSTIN and authorised-operations confirmation.
  2. Reconcile invoice series, HSN, taxable value and zero-rated reporting.
  3. Check valid LUT/bond and invoice declarations.
  4. Collect goods admission or service receipt endorsement.
  5. Reconcile GSTR-1 with books and refund statement.
  6. Verify ITC eligibility, reversals and refund formula.
  7. Track refund ARN, deficiency memo, reply, sanction and bank credit.

SEZ unit – monthly close

  1. Maintain separate GSTIN-wise purchase and sales registers.
  2. Reconcile DTA procurement invoices with inward register and authorised operations.
  3. Track endorsements and return documents provided to suppliers.
  4. Identify RCM transactions and discharge tax as required.
  5. Reconcile SEZ-to-DTA clearances with Bills of Entry and customs payment.
  6. Review common input services and ISD/cross-charge treatment.
  7. Retain Letter of Approval, approved operations and supporting procurement records.

17. Frequently asked questions and legal references

1. Are all supplies to an SEZ automatically zero-rated?

No. The supply must be to an SEZ unit/developer for authorised operations and satisfy the applicable documentation and refund conditions.

2. Who claims the refund for DTA-to-SEZ supplies?

Generally, the registered supplier making the zero-rated supply files the refund claim under the applicable provisions.

3. Is an SEZ GSTIN sufficient proof for zero-rating?

No. It establishes the recipient's registration identity, not that the goods/services were received for authorised operations.

4. Can a supplier issue an invoice under LUT without charging IGST?

Yes, where the supply qualifies as zero-rated and the supplier has furnished a valid LUT/bond and complies with the applicable conditions.

5. Is endorsement required for goods and services?

Rule 89 requires evidence of goods being admitted in full into the SEZ for authorised operations, or evidence of services being received for authorised operations, endorsed by the specified officer as applicable.

6. Can hotel or event services to an SEZ unit be zero-rated?

Potentially, but only where the service is for authorised operations and the prescribed evidence/endorsement is available. The SEZ recipient status alone is not sufficient.

7. How are goods cleared from SEZ to DTA taxed?

They are dealt with under the customs import/clearance framework. The DTA buyer should obtain and reconcile the applicable customs documents and import IGST.

8. Does a company with DTA and SEZ units need separate GST registrations?

Yes, the SEZ unit is treated as a separate business vertical for registration purposes under the relevant provisions.

9. Can the SEZ unit claim ITC of tax paid under RCM?

Subject to the RCM liability being correctly discharged and Sections 16 and 17 conditions, eligible ITC may be claimed. Review the exact RCM entry and recipient liability.

10. What happens if goods are delivered to DTA but invoice is raised to SEZ?

This is a significant documentation and tax risk. The actual recipient, delivery, use and authorised-operations evidence must be examined; an SEZ GSTIN alone cannot convert a DTA delivery into a zero-rated SEZ supply.

11. Is payment of IGST and refund always available for SEZ supplies?

Do not assume so. Check the current wording of Section 16(3), Rule 89 and the notifications governing the refund route for the relevant period and supplier.

12. What is the most important document for the refund?

The evidence/endorsement that goods were admitted in full or services were received for authorised operations, together with the invoice and return reconciliation.

Practical takeaway

For every SEZ transaction, identify the direction of supply first. For DTA-to-SEZ supplies, verify the recipient, authorised operations, valid LUT/IGST route and SEZ endorsement before claiming a refund. For SEZ-to-DTA goods, follow the customs clearance and Bill of Entry process. Maintain separate GSTIN-wise books and reconcile invoice, return, endorsement and refund data every month.
