Rule 24 – Migration of Persons Registered Under Existing Law

1. Rule 24 at a glance

Rule 24 of the CGST Rules, 2017 deals with migration of persons registered under the existing indirect-tax laws into the GST registration framework. It was designed for the transition from the pre-GST regime to GST from the appointed day.

Historical context matters: Rule 24 contains provisions and forms created for the 2017 GST migration exercise. It should not be presented as the normal route for obtaining a fresh GST registration today. Current new registrations are governed primarily by the contemporary registration provisions, including Rules 8 onward.

The rule historically connected existing registrations under laws such as VAT, Central Excise and Service Tax with provisional GST registration and subsequent final registration after verification of the required particulars.

2. Who was covered by the migration framework?

Rule 24(1) covered persons, other than specified categories such as persons deducting tax at source and Input Service Distributors, who were registered under an existing law and had a PAN under the Income-tax Act.

Such persons were required to enrol on the common portal by validating their email address and mobile number, directly or through an authorised facilitation centre.

3. Provisional GST registration

Under Rule 24(1), after enrolment, the eligible existing taxpayer was granted registration on a provisional basis. A certificate in FORM GST REG-25, incorporating the GSTIN, was made available on the common portal.

The rule contained safeguards for persons who had multiple registrations under the existing law on the basis of a single PAN. The migration framework was therefore designed to convert the old registration structure into the GST structure rather than simply reproduce every old registration automatically.

Centralised service-tax registrations

The rule also contained a specific CGST-rule provision for a person having centralised registration under the earlier service-tax framework, providing for one provisional registration in the relevant State or Union territory as specified in the rule.

Practical lesson: The migration process was not merely a data-upload exercise. The taxpayer had to ensure that the GST registration structure correctly reflected the legal entity, PAN and State/UT framework under GST.

4. Final registration process

Rule 24(2) provided the mechanism for converting the provisional migration into final registration. The taxpayer was required to submit the prescribed electronic application containing the information and documents specified in that application.

The rule provides a period of three months for furnishing the required information, or such further period as could be extended by the Commissioner.

Where the proper officer found the particulars and information correct and complete, the final certificate of registration in FORM GST REG-06 was made available electronically.

5. What if information was incomplete or incorrect?

Rule 24(3) addressed cases where the information or particulars required for finalisation were not furnished or were not found to be correct or complete.

The proper officer could proceed to cancel the provisional registration after following the prescribed notice and opportunity-of-being-heard procedure. The historical framework used FORM GST REG-27 for the show-cause process and FORM GST REG-28 for the cancellation order under the rule's migration mechanism.

Important for historical records: These forms should not be confused with the current ordinary GST registration/cancellation workflow. Rule 24's forms belong to the migration framework and must be read in the context of the transition to GST.

6. Deemed grant mechanism

Rule 24 also contains a deemed-grant mechanism connected with the officer's processing of the information furnished for final registration.

Where the certificate of registration was not made available within the prescribed period after furnishing the required information and no specified notice had been issued within that period, the rule provided for the registration to be deemed granted, with the certificate subsequently made available electronically.

This mechanism was intended to prevent an otherwise compliant migration application from remaining indefinitely unresolved merely because the administrative processing had not resulted in a certificate within the prescribed period.

7. Persons not liable to be registered under GST

Rule 24(4) contained a historical option for persons registered under an existing law who were not liable to be registered under GST. The rule permitted such persons, within the specified historical deadline, to submit an application electronically in FORM GST REG-29 for cancellation of the provisional migration registration.

The proper officer could conduct the enquiry considered necessary and cancel the registration.

Do not apply the old deadline as a current filing deadline. The provision relates to the original GST migration transition and should be treated as historical unless a specific legacy matter requires examination.

8. Why Rule 24 still matters in 2026

Although the original migration exercise occurred at the introduction of GST, Rule 24 remains useful when examining historical registration records, legacy GSTIN issues, old provisional registrations, migration-related notices, archived departmental files and disputes concerning the transition from VAT/CST/Excise/Service Tax to GST.

Historical due-diligence checklist

  • Identify the original pre-GST registration numbers.
  • Check the PAN-to-GSTIN relationship created during migration.
  • Confirm whether the provisional registration was converted to final registration.
  • Review old REG-25, REG-26, REG-27, REG-28 and REG-06 records where relevant.
  • Check whether the entity had multiple registrations under the earlier regime.
  • Verify State/UT-wise GST registration continuity.
  • Preserve historical migration correspondence for audit or litigation.

9. Practical examples

Example 1 — Existing VAT dealer at GST introduction

A business was registered under the State VAT law immediately before GST. During migration it enrolled on the GST portal and received provisional registration. It then furnished the prescribed information and documents for final registration.

Example 2 — Multiple old registrations under one PAN

An entity had multiple registrations under the earlier law based on a single PAN. Rule 24 contained a specific migration safeguard so that the GST migration process did not simply create unlimited provisional registrations from the old structure.

Example 3 — Historical audit file

An auditor in 2026 discovers a departmental file referring to a provisional GSTIN issued in 2017. The appropriate approach is to establish the migration history, determine whether final registration was granted and reconcile the GSTIN with the entity's current registration records.

Example 4 — Registration was not required

A person migrated from an existing law but was ultimately not liable for GST registration. The historical Rule 24(4) mechanism addressed cancellation of such provisional migration registration through the prescribed process.

11. Common mistakes when reading Rule 24

  • Treating Rule 24 as the normal method for obtaining a new GST registration in 2026.
  • Assuming every form mentioned in the original migration rule is still the normal form for the same current process.
  • Ignoring the historical context of the 2017 GST transition.
  • Failing to distinguish provisional GSTIN records from final GST registration.
  • Assuming an old migration GSTIN is automatically the same as the entity's current registration structure without checking the records.
  • Applying historical migration deadlines to a current matter without verifying the applicable law and amendments.

12. FAQs

What is Rule 24 of the CGST Rules?

Rule 24 deals with migration of persons registered under the existing indirect-tax laws into the GST registration framework.

Is Rule 24 used for new GST registration today?

No. Rule 24 was designed for the transition from the pre-GST regime. Current fresh registration is governed by the contemporary registration provisions.

What was REG-25?

REG-25 was the provisional GST registration certificate used in the migration process.

What was REG-26?

REG-26 was used in the historical migration process for existing taxpayers to furnish the prescribed enrolment information.

What was REG-06?

REG-06 is the GST registration certificate and was used for final registration after the prescribed migration information was accepted.

Can Rule 24 be relevant to an old departmental dispute?

Yes. Historical migration records can be relevant when determining how an entity moved from an existing-law registration into GST, whether provisional registration was finalised and how the historical GSTIN was created.

Does Rule 24 apply to every old tax registration?

The rule contained specific exclusions and conditions. In particular, the migration provision did not simply apply identically to persons such as tax deductors and Input Service Distributors.

13. Historical compliance checklist

  • ☐ Identify the pre-GST registration(s).
  • ☐ Identify the PAN used for migration.
  • ☐ Locate the provisional GST registration record, where applicable.
  • ☐ Locate the migration application and supporting information.
  • ☐ Confirm whether final GST registration was granted.
  • ☐ Preserve historical notices/orders if any.
  • ☐ Reconcile the old migration GSTIN with current GST registration records.
  • ☐ Do not apply historical migration deadlines to current matters without verification.
