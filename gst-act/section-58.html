GST ACT — SECTION 58

Section 58 CGST Act — Consumer Welfare Fund Utilisation, Accounts & Audit

Section 58 explains what happens after money is credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund. This guide explains in simple language how the Fund is used for consumer welfare, why separate accounts and records are required, how the annual statement is prepared, the role of the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India, and how Rule 97 gives the practical operating framework.

1. Why Section 58 matters

Searches such as “Section 58 CGST Act”, “Consumer Welfare Fund GST utilisation”, “CWF GST accounts”, “GST Consumer Welfare Fund audit”, “CAG audit Consumer Welfare Fund” and “Rule 97 GST” are all connected to this provision.

Section 57 creates the Consumer Welfare Fund. Section 58 explains what the Government must do with that Fund and how the Fund's financial records are maintained.

Simple idea: Money credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund is not ordinary Government money to be spent for any purpose. Section 58 requires it to be used for consumer welfare, with separate accounts and prescribed reporting.

2. Section 58 in very simple language

Think of the Consumer Welfare Fund as a separate bucket of money created for a specific purpose.

Section 57 tells us how money enters the bucket.

Section 58 tells us what the Government can do with the money and how it must keep track of it.

Section 57 → Money comes into the Fund
Section 58 → Money is used for consumers + accounts are maintained

This simple distinction makes Section 58 much easier to understand.

3. Why does Section 58 exist?

The Consumer Welfare Fund exists because some amounts cannot appropriately be returned directly to a taxpayer, particularly where the tax burden has already been passed on and the unjust-enrichment principle applies.

Once such money enters the Fund, the Government must ensure that it is used for its intended public purpose—welfare of consumers.

The Department of Consumer Affairs describes the Fund as supporting consumer awareness, protection and empowerment, including projects and financial assistance connected with consumer welfare. citeturn0search2

This is one of the easiest ways to remember the two provisions.

6. How can the Fund be used?

The statutory purpose is broad but specific: welfare of consumers.

It does not mean that every Government expenditure benefiting society can automatically be charged to the Consumer Welfare Fund. The expenditure must fall within the prescribed consumer-welfare framework.

Rule 97 provides the detailed operational framework, including grants, consumer-awareness activities, investment recommendations and other approved uses. citeturn0search1

7. Rule 97 connection

Section 58 is deliberately short. Rule 97 provides much of the detail required to operate the Fund.

Rule 97 is therefore essential when interpreting how Section 58 operates in practice. citeturn0search1turn0search5

8. Standing Committee

Rule 97 provides for a Standing Committee constituted by Government with a Chairman, Vice-Chairman, Member Secretary and other members.

The Committee makes recommendations for proper utilisation of money credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund.

The current refund-rules framework requires the Committee to meet as necessary and not less than once every three months. citeturn0search1

9. Consumer welfare grants

One of the most visible uses of the Fund is financial assistance/grants for consumer-welfare activities.

The Government's current Consumer Welfare Fund information explains that assistance can support consumer law centres, consumer literacy and awareness projects, State/UT consumer welfare corpus funds and similar activities. citeturn0search2

A grant is not automatic. The proposal is examined under the Rule 97 process and the Standing Committee recommends proper utilisation.

10. Who can seek assistance?

Rule 97 contains an explanation defining the types of applicants that can seek assistance under the Fund framework. These can include:

  • Central or State Governments;
  • regulatory authorities and autonomous bodies constituted under law;
  • eligible agencies or organisations engaged in consumer-welfare activities;
  • certain consumer cooperatives;
  • specified educational or research institutions; and
  • a consumer seeking reimbursement of eligible legal expenses after final adjudication of a consumer dispute.

The exact eligibility conditions and engagement periods prescribed by Rule 97 should be checked for the relevant application. citeturn0search5

11. GST consumer awareness

The Fund can also support publicity and consumer awareness relating to GST.

Government guidelines explain that the overall objective includes strengthening consumer awareness of GST provisions and their rights and responsibilities, and Rule 97 contains a mechanism under which a portion of the Fund can be made available to the Board for GST publicity or consumer awareness subject to the prescribed condition. citeturn0search8turn0search5

This is particularly relevant to consumers because GST is often included in the final price they pay.

12. Consumer legal-expense assistance

The Rule 97 framework also contemplates grants on a selective basis for reimbursement of legal expenses incurred by a complainant or class of complainants in a consumer dispute after final adjudication.

This demonstrates that the Fund is not limited to awareness campaigns; it can also support mechanisms that strengthen consumers' ability to enforce their rights, subject to the prescribed conditions and Committee recommendations. citeturn0search5

13. Separate accounts and records

Section 58(2) contains a very important financial-control requirement: the Government or specified authority must maintain proper and separate accounts and other relevant records relating to the Fund.

Why separate accounts?

  • to identify the Fund's opening balance;
  • to record credits into the Fund;
  • to track investment income;
  • to record payments/grants;
  • to identify amounts returned or paid to claimants;
  • to reconcile the closing balance; and
  • to make the Fund auditable.
In simple terms: the Government should be able to show exactly how much came into the Fund, how much went out, why it went out and how much remains.

14. Annual statement of accounts

Section 58(2) also requires an annual statement of accounts in the prescribed form.

This converts the Fund's day-to-day accounting records into an annual financial statement that provides a consolidated picture of the Fund.

Annual statement should help answerWhy it matters
Opening balanceShows the Fund position at the beginning of the year.
CreditsShows money received during the year.
IncomeShows investment/other income credited.
UtilisationShows amounts spent/transferred for approved purposes.
Refund paymentsShows amounts paid to claimants where ordered.
Closing balanceAllows reconciliation with the Fund's records.

15. Role of the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India

Section 58 requires the form of the annual statement of accounts to be prescribed in consultation with the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG).

This is an important accountability safeguard because the Fund contains public money and its use is restricted to a statutory purpose.

Rule 97 also provides that the accounts of the Fund maintained by the Central Government are subject to audit by the CAG. citeturn0search1turn0search7

16. Audit and accountability

Audit is not merely a formality. The Fund's framework contains multiple layers of accountability.

Statutory accounts

Separate Fund accounts are maintained.

Annual statement

Year-end financial reporting is prepared in prescribed form.

CAG audit

Fund accounts maintained by the Central Government are subject to CAG audit.

Grant audit

Accounts of grant applicants can be audited to ensure proper utilisation.

17. Investment of Fund money

Rule 97 gives the Standing Committee a role in recommending beneficial and safe sectors in which money available in the Fund may be invested.

This should be understood as a controlled investment mechanism rather than unrestricted investment freedom.

Income generated from investment is linked back to the Fund under Section 57(b), so the Fund's financial resources remain connected to its statutory purpose.

18. Committee powers and controls

Rule 97 gives the Committee powers that are important for protecting public money.

19. Misuse and recovery

If a grant recipient misuses the money or suppresses material information, Rule 97 provides mechanisms for recovery of the sanctioned grant and other consequences under the Rules/Act.

This is important because Section 58 is not simply an authorisation to spend the Fund; it creates a framework where utilisation must remain connected to consumer welfare and be capable of verification.

20. Section 57 connection

Section 57 and Section 58 should be read together.

Section 57 = Creation and credits to Consumer Welfare Fund
Section 58 = Utilisation + separate accounts + annual statement

For example, when an amount is credited to the Fund because it is not payable directly to a refund claimant, Section 57 explains the destination. Section 58 explains the public-purpose utilisation and accounting framework that applies after that.

21. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Refund amount credited to CWF

A refund claim is examined and an amount is required to be credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund. Section 57 identifies the Fund; Section 58 governs its subsequent utilisation and accounts.

Example 2 — Consumer awareness project

A Government-supported project proposes a large GST consumer-awareness campaign. The proposal must fit the prescribed CWF framework and be processed through the appropriate Committee mechanism.

Example 3 — University consumer-law centre

An eligible institution proposes a consumer-law research or awareness centre. Financial assistance may be considered under Rule 97 subject to eligibility and scrutiny.

Example 4 — State Consumer Welfare Corpus

Financial assistance may be provided for creating a State/UT Consumer Welfare Corpus Fund subject to the Government's applicable guidelines.

Example 5 — Grant audit

An organisation receives a CWF grant. It may be required to provide audited accounts and periodic utilisation reports.

Example 6 — Material suppression

A grant recipient hides material information. The Committee can require refund/recovery of the grant under the Rule 97 framework.

Example 7 — Fund investment income

The Fund earns investment income. The income remains connected with the Fund under Section 57 and is not simply treated as an unrelated receipt.

Example 8 — Claimant later wins appeal

An amount previously credited to CWF is later ordered to be paid to the claimant. The Rule 97 mechanism allows payment from the Fund pursuant to the appropriate order.

Example 9 — Separate accounting

The Fund receives ₹50 crore and spends ₹12 crore on approved purposes. The separate accounting system should allow the Government and auditors to trace the ₹50 crore credit, ₹12 crore utilisation and resulting balance.

Example 10 — CAG audit

The Fund accounts maintained by the Central Government are subject to CAG audit, creating an external accountability layer.

22. Business and refund examples

Construction company refund

A construction company files a refund claim. If the amount is not payable directly because of unjust enrichment, the amount may be routed to the Consumer Welfare Fund. The company's accounting should therefore distinguish between “refund claimed” and “refund legally receivable by the company”.

Infrastructure contractor

An infrastructure company should not recognise every GST refund application as an unconditional receivable. The refund order and treatment under Section 54/57 must be considered.

Customer recovery

If GST was separately recovered from customers, the company should preserve pricing, invoice and credit-note records because these can be relevant to the tax-incidence/unjust-enrichment analysis.

The Department of Consumer Affairs records that the present CWF framework under GST replaced the earlier Central Excise-era mechanism and that its guidelines have been revised over time. citeturn0search2

25. GST Council context

The GST Council's original refund framework included the Consumer Welfare Fund as part of the GST refund architecture.

The 14th GST Council agenda records the Fund's utilisation, Standing Committee and grant framework, including the ability to pay amounts from the Fund where a proper officer, appellate authority, Appellate Tribunal or court directs payment. citeturn0search4

This shows that Section 58 was designed from the beginning as a combination of public-purpose utilisation and financial accountability, rather than merely as a technical accounting provision.

27. Common mistakes

  • Thinking Section 58 itself creates the Consumer Welfare Fund.
  • Ignoring Section 57 while reading Section 58.
  • Assuming CWF money can be spent for any Government purpose.
  • Ignoring Rule 97.
  • Failing to maintain separate Fund accounts.
  • Not preserving annual statements and supporting records.
  • Ignoring CAG audit requirements.
  • Assuming every consumer-welfare proposal automatically qualifies for a grant.
  • Failing to monitor grant utilisation.
  • Not recovering misused grant amounts where required.

29. Section 58 GST FAQs

What is Section 58 of the CGST Act?

Section 58 deals with utilisation of the Consumer Welfare Fund and requires proper separate accounts, relevant records and an annual statement of accounts.

What is the main purpose of Section 58?

Its main purpose is to ensure that money credited to the Consumer Welfare Fund is used for the welfare of consumers and properly accounted for.

What is the Consumer Welfare Fund?

It is a statutory fund under Section 57 into which specified GST refund-related and other prescribed amounts are credited.

What is the difference between Section 57 and Section 58?

Section 57 deals with creation and credits to the Fund. Section 58 deals with utilisation of the Fund and its accounts.

What is Rule 97 GST?

Rule 97 provides the detailed operating framework for the Consumer Welfare Fund, including grants, Standing Committee functions, utilisation, audit and investment-related recommendations.

Who audits the Consumer Welfare Fund?

Rule 97 provides that the accounts of the Fund maintained by the Central Government are subject to audit by the Comptroller and Auditor-General of India.

Are separate accounts required?

Yes. Section 58(2) expressly requires proper and separate accounts and other relevant records relating to the Fund.

Is an annual statement required?

Yes. Section 58(2) requires an annual statement of accounts in the prescribed form.

Can CWF money be used for GST awareness?

Yes, the Rule 97 framework and Government guidelines provide for consumer awareness/publicity relating to GST subject to the prescribed conditions.

Can a consumer organisation get CWF funding?

Eligible organisations can apply under the Rule 97 framework, but assistance is subject to eligibility, scrutiny and Committee recommendations.

Can money from CWF later be paid to a taxpayer?

Yes, where an appropriate order directs payment to a claimant, Rule 97 provides for payment from the Fund.

Why does CAG have a role?

Because the Fund contains public money and has a restricted statutory purpose. Section 58 requires the annual statement framework to be prescribed in consultation with CAG, while Rule 97 provides for CAG audit.

31. Key takeaways

  • Section 58 deals with utilisation of the Consumer Welfare Fund.
  • All sums credited to the Fund must be used for the welfare of consumers in the prescribed manner.
  • Separate accounts and relevant records are mandatory.
  • An annual statement of accounts must be prepared in the prescribed form.
  • The annual statement framework is prescribed in consultation with the CAG.
  • Rule 97 provides the detailed operating mechanism.
  • The Standing Committee recommends proper utilisation and oversees grant-related safeguards.
  • CWF money can support consumer awareness, consumer protection and other approved activities.
  • Grant recipients can be subject to inspection, audit, reporting and recovery controls.
  • Section 57 explains where money enters the Fund; Section 58 explains how it is used and accounted for.
