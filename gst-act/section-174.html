CGST Act Section 174 – Repeal and Saving

Section 174 explains what happened to the major indirect-tax laws replaced by GST and, importantly, which rights, liabilities, investigations, assessments, appeals, recoveries and other proceedings continue even after repeal or amendment.

Section 174Repeal & SavingLegacy Tax MattersCentral ExciseService TaxHistorical Proceedings

Section 174 at a Glance

Core purposeTo repeal specified legacy indirect-tax laws while preserving specified legal consequences from the pre-GST period.
GST transitionSection 173 deals with amendment of Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994; Section 174 contains the broader repeal-and-saving framework.
Practical importanceOld audits, show-cause notices, demands, refunds, appeals, recoveries and liabilities can continue under the saving provisions.
In simple words: GST replaced several central indirect-tax laws from the appointed day, but repeal does not mean that every historical matter disappears. Section 174 preserves specified past rights, liabilities, proceedings and remedies so that the transition from the old tax regime to GST does not erase the legal consequences of the earlier period.

1. Statutory Text of Section 174

174. Repeal and saving.—

(1) Save as otherwise provided in this Act, on and from the date of commencement of this Act, the Central Excise Act, 1944 (1 of 1944) (except as respects goods included in entry 84 of the Union List of the Seventh Schedule to the Constitution), the Medicinal and Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act, 1955 (16 of 1955), the Additional Duties of Excise (Goods of Special Importance) Act, 1957 (58 of 1957), the Additional Duties of Excise (Textiles and Textile Articles) Act, 1978 (40 of 1978), and the Central Excise Tariff Act, 1985 (5 of 1986) (hereafter referred to as the repealed Acts) are hereby repealed.

(2) The repeal of the said Acts and the amendment of the Finance Act, 1994 (32 of 1994) (hereafter referred to as "such amendment" or "amended Act", as the case may be) to the extent mentioned in the sub-section (1) or section 173 shall not—
(a) revive anything not in force or existing at the time of such amendment or repeal; or
(b) affect the previous operation of the amended Act or repealed Acts and orders or anything duly done or suffered thereunder; or
(c) affect any right, privilege, obligation, or liability acquired, accrued or incurred under the amended Act or repealed Acts or orders under such repealed or amended Acts:
Provided that any tax exemption granted as an incentive against investment through a notification shall not continue as privilege if the said notification is rescinded on or after the appointed day; (d) affect any duty, tax, surcharge, fine, penalty, interest as are due or may become due or any forfeiture or punishment incurred or inflicted in respect of any offence or violation committed against the provisions of the amended Act or repealed Acts; or
(e) affect any investigation, inquiry, verification (including scrutiny and audit), assessment proceedings, adjudication and any other legal proceedings or recovery of arrears or remedy in respect of any such duty, tax, surcharge, penalty, fine, interest, right, privilege, obligation, liability, forfeiture or punishment, as aforesaid, and any such investigation, inquiry, verification (including scrutiny and audit), assessment proceedings, adjudication and other legal proceedings or recovery of arrears or remedy may be instituted, continued or enforced, and any such tax, surcharge, penalty, fine, interest, forfeiture or punishment may be levied or imposed as if these Acts had not been so amended or repealed;
(f) affect any proceedings including that relating to an appeal, review or reference, instituted before, on, or after the appointed day under the said amended Act or repealed Acts and such proceedings shall be continued under the said amended Act or repealed Acts as if this Act had not come into force and the said Acts had not been amended or repealed.

(3) The mention of the particular matters referred to in sub-sections (1) and (2) shall not be held to prejudice or affect the general application of section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 with regard to the effect of repeal.
Important: The wording above is the statutory Section 174 text. For a particular historical dispute, the applicable law can depend on the period, the relevant legacy enactment, amendments, notifications and the procedural history of the matter.

2. Section 174 Explained in Simple Language

Section 174 is the GST transition provision dealing with repeal and saving. Repeal removes specified old enactments from the forward-looking tax framework, while the saving clauses preserve specified consequences of the old law.

This is important because a tax period does not become legally irrelevant merely because GST started on 1 July 2017. A business may still have an old service-tax assessment, central-excise dispute, refund claim, audit objection, appeal, recovery or other proceeding relating to a period before GST.

QuestionSection 174 approach
Were specified old Acts repealed?Yes, subject to the exceptions and saving provisions stated in the Act.
Do old rights and liabilities automatically disappear?No. Section 174(2)(c) preserves specified rights, privileges, obligations and liabilities acquired, accrued or incurred.
Can an old tax demand still be pursued?Section 174(2)(d) and (e) preserve specified dues, proceedings and recovery mechanisms relating to the old law.
Can an old appeal continue?Yes. Section 174(2)(f) specifically addresses appeals, reviews and references.
Does repeal revive something that had already ceased to exist?No. Section 174(2)(a) expressly prevents such revival.

3. Which Laws Were Repealed?

Section 174(1) identifies the principal enactments covered by the repeal. The list is important when a company is reviewing legacy tax litigation or historical books.

Do not read “repealed” as “all old matters cancelled.” Section 174(2) is specifically designed to preserve a wide range of historical consequences and proceedings.

4. Why the Saving Provisions Matter

A repeal-and-saving provision avoids a legal vacuum. Without saving provisions, taxpayers and authorities could face difficult questions about whether a tax that had already become due, an appeal already filed, or a liability already incurred could still be dealt with after the statute was repealed.

Past operationThings validly done or suffered under the earlier law are not simply erased.
Rights & liabilitiesSpecified acquired rights and accrued obligations/liabilities continue to have legal significance.
ProceedingsInvestigation, audit, assessment, adjudication, recovery and other legal proceedings can continue within the saving framework.

The practical lesson for finance teams is simple: keep legacy tax records until the historical matter is fully closed and the applicable retention requirements have been satisfied.

5. Clause-by-Clause Explanation of Section 174(2)

Section 174(2)(a) – No revival of something already dead

Repeal does not revive something that was not in force or did not exist at the time of the amendment or repeal. The clause prevents a repealed framework from being used to resurrect a provision, right or legal state that had already ceased to exist before the relevant repeal.

Example: Suppose a provision had already expired or been validly discontinued before the repeal date. Section 174 does not create a fresh revival merely because the surrounding enactment is being repealed.

Section 174(2)(b) – Previous operation is protected

The previous operation of the amended or repealed Acts and orders, and anything duly done or suffered under them, is preserved. This covers the legal history created while the old law was operative.

Example: An assessment order passed under a legacy tax law for an eligible pre-GST period does not become meaningless merely because the underlying enactment is later repealed.

Section 174(2)(c) – Rights, privileges, obligations and liabilities

This clause protects specified rights, privileges, obligations and liabilities that were acquired, accrued or incurred under the amended/repealed laws or orders made under them.

For a business, this can include historical tax liabilities, procedural rights, claims, obligations and other legal consequences that had already arisen before the GST transition.

Investment incentive exception: The proviso says that a tax exemption granted as an incentive against investment through a notification does not continue as a privilege if that notification is rescinded on or after the appointed day.

Section 174(2)(d) – Tax, duty, interest, penalty and other consequences

The saving extends to duty, tax, surcharge, fine, penalty and interest that were due or may become due, as well as forfeiture or punishment incurred or inflicted for an offence or violation under the amended/repealed law.

Historical itemWhy it matters
Duty/tax already dueA historical liability can remain recoverable within the saving framework.
InterestInterest connected with a preserved historical liability is not automatically extinguished by repeal.
Penalty/fineHistorical violations and penalties can continue to have consequences as provided by the saving provisions.
Forfeiture/punishmentSpecified consequences for old offences or violations are preserved.

Section 174(2)(e) – Investigation, audit, assessment, adjudication and recovery

This is one of the most operationally important clauses. It expressly covers investigation, inquiry, verification, scrutiny, audit, assessment proceedings, adjudication, other legal proceedings, recovery of arrears and remedies.

It also says that these matters may be instituted, continued or enforced and that the relevant historical tax, surcharge, penalty, fine, interest, forfeiture or punishment may be levied or imposed as if the old Acts had not been amended or repealed.

Finance-team takeaway: A pre-GST period can still require active work today. Do not close a legacy tax file merely because GST replaced the old tax regime.

Section 174(2)(f) – Appeal, review and reference

Section 174 expressly preserves proceedings including an appeal, review or reference instituted before, on or after the appointed day under the amended/repealed Acts. The proceedings are to continue under those laws as if the CGST Act had not come into force and those Acts had not been amended or repealed.

Example: A company that had challenged a historical excise demand does not lose its appeal merely because GST commenced after the original tax period.

Section 174(3) – General Clauses Act saving

Section 174(3) states that the specific matters listed in Section 174(1) and (2) do not prejudice or affect the general application of section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 concerning the effect of repeal.

This means Section 174 contains specific saving language while also preserving the potential operation of the general repeal-saving principle in Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, subject to the law applicable to the particular issue.

6. What Is the “Appointed Day”?

In GST transitional provisions, the appointed day is the date from which the relevant GST framework commenced. For the provisions in Sections 140 to 163 and 165 to 174, India Code records 1 July 2017 as the enforcement date.

Date / conceptPractical meaning
1 July 2017GST became operational for the relevant CGST provisions, and Section 174's repeal-and-saving framework took effect.
Pre-GST tax periodHistorical central excise/service-tax matters may remain governed by the relevant legacy law through the saving provisions.
Post-GST periodCurrent GST law generally governs supplies and liabilities arising under GST, subject to transitional and legacy-law provisions where relevant.
Important distinction: The tax period, the date of the legal proceeding, and the date of the repeal are not necessarily the same. Always identify the tax period and procedural history before deciding which statute governs an issue.

7. Central Excise: What Section 174 Means in Practice

Central excise is one of the major legacy areas preserved for historical matters. The repeal in Section 174(1) does not wipe out the historical operation of the law because Section 174(2) expressly saves duties, liabilities, proceedings and remedies.

Common legacy excise matters

  • Classification disputes for pre-GST manufacture.
  • Valuation disputes relating to pre-GST clearances.
  • Show-cause notices concerning historical clearances.
  • Audit objections and investigations for old periods.
  • Demand, interest and penalty proceedings under the legacy law.
  • Appeals before the appropriate appellate forum concerning historical excise matters.
  • Recovery of historical arrears where the saving provisions apply.
Example: A manufacturing company receives a legacy excise demand relating to a period before 1 July 2017. The fact that GST replaced central excise for the relevant framework does not, by itself, extinguish the historical demand or the associated legal process.

8. Service Tax and the Finance Act, 1994

Section 173 separately deals with the omission of Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994, which contained the service-tax framework. Section 174(2) then provides saving rules for the amendment to the Finance Act, 1994 to the extent covered by Section 173.

Therefore, when reviewing an old service-tax matter, a finance or legal team should read Sections 173 and 174 together rather than treating Section 173 as an independent cancellation of all historical service-tax consequences.

Historical service-tax matterSection 174 relevance
Old service-tax demandPreservation of specified tax, interest, penalty and recovery consequences.
Show-cause notice / adjudicationSpecified investigations and adjudication can continue.
Old refund claimHistorical rights and proceedings may remain relevant under the saving framework.
Appeal / review / referenceSection 174(2)(f) expressly addresses continuation of such proceedings.

9. Appeals, Reviews and References After GST

Section 174(2)(f) is especially important for litigation teams. It prevents the commencement of GST from automatically terminating an appeal, review or reference connected with a legacy law.

Questions to ask when reviewing a legacy appeal

  1. What is the tax period?
  2. Which legacy enactment created the original liability or right?
  3. What was the nature of the order being challenged?
  4. Was the appeal, review or reference filed before or after 1 July 2017?
  5. Which appellate authority or court currently has jurisdiction over the preserved proceeding?
  6. Are there subsequent amendments, notifications or judicial orders affecting the matter?
  7. Has the tax, interest, penalty or other amount already been paid, adjusted or recovered?
Do not assume the GST appellate structure automatically replaces the legacy appellate route. Section 174 specifically contemplates continuation of proceedings under the amended/repealed Acts for preserved historical matters.

11. Practical Examples

Example 1 – Pre-GST excise demand: A manufacturer has a disputed excise demand relating to a pre-GST period. GST commencement does not, by itself, cancel the preserved historical demand or proceedings.
Example 2 – Service-tax audit: A service provider's historical service-tax records are examined after GST. The saving framework can permit continuation of specified inquiry, verification, scrutiny or audit concerning the legacy period.
Example 3 – Historical interest: A legacy tax amount was due under the old law and interest is connected with that liability. Section 174(2)(d) specifically addresses interest due or that may become due.
Example 4 – Appeal filed before GST: A company had already appealed an excise order before 1 July 2017. Section 174(2)(f) expressly protects continuation of such an appeal under the legacy law.
Example 5 – Appeal after GST: A preserved historical matter results in an appeal after the appointed day. Section 174(2)(f) expressly includes proceedings instituted before, on or after the appointed day.
Example 6 – Old assessment: A legacy assessment remains incomplete when GST begins. Section 174(2)(e) covers assessment proceedings within its saving framework.
Example 7 – Recovery of arrears: A historical duty remains unpaid. Section 174(2)(e) expressly refers to recovery of arrears and remedies in respect of preserved amounts.
Example 8 – Historical penalty: A penalty arose from a violation under the old law. Section 174(2)(d) preserves specified penalty consequences.
Example 9 – Investment exemption: A business had an investment-linked tax exemption notification. The proviso to Section 174(2)(c) contains a specific rule where that notification is rescinded on or after the appointed day.
Example 10 – Old legal act already completed: A valid assessment or other action was completed under the legacy law. Section 174(2)(b) protects the previous operation and things duly done or suffered.
Example 11 – Expired provision: A provision was no longer in force before the relevant repeal. Section 174(2)(a) prevents repeal from reviving it.
Example 12 – Refund dispute: A historical refund claim or dispute exists under the legacy framework. The legal team should examine the precise saving provision, procedural history and applicable legacy law rather than assuming the claim vanished on 1 July 2017.
Example 13 – Multiple statutes: A group has separate central-excise and service-tax disputes. Maintain separate legal files because Section 173 concerns the Finance Act, 1994 while Section 174 deals with the specified repealed Acts and the saving framework.
Example 14 – Audit and appeal overlap: A historical audit resulted in an order that was appealed. The audit record and the appellate record should be linked because Section 174 preserves both the historical proceedings and the appeal framework.
Example 15 – Recovered demand: A historical demand was partly paid and partly disputed. The finance team should reconcile payment, interest, penalty and appellate relief rather than treating the original demand amount as the current outstanding balance.
Example 16 – Historical records requested: A company receives a request for documents concerning a pre-GST tax period. Records should be retrieved by statute and tax period, with the legal team checking the exact nature of the proceeding.

12. Finance & Legal Workflow for Section 174 Matters

Step 1 – Identify the period

Start with the financial year, tax period and relevant transaction dates. Do not start by looking only at the date on the latest notice.

Step 2 – Identify the legacy law

Determine whether the matter concerns central excise, service tax or another historical provision connected with the repealed/amended framework.

Step 3 – Build the procedural timeline

Record SCN, audit, investigation, assessment, adjudication, appeal, review, reference, payment and recovery dates.

Step 4 – Map the Section 174 saving clause

Identify whether the issue concerns previous operation, rights/liabilities, duty/tax/interest/penalty, investigation/audit/assessment/recovery, or appeal/review/reference.

Step 5 – Reconcile the financial position

Match original demand or claim with payments, refunds, appellate relief, interest and recovery actions. This prevents an old gross demand from being mistaken for the present net exposure.

Step 6 – Preserve evidence

  • Original returns and statutory forms.
  • Invoices, ledgers and transaction-level supporting documents.
  • Audit reports and correspondence.
  • Show-cause notices and replies.
  • Orders and demand calculations.
  • Appeal papers, grounds and appellate orders.
  • Payment challans and electronic/manual accounting records.
  • Legal opinions and judicial orders where applicable.

Step 7 – Obtain period-specific legal advice

Where the matter is disputed or involves limitation, jurisdiction, recovery, exemption, penalty or appellate procedure, obtain advice based on the exact legacy statute and procedural history.

13. Section 173 vs Section 174

PointSection 173Section 174
Main subjectAmendment of Act 32 of 1994Repeal and saving
Legacy frameworkChapter V of the Finance Act, 1994Specified excise enactments plus saving of specified consequences of repealed/amended laws
Practical questionWhat happened to the service-tax chapter in the Finance Act, 1994?What was repealed and what historical rights, liabilities and proceedings survive?
Key compliance useUnderstanding historical service-tax transitionManaging legacy tax disputes, audits, assessments, recoveries and appeals
Read together?Yes. For a legacy service-tax matter, Section 173 and the saving provisions in Section 174 should be considered together.

Section 174 vs Section 172

Section 172 concerns removal of difficulties; Section 174 concerns repeal and saving. They perform different functions in the statutory transition.

Section 174 vs Section 6 of the General Clauses Act

Section 174(3) expressly preserves the general application of Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 regarding the effect of repeal. The precise legal effect in a dispute depends on the statutory text and facts of that matter.

14. Common Mistakes in Legacy Tax Files

  1. Assuming repeal means cancellation: Section 174 contains detailed saving clauses.
  2. Ignoring the tax period: The date of a later notice does not by itself identify the governing substantive law.
  3. Mixing GST and legacy demands: Keep pre-GST liabilities separately identified in the demand register.
  4. Closing old audit files too early: Preserved audit and assessment proceedings may continue.
  5. Ignoring appeals: Section 174(2)(f) expressly addresses appeal, review and reference.
  6. Using current GST provisions automatically: Historical matters may need the legacy law and its procedural framework.
  7. Not reconciling payments: Original demand, current outstanding amount and appellate relief can differ.
  8. Discarding old evidence: Preserve the documents needed to establish the historical transaction and procedural record.
  9. Overlooking the investment-exemption proviso: The proviso to Section 174(2)(c) has specific wording that should be checked when an incentive exemption is involved.
  10. Relying on a summary without checking the actual order: For litigation, read the SCN, order, appeal and subsequent directions.

15. Common Search Questions About Section 174

What is Section 174 of the CGST Act?
It is the repeal-and-saving provision dealing with specified legacy indirect-tax laws and preservation of specified historical rights, liabilities and proceedings.

Does Section 174 cancel old excise cases?
No. The repeal is accompanied by saving provisions covering specified historical liabilities and proceedings.

Can an old service-tax case continue after GST?
Specified historical service-tax matters can continue under the applicable saving framework; Section 173 and Section 174 should be read together.

Can a pre-GST tax demand be recovered after GST?
Section 174(2)(e) expressly refers to recovery of arrears and remedies for preserved historical liabilities.

What happens to an old appeal after GST?
Section 174(2)(f) expressly provides for continuation of proceedings including appeals, reviews and references.

What does Section 174(3) do?
It preserves the general application of Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 concerning the effect of repeal.

16. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the main purpose of Section 174?

To repeal specified legacy enactments from the GST transition date while preserving specified historical legal consequences.

2. Which major Acts are repealed?

Section 174(1) lists the Central Excise Act, 1944 subject to its stated exception, the Medicinal and Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act, 1955, the Additional Duties of Excise (Goods of Special Importance) Act, 1957, the Additional Duties of Excise (Textiles and Textile Articles) Act, 1978, and the Central Excise Tariff Act, 1985.

3. Is the Finance Act, 1994 repealed by Section 174?

Section 173 specifically deals with omission of Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994. Section 174(2) contains saving language for the amendment covered by Section 173.

4. Does repeal erase historical liabilities?

No. Section 174(2) expressly preserves specified rights, obligations, liabilities, duties, taxes, interest, penalties and proceedings.

5. Can a historical audit continue?

Section 174(2)(e) expressly includes investigation, inquiry, verification, scrutiny and audit within the matters that may continue under the saving framework.

6. Can old assessment proceedings continue?

Yes, where they fall within the saving provision. Assessment proceedings are expressly mentioned in Section 174(2)(e).

7. Can an old demand be recovered?

Section 174(2)(e) specifically mentions recovery of arrears and remedies relating to preserved historical amounts.

8. What about interest on an old liability?

Section 174(2)(d) expressly refers to interest that is due or may become due.

9. What happens to an old penalty?

Specified penalties and other consequences relating to offences or violations under the amended/repealed laws are preserved by Section 174(2)(d).

10. Does an appeal survive repeal?

Yes, Section 174(2)(f) expressly deals with proceedings including appeals, reviews and references.

11. Does Section 174 revive old provisions?

No. Section 174(2)(a) says repeal does not revive anything that was not in force or existing at the time of amendment or repeal.

12. What does “previous operation” mean?

It refers to the historical operation of the amended or repealed Acts and orders, including things duly done or suffered under them.

13. Are old rights and privileges protected?

Specified rights, privileges, obligations and liabilities acquired, accrued or incurred are addressed in Section 174(2)(c), subject to its proviso.

14. What is the investment incentive proviso?

It states that a tax exemption granted as an investment incentive through notification does not continue as a privilege if the notification is rescinded on or after the appointed day.

15. Why is Section 174 important for companies?

Companies can retain legacy tax exposure long after GST begins. Proper tracking prevents old demands, appeals and audit matters from being missed.

16. Should old excise and service-tax records be retained?

Yes, relevant historical records should be retained in accordance with applicable legal, tax, litigation and document-retention requirements.

17. Can Section 174 be used without checking the old law?

It should not be treated as a substitute for the underlying legacy enactment, rules, notifications and procedural record. The exact matter should be mapped to the relevant law.

18. Is Section 174 still relevant in 2026?

Yes. India Code records Sections 165 to 174 as having commenced on 1 July 2017, and Section 174 provides the statutory saving framework for specified historical matters that can remain active after the GST transition.

17. Quick Revision – Section 174

  • Section 174 = Repeal and saving.
  • Specified legacy excise enactments are repealed from the GST commencement date, subject to the Act's terms.
  • Section 174(2)(a): no revival of something already not in force/existing.
  • Section 174(2)(b): previous operation and things duly done/suffered are protected.
  • Section 174(2)(c): specified rights, privileges, obligations and liabilities are preserved, subject to the investment-exemption proviso.
  • Section 174(2)(d): historical duty/tax, surcharge, fine, penalty, interest and specified offence consequences are preserved.
  • Section 174(2)(e): investigation, audit, assessment, adjudication, legal proceedings, recovery and remedies can continue within the saving framework.
  • Section 174(2)(f): appeals, reviews and references can continue under the legacy law.
  • Section 174(3): Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 continues to have its general application regarding repeal.
  • For historical service tax, read Section 173 and Section 174 together.
