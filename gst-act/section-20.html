GST ACT — SECTION 20

Manner of Distribution of Credit by Input Service Distributor

Section 20 is the statutory core of the Input Service Distributor (ISD) mechanism. It governs how eligible input-service credit received centrally for distinct registrations is distributed to the appropriate recipient GST registrations, including the expanded mechanism for specified reverse-charge input services.

1. What Section 20 does

Section 20 answers a practical question: when a central office receives input-service invoices for multiple GST registrations, how should eligible ITC be passed to the correct registrations?

Under GST, registrations held by the same legal entity in different States or Union territories are treated as distinct persons for specified purposes. A head office may receive software, audit, professional consultancy, advertising, insurance, common subscriptions and other centrally procured services benefiting one or several registrations. The ISD mechanism creates a controlled route for distributing the corresponding input-service credit.

Current-law point: Section 20 was substituted with effect from 1 April 2025. The current framework expressly covers specified reverse-charge input services and requires the relevant office to be registered as an ISD. citeturn0search1turn0search0

2. What is an Input Service Distributor?

An ISD is an office of the supplier of goods or services or both that receives tax invoices for input services on behalf of distinct persons and is required to distribute eligible ITC to the appropriate recipients.

Central office
Receives eligible input-service invoices.
ISD registration
Operates the prescribed credit-distribution mechanism.
Recipient GSTINs
Receive credit based on attribution and the statutory formula.
GSTR-6
Reports the ISD distribution.

Section 24(viii) is the linked registration provision, while Section 20 governs distribution. The current text expressly requires the relevant office to be registered as an ISD. citeturn0search1

4. Mandatory ISD from 1 April 2025

The GST Council had considered whether ISD should be mandatory for common input services received by a head office and attributable to branches. The 50th Council recommended making the ISD procedure mandatory prospectively; the 53rd Council agenda records the proposed Section 20 and Rule 39 framework. citeturn0search3turn0search20

Historical caution: Do not apply the post-2025 mandatory regime retrospectively to every historical transaction. The amendment became effective from 1 April 2025.

1 July 2017–31 March 2025Historical ISD framework; the exact legal position must be tested for the relevant period and facts.
From 1 April 2025Current Section 20 requires the relevant office to register as ISD and distribute covered input-service credit through the ISD mechanism.

The Council's records describe the prospective policy for third-party input services attributable to multiple or particular branches and specified reverse-charge input services. citeturn0search23

5. Reverse-charge input services

The expanded regime specifically addresses input services on which tax is payable under reverse charge. Section 20 now refers to reverse charge under Section 9(3) and 9(4) of the CGST Act and, through the 2025 amendment, specified reverse-charge services under Section 5(3) and 5(4) of the IGST Act.

Practical significance: A covered RCM input service received centrally should not be treated as outside the ISD framework merely because tax was paid under reverse charge rather than charged by an ordinary supplier.

The 55th GST Council recommended the amendment expressly including inter-State RCM references in Section 20 with effect from 1 April 2025. citeturn0search19

6. Recipient GST registrations

The recipient is the GST registration entitled to receive the distributed credit. Correct recipient identification is therefore the first allocation control.

ServiceAnalysis
Exclusively benefits Maharashtra GSTINAnalyse distribution to Maharashtra only.
Exclusively benefits Telangana GSTINAnalyse distribution to Telangana only.
Benefits several registrationsUse the prescribed common-credit allocation mechanism.
No identifiable eligible recipientDo not mechanically distribute merely because several GSTINs share a PAN.

The principle is attribution first, allocation second.

7. Attributable vs common input services

Single-recipient services

If an input service can be directly attributed to one recipient registration, the credit should be analysed for distribution to that recipient rather than spread across all registrations.

Multi-recipient services

Where the service benefits multiple registrations, the prescribed proportionate distribution mechanism applies.

Common services

Central audit, enterprise software, group-level consultancy, common advertising and certain insurance arrangements can be common services depending on the facts.

Evidence matters: Maintain the agreement, invoice, cost centre, service description, internal allocation basis and business-user mapping supporting the recipient determination.

8. Distribution mechanics

Section 20 provides the statutory framework while Rule 39 supplies the detailed operational methodology.

  1. Identify the eligible input-service invoice.
  2. Determine whether it is attributable to one recipient or multiple recipients.
  3. Determine the credit legally available for distribution.
  4. Apply the prescribed allocation formula where common.
  5. Issue the prescribed ISD document.
  6. Report through the ISD return.
  7. Reconcile the distribution with recipient GSTIN records.

The Council material records the principle that credit attributable to one recipient should go only to that recipient and common credit should be distributed pro rata using the prescribed methodology. citeturn0search3

9. CGST and IGST treatment

Section 20(3) establishes the tax-character rule for distributed credit.

Credit received by ISDCurrent statutory possibility
CGST creditCGST or IGST, as prescribed.
IGST creditIGST or CGST, as prescribed.

The actual allocation must follow current Rule 39. An ERP should therefore not hard-code a simplistic “CGST always stays CGST” rule.

10. Turnover-based allocation for common services

Where credit is attributable to more than one recipient, the prescribed Rule 39 methodology uses a turnover-based proportion in the situations specified by the Rules.

Formula concept: recipient's relevant turnover ÷ aggregate relevant turnover of the recipients × common ITC available for distribution.

GST Council drafting material expressly described pro-rata distribution based on turnover in the State or Union territory for common credit. citeturn0search7

Because Rule 39 has changed over time, historical allocations should be tested against the rule applicable to the relevant month.

11. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — software used by one GSTIN

A centrally received software licence is exclusively used by Karnataka. Analyse it as attributable to Karnataka rather than distributing it to every registration.

Example 2 — common professional service

A centrally received professional service benefits Maharashtra, Telangana and Karnataka. After eligibility is established, distribute the common credit under the applicable Rule 39 methodology.

Example 3 — national advertising

A national campaign benefits several registrations. Retain campaign documents and establish the recipient population before applying the common-credit formula.

Example 4 — RCM service from 1 April 2025

A covered centrally received input service is subject to reverse charge. The current ISD framework must be considered; RCM status does not by itself remove it from Section 20.

Example 5 — over-distribution

An ISD cannot distribute more credit than is actually available. Invoice-level reconciliation should prevent over-distribution.

Example 6 — tax-head error

Even where total distributed credit is arithmetically correct, incorrect CGST/IGST reporting can create a compliance mismatch.

Example 7 — construction group

A central office incurs common software, audit and legal-service costs for multiple project GSTINs. The ISD master should identify which registrations benefit from each service and retain the allocation working.

Example 8 — recipient changes

If the group's GST registrations or business structure changes, update the recipient master before distributing recurring monthly services.

12. GSTR-6 and reconciliation

GSTR-6 is the return associated with the ISD mechanism. The ISD should reconcile books, source invoices, distributed credit and return data before filing.

ReconciliationCheck
Supplier invoice → booksInvoice received and eligible ITC recorded.
Books → allocationRecipient and common-credit basis supported.
Allocation → ISD documentAmount and tax heads agree.
ISD document → GSTR-6Reported distribution agrees with books.
GSTR-6 → recipient recordsRecipient has accounted for the distributed credit.
Monthly close: Do not leave ISD reconciliation until the annual audit. Differences in source invoices, recipient GSTINs and tax heads become harder to reconstruct later.

16. Common mistakes

  • Continuing an old head-office invoice model after 1 April 2025 without checking the mandatory ISD framework.
  • Distributing every central invoice equally to every GSTIN.
  • Ignoring actual service attribution.
  • Using management-accounting allocation instead of the prescribed statutory method.
  • Ignoring covered RCM input services.
  • Distributing more credit than available.
  • Using the wrong tax head.
  • Failing to reconcile GSTR-6 with recipient records.
  • Applying today's Rule 39 formula to a historical period without checking the applicable rule.
  • Assuming a common PAN alone proves that every GSTIN is a recipient of the service.

18. Practical workflow

  1. Identify centrally received input services.
  2. Check ITC eligibility.
  3. Check whether Section 20 covers the service.
  4. From 1 April 2025, apply the mandatory ISD framework where applicable.
  5. Classify the invoice as recipient-specific or common.
  6. Identify recipient GSTINs.
  7. Calculate distribution under current Rule 39.
  8. Validate tax heads.
  9. Issue the prescribed ISD document.
  10. Report in GSTR-6.
  11. Reconcile recipient ITC.
  12. Archive the supporting working.
ERP design: Build a recipient master, allocation engine, tax-head validation, GSTR-6 reconciliation and recipient confirmation report. This turns ISD into a controlled GST-close process rather than a spreadsheet-only exercise.

20. Key takeaways

  • Section 20 is the core statutory provision for ISD credit distribution.
  • From 1 April 2025, the expanded ISD framework is mandatory for covered input-service credit.
  • Specified reverse-charge input services are expressly included.
  • Attribution comes before allocation.
  • Common credit must follow the prescribed Rule 39 methodology.
  • Tax-head distribution matters.
  • Historical periods must be tested under the law applicable to those periods.
  • GSTR-6 should be reconciled with source invoices and recipient ITC.
  • A strong ISD system creates a source-invoice → allocation → ISD document → GSTR-6 → recipient trail.
