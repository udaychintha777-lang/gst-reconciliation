GST ACT — SECTION 30

Revocation of Cancellation of Registration

Section 30 provides the statutory route through which a GST registration cancelled by the proper officer on his own motion can be restored. It is especially important where cancellation resulted from return defaults, registration-related compliance issues, or an officer's cancellation order that the taxpayer believes should be reversed.

1. What Section 30 does

Cancellation and revocation are two different stages: Section 29 removes the registration; Section 30 provides the route for restoring a registration cancelled by the proper officer on his own motion.

Section 30 is therefore not a general “re-registration” provision. It is a statutory remedy against an officer-initiated cancellation. The taxpayer must satisfy the conditions prescribed under the Act and Rules and must apply within the applicable time.

Core principle: first identify the cancellation order, its effective date and its legal ground. Only then decide whether Section 30/Rule 23 revocation is the correct remedy.

Section 30 was brought into force on 22 June 2017 along with Sections 22–30 of the original CGST Act. The current Act uses an enabling formulation under which the manner, time, conditions and restrictions are prescribed through the Rules. citeturn1search0turn2search0

3. When revocation is available

Section 30 applies where the registration was cancelled by the proper officer on his own motion.

This distinction is critical:

Cancellation situationSection 30 revocation?
Officer cancelled registration under Section 29(2)Yes, subject to Section 30 and Rule 23.
Taxpayer voluntarily applied for cancellation under Section 29(1)No ordinary Section 30 revocation route. The person should examine fresh registration or another appropriate legal remedy.
Cancellation challenged through appealAppeal is a separate remedy; an appellate order can also affect the registration status.
Registration never existed/was not validly grantedSection 30 is not a substitute for establishing a valid registration.
Always read the cancellation order. The legal ground and effective date determine the appropriate compliance route.

4. Voluntary cancellation cannot normally be revoked under Section 30

A common mistake is to assume that every cancelled GSTIN can be “revoked”. Section 30 specifically covers cancellation by the proper officer on his own motion.

If the taxpayer voluntarily applied for cancellation and the application was accepted, the taxpayer does not ordinarily use Section 30 to reverse that voluntary cancellation.

If business resumes or the person again becomes liable to registration, the correct route may instead involve a fresh registration application, depending on the facts and applicable provisions.

5. Current 90-day timeline

The current Rule 23 framework provides that a registered person whose registration has been cancelled by the proper officer on his own motion may apply for revocation within 90 days from the date of service of the cancellation order.

This is different from the original Section 30 position, which contained a 30-day period in the Act. From 1 October 2023, Section 30 was amended to allow the detailed time limit to be prescribed through the Rules. Rule 23 now carries the 90-day period.

Current working rule: treat 90 days from service of the cancellation order as the normal Rule 23 application window, subject to the Rules and any applicable amendment on the relevant date.

The 53rd GST Council material records the Rule 23 proposal/current framework of 90 days, with a further extension mechanism. citeturn2search1turn2search6

6. Additional 180-day extension

Where the 90-day period is missed, Rule 23 provides an additional extension mechanism. On sufficient cause being shown and for reasons to be recorded in writing, the Commissioner or an officer authorised by him, not below the rank of Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner, may extend the period by a further period of up to 180 days.

StageCurrent framework
Normal Rule 23 period90 days from service of cancellation order.
Further extensionUp to 180 additional days on sufficient cause and written reasons.
Who exercises extensionCommissioner or authorised officer meeting the prescribed rank requirement.
Do not treat 180 days as an automatic grace period. It is an extension power requiring sufficient cause and recorded reasons.

The GST Council's legislative review recommended moving from the earlier 30-day/graded-extension structure to a 90-day period with a further extension of up to 180 days, which is reflected in the current Rule 23 framework. citeturn2search2turn2search7

7. Return-default cancellations

Where cancellation occurred because the taxpayer failed to furnish returns, Rule 23 imposes an important precondition: the taxpayer cannot seek revocation until the required pending returns have been furnished and the tax due under those returns, together with applicable interest, penalty and late fee, has been paid.

This means the revocation application should not be treated as the first step. The taxpayer should first prepare a complete return and payment reconciliation.

Step 1
Identify every return due up to the cancellation/effective date.
Step 2
File the pending returns.
Step 3
Pay tax, interest, penalty and late fee where applicable.
Step 4
Prepare the Rule 23 revocation application and supporting explanation.

This return-payment condition is expressly reflected in the Rule 23 framework reproduced in GST Council and CBIC materials. citeturn2search1turn1search2

8. Retrospective cancellation

Retrospective cancellation creates an additional compliance layer because the taxpayer may have a gap between the effective cancellation date and the date on which the revocation order is eventually passed.

Rule 23 specifically addresses this situation. Where registration was cancelled retrospectively, returns relating to the period from the effective cancellation date until the revocation order are required to be furnished within the prescribed period after revocation.

Example: Registration is cancelled with effect from 1 April, but the revocation order is passed on 20 September. The taxpayer must examine the return obligations for the period beginning from 1 April and comply within the Rule 23 framework after revocation.

The GST Council's 53rd meeting material specifically explains this interaction between retrospective cancellation and post-revocation return filing. citeturn2search1turn2search6

9. Rule 23 procedure

Rule 23 supplies the operational mechanism for Section 30. The principal steps are:

  1. Cancellation must have been made by the proper officer on own motion.
  2. Check the service date and effective date of the cancellation order.
  3. Complete any return/payment conditions where cancellation arose from non-filing.
  4. Apply for revocation within the applicable Rule 23 period.
  5. Meet any prescribed authentication or procedural requirement.
  6. Officer examines the application and supporting material.
  7. If satisfied, officer passes the revocation order.
  8. If not satisfied, officer issues a notice before rejecting the application.
  9. Taxpayer responds to the notice within the prescribed period.
  10. The officer passes the final decision.

11. Opportunity of being heard

Section 30(2) contains an express natural-justice safeguard: an application for revocation cannot be rejected unless the applicant has been given an opportunity of being heard.

This means that where the officer proposes rejection, the taxpayer should receive the prescribed notice and be given the opportunity to explain why cancellation should be revoked.

Professional response: address the actual cancellation ground, not merely state that the business is still operating. A good response connects each allegation with documentary evidence and completed compliance.

12. Approval of revocation

Where the proper officer is satisfied that sufficient grounds exist for revocation, the officer passes the revocation order within the period prescribed under Rule 23.

Approval restores the registration subject to the statutory and procedural consequences of the cancellation period. It does not automatically erase tax, interest or other liabilities that arose during that period.

After receiving the revocation order, immediately reconcile the GST portal status, effective dates, pending returns, ITC eligibility and invoice sequence before resuming normal billing.

13. Rejection and reply

If the officer is not satisfied, the Rules provide for a notice before rejection. The taxpayer must respond within the prescribed period.

A strong response should normally contain:

  • chronology of the cancellation and application;
  • reason the original cancellation ground no longer applies;
  • proof of pending-return filing where relevant;
  • tax, interest, penalty and late-fee payment evidence;
  • proof of actual business activity;
  • banking, sales, purchase, e-way bill and e-invoice evidence where relevant;
  • address proof and photographs where the cancellation allegation concerns place of business;
  • explanation for any historical non-compliance; and
  • request for revocation based on compliance and documentary evidence.

14. Returns after revocation

Revocation can create a second filing phase. Rule 23 requires returns due for the period from the cancellation order until the revocation order to be furnished within the prescribed period after revocation.

Where cancellation was retrospective, the return period begins from the effective date of cancellation rather than simply the date on which the cancellation order was issued.

SituationReturn review
Cancellation effective on order dateReview returns from cancellation/order date to revocation date.
Cancellation retrospectiveReview returns from the retrospective effective date.
Return-default cancellationClear the pre-revocation return/payment condition first.

15. ITC during the cancellation period

Revocation does not mean that every invoice during the cancellation period automatically becomes eligible for ITC. The taxpayer must separately examine Section 16 and the applicable return/furnishing conditions.

The GST Council in 2024 specifically examined the problem of ITC where registration is cancelled and subsequently revoked. The recommended framework addressed invoices/debit notes relating to the period between effective cancellation and revocation, subject to the statutory time limit and filing within the prescribed post-revocation period.

Practical control: after revocation, prepare a separate ITC reconciliation for the cancellation-to-revocation period. Do not simply restore the old ITC ledger balance without checking Section 16 conditions.

The 53rd GST Council agenda records this specific issue and the proposed statutory treatment. citeturn2search1

16. Rule 10B and authentication

Rule 23 operates subject to the prescribed authentication framework, including Rule 10B where applicable.

Accordingly, the taxpayer should check the GST portal's current authentication requirement before filing the revocation application. Authentication requirements can depend on the taxpayer category and the status of the registration.

Do not treat portal authentication as a substitute for satisfying the substantive cancellation/revocation conditions. It is a procedural requirement, not proof by itself that revocation must be granted.

17. CGST–SGST/UTGST coordination

Section 30(3) provides that revocation of cancellation under the corresponding State GST Act or Union Territory GST Act is deemed to be revocation under the CGST Act.

ProvisionCoordination
Section 26Deemed registration.
Section 28(3)Amendment approval/rejection.
Section 29(4)Cancellation.
Section 30(3)Revocation of cancellation.

This is intended to keep the Central and corresponding State/UT registration positions aligned.

21. Detailed examples

Example 1 — cancellation for non-filing

A registered trader's GSTIN is cancelled because required returns were not filed. Before applying for revocation, the trader should file the required returns and pay tax, interest, penalty and late fee as applicable.

Example 2 — application within 90 days

The cancellation order is served on 10 April. The taxpayer should apply under Rule 23 within the 90-day period, subject to the specific service date and applicable Rule.

Example 3 — missed 90 days

The taxpayer misses the normal 90-day period. A further extension may be available up to 180 days if sufficient cause is shown and the competent authority exercises the prescribed power.

Example 4 — retrospective cancellation

Registration is cancelled retrospectively from 1 January but the revocation order is passed later. The taxpayer must review and furnish the returns for the period from the effective cancellation date in accordance with Rule 23.

Example 5 — voluntary cancellation

A company voluntarily surrenders its GST registration. Section 30 is not the ordinary mechanism for reversing that voluntary cancellation.

Example 6 — officer cancellation for non-existent business

The officer cancels registration because no business was found at the declared premises. The taxpayer seeking revocation should provide lease/ownership documents, utility evidence, photographs, books, invoices and other evidence showing the business exists or explaining the premises issue.

Example 7 — fake invoice allegation

Cancellation is based on an allegation of invoices without supply. A revocation application should directly address the allegation with purchase orders, dispatch documents, e-way bills, GRNs, bank payments, stock records and customer confirmations where appropriate.

Example 8 — returns between cancellation and revocation

After revocation, the taxpayer must not assume the intervening period is automatically closed. Rule 23 requires the prescribed returns for that period to be furnished within the applicable post-revocation period.

Example 9 — ITC after revocation

A construction company has valid purchase invoices during the cancellation-to-revocation period. It should separately test ITC eligibility under Section 16 and the special post-revocation filing mechanism rather than automatically claiming all historical invoices.

Example 10 — appeal instead of revocation

If the taxpayer disputes the legal correctness of the cancellation order itself, appeal may be an additional or alternative remedy. Section 30 should not be treated as the only possible legal route in every cancellation dispute.

26. Common mistakes

  • Assuming Section 30 applies to voluntary cancellation.
  • Using the old 30-day statutory period instead of the current Rule 23 90-day framework.
  • Assuming the additional 180 days is automatic.
  • Failing to clear pending returns before applying where cancellation arose from return default.
  • Ignoring the cancellation's effective date.
  • Ignoring retrospective cancellation consequences.
  • Assuming revocation automatically restores every ITC claim without Section 16 analysis.
  • Failing to respond to a REG-23 notice.
  • Not preserving evidence explaining the original cancellation ground.
  • Confusing revocation with appeal against the cancellation order.
  • Failing to file returns for the cancellation-to-revocation period after restoration.
  • Relying on old circulars without checking subsequent amendments.

28. Key takeaways

  • Section 30 is the statutory route for revoking a cancellation made by the proper officer on his own motion.
  • It is not the ordinary route for reversing a voluntary cancellation.
  • The current Rule 23 framework provides 90 days from service of the cancellation order for the revocation application.
  • A further extension of up to 180 days can be available on sufficient cause and recorded reasons through the prescribed authority.
  • Return-default cancellations require the prescribed returns and dues to be regularised before revocation can be sought.
  • Retrospective cancellation requires special attention to returns for the period beginning from the effective cancellation date.
  • An application cannot be rejected without an opportunity of being heard.
  • After revocation, the taxpayer must complete the prescribed returns for the cancellation-to-revocation period.
  • ITC for the intervening period must be separately tested under Section 16 and the applicable special rules.
  • Section 30(3) coordinates revocation between CGST and the corresponding State/UT GST law.
