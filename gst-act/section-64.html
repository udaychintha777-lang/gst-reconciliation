GST ACT — SECTION 64

Section 64 CGST Act — Summary Assessment in Special Cases

Section 64 is an exceptional GST assessment provision. It allows the proper officer, with prior permission of an Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner, to make a summary assessment where evidence shows a tax liability and delay in assessment may adversely affect the interest of revenue. This guide explains the provision in simple language, the safeguards, ASMT-16, ASMT-17, ASMT-18, DRC-07 and practical examples.

1. Why Section 64 matters

Searches such as “Section 64 CGST Act”, “GST summary assessment”, “summary assessment under GST”, “ASMT-16”, “ASMT-17”, “ASMT-18”, “GST revenue protection assessment” and “30 days withdrawal of summary assessment” relate directly to this provision.

Section 64 is not the normal method by which every GST liability is assessed. It is a special and exceptional mechanism designed for situations where the department has evidence of a tax liability and believes that waiting for the ordinary process may adversely affect government revenue.

Simple idea: If there is a real tax liability and waiting could put the revenue at risk, Section 64 allows a special assessment route—but only after the statutory conditions and prior-approval requirement are satisfied.

2. Section 64 in very simple language

Imagine the department obtains reliable evidence that a person has a significant GST liability. At the same time, there is a reason to believe that delaying the assessment could harm revenue—for example, there may be a serious risk that the amount will become difficult to recover.

In such a special situation, the proper officer can use Section 64 after obtaining prior permission from an Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner.

Evidence of tax liability + risk from delay + prior approval → Summary assessment under Section 64

The assessment is issued immediately through ASMT-16. The law then provides a specific mechanism for the taxable person to seek withdrawal of the order within 30 days.

3. What is summary assessment?

“Summary” means that the law permits the department to determine the liability without following the ordinary full assessment sequence first, because the circumstances are considered urgent from a revenue-protection perspective.

It is therefore different from routine scrutiny or a normal tax determination proceeding.

4. Three core conditions

Section 64 should be read carefully because three central conditions work together.

1. Evidence of tax liability

Evidence showing that a person has a tax liability must come to the proper officer's notice.

2. Revenue protection

The assessment must be undertaken to protect the interest of revenue.

3. Delay creates risk

The proper officer must have sufficient grounds to believe that delay may adversely affect the interest of revenue.

4. Prior approval

The proper officer needs previous permission of the Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner.

These are not merely administrative preferences. They define the special circumstances in which Section 64 can be used. The current statutory text expressly contains these requirements. citeturn0search0

5. Prior approval requirement

Before making the Section 64 assessment, the proper officer must obtain previous permission of the Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner.

This is an important safeguard because Section 64 is an exceptional power. The approving senior officer should therefore be satisfied that the statutory conditions for summary assessment exist.

Practical distinction: “The officer has evidence of tax liability” is not by itself enough. The officer must also have sufficient grounds to believe that delay may adversely affect the interest of revenue, and prior approval is required.

6. Is a prior hearing required?

Section 64 does not contain the same express pre-assessment hearing requirement found in Section 63.

The reason is the exceptional nature of the provision: the law allows immediate summary assessment where delay may adversely affect revenue.

However, the taxpayer is given an important post-assessment remedy. Under Section 64(2), the taxable person can apply for withdrawal of the summary assessment order within 30 days of receiving it. The Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner can also withdraw an erroneous order on his own motion.

Do not confuse “no express prior hearing in Section 64” with “no remedy”. Section 64(2) specifically provides a withdrawal mechanism after the order.

7. Unascertainable taxable person and goods

Section 64 contains a special rule for cases involving goods.

If the taxable person to whom the liability belongs cannot be ascertained, the person in charge of the goods can be deemed to be the taxable person liable to be assessed and liable to pay tax and other amounts due under the section.

This is an unusual but important situation. It is designed to prevent uncertainty about the person liable from making immediate revenue protection impossible.

9. ASMT-16 assessment order

Rule 100 provides that the summary assessment order under Section 64(1) is issued in FORM GST ASMT-16.

ASMT-16 is therefore the key document a taxpayer receives when a Section 64 summary assessment has been made.

On receiving ASMT-16, immediately record:

  • date of receipt/service;
  • financial/tax period involved;
  • tax assessed;
  • interest and other amounts;
  • facts stated by the department;
  • approval details where available; and
  • the 30-day deadline for withdrawal application.

Rule 100 specifically prescribes ASMT-16 for Section 64(1). citeturn0search1

10. DRC-07 demand summary

The Section 64 assessment order is accompanied by an electronic summary uploaded in FORM GST DRC-07.

Therefore, the taxpayer should compare ASMT-16 with DRC-07 and verify that the demand appearing electronically corresponds with the assessment order.

11. ASMT-17 withdrawal application

If the taxable person believes the summary assessment is erroneous, Section 64(2) allows an application for withdrawal within 30 days from the date of receipt of the order.

Rule 100 prescribes FORM GST ASMT-17 for this application.

A strong application should explain why the summary assessment is incorrect and provide supporting documents. It should not simply state that the taxpayer disagrees.

12. Important 30-day period

The 30-day period is extremely important.

The taxpayer must make the withdrawal application within 30 days from the date of receipt of the Section 64 order.

Example: If ASMT-16 is received on 10 September, the taxpayer should immediately calculate the statutory 30-day period and prepare ASMT-17 without waiting until the last day.

The current Section 64(2) expressly provides this 30-day mechanism. citeturn0search0

13. ASMT-18 withdrawal/rejection order

Rule 100 provides that the order withdrawing the summary assessment, or rejecting the withdrawal application, is issued in FORM GST ASMT-18.

FormPurpose
ASMT-16Summary assessment order under Section 64(1).
ASMT-17Application by taxable person for withdrawal.
ASMT-18Order withdrawing the assessment or rejecting the withdrawal application.

This three-form sequence makes Section 64 different from the ordinary scrutiny process under Section 61.

14. What happens after withdrawal?

Withdrawal does not mean that the tax liability automatically disappears.

Section 64(2) provides that where the Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner considers the summary assessment erroneous, the order can be withdrawn and the department can follow the ordinary determination procedure under Section 73, Section 74 or Section 74A, as applicable.

Section 64 order → withdrawal → normal determination under applicable Section 73/74/74A

This gives the department a route to determine the actual liability through the appropriate ordinary procedure rather than leaving the revenue issue unresolved. citeturn0search0

16. What evidence can trigger Section 64?

The law does not say that one particular document automatically triggers Section 64. It refers broadly to any evidence showing a tax liability coming to the notice of the proper officer.

Unaccounted goods
Goods found without satisfactory records.
Transaction evidence
Invoices, contracts or other records showing taxable supply.
Departmental information
Information lawfully obtained through GST systems or proceedings.
Third-party evidence
Information connecting a person with taxable transactions.
Stock discrepancies
Evidence of taxable goods not properly accounted for.
Other reliable records
Any relevant evidence showing a tax liability.

The key is not simply possession of information. The officer must also satisfy the revenue-protection and delay-risk conditions.

17. Meaning of “protect the interest of revenue”

This phrase is central to Section 64.

In simple language, the provision is meant for situations where waiting for the normal assessment route could create a real risk to government revenue.

Examples could include circumstances where:

  • taxable goods are found and immediate identification/payment is necessary;
  • there is a credible risk that the tax liability will become difficult to recover;
  • the relevant transaction or taxable person may otherwise become untraceable; or
  • delay would materially prejudice collection of the tax.
Section 64 should not be treated as a shortcut merely because an officer wants a faster assessment. The statutory condition concerning adverse impact on revenue must exist.

21. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Unaccounted goods

Departmental officers find goods in a vehicle or godown and the person liable to tax is not immediately ascertainable. The Section 64 goods proviso can become relevant if the statutory conditions are satisfied.

Example 2 — Evidence of large tax liability

The department obtains reliable evidence of taxable supplies and has sufficient grounds to believe that delay could prejudice revenue. After prior approval, a summary assessment may be made.

Example 3 — Prior approval missing

If the statutory prior permission of the Additional/Joint Commissioner has not been obtained, the Section 64 procedure raises a fundamental procedural issue.

Example 4 — Taxpayer disputes ASMT-16

The taxpayer receives ASMT-16 and believes the tax liability is wrongly computed. The taxpayer can apply for withdrawal within 30 days using ASMT-17.

Example 5 — Erroneous order recognised by senior officer

The Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner independently considers the order erroneous. The officer can withdraw it on own motion and the applicable normal determination procedure can follow.

Example 6 — Goods owner cannot be identified

Goods are found, but the taxable person to whom the liability belongs cannot be ascertained. The person in charge of the goods may be treated as the taxable person under the special proviso, subject to Section 64 conditions.

Example 7 — Delay not harmful to revenue

If the facts show only an ordinary tax dispute without a genuine revenue-risk reason for immediate action, Section 64 should not be treated as a routine shortcut.

Example 8 — Construction material

Taxable construction materials are found at a site without proper accounting and the underlying taxable person cannot be readily ascertained. The special goods proviso may become relevant depending on the facts.

Example 9 — Withdrawal and normal assessment

The summary assessment is withdrawn as erroneous. The department can then use the appropriate Section 73/74/74A process to determine the actual liability.

Example 10 — DRC-07 reconciliation

The taxpayer receives ASMT-16 and sees a corresponding DRC-07 demand. Both should be reconciled before payment or withdrawal proceedings are initiated.

22. Construction and infrastructure examples

Unaccounted project material

Construction projects often hold large quantities of steel, cement, machinery and other materials. If taxable goods are found without proper supporting records, the department may investigate ownership and tax liability.

Site records

Gate registers, GRNs, purchase invoices, delivery challans, e-way bills and project stock registers can be critical evidence to establish the actual position.

Subcontractor material

Where materials belong to a principal contractor but are held at a subcontractor's site, ownership and movement documents should be maintained carefully.

RA bills and services

Section 64 can also be relevant to evidence showing tax liability on services, but the special urgency/revenue-risk conditions must still be satisfied.

26. Common mistakes

  • Using Section 64 as though it were a routine assessment provision.
  • Ignoring the requirement for prior Additional/Joint Commissioner permission.
  • Failing to establish actual evidence of tax liability.
  • Ignoring the requirement that delay may adversely affect revenue.
  • Confusing ASMT-16 with ASMT-13 or ASMT-15.
  • Missing the 30-day ASMT-17 withdrawal period.
  • Assuming withdrawal automatically means no tax can ever be demanded.
  • Failing to reconcile DRC-07 with the assessment order.
  • Ignoring the special rule for unascertainable taxable persons where goods are involved.
  • Using old articles that do not mention the current Section 74A cross-reference.

28. Section 64 GST FAQs

What is Section 64 of the CGST Act?

Section 64 provides for summary assessment in special cases where evidence shows a tax liability, delay may adversely affect revenue, and prior permission of an Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner has been obtained.

What is summary assessment under GST?

It is an exceptional assessment mechanism that allows the proper officer to determine tax without first following the ordinary full assessment route when the statutory revenue-protection conditions are satisfied.

What is ASMT-16?

ASMT-16 is the summary assessment order issued under Section 64(1) and Rule 100.

What is ASMT-17?

ASMT-17 is the application made by the taxable person for withdrawal of the Section 64 summary assessment order.

What is ASMT-18?

ASMT-18 is the order withdrawing the summary assessment or rejecting the withdrawal application.

How many days are available to challenge/withdraw a Section 64 order?

Section 64(2) provides 30 days from receipt of the order for the taxable person to apply for withdrawal.

Is prior approval required for Section 64?

Yes. The proper officer must obtain previous permission from an Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner.

Does Section 64 require a prior hearing?

Section 64 does not contain the same express pre-assessment hearing requirement as Section 63. Its special mechanism instead provides a post-assessment withdrawal route under Section 64(2).

What happens after a Section 64 order is withdrawn?

The Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner may follow the procedure under Section 73, 74 or 74A, as applicable.

Can Section 64 apply to unregistered persons?

Yes. Section 64 is not limited to registered persons. Its text refers to “a person” and contains a specific goods-related rule where the taxable person cannot be ascertained.

Can Section 64 apply to goods found in a vehicle?

Potentially, particularly where the taxable person to whom the liability pertains cannot be ascertained. The statutory conditions must still be satisfied.

Is Section 64 the same as Section 62?

No. Section 62 is the best-judgment assessment mechanism for specified registered non-filers. Section 64 is an exceptional summary-assessment mechanism based on revenue-protection conditions.

Is Section 64 the same as Section 63?

No. Section 63 specifically addresses specified unregistered/cancelled-registration persons. Section 64 addresses special cases where immediate assessment is necessary to protect revenue.

30. Key takeaways

  • Section 64 is an exceptional summary-assessment provision.
  • There must be evidence showing a tax liability.
  • The assessment must be for protecting the interest of revenue.
  • The officer must have sufficient grounds to believe delay may adversely affect revenue.
  • Prior permission of an Additional Commissioner or Joint Commissioner is required.
  • ASMT-16 is the summary assessment order.
  • ASMT-17 is the taxpayer's withdrawal application.
  • The withdrawal application must be made within 30 days from receipt of the order.
  • ASMT-18 records withdrawal or rejection.
  • After withdrawal, the applicable Section 73/74/74A procedure can follow.
  • Section 64 should not be confused with Sections 61, 62 or 63.
