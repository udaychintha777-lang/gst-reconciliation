1. First response: what to do when a GST notice arrives

A GST notice should be treated as a time-bound legal and accounting task—not as an ordinary email. Identify the provision, the period, the allegation, the amount, the reply form and the deadline before deciding whether to accept, partly accept or contest the proposed liability.

First 24 hours

Download the complete notice and annexures, note the date and method of service, verify the reference number, and save a PDF copy of the portal screen.

First 2–3 working days

Assign an owner, reconcile the allegation with returns/books, identify the evidence gap, and prepare a deadline calendar. Do not wait until the last day to request records.

Before submission

Recalculate tax, interest and penalty separately; check the correct GSTIN, tax period, Act, section and reply form; cross-reference each annexure.

After submission

Save the ARN/acknowledgement, filed reply, annexures, hearing request and subsequent communications in one case folder.

Golden rule: A mismatch, a pre-notice intimation, a formal show-cause notice and a final demand order are different stages. A reply that is adequate for a data mismatch may be inadequate for a fraud allegation or a final adjudication proceeding.

4. Check authority, contents, service and document authenticity

4.1 Verify the communication

  • Confirm GSTIN/legal name, tax period, issuing office, officer designation, statutory provision and reference number.
  • For portal-generated documents, verify the Reference Number (RFN) through the GST portal verification facility where applicable.
  • For CBIC communications issued outside the GST portal, consider the applicable DIN / verifiable issue-number requirements. A portal RFN and an external communication’s identifier are not necessarily interchangeable.
  • Check whether the document was served through a method recognised by Section 169 and whether the portal shows a service date.
  • Preserve the complete PDF, email headers where relevant, portal download date and screenshots. Do not assume an email notification is the only legally relevant service event.

4.2 Test the substance of a demand notice

  • Are the facts and transactions identified clearly enough to answer?
  • Are the legal grounds, period, tax head, calculation and interest/penalty basis disclosed?
  • Are relied-upon documents and calculation sheets provided or identifiable?
  • Does the notice allege fraud/suppression and explain the factual basis, or merely use labels?
  • Is the proper officer competent for the provision and assigned jurisdiction?
  • Does the proposal stay within the allegations in the notice, and does the final order address the reply?

Do not assume a notice is invalid solely because of a formatting defect or because the taxpayer has not seen an email. Assess the applicable law, prejudice caused, service record and the facts; raise a specific procedural objection while also protecting the substantive defence.

6. Sections 73, 74 and 74A: which demand framework applies?

The legal framework changed for later tax periods. The relevant section should be determined by the period and the law in force, not merely by the date the officer sends the notice.

ProvisionGeneral frameworkDefence focus
Section 73Determination of tax not paid/short paid, erroneous refund or wrongly availed/utilised ITC for reasons other than fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression to evade tax, for periods governed by this section.Test the factual tax shortfall, eligibility, calculation, interest and penalty; explain bona fide accounting or interpretive issues with evidence.
Section 74Demand framework for periods governed by the fraud, wilful misstatement or suppression-to-evade-tax route.Challenge unsupported intent allegations with contemporaneous records; also answer the underlying tax issue. Avoid assuming that a procedural argument alone resolves the merits.
Section 74ACommon demand determination framework introduced for the prescribed tax periods beginning with FY 2024–25, with separate consequences depending on whether fraud/wilful misstatement/suppression is alleged.Check the relevant subsection, payment options, statutory time limits, penalty route and the exact notice/order language.

6.1 Why classification matters

Section selection affects the allegation, limitation, payment options and penalty exposure. For example, a difference caused by an invoice uploaded by a supplier in a later month is not automatically proof of fraud. Conversely, evidence of knowingly fabricated invoices may require a materially different defence.

6.2 Tax, interest and penalty are separate issues

Prepare a three-column calculation: tax principal, interest (with rate and period), and penalty (with the precise statutory basis). If the tax demand is disputed, explain why; if only interest or penalty is disputed, identify the exact calculation or legal condition. Do not accept the full amount simply because the portal displays a consolidated total.

6.3 Section 75 procedural safeguards

  • Check whether the order gives reasons and addresses material submissions.
  • Where a personal hearing is requested or an adverse decision is contemplated, examine the statutory hearing requirements.
  • Compare the demand confirmed in the order with the notice and the opportunity actually provided.
  • If an appellate forum holds that fraud allegations under Section 74 are not established, Section 75 contains a mechanism concerning treatment under the non-fraud route; the result depends on the facts and statutory conditions.

7. How to draft a strong GST notice reply

A good reply is an indexed defence, not a long narrative. For each allegation, show: what the department says, what the records show, the law applied, the calculation and the evidence reference.

  1. Opening: identify the notice, GSTIN, period, section, date of service and whether the reply is preliminary or complete.
  2. Brief facts: describe the business model and transaction facts relevant to the allegation.
  3. Preliminary objections: jurisdiction, limitation, service, missing relied-upon documents, vagueness or other procedural issues—only where supported.
  4. Point-by-point reply: reproduce each allegation in a short heading and answer it individually.
  5. Legal position: connect the statute/rule/circular/case principle to the actual transaction. Do not paste irrelevant legal extracts.
  6. Reconciliation: give a quantified table that ties the disputed amount to the books and GST returns.
  7. Relief requested: drop the proposal, correct the amount, provide relied-upon records, grant a hearing, or allow further evidence as appropriate.
  8. Verification and annexures: ensure statements are accurate, authorised and cross-referenced.

Illustration: GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B difference

Suppose the notice alleges ₹5,00,000 additional taxable value and ₹90,000 tax. The books show ₹5,00,000 relates to invoices reported in GSTR-1 in March but tax discharged in the next return because of a documented reporting/cut-off issue. The reply should provide invoice list, return-period reconciliation, tax payment proof, electronic liability/cash/credit ledger evidence and interest analysis. Merely saying “tax already paid” without mapping the payment to the invoices and tax period is weak.

Reply style: be respectful and factual. Avoid emotional language, accusations or unsupported statements such as “the notice is illegal” without explaining the legal defect and its consequence.

Annexure discipline

  • Use Annexure A, B, C… with a one-line description and page/row range.
  • Give each reconciliation a “source”, “difference”, “reason”, “tax impact” and “evidence” column.
  • Redact irrelevant personal data and confidential third-party information where legally appropriate; do not alter underlying evidence.
  • Keep the original files and a submitted-copy folder. Make sure every annexure referred to in the reply is actually uploaded.
  • Where the portal has file-size or file-type limits, split annexures logically and list all filenames in the reply.

9. ITC mismatch notices: supplier default, 2B, eligibility and blocked credit

9.1 Missing in GSTR-2B

First determine whether the invoice is genuinely absent, appears in a later period, is reported under a different GSTIN/invoice number, or is a credit note/debit note timing issue. Prepare a supplier-wise follow-up tracker and apply the law applicable to the tax period, including the relevant statutory conditions and time limits.

9.2 Invoice appears in 2B but ITC is disputed

GSTR-2B visibility does not by itself establish every ITC condition. Test the invoice, receipt of goods/services, business use, tax invoice validity, restrictions under Section 17(5), payment-related rules, reversals, and any applicable restrictions or time limits.

9.3 Supplier has not paid tax

Do not assume the issue is resolved by showing the invoice in 2B. Collect supplier correspondence, purchase/receipt evidence, payment proof and available compliance records. Explain the taxpayer’s own due diligence and the applicable legal position; the result may depend on the statutory period and judicial interpretation.

9.4 Numerical example

Purchase register ITC is ₹12,00,000; GSTR-2B shows ₹10,80,000. The ₹1,20,000 difference should not automatically be treated as ineligible. Classify it into (a) invoices appearing next month, (b) supplier GSTIN/invoice number errors, (c) credit notes, (d) blocked/ineligible ITC, and (e) truly missing documents. Show tax-period and invoice-level impact. If ₹70,000 appears next month and ₹20,000 is blocked credit, only the remaining ₹30,000 requires further factual/legal investigation—subject to the relevant period’s law.

10. Turnover, valuation, rate, place of supply and e-invoice issues

Financial statements vs returns

Reconcile revenue, unbilled revenue, advances, capital receipts, credit notes, sale of assets, forex differences and prior-period adjustments.

Rate/classification

Match HSN/SAC and rate to product specifications, scope of work, composite/mixed supply principles and the relevant notification.

Place of supply

Check supplier location, recipient location, nature of service/goods, project/site location and whether IGST or CGST/SGST was appropriate.

E-invoice / e-way bill

Investigate missing IRNs, cancellations, duplicate invoices, movement records and differences between transport data and returns.

Example: year-end unbilled revenue

A contractor records ₹2 crore unbilled revenue in audited accounts. That accounting entry alone does not automatically settle GST time of supply or taxability. Read the contract, milestone certification, invoice obligation, receipt of payment, completion/acceptance and applicable time-of-supply rules. Present the timeline and explain why the accounting and GST periods differ, if they legitimately do.

Example: credit note mismatch

Where a credit note is recorded in books but does not reduce output tax in the return, distinguish a financial/commercial credit note from a GST credit note and check the statutory conditions, recipient ITC adjustment and reporting period. Include invoice linkage and customer acknowledgement where relevant.

12. Personal hearing: preparation and conduct

  • Prepare a two-page synopsis: disputed issue, amount, core facts, legal position, key evidence and relief requested.
  • Bring the notice, reply, indexed annexures, reconciliation workbook, authorisation letter and a list of documents already submitted.
  • Identify who will answer technical/accounting questions and who is authorised to make submissions.
  • Do not make admissions or promises that have not been checked against the books and law.
  • If the officer raises a new allegation or relies on material not previously supplied, request a copy and reasonable opportunity to respond.
  • After the hearing, prepare an internal attendance note recording date, participants, questions raised and documents requested. Submit additional material through the permitted channel and preserve acknowledgement.
  • If an adjournment is necessary, request it in writing before the hearing, state specific reasons and suggest a practical alternative date.
Hearing record: A written reply should remain complete on its own. Do not rely on an oral explanation as a substitute for a material argument or supporting document.

13. Payment during proceedings: DRC-03, interest and closure

Payment may be appropriate where the liability is accepted, partly accepted, or payment is being made under a statutory option. Before paying, decide exactly what is being paid and why.

Payment control checklist

  • Break the amount into tax, interest and penalty; verify the tax head and period.
  • Confirm the statutory route and consequence of payment at that stage, including any time-bound reduced-penalty option available under the applicable provision.
  • Ensure DRC-03 correctly refers to the relevant notice/proceeding where the form allows it.
  • Save challan, ARN, electronic cash/credit ledger evidence and any acknowledgement/closure form.
  • If making a partial payment, expressly identify which issue and amount are accepted and which remain disputed.
  • Check whether the officer’s order or portal liability register has been updated. A payment entry alone may not prove that the whole proceeding is concluded.

Example: partly accepted demand

A notice proposes ₹10 lakh tax, ₹2 lakh interest and ₹10 lakh penalty. After reconciliation, the taxpayer accepts ₹3 lakh tax but disputes ₹7 lakh tax. The reply should explain the accepted and disputed portions, show the computation and identify the legal payment route. Do not make an unqualified statement accepting the whole notice merely because a partial payment is being made.

Section 128A relief was a specific statutory scheme for qualifying Section 73 demands for FY 2017–18, 2018–19 and 2019–20 subject to prescribed conditions and notified dates. Do not assume it is a general waiver for every GST demand or that it automatically applies to fraud-based proceedings or other financial years.

14. After the order: reasons, demand ledger and recovery

Read the complete adjudication order, not only the DRC-07 summary. Check whether each allegation, defence and document has been considered, and whether the amount confirmed is supported by a calculation.

Order review checklist

  • Does the order identify the notice and stay within the proposals raised?
  • Does it discuss the reply and evidence, rather than merely state that the reply is unsatisfactory?
  • Are tax, interest, penalty and period-wise computations arithmetically correct?
  • Are CGST/SGST/IGST heads and the correct GSTIN used?
  • Does it explain the legal basis for any rejected ITC, valuation or classification?
  • Is the date of communication recorded, and has the appeal deadline been diarised?
  • Does the electronic liability register reflect the order and any payments, rectification or stay?

Section 78 generally provides a period before recovery action following service of an order, subject to the statutory framework and exceptions. Do not rely on a general “three months” assumption if the department invokes an exception or an applicable instruction. If an appeal or stay is contemplated, act quickly and confirm the recovery position in writing.

Rectification vs appeal

Rectification is generally intended for an error apparent from the record and is not a substitute for re-arguing every disputed factual or legal issue. Appeal is the ordinary route to challenge substantive findings. The right remedy, deadline and pre-deposit depend on the order and statute.

15. Appeal strategy under Section 107

For an order appealable under Section 107, the general filing period is three months from communication of the order. The Appellate Authority may admit an appeal within a further period of up to one month where sufficient cause is shown, subject to the statute. Confirm the exact applicable provisions and portal requirements before calculating the last date.

Appeal file should normally include

  • Order-in-original and DRC-07, notice and DRC-01, taxpayer reply, hearing record and proof of service.
  • Statement of facts, grounds of appeal and prayer for relief.
  • Issue-wise tax/interest/penalty computation and supporting reconciliation.
  • Payment/pre-deposit proof and applicable appeal form/verification.
  • Request for personal hearing and stay-related submissions, where appropriate.

Grounds should be precise

Instead of writing “the order is against law and facts,” specify the error: for example, the order treated a timing difference as unreported turnover despite invoice-level proof of tax discharge in the following period; ignored an invoice-wise eligibility reconciliation; applied a provision to a period outside its scope; or confirmed an amount not proposed in the notice. Link each ground to the order paragraph and evidence.

Do not miss appeal limitation. Internal discussions, requests for reconsideration, rectification applications or meetings with the officer do not automatically extend the statutory appeal deadline.

17. Practical reply structure and sample wording

17.1 Opening paragraph

“This reply is submitted in response to Notice No. [reference] dated [date] for the period [period], issued under [provision]. The taxpayer has reviewed the allegations and enclosed an issue-wise response with supporting reconciliations and documents. The submissions are without prejudice to the taxpayer’s other rights and contentions available under law.”

17.2 Issue-wise response table

SCN allegationTaxpayer’s positionQuantificationEvidenceRelief sought
Quote the allegation and page/paragraph.State the facts and applicable legal position.Show amount proposed, amount accepted (if any), amount disputed and computation.Annexure and page/row reference.Drop/correct the proposal or provide an opportunity to address missing material.

17.3 Request for relied-upon documents

“To enable an effective response to the allegation at paragraph [x], kindly provide access to the relied-upon documents/data and the complete computation referred to in the notice, if not already furnished. The taxpayer requests reasonable time after receipt of the material to submit a supplementary response, without prejudice to the objections and submissions already made.”

17.4 Request for personal hearing

“The taxpayer requests an opportunity of personal hearing before any adverse order is passed, in accordance with the applicable statutory provisions. The authorised representative details are enclosed. The taxpayer also requests that any additional material proposed to be relied upon be provided with sufficient opportunity to respond.”

These are drafting starting points only. Replace placeholders, verify the legal provision and tailor the wording to the notice. Do not copy a sample as a substitute for factual analysis.

18. Common mistakes that weaken GST replies

  • Replying only to the portal summary and ignoring the detailed SCN or annexures.
  • Using one generic reply for several unrelated allegations.
  • Giving a total reconciliation without invoice-level supporting data.
  • Mixing tax, interest and penalty into one unexplained figure.
  • Assuming DRC-01A is always the final demand, or assuming payment of one amount automatically closes every issue.
  • Ignoring the difference between Section 73/74 proceedings for earlier periods and the Section 74A framework applicable to later periods.
  • Claiming that fraud is absent without explaining the transaction facts and evidence.
  • Failing to request relied-upon documents or hearing where genuinely needed.
  • Uploading corrupted, unreadable or unindexed files, or referring to annexures not actually attached.
  • Waiting for an officer’s informal response and missing the statutory appeal deadline.
  • Paying under the wrong tax head or failing to link payment to the relevant proceeding.
  • Making inaccurate admissions, altering source documents or submitting unsupported legal citations.

19. End-to-end GST notice response checklist

  • Download notice, annexures and portal status.
  • Verify GSTIN, tax period, section, issuing authority and RFN/DIN/issue identifier as applicable.
  • Record service date, reply deadline, hearing date and appeal deadline.
  • Classify the communication: scrutiny, pre-SCN intimation, SCN, refund, registration, detention, order or recovery.
  • Assign internal owner and reviewer.
  • Build a legal issue list and a separate factual issue list.
  • Reconcile proposed amount with books, returns and ledgers.
  • Calculate tax, interest and penalty separately.
  • Gather invoice-level and transaction-level evidence.
  • Request relied-upon documents or extension promptly if required.
  • Prepare point-by-point reply with annexure index.
  • Obtain authorised signatory approval and file through the prescribed channel.
  • Save acknowledgement/ARN and exact copy of submission.
  • Prepare hearing synopsis and attend or seek adjournment in writing.
  • Read the final order and verify whether it addresses the reply.
  • Decide rectification, appeal, payment or other remedy before the deadline.
  • Update liability register and case tracker after closure.

20. Frequently asked questions

Is DRC-01 itself the complete show-cause notice?

DRC-01 is the prescribed electronic summary associated with the show-cause notice. Read the detailed notice, grounds, calculations and relied-upon material rather than relying on the summary alone.

Do I have to pay after receiving DRC-01A?

DRC-01A is a pre-notice intimation of ascertained liability. Review the computation and legal basis; the response may involve payment, a submission or partial payment depending on the facts and applicable procedure. Do not treat the intimation as an automatically confirmed final demand.

Can I submit additional documents after filing DRC-06?

It depends on the proceeding, stage, officer’s directions and applicable procedure. Submit a clear supplementary representation through the permitted channel, explain why the material is relevant and retain acknowledgement. Do not assume late documents will automatically be accepted.

Can a demand be confirmed without a personal hearing?

The statutory hearing requirement depends on the nature of the adverse decision and the applicable provision. Where a hearing is legally required or has been requested in circumstances covered by law, preserve the request and challenge a material denial through the proper remedy.

What if the notice calculation is wrong but some tax is payable?

Quantify the correct amount, explain each adjustment and distinguish accepted from disputed portions. Consider payment of the accepted part only through a legally appropriate route after evaluating its consequences.

Does filing an appeal automatically stop recovery?

Not in every situation. Statutory pre-deposit, stay rules, recovery provisions and the facts matter. Check the applicable law and the portal liability status immediately after filing.

Should every notice be answered by a CA or lawyer?

Routine data mismatches may be handled by a competent tax team, but substantial demands, fraud allegations, summons, detention, large ITC disputes or imminent recovery generally warrant experienced professional review.

What is the difference between rectification and appeal?

Rectification generally addresses an error apparent from the record within the applicable statutory framework; appeal challenges findings or legal conclusions. One does not automatically replace or extend the deadline for the other.
