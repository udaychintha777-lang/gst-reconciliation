1. Why Section 50 matters

Searches such as “Section 50 CGST Act”, “GST interest calculation”, “GSTR-3B interest”, “18% GST interest”, “interest on delayed GST payment”, “interest on wrong ITC” and “Rule 88B” all lead to the same practical question: when do I have to pay interest to the GST department, and on what amount?

Section 50 answers that question. It deals mainly with two situations: tax paid late and input tax credit that was wrongly availed and utilised.

Simple idea: Interest is compensation for keeping Government tax money unpaid for a period, or for wrongly using tax credit in the circumstances covered by Section 50(3).

2. Section 50 in very simple language

Imagine your GST payment is like paying a bill.

🧾 You owe GST

Suppose your return says you have ₹1,00,000 of output tax to pay.

💳 You have ITC

You have ₹80,000 eligible ITC. That can reduce the amount that needs to be paid in cash, subject to the utilisation rules.

💰 Cash balance

You finally need ₹20,000 from cash. If that tax is paid after the due date, Section 50(1) interest can arise on the applicable unpaid/cash portion.

⚠️ Wrong ITC

If you take ITC that you were not entitled to and actually use it, Section 50(3) can create interest on that wrongly availed and utilised amount.

So, Section 50 is not simply “pay 18% whenever a return is late”. The amount on which interest is calculated depends on why the interest arose and the applicable rules.

3. What is GST interest?

Interest under Section 50 is a statutory amount payable when tax remains unpaid beyond the prescribed time, or when ITC has been wrongly availed and utilised in the circumstances covered by Section 50(3).

It is different from a penalty. Interest is generally linked to the period and amount of financial default; penalty is a separate consequence under other provisions.

5. Section 50(1): delayed payment of tax

In simple terms, if GST that should have reached the Government by a particular date is actually paid later, interest can arise for the period of delay.

The statutory maximum rate is 18%, and Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax notified 18% per annum for Section 50(1). citeturn0search0turn2search1

The basic formula is:

Interest = Applicable tax amount × 18% × Number of days of delay ÷ 365

But the important question is: what tax amount? For a delayed GSTR-3B return covered by the proviso to Section 50(1), interest is generally on the portion of tax paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger, subject to the statutory exception where proceedings under Section 73 or 74 have commenced.

6. GSTR-3B and net cash liability

This is one of the most important practical concepts in Section 50.

Suppose:

ParticularAmount
Total GST payable in GSTR-3B₹5,00,000
Eligible ITC used₹4,00,000
Tax paid through cash₹1,00,000

If the return is filed late and the Section 50(1) proviso applies, the starting point for interest is generally the ₹1,00,000 cash-paid portion, not automatically the full ₹5,00,000.

Layman explanation: If ₹4 lakh was already covered by valid ITC and only ₹1 lakh actually needed to come from cash, the delayed cash portion is the amount that matters under the specific proviso.

The GST Council has reproduced this statutory proviso in its official 53rd-meeting material. citeturn2search9

7. Important exception: proceedings

The net-cash proviso contains an important exception. It applies to tax payable in respect of supplies declared in a return furnished after the due date, except where the return is furnished after commencement of proceedings under Section 73 or Section 74 for that period.

Therefore, a professional interest calculation should not blindly apply the net-cash approach without checking whether demand proceedings have already commenced.

8. From which day is interest calculated?

Section 50(2) says interest under Section 50(1) is calculated from the day immediately after the tax was due, in the prescribed manner.

Example: if the tax was due on 20 September and was paid on 25 September, the interest period normally begins on 21 September and runs according to the applicable calculation rules until payment.

10. Rule 88B: how the calculation works

Section 50 gives the legal principle. Rule 88B gives the practical calculation method.

CaseRule 88B approach
Delayed return/tax under Section 50(1) provisoInterest is calculated on the amount of tax that remains unpaid in the manner prescribed, including the relevant cash-ledger portion.
Other delayed tax-payment casesInterest is calculated on the tax remaining unpaid from the due date until payment.
Wrongly availed and utilised ITCInterest is calculated on the wrongly availed and utilised amount from the prescribed utilisation date until reversal/payment.

Rule 88B also provides the important test for when wrongly availed ITC is considered to have been utilised. citeturn0search19

11. Gross liability vs cash liability

Never calculate GSTR-3B interest merely by taking the total output tax.

First identify:

  1. tax payable;
  2. eligible ITC actually available and utilised;
  3. cash portion;
  4. due date;
  5. actual filing/payment date; and
  6. whether Section 73/74 proceedings had commenced.

This is why a good GST reconciliation system should keep tax liability, ITC utilisation and cash payment as separate fields.

12. Section 50(3): wrong ITC

Section 50(3) deals with interest where ITC has been wrongly availed and utilised.

The phrase “and utilised” is critical. A mere wrong entry or wrong availment does not automatically mean the credit has been utilised on the same day for Section 50(3) purposes.

The current statutory wording provides for interest at a rate not exceeding 24%, as notified by the Government. citeturn0search1

13. Wrongly availed vs wrongly utilised

SituationSimple explanation
Wrong ITC appears in credit ledger but is not utilisedDo not automatically calculate Section 50(3) interest without applying Rule 88B's utilisation test.
Wrong ITC is availed and then actually usedSection 50(3) interest can arise from the prescribed utilisation date.
Correct ITC later reversed voluntarilyCheck whether the original availment was actually wrong and whether it was utilised.
Important: “Wrongly availed” and “wrongly availed and utilised” are not automatically the same thing for interest calculation.

14. Date of ITC utilisation

Rule 88B contains a specific test. Broadly, wrongly availed ITC is treated as utilised when the balance in the electronic credit ledger falls below the amount of wrongly availed ITC.

The rule then determines the date of utilisation differently depending on whether the fall in the credit balance occurs because of payment through the return or through another debit in the electronic credit ledger. citeturn0search19

This is why an interest working for wrong ITC should not simply use the invoice date or the date on which the ITC was uploaded.

15. 24% interest

The 24% rate is associated with Section 50(3), not ordinary delayed payment of GST.

So:

Tax paid late
Generally 18% under Section 50(1).
Wrong ITC availed and utilised
24% under Section 50(3).

Finance Act 2022 amended the wording of Section 50(3) retrospectively from 1 July 2017 to focus the provision on ITC wrongly availed and utilised. This amendment is particularly important when analysing historical demands. citeturn1search1

16. ITC reversal and interest

ITC reversal does not have one universal interest answer. The finance team must first identify why the ITC is being reversed.

ReasonInterest question
Wrongly availed ITCCheck Section 50(3) and Rule 88B.
Rule 37/180-day payment reversalCheck the specific Rule 37 interest framework and period.
Rule 42/43 exempt/non-business reversalApply the relevant annual/monthly reversal and interest rules where applicable.
Blocked credit under Section 17(5)Check when the credit was availed/utilised and the applicable demand/interest provisions.

17. Section 16 interaction

Section 16 tells you whether ITC can be claimed. Section 50 tells you about interest consequences in specified cases where tax remains unpaid or ITC is wrongly availed and utilised.

Therefore, a proper ITC review should happen before GSTR-3B filing. If ineligible ITC is removed before utilisation, the interest analysis can be different from a situation where it was actually used to reduce the tax payable.

18. Reverse charge

Reverse charge is a common source of confusion. The tax under reverse charge is payable by the recipient. Section 49 payment rules require RCM liabilities to be paid through the appropriate cash mechanism.

If the recipient pays RCM late, interest under Section 50(1) can arise because the tax itself remained unpaid.

Simple rule: First pay the RCM tax as required. Only after the legal conditions are satisfied can the corresponding ITC become available.

19. Interest vs late fee

A late GSTR-3B can therefore have both interest and late fee, because they arise under different sections.

20. Interest vs penalty

Interest is not automatically a penalty. Interest generally compensates for delayed payment or specified wrongful credit utilisation. Penalty is imposed under separate provisions when their statutory conditions are satisfied.

21. Interest and demand proceedings

Interest can be payable voluntarily without waiting for a separate demand in cases where the taxpayer knows the tax was paid late. However, if the department initiates proceedings, the interest liability may form part of the demand and the calculation must follow the applicable statutory provisions.

Section 50 should therefore be read with Sections 73, 74 and, for newer periods, Section 74A where applicable.

22. Voluntary payment

Section 50 uses the words “pay, on his own” for interest on delayed tax. In practical terms, the taxpayer should calculate and discharge applicable interest rather than waiting for the department to first send a notice.

For a delayed return, reconcile the tax, interest and late fee before filing. Save the calculation and payment evidence.

24. Easy numerical examples

Example 1 — ₹1 lakh tax paid 10 days late

If ₹1,00,000 is the relevant amount on which Section 50(1) interest applies, at 18% per annum for 10 days: ₹1,00,000 × 18% × 10 ÷ 365 = approximately ₹493.

Example 2 — GSTR-3B has ITC

Tax payable ₹5,00,000; eligible ITC utilised ₹4,00,000; cash payment ₹1,00,000. For a delayed return to which the Section 50(1) proviso applies, interest is generally worked on the ₹1,00,000 cash-paid portion rather than the entire ₹5,00,000.

Example 3 — wrong ITC ₹50,000

If ₹50,000 ITC was wrongly availed and utilised for 20 days, Section 50(3) at 24% gives: ₹50,000 × 24% × 20 ÷ 365 = approximately ₹658.

Example 4 — wrong ITC not utilised

Wrong ITC of ₹50,000 remains in the credit ledger and the balance never falls below the wrongly availed amount. Apply Rule 88B's utilisation test before calculating Section 50(3) interest.

Example 5 — RCM paid late

RCM tax of ₹20,000 is paid 15 days late. Interest is calculated on the applicable unpaid tax for the relevant delay period.

Example 6 — late fee but no tax interest

A nil return is filed late. Late fee may arise, but there is no delayed tax payment on which Section 50(1) interest would arise merely because the return was late.

Example 7 — interest plus late fee

Tax of ₹1 lakh is paid late and the GSTR-3B is also filed late. Interest and late fee are separate liabilities and both must be checked.

Example 8 — tax fully paid in cash

If ₹3 lakh tax was due and the entire amount was paid after the due date, the relevant unpaid tax amount is the starting point for Section 50(1) calculation.

Example 9 — construction company

A contractor delays GSTR-3B because project-level data arrived late. The tax team should separate ITC, cash tax, RCM, interest and late fee rather than treating the entire return liability as one figure.

Example 10 — demand proceedings

If proceedings under Section 73/74 have already commenced for the period, do not automatically apply the normal delayed-return net-cash proviso without checking the statutory exception.

Construction and infrastructure businesses can have large monthly tax liabilities and ITC balances. Section 50 becomes important when site data, subcontractor invoices or RCM information arrives after the return cut-off.

26. Accounting treatment

For management accounts, interest under Section 50 should be separately identifiable from GST tax and late fee.

A useful monthly reconciliation is:

Tax payable → ITC utilised → Cash tax paid → Interest → Late fee → Closing liability

Keep the calculation working, return acknowledgement, challan and payment reference together. This makes future audit or departmental verification much easier.

29. GST Council development

The GST Council has repeatedly considered interest because the practical question is not simply whether tax was paid late, but which amount remained unpaid and when.

The statutory net-cash proviso for delayed returns and the later amendments to Section 50(3) reflect this distinction. The 53rd GST Council material reproduces the current Section 50(1) proviso, while the 2022 legislative change refined Section 50(3) to focus on wrongful availment and utilisation of ITC. citeturn2search9turn1search1

32. Common mistakes

  • Applying 18% interest automatically to the entire GSTR-3B tax liability.
  • Ignoring the Section 50(1) cash-paid portion proviso.
  • Confusing wrong availment with wrong availment and utilisation of ITC.
  • Applying 24% interest to every ITC reversal.
  • Using invoice date as the automatic start date for Section 50(3) interest.
  • Ignoring Rule 88B.
  • Confusing interest with Section 47 late fee.
  • Using ITC to pay interest.
  • Ignoring RCM interest where RCM tax was paid late.
  • Not checking whether Section 73/74 proceedings had commenced.
  • Not retaining the interest calculation working.

33. Section 50 GST FAQs

What is Section 50 of the CGST Act?

Section 50 deals with interest on delayed payment of GST and interest on ITC that has been wrongly availed and utilised in the circumstances covered by Section 50(3).

What is the GST interest rate?

For delayed payment of tax under Section 50(1), the notified rate is 18% per annum. For wrongly availed and utilised ITC under Section 50(3), the notified rate is 24% per annum.

Is GST interest charged on the entire GSTR-3B liability?

Not automatically. For a delayed return covered by the Section 50(1) proviso, interest is generally on the portion of tax paid by debiting the electronic cash ledger, subject to the statutory exception relating to proceedings under Sections 73/74.

From which date is GST interest calculated?

Section 50(2) provides that interest is calculated from the day succeeding the day on which the tax was due, in the prescribed manner.

Is interest calculated at 18% per month?

No. The notified rate is 18% per annum. The daily amount is calculated using the applicable annual rate and number of days.

When does 24% GST interest apply?

It applies to interest under Section 50(3) for ITC that has been wrongly availed and utilised, at the notified rate of 24%.

Is wrong ITC automatically subject to 24% interest?

No. Section 50(3) concerns ITC wrongly availed and utilised. Rule 88B provides the utilisation test.

Can ITC be used to pay GST interest?

No. Interest is generally paid through the electronic cash ledger.

Can interest and late fee both apply?

Yes. Interest under Section 50 and late fee under Section 47 arise from different triggers and can both apply to a delayed return/payment.

Does reverse charge attract interest if paid late?

Yes. If RCM tax remains unpaid after its due date, Section 50(1) interest can arise for the applicable period.

What is Rule 88B?

Rule 88B prescribes how interest under Section 50 is calculated, including the cash-paid portion for delayed returns and the utilisation test for wrongly availed ITC.

What is the difference between Section 50(1) and 50(3)?

Section 50(1) primarily deals with delayed payment of tax. Section 50(3) deals with interest on ITC wrongly availed and utilised.

Can the GST Portal calculate interest?

The portal may calculate/display applicable interest, but a taxpayer should maintain an independent working, particularly for historical periods, wrong ITC and demand cases.

Does a late nil return automatically create Section 50 interest?

No. If there is no tax remaining unpaid, Section 50 interest on delayed tax payment does not arise merely because the return was filed late. Late fee may still apply under Section 47.

Is GST interest a penalty?

No. Interest and penalty are separate statutory consequences.

35. Key takeaways

  • Section 50 deals with GST interest, not late fee.
  • Delayed tax payment is generally subject to 18% annual interest.
  • Wrongly availed and utilised ITC is generally subject to 24% annual interest.
  • For a delayed GSTR-3B covered by the Section 50(1) proviso, interest is generally based on the cash-paid portion rather than automatically the entire tax liability.
  • Section 50(1) contains an important exception where proceedings under Sections 73/74 have commenced.
  • Section 50(3) requires both wrongful availment and utilisation; Rule 88B determines the utilisation date.
  • Interest starts from the day after the tax was due, subject to the prescribed calculation rules.
  • Interest, late fee and penalty are different liabilities.
  • RCM paid late can create interest even though the taxpayer may subsequently receive ITC.
  • A proper GST reconciliation should separately track tax, ITC, cash payment, interest and late fee.
