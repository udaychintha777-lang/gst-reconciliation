Why Rule 3 is important for a business

Rule 3 of the CGST Rules, 2017 explains how a person communicates the option to pay tax under the composition levy framework of Section 10 of the CGST Act.

In simple words: Section 10 provides the composition levy framework. Rule 3 tells you the prescribed intimation / filing route for choosing that option in different situations, and it also connects the option with important stock and input-tax-credit related compliance.

This Rule is therefore important for small traders, manufacturers, restaurants and other eligible businesses considering composition. It is also important for accountants because choosing composition changes how the business handles invoices, tax collection, input tax credit, returns and books.

Rule 3 — statutory text

3. Intimation for composition levy.—

(1) Any person who has been granted registration on a provisional basis under the applicable registration provision and who opts to pay tax under section 10 shall electronically file an intimation in FORM GST CMP-01, duly signed or verified through electronic verification code, on the common portal, within the prescribed time.

Where the intimation in FORM GST CMP-01 is filed after the appointed day, the registered person shall not collect any tax from the appointed day and shall issue a bill of supply for supplies made after that day.

(2) A person applying for registration may give the option to pay tax under section 10 in the prescribed part of FORM GST REG-01, which is treated as an intimation to pay tax under section 10.

(3) A registered person who opts to pay tax under section 10 shall electronically file an intimation in FORM GST CMP-02 before the commencement of the financial year for which the option is exercised and shall furnish the applicable FORM GST ITC-03 statement within the prescribed period.

(4) A person filing the specified intimation shall furnish the prescribed details of stock, including relevant inward supplies from unregistered persons, held immediately before the date from which composition is opted, in FORM GST CMP-03 within the prescribed period, subject to the applicable Rule and extensions.

(5) An intimation under the specified sub-rules in respect of a place of business in any State or Union territory is treated as an intimation in respect of the other places of business registered on the same PAN, subject to the applicable composition provisions.

Historical-period caution: Rule 3 has been amended several times. The exact form, deadline and wording applicable to a transaction must be checked against the version of the Rules applicable to that period. Do not use an old 2017/2018 checklist for a current filing without verification.

Rule 3 explained in practical language

Rule 3 is easier to understand if you treat it as a composition entry procedure. The taxpayer first determines whether composition is legally available under Section 10 and the connected provisions. If eligible, the taxpayer then communicates the option through the prescribed GST procedure.

Sub-rule 1 — FORM GST CMP-01: understand the practical effect

The first sub-rule deals with a specified category of persons who have registration on a provisional basis and choose composition. The prescribed electronic intimation is FORM GST CMP-01.

The important practical point is the consequence of a late intimation. The Rule makes it clear that where the intimation is filed after the relevant appointed day, the taxpayer cannot simply continue collecting GST as if the composition option had not been exercised. The taxpayer has to follow the composition invoicing treatment from the applicable date.

Accounting control: When composition status begins, immediately communicate the effective date to billing, sales, purchase, accounts receivable, accounts payable and ERP teams. A GST status change should never remain only with the tax consultant.

Sub-rule 2 — composition option through FORM GST REG-01

Where a person is applying for GST registration, the Rule permits the composition option to be communicated through the prescribed part of FORM GST REG-01. That option is treated as an intimation for composition under Section 10.

This is practically useful because a new business does not necessarily have to complete an entirely separate composition-intimation exercise when the registration application itself provides the prescribed mechanism.

Important: Selecting composition in a registration application does not remove the need to satisfy the substantive eligibility conditions. The form is an intimation mechanism; it is not a waiver of Section 10 conditions.

Sub-rule 3 — existing registered person choosing composition

This is one of the most important situations for an established business. A registered taxpayer who wants to move into composition for a financial year has to furnish the prescribed FORM GST CMP-02 before the commencement of that financial year, subject to the applicable Rule and amendments.

The Rule also connects the composition option with FORM GST ITC-03. This is commercially important because a business moving from the normal GST regime into composition must deal with the input-tax-credit consequences prescribed by the law.

Practical example

Suppose an existing trader is registered under regular GST and wants to opt for composition from the beginning of a financial year. The finance team should not merely file CMP-02. It should first calculate the relevant ITC position, identify stock and other affected balances, determine the ITC reversal/payment consequences under the applicable provisions, and then complete the prescribed follow-up filing.

Sub-rule 4 — stock details and FORM GST CMP-03

Sub-rule 4 requires prescribed stock details in the specified situation. This is not a routine formality. The stock position around the date of transition to composition can affect the taxpayer's GST compliance and the treatment of goods held in stock.

The finance team should prepare a reconciliation of:

  • Opening stock quantity and value;
  • Purchase invoices supporting the stock;
  • GST charged on relevant purchases;
  • Goods received from registered and unregistered suppliers where relevant;
  • Stock physically available on the relevant date;
  • Stock appearing in the books and GST records; and
  • Any differences requiring explanation.
Best practice: Keep the stock working paper, purchase register and physical-stock reconciliation together with the composition records. This can be valuable during a future GST audit.

Sub-rule 5 — the PAN-level impact

One of the most important practical points in Rule 3 is sub-rule (5). An intimation for composition in respect of a place of business in one State or Union territory is treated as an intimation in respect of the other places of business registered on the same PAN, subject to the applicable law.

This means a business with multiple GST registrations must not treat composition as a completely isolated GSTIN-level decision.

Example — multiple States

A company has GST registrations in Telangana, Karnataka and Maharashtra under the same PAN. The finance team should not decide that the Telangana registration will be treated as composition while ignoring the legal effect on the other registrations. The PAN-level consequence has to be examined before the option is exercised.

Management control: Before filing a composition option, prepare a PAN-wide GSTIN matrix showing every registration, State/UT, nature of business, turnover, supply profile and proposed tax status.

How to decide whether composition is suitable

Rule 3 should never be approached as merely a form-filing exercise. Before choosing composition, management should perform a financial and operational comparison.

Real business examples

Example 1 — Small local trader

A trader mainly sells to consumers, has relatively low input GST and satisfies the applicable composition conditions. Composition may be commercially attractive, but the trader should still prepare a comparison of regular GST versus composition before filing.

Example 2 — B2B supplier with high-input purchases

A manufacturer sells mainly to GST-registered customers and incurs significant GST on raw materials. Even if eligible, management should calculate the effect of the composition restrictions on ITC and customer pricing before deciding.

Example 3 — Multiple State registrations

A business operates in three States under one PAN. The accounts team proposes composition for only one registration. Rule 3(5) requires the team to stop and examine the PAN-wide consequence before filing.

Example 4 — Composition chosen but ERP not changed

A taxpayer opts for composition from the beginning of the financial year but the billing software continues generating normal tax invoices and charging GST. This creates an avoidable compliance risk. The tax status, invoice format and accounting configuration should be changed from the applicable effective date.

Example 5 — Stock records do not match books

A taxpayer has ₹40 lakh of stock in the books but the physical count around the composition transition date is materially different. The difference should be investigated and documented before filing the required stock information.

Common mistakes under Rule 3

  • Checking only turnover: composition eligibility involves more than a turnover comparison.
  • Filing the form without checking the effective date: the tax treatment of supplies must align with the applicable date.
  • Ignoring ITC consequences: the transition into composition can require specific ITC-related compliance.
  • Not preparing stock records: transition-period stock information can become important during audit.
  • Considering only one GSTIN: the PAN-level effect under Rule 3(5) must be examined.
  • Continuing to charge GST after composition begins: billing must be aligned with the composition status.
  • Using outdated form instructions: verify the current Rule and portal process applicable to the year.
  • Failing to obtain management approval: composition should be a documented business decision, not merely an accountant's filing decision.

Audit and GST notice relevance

Rule 3 can become important during a GST audit or departmental notice where the department questions whether the taxpayer validly opted for composition or complied with the conditions associated with the option.

A strong audit file should establish the following chain:

  1. Eligibility under Section 10 and the applicable provisions.
  2. All GST registrations under the PAN.
  3. Composition intimation and acknowledgement.
  4. Effective date of the composition status.
  5. ITC transition/reversal working and applicable ITC-03 compliance.
  6. Stock statement and supporting reconciliation where applicable.
  7. Invoices/bills of supply generated after the effective date.
  8. Returns and tax payments consistent with the composition status.
For a departmental reply: do not simply attach the composition form. Explain the legal eligibility, filing date, effective date, PAN-wide position, stock/ITC compliance and subsequent return treatment in a chronological manner.

Frequently asked questions about CGST Rule 3

What is Rule 3 of the CGST Rules?

Rule 3 deals with the intimation for composition levy. It prescribes how eligible persons communicate their option to pay tax under Section 10 through the applicable GST forms and procedures.

What is GST CMP-02?

FORM GST CMP-02 is the prescribed intimation used by an existing registered person opting to pay tax under the composition levy, subject to the applicable Rule and filing requirements.

What is GST CMP-01?

FORM GST CMP-01 is the composition-related intimation prescribed for the specified category of persons covered by Rule 3(1).

What is GST CMP-03?

FORM GST CMP-03 is used for furnishing the prescribed stock details in the situation covered by Rule 3(4).

What is GST ITC-03 in composition?

FORM GST ITC-03 is connected with the input-tax-credit consequences when a registered person opts for composition in the circumstances covered by Rule 3. The applicable time limit and calculation should be verified for the relevant period.

Does Rule 3 itself make a business eligible for composition?

No. Rule 3 provides the procedural mechanism. Eligibility must be established under Section 10 and the applicable conditions and restrictions.

Does composition apply only to one GST registration?

Rule 3(5) creates a PAN-level consequence for the specified composition intimation. A business with multiple GST registrations should therefore evaluate the effect across all registrations under the same PAN before filing.

Can a business continue charging GST after opting for composition?

The composition framework contains restrictions on collection of tax. Once the composition status becomes applicable, invoicing and billing must be aligned with the applicable composition provisions.

Practical management checklist before filing

  1. Confirm Section 10 eligibility.
  2. Prepare a PAN-wise list of all GST registrations.
  3. Calculate current and projected turnover.
  4. Review customer profile and pricing impact.
  5. Calculate ITC transition consequences.
  6. Prepare stock reconciliation where applicable.
  7. Verify the current prescribed form and filing deadline.
  8. Document the intended effective date.
  9. Update ERP, invoice templates and tax codes.
  10. Keep acknowledgement and supporting workings in the permanent GST file.

Practical takeaway

Remember Rule 3 like this: Section 10 provides the composition option; Rule 3 provides the prescribed intimation route. The decision should be made only after checking eligibility, PAN-wide impact, ITC consequences, stock, effective date and the commercial effect on customers.

For a business, the safest approach is to treat composition as a tax and commercial decision, followed by a controlled compliance implementation. Filing the form is only one part of the process.
