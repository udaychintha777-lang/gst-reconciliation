1. What is BOCW welfare cess?

The Building and Other Construction Workers’ Welfare Cess is commonly called BOCW cess or “labour cess”. It is a statutory levy intended to fund welfare and social-security measures for building and other construction workers. It is not the same as GST, GST TDS, income-tax TDS, or a deduction for wages.

In a construction contract, the owner or public authority may deduct the cess from the contractor’s running account (RA) bill and deposit it with the relevant Welfare Board. In other situations, the employer may be required to self-assess and pay the cess. The fact that the amount is deducted from a contractor’s bill does not, by itself, settle who is the statutory employer or how the contract allocates the economic burden.

Keep three questions separate: (1) Who is liable under the labour/social-security law? (2) Who bears the cost under the contract? (3) What is the GST taxable value of the contractor’s supply? These questions are related but are not interchangeable.

2. Current legal framework: important 2025–2026 transition

The older framework was the Building and Other Construction Workers’ Welfare Cess Act, 1996 and the BOCW Welfare Cess Rules, 1998. The legal framework changed with the commencement of the Code on Social Security, 2020.

Period / instrumentPosition to consider
Up to 20 November 2025The earlier BOCW Act, 1996 and Cess Act, 1996 framework applied, subject to the commencement and State implementation details then in force.
From 21 November 2025The Code on Social Security, 2020 came into force for the relevant provisions, including Chapter VIII dealing with social security and cess for building and other construction workers. The levy provision is Section 100.
From 9 May 2026Notification S.O. 2322(E), dated 8 May 2026 and published in the Gazette, specifies the cess rate at 1% of the cost of construction incurred by an employer. It supersedes the earlier 1996 rate notification.
From 8 May 2026Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026 were notified and came into force on publication. They supersede the 1998 Central Cess Rules, except in respect of things already done or omitted before supersession. State-level implementation and designated authorities should still be checked for the relevant project.
Do not blindly copy an old BOCW checklist. For a transaction or project after the labour-code transition, cite Section 100 of the Code on Social Security and the 2026 rate notification/rules where applicable. For earlier periods, preserve the then-applicable law and transitional records. Verify State rules, notifications, portal instructions and the project’s jurisdiction before filing or paying.

What Section 100 broadly provides

  • Cess is levied for social security and welfare of building workers at a rate notified by the Central Government within the statutory range.
  • The cost-of-construction definition excludes the cost of land and compensation paid/payable to an employee or their kin under the relevant chapter.
  • Collection can include deduction at source for Government or public-sector construction works, and advance collection through local authorities where approval is required.
  • The cess is subject to assessment and the collection/adjustment mechanism prescribed under the Code and rules.

The current notified rate is 1%. This is not a GST rate and should not be entered as output GST or input tax credit.

3. When does BOCW cess apply?

The analysis begins with the nature of the work and the statutory definition of building or other construction work, the establishment/employer, worker engagement and any applicable exclusion. The concept is broader than construction of a residential building: it can cover roads, bridges, irrigation works, pipelines, buildings and other qualifying construction activities.

Irrigation, canal, pipeline and water infrastructureCheck whether the activity is construction/alteration/repair/maintenance covered by the applicable definition.Classify the actual work, not merely the project name.

Also distinguish construction workers from employees performing the ordinary manufacturing or operating activity after the facility is functional. Where the facts are mixed, document the work packages and workforce separately.

4. Who is the employer and who must pay?

Under the earlier BOCW framework, the definition of “employer” included the contractor in relation to construction work carried on through a contractor or workers supplied by a contractor. The Code on Social Security now governs the current period, and the employer/contractor obligations must be read with the Code’s definitions and Chapter VIII collection provisions.

For Government or public-sector works, the statute/rules provide for deduction from bills in the prescribed circumstances. For other projects, the employer may have to self-assess and pay cess through the prescribed process. The party who physically deducts money from an RA bill is not necessarily the only party with statutory responsibilities.

Practical contract test: Read the clauses on statutory levies, labour cess, deductions, taxes, change in law, reimbursement and employer/contractor responsibilities. Record (a) statutory liable person, (b) bill-deduction agent, (c) party bearing the cost, and (d) proof of deposit.

Example

A government department awards a road contract to Main Contractor A. A executes the work partly through Subcontractor B. The department deducts BOCW cess from A’s certified bills and deposits it with the Welfare Board under the prescribed mechanism. A also deducts an agreed amount from B’s RA bill. A must not assume that the department’s deduction automatically settles every statutory and contractual issue in the A–B subcontract. Reconcile the deduction chain and avoid charging/deducting the same cess twice without credit.

5. What is the cost-of-construction base?

The cess base is not automatically identical to GST taxable turnover, the contractor’s RA bill value, labour charges, or the project’s total contract value. It is the cost of construction determined under the applicable labour/social-security law and rules, including the prescribed exclusions and assessment method.

  • Section 100 expressly excludes the cost of land and compensation paid/payable to an employee or their kin under the relevant chapter.
  • The 2026 Central Rules prescribe self-assessment using applicable PWD/CPWD schedule rates or other specified construction rates, with a chartered-engineer certification process in the circumstances described in the rules.
  • For Government/PSU construction works, the rules provide for deduction at notified rates from bills and deposit with the State Building Workers’ Welfare Board.
  • Advance cess, periodic information, completion returns and final assessment/adjustment must be tracked under the applicable rules.
Do not apply 1% to every invoice automatically. Determine whether the invoice relates to covered construction work, who is the employer, what cost base the applicable law/rules prescribe, what has already been deposited, and whether the contract allows bill-wise deduction or reimbursement.

Materials and separate schedule items

Some tenders define the cess base by reference to a specified cost-of-construction measure and may treat separately supplied materials differently. The tender clause is relevant to the contractor’s commercial pricing and bill deduction, but it cannot override the statutory base. Retain the BOQ, material-supply schedule, measurement books, certified RA bills and the applicable assessment methodology. Do not assume that all material value is excluded merely because a tender has a separate schedule item.

6. Is GST payable on BOCW / labour cess?

This is the central GST question. Section 15(2)(a) of the CGST Act provides that the value of a supply includes taxes, duties, cesses, fees and charges levied under a law other than GST and GST Compensation Cess, if charged separately by the supplier. Section 15(2)(b) can also be relevant where an amount that the supplier is liable to pay in relation to the supply is incurred by the recipient and is not included in the price.

A published FAQ issued by the West Bengal Labour Commissionerate specifically addresses BOCW cess and GST. Its stated view is that where BOCW cess is deducted from the bill value rather than separately charged, the bill value before GST remains inclusive of the cess deduction; therefore GST is charged on that amount. It also explains that GST TDS is computed after excluding GST components from the invoice, not by first reducing the taxable base for BOCW cess.

Important qualification: This FAQ is an administrative clarification published by a State Labour Department, not a binding CBIC circular or a Supreme Court ruling on Section 15. Apply the statutory text to the actual contract and invoice. The GST result can require a different analysis where the cess is independently paid by the recipient in its own capacity, is not an amount charged by the supplier, or the contractual price/valuation facts differ. Keep a written position for material contracts rather than treating one State FAQ as a nationwide blanket ruling.

These deductions are not mutually exclusive. A single bill can attract more than one deduction if each statute independently applies. The West Bengal Labour Commissionerate FAQ expressly recognises that income-tax TDS, GST TDS and BOCW cess may all be deducted from the same bill where their respective conditions are met.

When the contractor receives evidence that the amount has been deposited and the statutory liability is discharged, clear the recoverable against the relevant BOCW cess payable/expense or statutory liability account according to who is legally liable and the company’s accounting policy. If the contract makes the contractor bear the cess and the customer merely deducts/deposits it, the ledger should not leave an unexplained long-outstanding receivable.

C. Main contractor records cess deducted from subcontractor

If the main contractor is legally required or contractually authorised to deduct cess from a subcontractor’s RA bill, maintain a separate BOCW cess payable/withholding ledger. On deposit, clear the payable against bank. Do not book the deduction as a reduction of subcontract cost or output GST automatically; first establish the contractual and statutory treatment.

D. GST TDS accounting is separate

For the supplier, GST TDS when deducted is generally tracked as a receivable/claim to be reconciled with the electronic cash ledger, not as ITC and not as a reduction of revenue. For the deductor, record the statutory GST TDS payable and clear it on deposit/reporting. Do not combine it with the BOCW cess ledger.

10. Contract clauses, reimbursement and double-deduction risks

Before signing a tender or subcontract, review these clauses and record the agreed commercial treatment:

  • Statutory levies: Who bears BOCW cess, and is it included in the quoted price?
  • Deduction from RA bills: Is the owner entitled to deduct cess from each bill, and at what base?
  • Deposit responsibility: Who deposits the deducted amount and supplies the challan/certificate?
  • Subcontract flow-down: Can the main contractor recover cess from subcontractors? Is the same work already subject to a project-level deduction?
  • Separate supply/material schedules: Are material supplies separately priced? Does the cess assessment law permit exclusion? Do not rely on contract labels alone.
  • Change in law: Who bears a change in statutory rate or method after bid submission?
  • Reconciliation and refund: How will excess cess, duplicate deductions and final assessment differences be adjusted?
Common double-deduction example: The department deducts 1% from Main Contractor A’s RA bill. A then deducts another 1% from Subcontractor B’s bill for the same work package without checking whether B’s cost is already included in A’s cess base or whether the subcontract requires a separate deduction. The result can be duplicate economic recovery or an unreconciled cess credit. Map each deduction to the relevant employer, work package, statutory assessment and deposit proof.

A contractual recovery from a subcontractor is not automatically a second statutory cess liability. Determine whether it is (a) a statutory deduction, (b) reimbursement of cess paid on the subcontractor’s behalf, (c) a price adjustment, or (d) a commercial deduction. Its GST treatment must be evaluated from its real legal and commercial character.

12. Audit risks and documents to retain

Frequent mistakes

  • Applying 1% to the full contract value without determining the statutory cost-of-construction base.
  • Assuming all construction-related invoices or all mining-site expenditure attract BOCW cess.
  • Using the 1998 Cess Rules for a post-May 2026 event without considering the 2026 Social Security Rules.
  • Reducing GST taxable value by the cess deduction without analysing Section 15.
  • Calculating GST TDS after reducing the invoice base by BOCW cess.
  • Booking BOCW cess as GST input tax credit.
  • Deducting cess twice at owner and main-contractor/subcontractor levels.
  • Not reconciling the deduction to a Welfare Board challan or assessment.
  • Assuming a public-sector customer’s deduction automatically resolves the contractor’s own obligations.

Project-wise evidence file

  • Executed contract, tender, BOQ, amendments and statutory-levy clauses.
  • Work package and employer/contractor responsibility matrix.
  • Cost-of-construction calculation and engineer certification, where required.
  • RA bills, measurement books, work completion certificates and material schedules.
  • GST tax invoices and output tax reconciliation.
  • BOCW cess deduction statements, assessment orders, challans and Welfare Board receipts.
  • GST TDS certificates/portal credit and income-tax TDS certificates.
  • Ledger extracts, bank statements, deduction recovery workings and final settlement.
  • Final assessment, advance adjustment, refund or balance-payment documents.

13. Frequently asked questions

1. Is BOCW cess always 1%?

The Central Government notification effective from 9 May 2026 specifies 1% of the cost of construction incurred by an employer. For earlier periods, verify the notification and law applicable to that period. Also confirm that the project/work is covered and determine the correct cost base.

2. Is labour cess charged on labour charges only?

No. It is a cess on the statutory cost-of-construction base, not simply a levy on the labour component. The applicable statute/rules and assessment method determine the base.

3. Does GST apply on BOCW cess?

Section 15(2)(a) includes separately charged non-GST cesses/fees in value. A West Bengal Labour Commissionerate FAQ also states that where cess is deducted from the bill value, GST is charged on the bill value before that deduction. This is a State administrative FAQ, not a CBIC circular; assess the exact contract and payment arrangement.

4. Can I reduce the taxable value by 1% BOCW cess?

Do not reduce it automatically. A deduction from the receivable is not necessarily a reduction in consideration or taxable value. Analyse Section 15, the invoice, the contract and who is legally liable to pay the cess.

5. Is GST TDS calculated after deducting BOCW cess?

The West Bengal Labour Commissionerate FAQ says GST TDS is calculated after excluding GST components from the invoice, not after reducing the taxable base by BOCW cess. Independently apply Section 51 and verify the deductor and threshold.

6. Can BOCW cess and GST TDS both be deducted from one RA bill?

Yes, where the respective statutes and conditions independently require them. The deductions are not mutually exclusive. Maintain separate calculations and ledgers.

7. Can the main contractor deduct BOCW cess from a subcontractor?

Only after reviewing the statutory responsibility, project-level cess arrangement and subcontract clause. A contractual recovery does not automatically mean a separate statutory liability. Prevent duplicate deduction and obtain deposit evidence.

8. Is BOCW cess eligible for ITC?

No GST input tax credit arises merely because BOCW cess is paid or withheld. It is a separate statutory levy, not GST charged by a supplier as input tax.

9. Does every road or mining contract attract cess?

Road construction is within the broad construction-work concept, but assess the actual work, employer, worker engagement, applicable exclusions and current rules. Mining operations should be separated from qualifying civil construction work; do not apply cess to every mining invoice automatically.

10. Which law should be quoted in 2026?

For the current period, review Chapter VIII and Section 100 of the Code on Social Security, 2020, the 1% rate notification S.O. 2322(E) published on 9 May 2026, and the Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026. For earlier periods, apply the law and rules then in force and preserve the transitional history.
