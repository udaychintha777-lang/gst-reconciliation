CGST RULES • COMPOSITION LEVY

Rule 5 – Conditions and Restrictions for Composition Levy

A practical explanation of the conditions a composition taxpayer must satisfy, including stock restrictions, reverse charge obligations, prohibited manufacturer categories, bill-of-supply wording and display requirements.

At a glance

What Rule 5 does

Rule 5 lays down the conditions and restrictions that a person exercising the composition option under Section 10 must follow. It is one of the most important operational rules for a composition taxpayer because it converts the broad statutory scheme into day-to-day compliance requirements.

Key idea: Choosing composition is not enough. The taxpayer must continue to satisfy the applicable conditions and follow the required invoicing, reverse-charge and display requirements.

The current CBIC composition-rules page lists seven conditions in Rule 5(1) and then provides that a registered person paying tax under Section 10 does not need to file a fresh intimation every year merely to continue under the scheme, subject to the Act and Rules.

1. Rule 5 — statutory framework

Rule 5(1) requires a person exercising the composition option under Section 10 to comply with the following conditions:

1Not a casual taxable person or non-resident taxable person.

A person exercising composition cannot be a casual taxable person or a non-resident taxable person.

2Specified stock must satisfy the prescribed source conditions.

For the option exercised through the route covered by Rule 3(1), the goods held in stock on the appointed day must not have been purchased through inter-State trade or commerce, imported from outside India, or received from a branch, agent or principal outside the State, as covered by the rule.

3Stock purchased from unregistered persons.

The prescribed condition requires that goods held in stock have not been purchased from an unregistered person and, where such purchase has occurred in the circumstances covered by the rule, the applicable tax under Section 9(4) is to be paid.

4Tax on specified inward supplies.

The composition taxpayer must pay applicable tax under Section 9(3) or Section 9(4) on inward supplies of goods or services or both received from unregistered persons, as prescribed.

5Specified manufacturers.

The person must not have been engaged during the preceding financial year in manufacturing goods notified under Section 10(2)(e), which are excluded from the composition benefit.

6Bill of supply wording.

The words “composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies” must be mentioned at the top of the bill of supply issued by the taxpayer.

7Display at business premises.

The words “composition taxable person” must be displayed on every notice or signboard at a prominent place at the principal place of business and every additional place or places of business.

Important legal reading: Rule 5 conditions should not be read in isolation. The taxpayer must also satisfy the requirements of Section 10 and the other applicable composition provisions.

2. Rule 5 in simple language

In everyday terms, Rule 5 says: “If you want to use composition, your business and your compliance behaviour must fit the composition model.”

Who can use it?
The person must not fall into the excluded registration categories such as casual or non-resident taxable person.
What about old stock?
Certain stock-source restrictions apply, particularly to the stock covered by the specified transition route.
What if reverse charge applies?
Composition status does not automatically remove separate reverse-charge liabilities under Section 9.
What must customers see?
The bill of supply must carry the prescribed composition wording and the business premises must display the required status.

3. Clause-by-clause practical explanation

Condition 1 — Casual taxable person / non-resident taxable person

A casual taxable person and a non-resident taxable person are outside the composition option. Therefore, a business should not assume that obtaining temporary or non-resident registration can be combined with composition treatment.

Business decision: Before selecting composition, confirm the registration status and nature of the taxable person, not merely the turnover.

Condition 2 — Inter-State/imported/foreign-branch stock restriction

This condition is particularly important when a business is moving into composition with opening inventory. The rule addresses certain goods held in stock and restricts the source of that stock for the specified option route.

Finance teams should therefore prepare an opening-stock statement identifying major purchases, source State, import status and branch/agent/principal arrangements before switching to composition.

Condition 3 — Purchases from unregistered persons

The rule deals with goods purchased from unregistered persons and links the applicable tax treatment to Section 9(4). The correct approach is not to treat every purchase from an unregistered supplier as automatically taxable; the relevant statutory conditions and the supplies to which Section 9(4) applies must be checked.

Condition 4 — Reverse charge on inward supplies

Composition status does not mean “no GST on purchases.” Where Section 9(3) or the applicable Section 9(4) provision creates reverse-charge liability, the composition taxpayer has to discharge that liability separately.

Common misunderstanding: “I am a composition dealer, so I never pay GST on purchases.” This is incorrect. Composition status changes the normal output-tax mechanism; it does not erase applicable reverse-charge obligations.

Condition 5 — Notified manufacturers

Certain categories of manufacturers are excluded from the composition benefit. Therefore, a manufacturer must identify the nature of goods manufactured and check the notified exclusions before opting for composition.

Condition 6 — Bill of supply declaration

The prescribed wording is not optional decoration. It communicates to the recipient that the supplier is a composition taxable person and is not eligible to collect tax on supplies in the normal manner.

Condition 7 — Display at business premises

The required words must be displayed prominently at the principal place of business and every additional place of business. A finance team should coordinate this with administration and branch/site teams rather than treating it as an accounting-only requirement.

4. Opening-stock review before composition

For businesses switching into composition, opening stock deserves special attention. A practical stock review should contain:

CheckWhat finance should record
Supplier statusRegistered / unregistered and GST details where applicable.
Purchase locationState of purchase and whether the transaction involved inter-State movement.
Import statusWhether goods were imported from outside India.
Branch / agent / principalWhether stock was received through an outside-State branch, agent or principal arrangement.

Recommended control: Do a one-time “composition opening-stock audit” before the option becomes effective. This is much safer than trying to reconstruct the stock position during a future departmental audit.

5. Reverse charge — practical understanding

One of the most important practical aspects of Rule 5 is that composition does not eliminate reverse-charge obligations.

SituationWhat to check
Specified service/goods covered by Section 9(3)Determine whether reverse charge applies and discharge the applicable tax.
Purchase from unregistered personCheck whether the particular supply is within the applicable Section 9(4) framework.
Normal registered supplier invoiceComposition taxpayer generally pays the supplier's GST as part of the purchase cost and does not use normal ITC in the composition scheme.

Maintain a separate reverse-charge register. This prevents composition output-tax workings from obscuring purchase-side tax liabilities.

6. Bill of supply and business communication

A composition taxpayer issues a bill of supply rather than collecting GST through a normal tax invoice for its taxable outward supplies under composition treatment.

Mandatory wording: “composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies” must appear at the top of the bill of supply as prescribed by Rule 5.

ERP implementation

  • Create a dedicated composition bill-of-supply template.
  • Lock the prescribed declaration into the template rather than relying on users to type it manually.
  • Prevent normal GST tax-invoice templates from being selected for composition outward supplies.
  • Review sample invoices after every ERP change.
Practical risk: An otherwise eligible composition taxpayer can create avoidable compliance problems if its ERP continues generating normal tax invoices or omits the prescribed composition declaration.

7. Display requirement at business premises

The rule requires the words “composition taxable person” to be displayed prominently at the principal place of business and at every additional place or places of business.

This is particularly relevant for businesses with multiple branches, warehouses, project offices or additional places of business. The responsibility should be assigned to an administration or branch-control owner, with evidence retained.

Principal place
Display the required composition status prominently.
Additional places
Repeat the display requirement at every applicable additional place of business.

8. Do you need to file a fresh composition intimation every year?

Rule 5(2) provides that a registered person paying tax under Section 10 may continue to pay tax under that section without filing a fresh intimation every year, subject to the Act and Rules.

Practical meaning: Annual continuation is not the same as automatic eligibility. The taxpayer still has to satisfy the statutory conditions throughout the period.

Therefore, the finance team should not create an annual process that merely asks “Did we file CMP-02 again?” The better control is “Are we still legally eligible and compliant?”

9. Practical business examples

Example 1 — Manufacturer switching to composition

A manufacturer plans to opt for composition from the beginning of a financial year. Before doing so, the finance team reviews the nature of manufactured goods, opening stock and purchase sources. If the goods manufactured fall into a notified excluded category, the business cannot simply proceed because its turnover is within the threshold.

Example 2 — Composition taxpayer receives reverse-charge supply

A composition dealer receives a supply on which Section 9(3) requires the recipient to pay tax. The composition status does not remove that reverse-charge liability. The business should record and pay the applicable tax separately and maintain supporting documentation.

Example 3 — ERP generates a normal tax invoice

A composition dealer's ERP is accidentally configured as a regular taxpayer and generates an invoice showing GST separately. This is an operational control failure. The tax master, invoice template and GST registration status should be corrected immediately and the affected transactions reviewed.

Example 4 — Multiple branches

A business has a principal place of business and three additional registered places. The prescribed composition display requirement should be implemented at all applicable locations, not just at headquarters.

10. Audit and GST notice relevance

Rule 5 is highly relevant during an audit because many of its requirements are documentary and operational. An officer can examine whether the taxpayer actually complied with the conditions rather than merely checking whether composition tax was paid.

Maintain these records

  • Composition option/intimation and registration records.
  • Opening-stock statement and purchase-source analysis.
  • Purchase register and reverse-charge working.
  • Evidence supporting the nature of manufactured goods.
  • Sample bills of supply showing the mandatory declaration.
  • Photographic/internal compliance evidence of required business-premises display where appropriate.
  • Monthly composition eligibility review.
  • ERP tax-master configuration and change logs.
Notice risk: If a condition was not satisfied, the issue can extend beyond a missing disclosure. It may affect the taxpayer's eligibility for composition and can lead to regular GST liability from the relevant date under the connected validity provisions.

12. Common mistakes under Rule 5

  1. Thinking turnover alone determines eligibility. Rule 5 contains additional conditions.
  2. Ignoring opening-stock source restrictions.
  3. Assuming composition means no reverse-charge tax.
  4. Using a normal tax invoice instead of the appropriate bill of supply.
  5. Missing the mandatory composition declaration on bills of supply.
  6. Displaying the composition status only at the head office while ignoring additional places.
  7. Assuming a fresh annual intimation is the only compliance control.
  8. Failing to review the nature of manufactured goods.
  9. Not documenting continuing eligibility.

13. Management decision framework

Before opting for or continuing composition, management should answer these questions:

1. Are we an eligible person?
Check the taxpayer category and Section 10.
2. Does our stock satisfy the applicable conditions?
Review purchase sources, imports and branch/agent/principal movements.
3. Do we have reverse-charge exposure?
Map purchases and services against Section 9.
4. Are our products eligible?
Review notified manufacturing exclusions.
5. Is the ERP correct?
Test bills of supply and tax masters.
6. Are all locations compliant?
Check display requirements and branch controls.

15. Frequently asked questions

What is GST Rule 5?

Rule 5 of the CGST Rules contains conditions and restrictions that a person exercising the composition option under Section 10 must comply with.

Can a casual taxable person opt for composition?

No. Rule 5 specifically requires that the person exercising the option is neither a casual taxable person nor a non-resident taxable person.

Does a composition taxpayer pay reverse-charge GST?

Yes, where an applicable supply attracts reverse charge under Section 9(3) or the applicable Section 9(4) framework. Composition status does not by itself remove reverse-charge liability.

What wording must appear on a composition bill of supply?

The prescribed wording is “composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies” and Rule 5 requires it to be mentioned at the top of the bill of supply.

What must a composition taxpayer display at its business premises?

The words “composition taxable person” must be displayed at a prominent place at the principal place of business and every additional place or places of business.

Does a composition taxpayer need to file a fresh option every year?

Rule 5(2) provides that a registered person paying tax under Section 10 may continue without filing a fresh intimation every year, subject to the Act and Rules.

Does Rule 5 itself determine all composition eligibility?

No. Rule 5 contains important conditions and restrictions, but the complete eligibility analysis requires Section 10 and the other applicable composition provisions.

Why is Rule 5 important for an audit?

Because several Rule 5 requirements are operational and documentary: stock source, reverse charge, invoice wording, product eligibility and business-premises display can all require evidence.

16. Practical Rule 5 compliance checklist

  • Confirm the taxpayer is not a casual or non-resident taxable person.
  • Review opening stock and applicable source restrictions.
  • Review purchases from unregistered persons under the applicable Section 9 framework.
  • Maintain a reverse-charge register.
  • Check whether manufactured goods fall within notified exclusions.
  • Configure the bill-of-supply declaration in ERP.
  • Verify the composition wording on actual bills of supply.
  • Display “composition taxable person” at the required business locations.
  • Review continuing eligibility periodically.
  • Retain evidence for audit and GST notice response.

17. Practical takeaway

Rule 5 is the operational backbone of the composition scheme. A taxpayer can have turnover within the applicable limit and still face a problem if it fails one of the relevant conditions.

Best compliance sequence: Check eligibility → review stock → map reverse charge → check product restrictions → configure bill of supply → complete location display → monitor continuing compliance.

For management, the most useful control is a documented periodic composition checklist. For finance teams, the most important controls are the opening-stock review, reverse-charge reconciliation, invoice configuration and evidence of continuing eligibility.
