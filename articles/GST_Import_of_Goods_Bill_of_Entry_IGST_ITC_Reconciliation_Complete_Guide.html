GST on Import of Goods – Bill of Entry, IGST ITC & GSTR-2B Reconciliation

A practical guide for importers, accountants and finance teams covering customs documents, import IGST, ICEGATE data, the Import of Goods section in IMS, accounting entries and month-end reconciliation.

Quick answer: can an importer claim ITC of IGST paid on imported goods?

A GST-registered importer can generally claim input tax credit of Integrated GST (IGST) actually paid on import of goods, where the imported goods are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business and the conditions under the CGST Act and Rules are satisfied. The prescribed document is generally the Bill of Entry or a similar customs document for assessment of IGST on imports.

Social Welfare Surcharge
Not GST ITC; generally part of cost.
Remember: Payment of customs duty alone does not establish ITC eligibility. Verify the importer GSTIN, Bill of Entry, actual IGST payment, receipt and business use of goods, restrictions under Section 17, and the relevant GSTR-2B/IMS record.

1. Legal framework governing GST on import of goods

Import of goods into India is treated as an inter-State supply for levy of IGST. IGST on imported goods is generally levied and collected under the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, at the time and in the manner applicable to customs clearance. The customs assessment and Bill of Entry therefore become central to the importer's GST records.

ProvisionWhy it matters
Section 7(2), IGST ActDeems import of goods into India to be a supply in the course of inter-State trade or commerce until they cross the customs frontiers of India.
Section 3(7), Customs Tariff Act, 1975Provides for levy of IGST on imported articles, subject to the applicable law and notifications.
Section 16, CGST ActSets out the basic entitlement and conditions for taking input tax credit.
Section 17, CGST ActProvides for apportionment and blocked-credit restrictions, including personal use and other specified categories.
Rule 36(1)(d), CGST RulesRecognises a Bill of Entry or similar customs document for assessment of IGST on imports as an ITC document.
Section 20, IGST ActApplies relevant CGST provisions, including ITC provisions, to integrated tax subject to the IGST Act.
Practical distinction: Import of goods and import of services are different GST events. This article deals with goods cleared through customs. Imported services generally involve reverse charge under the IGST Act and require a separate analysis.

2. Which customs components are eligible for GST input tax credit?

A customs Bill of Entry may contain several components. Do not post the entire customs payment into the GST input tax credit ledger. Classify each levy separately.

Customs componentGST ITC treatmentTypical accounting treatment
Basic Customs Duty (BCD)Not eligible as GST ITCInventory / capital asset cost, as applicable
Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS)Not eligible as GST ITCInventory / capital asset cost, as applicable
IGST on importPotentially eligible, subject to ITC conditionsInput IGST ledger
Compensation cess, where applicableSeparate cess-credit rules and utilisation restrictions applySeparate input cess ledger, where eligible
Customs interest, fine or penaltyNot ordinary input tax creditExpense / recoverable / other appropriate account based on facts
Do not confuse tax with duty. BCD and SWS are customs levies, not CGST/SGST/IGST charged as GST on a domestic tax invoice. Their payment does not create credit in the electronic credit ledger under the CGST Act.

3. Conditions to claim ITC of IGST paid on import

Before taking credit, complete the following checks. The fact that the Bill of Entry appears in GSTR-2B is an important system control, but it does not replace the legal eligibility test.

  1. Registered recipient: The GSTIN claiming credit should be the eligible importing recipient to whom the customs document relates. Verify the GST registration and State/Union Territory registration particulars.
  2. Prescribed document: Maintain the assessed Bill of Entry or other prescribed customs document evidencing assessment of IGST on import.
  3. IGST actually paid: Verify the IGST amount assessed and paid through the customs process. Do not claim credit merely from a proforma document or an unassessed draft.
  4. Receipt of goods: Confirm that the goods have been received. Where goods are received in lots or instalments, apply the specific receipt condition in Section 16(2).
  5. Business use: The goods must be used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business.
  6. No blocked credit: Check Section 17(5), including cases involving personal consumption, gifts/free samples, goods lost or destroyed, and other statutory restrictions.
  7. Return filed: The registered person must furnish the relevant return under Section 39.
  8. Time limit: Apply Section 16(4) and any applicable special statutory provisions. Do not defer old import credits without checking the time limit for the relevant financial year and the date of the document.
  9. GSTR-2B / IMS control: Check that the Bill of Entry data is available and correctly reflected in the relevant import section of GSTR-2B after the applicable IMS action and recomputation.
Potentially eligible exampleA registered manufacturing company imports a machine for use in taxable manufacturing operations, pays IGST at customs, receives the machine and holds the correct Bill of Entry. Subject to all other conditions and restrictions, the import IGST can generally be claimed as ITC.
Not eligible exampleA proprietor imports goods exclusively for personal use. The fact that IGST was paid at customs and a Bill of Entry exists does not make the credit eligible for business ITC.
Section 16(2)(aa) clarification: The supplier-uploading condition for ordinary supplier invoices should not be mechanically applied to a Bill of Entry as if the foreign seller were required to upload a domestic GSTR-1 invoice. Rule 36(1)(d) specifically recognises the customs document for import IGST. The import-data flow through ICEGATE and GSTR-2B/IMS should nevertheless be reconciled and documented.

4. Bill of Entry – what should the accounts team verify?

The Bill of Entry is the key document supporting the import IGST credit. The accounts team should obtain the final assessed document and match it with the customs payment evidence, goods receipt and purchase/import records.

Documents to retain in the import ITC file

  • Final assessed Bill of Entry and any amendment documents.
  • Customs duty payment challan / payment confirmation.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill and transport documents.
  • Goods receipt note, inward register or asset receipt evidence.
  • ICEGATE / GST portal import record and GSTR-2B download.
  • IMS action history and recomputed GSTR-2B, where applicable.
  • Import purchase ledger and ITC working paper.

5. How import IGST appears in GSTR-2B through ICEGATE

GSTR-2B includes import-of-goods information received through the ICEGATE system, including relevant inward supplies from SEZ units/developers. This is different from a domestic supplier invoice appearing in the regular B2B tables.

Customs Bill of Entry→ICEGATE data→GST system→IMS Import of Goods→GSTR-2B→GSTR-3B

For import reconciliation, do not search only in the domestic B2B invoice table. Identify the import-related sections and match by Bill of Entry number, date, port code, importer GSTIN and IGST amount. The GSTR-2B Excel download contains separate import worksheets depending on the type of record and amendment.

What if IGST paid on import is not appearing in GSTR-2B?

  1. Check that the Bill of Entry is finally assessed and the IGST payment is complete.
  2. Verify that the GSTIN declared in the Bill of Entry is correct and active for the importing entity.
  3. Check the Bill of Entry number, date, port code and other particulars against the customs record.
  4. Allow for the customs-to-GST system data transmission and applicable statement generation cycle.
  5. Use the GST Portal's Bill of Entry search / self-service facility, where available, to search for the record and request retrieval from ICEGATE.
  6. Check the Import of Goods section in IMS and take the appropriate action on the record.
  7. Recompute GSTR-2B if an action is changed or taken after draft generation, as required by the portal workflow.
  8. If the record remains missing, raise a grievance with the relevant Bill of Entry details and supporting customs evidence. Retain the grievance reference and follow-up.
Do not create an artificial domestic purchase invoice or enter the foreign supplier's commercial invoice as if it were a GST tax invoice merely to force the credit into GSTR-2B. The prescribed import document is the Bill of Entry.

6. Import of Goods in IMS – important functionality from October 2025

The GST Portal introduced an Import of Goods section in the Invoice Management System from the October 2025 tax period. This brings Bills of Entry for imports from overseas and imports from SEZ into IMS for recipient-side action.

IMS sectionWhat it contains
IMPGOriginal Bills of Entry for imports from outside India.
IMPG (Amendments)Amendments to overseas import Bills of Entry, including value and GSTIN amendments.
IMPGSEZOriginal Bills of Entry for imports from SEZ.
IMPGSEZA (Amendments)Amendments to Bills of Entry for imports from SEZ, including value and GSTIN amendments.

Actions available for Bills of Entry

ActionEffectPractical control
AcceptAccepted record becomes part of the ITC Available section of the relevant GSTR-2B, subject to the applicable rules. Eligible credit is auto-populated in GSTR-3B.Accept only after checking the Bill of Entry and actual ITC eligibility.
PendingRecord is held and does not flow into GSTR-2B / GSTR-3B until accepted.Use where verification is incomplete or a discrepancy needs resolution.
No actionRecord is treated as deemed accepted at the time of GSTR-2B generation.Do not leave the dashboard unattended; review records before the return is filed.
RejectNot available for Bills of Entry under the advisory.Do not expect a reject option as available for ordinary supplier invoices.
Important operating point: The advisory states that draft GSTR-2B is generated on the 14th of the subsequent month. Actions can be changed after draft generation until filing of the corresponding GSTR-3B, but GSTR-2B must be recomputed where the prescribed conditions apply. Always follow the live portal workflow and current advisories.

Recommended monthly IMS process

  1. Download or review the Bills of Entry displayed in IMPG, IMPGA, IMPGSEZ and IMPGSEZA.
  2. Match each record with the customs Bill of Entry and the import register.
  3. Identify duplicate, wrong-GSTIN, wrong-value and unverified records.
  4. Accept verified records and keep unresolved records pending, where the portal permits.
  5. Review all no-action records because they may be deemed accepted.
  6. Recompute GSTR-2B after any action change where required.
  7. Download the recomputed GSTR-2B and reconcile it with the books before filing GSTR-3B.

7. Bill of Entry amendments – value change and GSTIN change

Customs amendments can affect the amount or the GST registration to which import credit is attributed. The finance team must separately track value amendments and GSTIN amendments. A GSTIN amendment is especially important where the original GSTIN has already availed the credit.

A. Value amendment

Where customs changes the assessable value or the IGST amount, compare the original and amended records. Ensure that the latest amendment is considered and that the same credit is not claimed twice. The IMS advisory states that where multiple value amendments exist, the latest record is displayed in IMS in the described workflow.

B. GSTIN amendment – transfer of credit to the correct registration

Suppose a Bill of Entry was originally filed with GSTIN G1, but customs later amends it to GSTIN G2. If G1 has already availed ITC, G1 is required to reverse the ITC availed on that Bill of Entry. The IMS workflow provides a mechanism for the previous GSTIN to declare the amount of ITC requiring reduction in the relevant cases.

Situation at old GSTIN (G1)Practical action described in IMS advisory
No ITC was availed earlier, or the credit was already fully reversedDeclare zero as the amount requiring further reduction, where the portal asks for the declaration.
ITC was only partly reversed earlierDeclare the remaining amount requiring reversal, subject to the original Bill of Entry credit.
Full original ITC was availed and no reversal was madeFull reversal is required through the applicable IMS process.
No declaration/action is provided where the portal requires oneThe advisory describes deemed acceptance of the reversal entry; therefore the old GSTIN must monitor the record rather than ignore it.
Do not claim the same import IGST in both GSTINs. The old GSTIN must reconcile its earlier claim and reversal with the amended record made available to the new GSTIN. Maintain a Bill of Entry-wise audit trail showing original GSTIN, amended GSTIN, credit originally availed, reversal already made and balance reversal.

Illustration

Original Bill of Entry IGST: ₹1,80,000. GSTIN G1 had availed ₹1,80,000. Customs subsequently amends the GSTIN to G2. If G1 has not reversed any credit, the full ₹1,80,000 is to be reversed by G1 under the applicable IMS workflow, and the eligible credit is made available to G2 through the amended record. If G1 had already reversed ₹60,000, the remaining amount to be addressed is ₹1,20,000, subject to portal data and the exact amendment record.

The illustration explains the mechanism only. Use the amount shown in the live IMS record and the actual credit previously availed/reversed; do not manually override the portal amount without supporting evidence.

8. Import of goods from an SEZ unit or developer

Goods received from an SEZ into the Domestic Tariff Area (DTA) involve a customs-facing process and should not automatically be treated like a normal domestic B2B purchase. The applicable customs and GST treatment depends on the transaction and the clearance documentation.

For eligible clearances, the Bill of Entry / customs documentation and IGST paid at the time of clearance are central to the recipient's import ITC working. GSTR-2B and IMS provide separate import-related categories for SEZ goods, including IMPGSEZ and IMPGSEZA for the relevant original and amended records.

SEZ import verification points

  • Confirm whether the transaction is a clearance from SEZ to DTA and identify the DTA recipient.
  • Obtain the relevant Bill of Entry and customs assessment/payment evidence.
  • Verify the GSTIN appearing in the customs document.
  • Check whether the IGST was actually levied and paid or whether a specific exemption applies.
  • Match the record with the SEZ import section in IMS/GSTR-2B.
  • Apply normal ITC eligibility and blocked-credit checks before claiming credit.
Do not confuse two situations: goods imported by an SEZ unit for authorised operations and goods cleared from an SEZ unit into the DTA can have different tax consequences. Check the precise direction of movement, exemption notification and customs document before deciding the recipient's ITC treatment.

9. High-sea sales – who claims import IGST credit?

A high-sea sale is a sale of imported goods by the original importer to another buyer after the goods have been dispatched from the foreign port but before they are cleared for home consumption in India. The customs declaration and Bill of Entry are generally filed by the buyer who purchases the goods in the high-sea sale and clears them from customs.

IGST on the imported goods is generally collected at the time of import clearance, with the high-sea sale value additions included in the assessable value in accordance with customs valuation rules. The buyer who is the importer/recipient reflected in the customs documents and who bears the import IGST should examine the ITC entitlement, subject to normal conditions.

Document / transactionWhat the finance team should check
High-sea sale agreement / endorsementEvidence of transfer of title before customs clearance and the identity of the final buyer.
Bill of EntryFinal buyer's importer details and GSTIN, where applicable.
Customs IGST paymentIGST assessed and paid at import clearance.
Commercial settlementReconcile the high-sea sale consideration and additions with the customs assessable value.
ITC claimClaim only by the eligible GST registration supported by the customs document and business-use evidence.
Important: The original foreign supplier's commercial invoice or the high-sea sale agreement alone should not be treated as the document for claiming import IGST credit. Verify the final Bill of Entry and the customs tax payment.

10. Import of capital goods, machinery and equipment

Import IGST on machinery, plant and equipment used for taxable business operations may generally qualify for ITC, subject to Sections 16 and 17. The fact that the imported item is capitalised in the books does not by itself deny ITC. Equally, capitalisation does not override a specific blocked-credit provision.

Practical checks for imported capital goods

  • Identify the asset and the GST registration that imported it.
  • Match the Bill of Entry, IGST payment, fixed asset register and goods receipt.
  • Check whether the asset is used for taxable, exempt or non-business purposes.
  • Apply the relevant apportionment rules where common use or exempt supplies are involved.
  • Review Section 17(5), particularly where the asset is a motor vehicle or relates to construction of immovable property on own account.
  • Do not claim depreciation under the Income-tax Act on the GST component for which ITC is claimed, as required by Section 16(3) of the CGST Act.
Construction of immovable property – special review requiredImported machinery may be eligible, but imported goods used in construction of an immovable property on own account can raise Section 17(5)(d) issues. Determine whether the item qualifies as plant and machinery under the statutory definition and examine the actual facts before claiming credit.

12. Import IGST reconciliation – books vs ICEGATE vs GSTR-2B

A robust monthly reconciliation should connect three records: the import register maintained by accounts, the customs/ICEGATE Bill of Entry data and the import tables in GSTR-2B after IMS actions. A simple total-level comparison is not sufficient because one Bill of Entry can be amended, split, corrected or attributed to a different GSTIN.

Recommended reconciliation columns

ColumnPurpose
GSTIN / State registrationRegistration to which the import credit belongs.
Bill of Entry number and datePrimary document key.
Port codeAdditional matching key.
Supplier / countryCommercial traceability.
Assessable valueValue comparison.
IGST as per Bill of EntryCustoms document amount.
IGST as per customs paymentPayment verification.
IGST as per GSTR-2BAuto-drafted statement amount.
IMS actionAccepted / Pending / Deemed accepted / amendment status.
ITC claimed in GSTR-3BActual return claim.
Difference / reason / ownerException management and closure.

Suggested exception categories

  • Matched: Bill of Entry, customs payment, GSTR-2B and books agree.
  • Missing in GSTR-2B: Bill of Entry exists in books/customs data but import record is not reflected.
  • GSTIN mismatch: Bill of Entry is mapped to a different registration.
  • IGST value mismatch: Amount in books differs from the customs or GSTR-2B record.
  • Pending in IMS: Record has not yet been accepted and is held from the relevant statement.
  • Amendment pending: Value or GSTIN amendment needs reconciliation.
  • Duplicate risk: Original and amended records or repeated postings may have caused duplicate credit.
  • ITC not eligible: Credit is available in the statement but fails business-use or statutory eligibility tests.
  • Claimed in 3B but not supported: Return claim cannot be traced to the final Bill of Entry and eligible IGST.

Month-end control formula

Eligible import IGST as per books = IGST paid on valid Bills of Entry less ineligible credit, duplicate credit, credit attributable to non-business/exempt use and credit requiring reversal, plus/minus eligible amendments and prior-period corrections.

Compare this figure with the eligible import IGST reflected in GSTR-2B and the actual amount reported in GSTR-3B. Document every difference and its resolution month.

14. Monthly import ITC checklist for finance teams

  1. Obtain the import register from the purchase/import team.
  2. Collect final Bills of Entry and customs payment confirmations.
  3. Verify importer GSTIN and registration State.
  4. Match goods receipt / stores records or fixed asset receipt.
  5. Separate IGST from BCD, SWS, cess and other customs charges.
  6. Download GSTR-2B and identify import and SEZ import sections.
  7. Review IMPG, IMPGA, IMPGSEZ and IMPGSEZA records in IMS.
  8. Accept verified records and keep unresolved records pending where permitted.
  9. Review no-action records and deemed acceptance.
  10. Recompute GSTR-2B after relevant action changes.
  11. Reconcile import IGST in books with customs records and GSTR-2B.
  12. Check Section 16 conditions, Section 17 restrictions and time limits.
  13. Verify the amount actually claimed in GSTR-3B.
  14. Prepare an exception report and obtain finance manager approval.
  15. Retain the Bill of Entry-wise working and supporting documents.
Recommended responsibility matrix: Import/purchase team should provide documents and shipment status; stores should confirm receipt; accounts should verify customs tax and book entries; GST team should review IMS/GSTR-2B and ITC eligibility; finance manager should approve unresolved differences before return filing.

15. Frequently asked questions

1. Can we claim ITC of IGST paid on imported goods?

Generally yes, if the importer is eligible, holds the prescribed Bill of Entry, the IGST has been paid, goods have been received and used or intended for business, the return is furnished and no restriction or time limit prevents the claim.

2. Can we claim ITC of Basic Customs Duty?

No. Basic Customs Duty is not GST input tax credit. It is generally considered as part of inventory or asset cost, as appropriate.

3. Is a Bill of Entry sufficient for claiming import IGST credit?

It is the prescribed document under Rule 36(1)(d), but the other Section 16 conditions and applicable restrictions must also be satisfied. Retain evidence of customs IGST payment and receipt of goods.

4. Does import IGST have to appear in GSTR-2B?

Import data is received through ICEGATE and reflected in the import-related sections of GSTR-2B. Review the relevant import record and current portal process. If it is missing, use the GST Portal's available Bill of Entry search/self-service process and raise a grievance if needed.

5. What is the Import of Goods section in IMS?

It is a GST Portal functionality introduced from the October 2025 tax period for Bills of Entry relating to overseas imports and imports from SEZ. The recipient can accept or keep eligible records pending; rejection is not available for Bills of Entry under the advisory.

6. What happens if no action is taken on a Bill of Entry in IMS?

The October 2025 advisory states that a record on which no action is taken is treated as deemed accepted at the time of GSTR-2B generation. Taxpayers should therefore review the dashboard rather than assume that no action means the record is excluded.

7. Can a Bill of Entry be amended if the GSTIN is wrong?

Customs amendment procedures may permit correction, depending on the facts and applicable customs process. After a GSTIN amendment is transmitted to the GST system, the IMS advisory provides a reversal mechanism for the previous GSTIN and credit flow to the amended GSTIN. Reconcile the credit already claimed and reversed.

8. Can ITC be claimed on goods imported for exempt supplies?

Credit attributable to exempt supplies may be restricted or require reversal under the applicable provisions, including Section 17 and Rules 42/43 where relevant. Perform the appropriate apportionment rather than claiming the full amount automatically.

9. Can ITC be claimed on imported machinery?

Generally, import IGST on machinery used for taxable business operations may be eligible. Check business use, blocked-credit provisions, construction-related restrictions and Section 16(3) before claiming.

10. Who can claim ITC in a high-sea sale?

The eligible buyer/importer whose details appear in the final customs Bill of Entry and who satisfies the ITC conditions should evaluate the import IGST credit. The high-sea sale agreement alone is not a substitute for the customs document.

11. Is IGST on import of services covered in this article?

No. Import of services generally involves a separate reverse-charge analysis under the IGST Act. The document, tax payment and credit process differ from import of goods supported by a Bill of Entry.

12. Can we claim import IGST in a later month if it was kept pending in IMS?

The October 2025 advisory states that pending Bills of Entry remain on the IMS dashboard and can be accepted in future months. Check the current portal status, applicable return period and statutory time limit before claiming.
