1. At a glance: the practical answer

Guarantees are common in corporate borrowing: a parent company may guarantee a subsidiary’s loan, a group entity may support another group entity, a director may give a personal guarantee, or a bank may issue a guarantee in favour of a lender or project owner. These arrangements look similar commercially but are not the same transaction for GST.

Core distinction: a corporate guarantee supplied by one related person to another in support of a bank/financial-institution facility is specifically valued under Rule 28(2) of the CGST Rules, subject to its precise scope and exceptions. A director’s personal guarantee is not valued under Rule 28(2); CBIC Circular 204/16/2023-GST gives a separate approach. A bank guarantee issued by a bank is a service supplied by the bank and is not automatically valued under the related-party corporate-guarantee formula.

This guide explains the legal framework and practical accounting, documentation, invoicing, return reporting and audit controls. The applicable treatment depends on the actual parties, guarantee instrument, date, consideration, place of supply, recipient’s location and whether the arrangement falls within the notified rule.

2. Understand the three different guarantee arrangements

1. Corporate guarantee

A company or other legal entity undertakes an obligation to a lender to answer for another entity’s debt or performance if the borrower defaults. In the GST context, the specific Rule 28(2) mechanism is relevant to a supplier providing a corporate guarantee to a banking company or financial institution on behalf of a related recipient, as described in the rule.

2. Personal guarantee by a director or promoter

An individual, commonly a director, promises the lender that the individual may be personally liable if the company does not meet its borrowing obligations. The individual and company may be related persons for GST purposes. This must be analysed under Schedule I and the specific valuation clarification for personal guarantees, not by simply applying the corporate-guarantee percentage.

3. Bank guarantee

A bank issues an undertaking in favour of a beneficiary, typically against an application, commission, margin money or other bank terms. The bank is the service provider to its customer. This is distinct from a company giving a corporate guarantee to support a related company’s borrowing.

3. GST legal framework and key provisions

Section 7 and Schedule I – supply without consideration

Section 7 of the CGST Act defines supply. Schedule I includes specified activities between related persons or distinct persons in the course or furtherance of business even where no consideration is charged. A guarantee between related entities therefore requires a supply analysis even if the guarantor receives no fee.

Section 15 – value of taxable supply

Section 15 is the general valuation provision. For related-party supplies, Rule 28 provides the valuation framework. The special corporate-guarantee provision in Rule 28(2) operates notwithstanding the general Rule 28(1) mechanism, subject to the rule’s wording and its provisos.

Rule 28(1) – related/distinct persons

In general, value is determined by open market value, then like-kind-and-quality value, and thereafter Rules 30 or 31 in the prescribed order. The second proviso to Rule 28(1) deems the invoice-declared value to be open market value where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit, subject to its terms.

Rule 28(2) – specific corporate guarantee valuation

Rule 28(2), inserted by Notification No. 52/2023-Central Tax dated 26 October 2023 and subsequently amended, prescribes a deemed value for the specified corporate guarantee service. The rule now refers to a recipient who is a related person located in India and provides for one per cent of the amount of guarantee offered per annum, or actual consideration, whichever is higher. It also contains an exception to the extent of the second proviso to Rule 28(1), as reflected in the amended text.

Important: Do not use an isolated extract of the original 2023 wording. Apply the amended Rule 28(2), including its India-recipient wording, per-annum basis and exception, for the relevant transaction period. Confirm the latest Gazette text and any subsequent amendments before filing.

Section 31, Rule 47 and tax invoice

Where the transaction is a taxable supply, determine invoice timing and particulars under the applicable provisions. For a continuing guarantee, the commercial document, effective period, renewal/extension and valuation period should be documented so the tax invoice reflects the legally relevant supply period and value.

Sections 12/13 and 20 – time of supply and IGST

Determine whether the service is intra-State or inter-State using the supplier and recipient locations and the applicable place-of-supply provisions. For cross-border or non-resident group arrangements, separately analyse import/export of services, intermediary or other classification issues where relevant, and the applicable reverse-charge notification. Do not assume that every guarantee attracts the same tax head or payment mechanism.

5. Corporate guarantee: applying Rule 28(2)

Begin with the legal instrument and the parties. Identify the entity providing the guarantee, the entity whose obligation is supported, the lender/financial institution, the amount guaranteed, the guarantee’s effective period and any guarantee fee or other consideration.

Step-by-step application

  1. Confirm the guarantor and borrower/recipient are related persons within the Act and that the recipient is located in India for the amended Rule 28(2) scope.
  2. Confirm the guarantee is provided to a banking company or financial institution on behalf of the related recipient, and is the kind of corporate guarantee covered by the rule.
  3. Identify the amount of guarantee offered under the instrument and the relevant effective period.
  4. Calculate the rule-based amount at one per cent per annum, proportionately for the relevant period where applicable, and compare it with actual consideration.
  5. Use the higher amount, subject to the statutory exception/proviso and the precise rule applicable to the period.
  6. Document the tax rate, tax head, place of supply, invoice, accounting entry and return disclosure.
Illustration – guarantee for a full year: Parent P Ltd guarantees ₹10 crore of a related Indian subsidiary’s bank borrowing. Assuming the arrangement is within Rule 28(2), the relevant annual deemed value at 1% is ₹10 lakh. If actual consideration for the relevant annual supply is ₹12 lakh, compare and use the higher figure under the rule. If the agreed/actual consideration is ₹6 lakh, the rule-based ₹10 lakh amount is higher. The output GST is then computed on the applicable taxable value at the correct rate and tax head.
Do not confuse guarantee amount with loan disbursement. The guaranteed amount in the guarantee instrument is the starting figure; it is not automatically the same as the amount drawn, outstanding on a particular date, sanctioned facility, or total group borrowing. Read the instrument and relevant rule carefully.

6. Guarantee period, renewal, amendment and multiple facilities

Rule 28(2) uses a per-annum measure. The working paper should therefore capture the period for which the guarantee is offered and any changes in the amount or coverage. Avoid mechanically applying 1% once to every renewal, or applying 1% every month without a legal basis.

  • New guarantee: record execution date, effective date, expiry date and guaranteed amount.
  • Renewal: determine whether the renewal creates a further period of guarantee service or modifies an existing undertaking; document the contract and period.
  • Increase in limit: compare the original and revised guarantee amounts and establish the incremental guarantee period/amount.
  • Reduction or release: retain lender confirmation and release documentation; assess the effect on the taxable value and any adjustment under the applicable law.
  • Multiple lenders: maintain a lender-wise register and avoid double counting the same undertaking while ensuring separate guarantees are not omitted.

Where the guarantee is valid for less than a year, prepare a transparent period-based computation consistent with the notified per-annum language, the contract and applicable tax rules. Obtain a written tax position for unusual or disputed date/period situations.

7. Full ITC recipient and the Rule 28 proviso

The second proviso to Rule 28(1) generally deems the value declared in the invoice to be open market value where the recipient is eligible for full input tax credit. The amended Rule 28(2) expressly preserves the operation of that proviso through its exception wording. Accordingly, an eligible recipient’s position must be examined before mechanically applying the one-per-cent formula.

For this purpose, do not rely only on the recipient’s GST registration or a broad statement that the group claims ITC. Verify eligibility for full ITC on the specific supply, including blocked credits, exempt/non-business use, proportionate reversals, registration and documentation. Keep the recipient’s declaration and supporting analysis on file.

Practical control: Obtain an annual certificate or transaction-specific confirmation from the recipient describing its eligibility for full ITC for the guarantee service. Independently review the GST return profile and nature of business. If full ITC is not available, retain the computation under Rule 28(2) and actual consideration comparison.

8. Personal guarantee by directors and promoters

Circular No. 204/16/2023-GST addresses a director’s personal guarantee to a bank or financial institution for credit facilities to the company. It treats the director and company as related persons and recognises the Schedule I supply question even when no consideration is charged. However, the circular explains that the valuation is to be considered under Rule 28(1), not the specific corporate-guarantee Rule 28(2).

The circular states that where the director provides the guarantee without consideration and the open market value is not available, the value may be treated as zero in the circumstances described in the circular, having regard to the RBI directions that restrict companies from paying remuneration/commission to directors for providing personal guarantees. The conclusion is fact-specific and should not be generalized to every promoter or individual guarantee.

Personal guarantee checklist

  • Is the guarantor a director, promoter, shareholder, or unrelated individual?
  • Is the guarantee provided in the director’s personal capacity, or by an entity?
  • Is any guarantee fee, commission, indemnity, benefit or other consideration paid or payable?
  • Are the relevant RBI directions and the company’s governance documents applicable?
  • Does the guarantee involve a related-party transaction and appropriate board disclosure?
  • Has the file documented why Rule 28(2) is not being used and how value was determined under Rule 28(1)?
Fee or consideration exists: if the director is paid or receives consideration, do not automatically conclude that taxable value is nil. Examine the actual consideration, the relevant valuation rules, legal restrictions and the precise facts. Obtain advice where a director’s fee or other benefit is proposed.

9. Bank guarantees: GST on the bank’s service

When a bank issues a bank guarantee for its customer, the bank is ordinarily supplying a financial service to the customer. The bank may charge commission, processing fees, documentation charges and applicable taxes. That transaction is different from a corporate guarantee issued by a parent company for a related borrower.

For a bank guarantee, review the bank advice, debit note, facility letter and tax invoice. Check the service description, taxable fees, GST rate, place of supply, recipient GSTIN and whether the invoice is issued to the correct legal entity and registration.

ItemFinance team action
Bank commission/feeBook to the appropriate finance/bank charges ledger and reconcile with bank advice.
GST on bank feeEvaluate ITC eligibility, business use, blocked-credit restrictions and invoice compliance.
Margin money / cash collateralNormally track separately as deposit/earmarked balance based on bank terms; do not assume it is the service fee.
Guarantee amendment/extension feeReview each bank charge and ensure the invoice and accounting period are correct.

A bank guarantee may be backed by cash margin, fixed deposit lien, property collateral or counter-guarantee. Those supporting arrangements require separate analysis and should not be conflated with the bank’s guarantee commission.

10. Accounting treatment and illustrative journal entries

Accounting depends on the applicable accounting framework, contractual rights and obligations, likelihood of invocation, financial-statement disclosures and whether the guarantor has a reimbursement/indemnity right. GST valuation and financial accounting recognition are related but are not identical questions.

Use appropriate account names and record the tax liability in the period determined under the applicable time-of-supply rules. Where there is no commercial fee but a deemed taxable value applies, the accounting recognition of income/receivable and any related-party balance needs a documented accounting-policy assessment; do not assume the GST deemed value automatically dictates the financial-accounting entry.

Classify the underlying cost consistently with applicable accounting standards and the facts. ITC is recognized only if the statutory conditions are satisfied, the invoice is valid, the supply is received, and no restriction/reversal applies.

C. Bank guarantee commission

Debit bank charges/finance cost (or another appropriate account) and eligible input GST, and credit bank or bank payable. Assess whether borrowing costs are directly attributable to a qualifying asset and whether capitalization is required under the applicable accounting framework.

D. Director personal guarantee

Where no consideration is paid and the documented GST valuation conclusion under Circular 204 results in nil value, ordinarily there is no output tax entry for a notional GST amount. The company should still retain guarantee documentation and evaluate financial-statement contingent-liability/related-party disclosures under the applicable accounting framework.

11. Invoicing, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and ITC workflow

Supplier / guarantor workflow

  1. Maintain a guarantee register with recipient GSTIN, relationship, instrument number, lender, amount, validity, consideration, full-ITC status and valuation method.
  2. Prepare a tax position note establishing supply, applicable rule, taxable value, rate, place of supply and tax head.
  3. Issue the tax invoice to the correct recipient in accordance with Section 31 and Rule 47, including required particulars and a clear description of the guarantee service and period.
  4. Report taxable outward supply in the appropriate GSTR-1 table and pay the tax through GSTR-3B, subject to the applicable return instructions and classification.
  5. Reconcile invoice register, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, electronic liability ledger and recipient confirmation.

Recipient / borrower workflow

  1. Match the supplier invoice to the guarantee instrument and internal approval.
  2. Verify invoice details and appearance in the relevant auto-drafted statement as applicable.
  3. Determine ITC eligibility and apportionment/reversal under Sections 16 and 17 and applicable rules.
  4. Record ITC only when conditions are met and maintain invoice-wise evidence.
  5. Reconcile ITC claimed to books, GSTR-2B and the supplier’s tax invoice; follow up on missing or incorrect reporting.
Return table numbers and portal validations can change. Confirm the current GST portal instructions for the relevant tax period before filing; the article intentionally describes the workflow rather than hardcoding a potentially outdated table number.

12. Cross-border and distinct-person situations

Cross-border guarantee arrangements require a separate analysis and should not be mechanically treated like a domestic parent-subsidiary guarantee. Identify the actual supplier and recipient, their establishments, the location of the recipient, whether the recipient is related and located in India for Rule 28(2), and the place-of-supply rule that applies to the service.

Where a foreign parent guarantees an Indian subsidiary’s borrowing, examine the statutory wording of Rule 28(2), the place of supply, import-of-services provisions and reverse-charge notifications in force for the relevant period. Where an Indian entity guarantees a foreign affiliate’s borrowing, separately evaluate export-of-services conditions, recipient location, consideration, place of supply and any other applicable restriction. The existence of a foreign lender alone does not establish that the recipient of the guarantee service is outside India.

Also distinguish a guarantee between distinct registrations of the same legal entity from a guarantee between separate legal entities. Distinct-person rules under Section 25 and Schedule I may be relevant to transactions between registrations, but Rule 28(2) should only be applied where its stated conditions are met. Maintain a legal-entity and GST-registration map for group companies.

13. Worked examples for finance teams

Example 1 – no separate fee, domestic related company

Alpha Ltd provides a guarantee of ₹25 crore for a related Indian subsidiary’s bank facility for one year. Subject to Rule 28(2) applying and the recipient not qualifying for the relevant full-ITC proviso treatment, the rule-based annual value is ₹25 lakh. Compare this with actual consideration, if any, and apply the higher value as prescribed.

Example 2 – actual consideration exceeds deemed value

Beta Ltd provides a covered guarantee of ₹8 crore for one year and receives a documented guarantee fee of ₹11 lakh. The annual one-per-cent amount is ₹8 lakh. On the stated assumptions, actual consideration of ₹11 lakh is higher and is used for valuation.

Example 3 – six-month guarantee

Gamma Ltd provides a covered guarantee of ₹12 crore for six months. The simple period illustration of one per cent per annum gives ₹6 lakh for six months before comparing with actual consideration and considering the rule’s precise operation and facts. Retain the executed start/end dates and a clear day/month computation.

Example 4 – director’s personal guarantee

A director signs a personal guarantee without receiving consideration for the company’s bank facility. The company and director document the facts and apply Circular 204/16/2023-GST and Rule 28(1) valuation analysis. Do not apply the one-per-cent corporate-guarantee rule to the director’s personal guarantee.

Example 5 – bank issues a guarantee

A bank charges ₹2 lakh commission for issuing a bank guarantee. The bank’s service fee is analysed under the bank’s applicable GST treatment and invoice. The corporate-guarantee deemed valuation formula is not applied merely because the instrument is called a “guarantee.”

14. Documents and audit evidence to retain

  • Executed corporate guarantee / personal guarantee / bank guarantee and amendments.
  • Loan sanction letter, facility agreement and lender correspondence.
  • Board resolutions, delegated authority and related-party approvals.
  • Group structure chart, relationship analysis and GST registration details.
  • Guarantee amount and period reconciliation, including lender-wise facility schedule.
  • Actual fee/consideration agreement, debit notes, invoices and payment evidence.
  • Full-ITC eligibility confirmation and supporting review where relying on the Rule 28 proviso.
  • Valuation calculation, tax opinion and approval by the tax/finance head.
  • GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, electronic liability ledger and recipient ITC reconciliation.
  • Financial statement accounting memo and related-party/contingent liability disclosures.

For audit, the guarantee register should reconcile to bank confirmations, loan schedules, board minutes, related-party disclosures, and tax returns. Confirm that guarantees issued near year-end or renewed during the year have not been omitted.

16. Common mistakes and how to avoid them

  1. Applying one per cent to every guarantee regardless of supplier, recipient, instrument or statutory scope.
  2. Applying the corporate-guarantee formula to a director’s personal guarantee.
  3. Assuming every bank guarantee is a corporate guarantee supplied by the borrower’s group.
  4. Using the sanction limit or loan outstanding without reading the amount actually guaranteed.
  5. Ignoring the per-annum wording, guarantee term, renewal, amendment or partial release.
  6. Ignoring the full-ITC proviso or relying on an unsupported blanket certificate.
  7. Failing to distinguish the lender/beneficiary from the GST recipient of the guarantee service.
  8. Issuing an invoice to the wrong GST registration or reporting under the wrong tax head.
  9. Assuming a GST deemed value automatically equals accounting revenue or a receivable.
  10. Not reconciling guarantees in bank confirmations with GST returns and related-party disclosures.

17. Case law and interpretive status

The principal directly relevant official guidance identified for the director/corporate guarantee issue is CBIC Circular No. 204/16/2023-GST dated 27 October 2023, read with Rule 28(2) and its amendments. The statutory rule and binding circular should be analysed before relying on a general judicial decision concerning valuation or related-party supplies.

This article does not name a court judgment as directly deciding the precise current Rule 28(2) formula unless the judgment, relevant paragraphs, appeal status and applicability have been verified. A decision on a different service, a pre-GST guarantee, a bank’s guarantee commission, or an earlier version of Rule 28 may not decide the current issue. For a live assessment, search current High Court/Supreme Court and advance-ruling databases using the exact transaction facts and confirm whether a ruling is binding only within its statutory jurisdiction.

Research discipline: Before citing a case in a notice reply or opinion, verify the full judgment from an official court or reliable legal database, the exact issue decided, date, jurisdiction, subsequent appeal/stay status, and whether the case concerns the same statutory version. Do not treat an advance ruling for another applicant as universally binding.

18. Practical year-end checklist

  1. Obtain the complete list of guarantees issued, received, renewed, amended and released during the year.
  2. Match each item to its signed instrument and bank confirmation.
  3. Classify as corporate guarantee, personal guarantee, bank guarantee or another arrangement.
  4. Identify supplier, recipient, related-person status, location and GST registration.
  5. Confirm applicable Rule 28 version and whether Rule 28(2) conditions are satisfied.
  6. Document guarantee amount, validity period, actual consideration and valuation calculation.
  7. Test full-ITC eligibility before relying on the relevant Rule 28(1) proviso.
  8. Review place of supply, tax head, rate, time of supply and invoice compliance.
  9. Reconcile output tax, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and recipient ITC records.
  10. Review accounting treatment, contingent liabilities and related-party disclosures.
  11. Obtain finance head sign-off and retain a complete audit trail.

19. Frequently asked questions

Is a corporate guarantee taxable if no fee is charged?

It may be treated as a supply between related persons under Schedule I even without consideration. Where the specified Rule 28(2) conditions apply, valuation follows that rule, subject to its terms and exceptions.

Is the 1% based on loan outstanding?

Do not assume so. Read the guarantee instrument and apply the rule to the amount of guarantee offered and the relevant period.

Does Rule 28(2) apply to a director’s personal guarantee?

No. Circular 204/16/2023-GST specifically distinguishes director personal guarantees and directs valuation under Rule 28(1), not Rule 28(2).

What if the recipient can claim full ITC?

Examine the second proviso to Rule 28(1) and the exception wording in Rule 28(2). Confirm full ITC eligibility for that specific supply and maintain evidence.

Is a bank guarantee the same as a corporate guarantee?

No. A bank guarantee is ordinarily a bank’s service to its customer. A corporate guarantee is an undertaking given by an entity, often to support a related entity’s borrowing.

Does a guarantee create accounting income at the deemed GST value?

Not automatically. GST valuation and financial accounting recognition are separate analyses. Document the applicable accounting framework and contractual rights/obligations.

What should be checked for a foreign group company?

Determine the actual recipient, its location, the Rule 28(2) scope, place of supply, import/export conditions and reverse-charge provisions applicable to the period.

Can this guide be used as a legal opinion?

No. It is a practical educational guide. Apply the law and notifications effective for the transaction date and obtain professional advice for material or disputed exposures.
