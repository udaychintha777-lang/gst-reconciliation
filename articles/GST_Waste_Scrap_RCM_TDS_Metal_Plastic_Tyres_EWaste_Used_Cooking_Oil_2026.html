GST on Waste & Scrap – New RCM and 2% TDS Rules for Metal, Plastic, Tyres, E-Waste & Used Cooking Oil

Buying or selling scrap? From 1 November 2026, the GST framework covers additional specified waste and scrap categories. Learn when the buyer must deduct 2% GST TDS, when RCM applies, how to identify HSN coverage, and how to account for tax and ITC without confusing the two mechanisms.

1. Executive summary – first identify the supplier's GST status

The most important distinction is whether the scrap supplier is registered or unregistered. For specified categories, a registered buyer may have to deduct GST TDS when buying from a registered supplier, but pay GST under reverse charge when buying from an unregistered supplier. These are alternative mechanisms for the same purchase; do not apply both to the same supply.

Registered seller → registered buyer

Buyer deducts GST TDS at 2%, subject to Section 51 and its conditions. Supplier reports the sale and receives TDS credit in the electronic cash ledger.

Unregistered seller → registered buyer

Buyer pays GST under RCM on specified goods. Buyer reports and pays the liability; eligible ITC is a separate question.

Registered seller → unregistered buyer

No GST TDS under this specified registered-recipient mechanism. Seller follows the normal forward-charge treatment, if taxable.

Unregistered seller → unregistered buyer

Neither the specified recipient-side RCM nor GST TDS framework is triggered merely by this entry. Check normal registration and taxability rules.

Key date The expanded framework for specified plastic scrap, tyre scrap, electrical and electronic waste and scrap, and used cooking oil is effective from 1 November 2026. Metal scrap was already covered by the earlier framework from 10 October 2024.

2. What changed from 1 November 2026?

The 57th GST Council recommended extending the existing metal-scrap TDS and RCM model to specified waste and scrap of plastics, tyres, electrical and electronic goods, and used cooking oil. The Government issued amendments to the rate, registration and TDS notifications to implement the change from 1 November 2026.

MechanismEarlier positionPosition from 1 November 2026
Metal scrapRCM on unregistered-to-registered supplies and 2% TDS on registered-to-registered supplies from 10 October 2024.Continues under the existing framework.
Plastic waste and scrapNot covered by this special scrap RCM/TDS entry.Included for specified HSN 3915 supplies.
Waste and scrap of tyresNot covered by this special scrap RCM/TDS entry.Included for specified HSN 4004 supplies.
Electrical and electronic waste and scrapNot covered by this special scrap RCM/TDS entry.Included for specified HSN 8549 supplies.
Used cooking oilNot covered by the earlier metal-scrap entry.Included in the specified TDS and RCM framework under heading 1518, subject to the notification wording.
Important: This is a classification-driven entry. The word “scrap” in a commercial description is not enough. Match the goods to the exact tariff heading and notification description before applying TDS or RCM.

3. Which goods are covered? HSN-based scope

Classification warning: A discarded item may still be classifiable as a second-hand usable product rather than waste or scrap. For example, a working used laptop sold for reuse is not automatically electrical and electronic waste under heading 8549. Obtain product specifications, photographs, weighment details, purchase records and an HSN classification note.

For mixed loads, separate the goods by category and classification. A single purchase order labelled “scrap” may contain different tariff headings and different tax treatments.

Remember TDS is not the GST on the sale. It is an amount withheld from the supplier's payment and credited to the supplier's electronic cash ledger. RCM is the GST liability on the purchase that the registered recipient must pay to the Government.

5. 2% GST TDS when registered supplier sells to registered buyer

From 1 November 2026, a registered person receiving specified supplies from another registered person is brought within the amended notification under Section 51. The recipient is required to deduct GST TDS at the prescribed aggregate rate of 2%, subject to the statutory conditions of Section 51.

How is 2% split?

Illustration

A registered scrap dealer sells qualifying plastic scrap to a registered manufacturing company for a taxable value of ₹5,00,000. Assume the applicable GST on the sale is charged separately and the Section 51 threshold and other conditions are met.

ParticularsAmount
Taxable value₹5,00,000
GST on invoice (illustrative 18%; actual rate depends on classification)₹90,000
Invoice total₹5,90,000
GST TDS at 2% of taxable value, excluding GST shown separately₹10,000
Net amount payable to supplier₹5,80,000

The buyer deposits the ₹10,000 TDS with the Government and files GSTR-7. The supplier can claim the TDS amount credited to its electronic cash ledger, subject to portal reflection and reconciliation.

Threshold and base: Apply the conditions in Section 51 and the relevant rules, including the prescribed contract-value threshold where applicable. TDS is calculated on the value of taxable supply excluding CGST, SGST, IGST and cess indicated in the invoice. Do not calculate 2% on the GST-inclusive invoice total.

Where the contract covers multiple deliveries, examine the aggregate value under the contract rather than looking only at one invoice in isolation. Also check the statutory exclusions and the identities of the supplier and recipient.

6. RCM when an unregistered person sells specified scrap to a registered buyer

Notification No. 06/2024-Central Tax (Rate) already brought metal scrap supplied by an unregistered person to a registered person under RCM from 10 October 2024. The 2026 amendments extend the corresponding framework to the newly specified categories from 1 November 2026.

Does RCM apply if the unregistered seller is below the registration threshold?

Yes. The 57th GST Council FAQ specifically clarifies that the registered recipient must pay GST under RCM even where the unregistered supplier is below the normal turnover threshold for registration.

Illustration

A registered factory purchases qualifying e-waste scrap from an unregistered individual for ₹2,00,000. The seller is below the registration threshold and does not charge GST.

ParticularsAmount
Purchase value₹2,00,000
Assumed applicable GST rate for illustration only18%
RCM payable by registered buyer₹36,000
Payment to unregistered seller₹2,00,000
GST payable separately by buyer under RCM₹36,000

The rate in this illustration is only a calculation assumption. The buyer must establish the actual HSN and applicable rate for the goods purchased. RCM tax is discharged by the recipient through the electronic cash ledger; eligible ITC is evaluated separately after payment.

Does the seller need to issue a GST tax invoice?

An unregistered seller cannot issue a GST tax invoice. The registered recipient must examine the self-invoice requirement under Section 31(3)(f), issue the prescribed document where applicable, and issue a payment voucher under Section 31(3)(g) when making payment to the supplier, as relevant to the transaction and rules.

7. Does a scrap supplier have to register from the first rupee?

No. The 2026 amendment to the registration exemption notification removes the specified waste and scrap categories from the general exemption available to persons making supplies liable to tax under Section 9(3). The supplier is required to obtain registration when the aggregate turnover crosses the applicable threshold under Section 22 or when another compulsory-registration provision applies.

Supplier situationPractical result
Unregistered supplier below thresholdMay remain unregistered if no other registration trigger applies. However, a registered buyer must pay RCM on the specified supply.
Supplier crosses applicable thresholdMust obtain GST registration within the statutory framework and charge GST under forward charge on subsequent taxable supplies.
Registered scrap supplier sells to registered buyerNormal forward-charge invoice; recipient-side 2% TDS applies where Section 51 conditions are satisfied.
Supplier is registered only because of other taxable activitiesRegistered status must be checked at the time of supply; the scrap purchase from that supplier may fall under the TDS route.
Do not confuse registration exemption with RCM. The seller's turnover being below the registration threshold does not cancel the registered buyer's RCM liability. These are separate questions.

8. GST rate and valuation – there is no single “scrap GST rate”

The RCM/TDS notifications identify the supplier-recipient mechanism and specified goods. They do not mean every scrap category has one common GST rate. The rate depends on the exact HSN classification, description and applicable rate notification.

QuestionCorrect approach
Is GST always 18% on scrap?No. Determine the correct HSN and rate entry for the exact goods and tax period.
Does TDS change the supplier's GST rate?No. TDS is a withholding mechanism. The supplier's output tax rate remains governed by the rate notification.
Is RCM paid on the value net of freight?Apply Section 15 and the valuation rules. Include amounts that form part of the consideration/value of supply; examine freight and incidental charges based on contract and supply terms.
Is GST payable on weighment, sorting or processing charges?Identify whether these are separate services or part of a composite supply, and determine the supplier and charge mechanism for each.
Does a sale of reusable second-hand goods count as scrap?Not automatically. Determine classification from condition, intended use and tariff description.

Maintain a written HSN classification note for recurring scrap purchases. The note should describe the physical nature of the goods, condition, intended use, processing undertaken and supporting tariff references.

9. Documents and invoicing – buyer's checklist

Purchase from registered scrap supplier

  • Obtain supplier GSTIN and verify active registration status on the date of supply.
  • Obtain tax invoice with correct HSN, description, quantity, taxable value and GST rate.
  • Identify whether goods fall within the specified waste/scrap headings.
  • Check Section 51 threshold and deduct TDS where applicable.
  • Record TDS payable separately and reconcile GSTR-7 with supplier ledger.
  • Retain weighment slips, gate entry, inspection report, purchase order and transport documents.

Purchase from unregistered scrap supplier

  • Obtain seller declaration of unregistered status and PAN/address details.
  • Obtain purchase voucher, delivery challan or commercial document describing the goods.
  • Prepare HSN classification and RCM applicability working.
  • Issue self-invoice under Section 31(3)(f), where required, and payment voucher under Section 31(3)(g).
  • Calculate GST under RCM at the correct rate and discharge through cash ledger.
  • Retain bank proof, weighment slip, gate entry, vehicle details, e-way bill where applicable and stock entry.

10. GSTR-7, TDS certificate and supplier's cash ledger

Where GST TDS applies, the recipient/deductor must deposit the deduction and furnish Form GSTR-7 within the prescribed timeline. The supplier/deductee should monitor the TDS credit reflected on the GST portal and reconcile it with the buyer's certificate and ledger.

Compliance stepResponsible partyControl
Deduct GST TDSRegistered buyerDeduct at 2% on the prescribed taxable value, subject to Section 51.
Deposit and file GSTR-7Registered buyerFile by the 10th day of the month succeeding the month of deduction, subject to current due-date notifications/extensions.
Issue TDS certificate in GSTR-7ARegistered buyer / portal processVerify certificate availability and particulars after GSTR-7 filing.
Claim TDS amountSupplierReconcile amount credited to electronic cash ledger; this is not input tax credit.
Important distinction: GST TDS credit is reflected in the supplier's electronic cash ledger and can be used in accordance with the GST law. It is not ITC in the electronic credit ledger and should not be posted as input GST credit in the buyer's books.

11. RCM payment, GSTR-3B and ITC eligibility

  1. Determine the taxable value and correct GST rate for the specified goods.
  2. Report RCM liability in the appropriate GSTR-3B table for inward supplies liable to reverse charge.
  3. Discharge the RCM tax through the electronic cash ledger; existing ITC balance cannot be used to pay RCM liability.
  4. After payment, evaluate ITC under Sections 16 and 17 and the applicable rules.
  5. Reconcile RCM liability, cash payment and eligible ITC separately; do not assume that tax paid under RCM is automatically eligible as credit.
Buyer / use of scrapITC outcome
Regular taxpayer using scrap in taxable manufacturing/businessPotentially eligible after RCM payment, subject to Section 16 conditions and restrictions.
Composition taxpayerMust discharge applicable RCM but cannot claim ITC while under composition scheme.
Scrap used for exempt suppliesITC attributable to exempt supplies is restricted under Section 17(2) and Rules 42/43, as applicable.
Personal or non-business useITC is not available to the extent of non-business use.
Blocked credit category or other statutory restrictionCheck Section 17(5) and other conditions independently.
Cash-flow point: A business may have to pay RCM in cash first and claim eligible ITC separately. The tax can be creditable in substance, but it still creates a cash-flow and compliance obligation.

14. Internal controls for factories, contractors and scrap dealers

  1. Create separate supplier master flags: Registered / Unregistered / Composition, with effective-from and effective-to dates.
  2. Capture HSN at item-master level and restrict the special RCM/TDS logic to the specified tariff headings.
  3. Require a purchase approval note confirming whether the goods are reusable second-hand items or waste/scrap.
  4. Capture weighbridge quantity, quality grade, moisture/contamination deductions and final accepted quantity.
  5. Separate registered and unregistered purchase workflows in the ERP.
  6. For registered suppliers, auto-calculate 2% TDS only after checking Section 51 applicability and contract threshold.
  7. For unregistered suppliers, calculate RCM at the applicable HSN rate and block payment closure until RCM liability is reviewed.
  8. Reconcile GSTR-7, GSTR-7A and supplier ledgers monthly.
  9. Reconcile RCM purchase register with GSTR-3B and cash ledger monthly.
  10. Review supplier registration status before each significant purchase and when the supplier informs of a GSTIN change.
  11. Retain purchase orders, weighment slips, gate entry, delivery challans, transport records, photos and quality inspection reports.
  12. Review the HSN classification and rates when tariff descriptions or notifications change.
Recommended ERP report Prepare a monthly “Scrap GST Control Register” with supplier GSTIN, status, HSN, goods description, taxable value, forward-charge GST, TDS deducted, RCM liability, cash paid, ITC claimed, GSTR-7 reference and GSTR-3B period.

15. Common mistakes that trigger GST notices

  • Deducting 2% TDS on a purchase from an unregistered supplier instead of paying RCM.
  • Paying RCM on a purchase from a registered supplier where forward charge applies, resulting in duplicate tax treatment.
  • Applying 2% TDS to every item described as scrap without verifying the HSN and notification scope.
  • Assuming all scrap attracts 18% GST without checking the rate notification.
  • Calculating TDS on the GST-inclusive invoice amount instead of the prescribed taxable value.
  • Failing to check the Section 51 contract-value threshold and exclusions.
  • Claiming RCM ITC before discharging the RCM liability in cash.
  • Treating GST TDS as ITC rather than a cash-ledger credit of the supplier.
  • Failing to issue self-invoice/payment voucher where required for purchases from unregistered persons.
  • Not reporting RCM in GSTR-3B or not filing GSTR-7 for TDS deductions.
  • Classifying usable second-hand equipment as e-waste without a tariff analysis.
  • Not maintaining weighment and delivery evidence to establish the actual quantity and nature of scrap.

16. Frequently asked questions

1. From which date do the new scrap TDS and RCM provisions apply?

The expanded framework for plastic scrap, tyre scrap, electrical and electronic waste and scrap, and used cooking oil applies from 1 November 2026. Metal scrap was already covered from 10 October 2024.

2. Is GST TDS applicable on every scrap purchase?

No. The specified TDS mechanism applies to covered goods supplied by a registered person to a registered recipient, subject to Section 51 and its conditions.

3. Is RCM applicable when scrap is purchased from an unregistered person?

For the specified categories and tariff headings, yes, where an unregistered supplier supplies to a registered recipient. The recipient pays GST under RCM.

4. If the unregistered seller has turnover below ₹20 lakh, is RCM still payable?

Yes. The GST Council FAQ specifically clarifies that the registered buyer must pay RCM even if the unregistered seller is below the registration threshold.

5. Is 2% TDS deducted from the GST-inclusive amount?

Generally no. Section 51 and the rules provide for deduction on the value of taxable supply excluding the tax components shown in the invoice. Apply the statutory threshold and calculation rules.

6. Who pays GST when a registered dealer sells scrap to a registered factory?

The registered supplier pays GST under forward charge. The registered buyer deducts 2% GST TDS where the specified entry and Section 51 conditions are satisfied.

7. Can a composition taxpayer be liable for RCM on scrap?

A registered composition taxpayer receiving a notified RCM supply should evaluate and discharge the RCM liability. A composition taxpayer cannot claim ITC while under the composition scheme.

8. Can the buyer claim ITC of RCM paid on scrap?

A regular taxpayer may claim eligible ITC after payment, subject to Sections 16 and 17, business use, exempt supplies and other statutory conditions. RCM payment alone does not guarantee ITC.

9. Does GST TDS credit go to the buyer's ITC ledger?

No. TDS credit is reflected in the supplier's electronic cash ledger. It is not input tax credit for the buyer.

10. Is every old computer or used machine e-waste?

No. A functional second-hand product sold for reuse may not be waste or scrap under heading 8549. Examine condition, classification and the tariff description.

11. Is the GST rate on all scrap 18%?

No. The rate depends on the exact goods and applicable HSN rate entry. The RCM/TDS notifications do not create a uniform rate for all scrap.

12. Does the buyer deduct income-tax TDS also?

Income-tax TDS and GST TDS are separate laws. Examine the Income-tax Act independently based on the nature of the transaction, seller and applicable provisions.

13. What return is filed for GST TDS?

The deductor files Form GSTR-7 and the supplier reconciles the corresponding TDS credit and certificate.

14. Does RCM apply if the buyer is also unregistered?

The specified recipient-side RCM entry applies where the recipient is a registered person. Other normal taxability and registration provisions must still be checked.

Practical takeaway

For every scrap purchase, first confirm the exact HSN and whether the seller is registered on the date of supply. If both parties are registered, test GST TDS under Section 51. If the seller is unregistered and the buyer is registered, test RCM and pay the tax in cash. Maintain separate TDS and RCM ledgers, reconcile GSTR-7 and GSTR-3B, and claim ITC only after independently testing eligibility.
