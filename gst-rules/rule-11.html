CGST Rule 11 – Separate Registration for Multiple Places of Business

Understand when a business can obtain separate GST registrations for multiple places of business within the same State or Union Territory, the conditions attached to each registration, inter-unit supplies and the practical finance controls required.

1. First, Understand the Current Rule 11

Current Rule 11 is about separate registration for multiple places of business within a State or Union territory. It is important not to rely on older articles that describe Rule 11 as “separate registration for multiple business verticals.” That was the earlier version of the rule.

Current practical meaning: If a person has multiple places of business within the same State/UT and seeks separate registration for those places under Section 25(2), Rule 11 sets out the conditions and application process.

The rule was substituted with effect from 1 February 2019. Therefore, for current compliance, the operative concept is multiple places of business, not the old “business vertical” test.

2. Rule 11 – Clause-by-Clause Explanation

Rule 11(1) – Separate registration for each eligible place

A person having multiple places of business within a State or Union territory, where separate registration is required/opted for under Section 25(2), may be granted separate registration for each such place, subject to the conditions prescribed in Rule 11.

Condition (a) – More than one place of business

The person must have more than one place of business as defined in Section 2(85) of the CGST Act.

This is important because a company cannot create an artificial second GST registration merely by giving a different internal department, project code or cost centre the label of a “place.” There must be a legally relevant place of business.

Condition (b) – Composition restriction

A person cannot have one separately registered place paying tax under Section 10 while another place of the same person is paying tax under Section 9, subject to the rule's specific wording.

Important: The decision to obtain multiple registrations can therefore affect composition eligibility across the separately registered places. Do not evaluate the registration structure without simultaneously checking the intended tax regime of every place.

Condition (c) – Supplies between separately registered places

Where separately registered places of the same person supply goods or services to another separately registered place of the same person, the supply is treated as a taxable supply under the GST framework and the supplying registration must issue the appropriate document — a tax invoice or bill of supply, as applicable.

This is one of the most important practical consequences of Rule 11. Although the registrations belong to the same legal entity, they are treated as separate registered persons for GST purposes in the manner prescribed by the Act.

Explanation to clause (b)

If a separately registered place that had been permitted to pay tax under Section 10 becomes ineligible for the composition scheme, the other separately registered places of that person also become ineligible to pay tax under Section 10.

Rule 11(2) – Separate REG-01 application

A registered person opting for separate registration for a place of business submits a separate application in FORM GST REG-01 in respect of that place.

Rule 11(3) – Rule 9 and Rule 10 apply

The verification and grant process under Rules 9 and 10 applies, with necessary changes, to an application made under Rule 11.

Meaning: Separate registration under Rule 11 is not an informal internal election. The additional GSTIN goes through the prescribed registration workflow and results in a separate registration record.

3. How Multiple Registrations Work in Practice

2Apply in REG-01

Once separate registrations exist, the finance team must stop treating the registrations as merely different cost centres. Each GSTIN needs its own compliance, invoicing, return and reconciliation discipline.

4. What Counts as a “Place of Business”?

For Rule 11, the key reference is the definition of “place of business” in Section 2(85). Broadly, it includes a place from where the business is ordinarily carried on and can include a warehouse, godown or other place where a taxable person stores goods, supplies or receives goods or services, and a place where the taxable person maintains books of account, subject to the statutory definition.

Therefore, before applying for a separate GSTIN, management should establish why the proposed location is genuinely a place of business and maintain appropriate supporting records.

5. Separate GSTINs Within the Same State – Business Example

Example – Construction company:
A company has its registered office in Hyderabad and operates two substantial business locations in Telangana. Management wants separate GST registrations so that the locations can independently manage project billing, records and GST compliance. If the statutory conditions for separate registration are satisfied, Rule 11 provides the mechanism for separate registration for the eligible places.

Once separate registrations are obtained, the company should create separate GSTIN masters, return calendars, invoice series and reconciliation controls for each registration.

6. Inter-Registration Supplies – The Most Important Practical Effect

Suppose Company A has two separately registered places in the same State:

RegistrationRoleTransaction
GSTIN-1Head office / location 1Provides a taxable service to GSTIN-2
GSTIN-2Location 2Receives the service

The transaction should not simply be posted as a normal internal transfer and ignored for GST. The GST treatment must be determined and the appropriate tax document issued because separately registered places are treated distinctly for GST purposes.

Common error: “It is the same company, so there is no GST.” That reasoning can be incorrect once separate registrations exist.

7. Composition Levy – Why Rule 11 Can Affect the Decision

Businesses sometimes consider separate registration for administrative convenience while another part of the business wants to use the composition scheme. Rule 11 specifically restricts mixing the Section 10 and Section 9 tax positions across separately registered places.

Before choosing the structure, management should prepare a place-wise matrix:

The final decision must be tested against the current composition provisions and all relevant facts before implementation.

8. Separate Registration vs Additional Place of Business

This distinction is critical. A business can have additional places of business disclosed under its GST registration without necessarily obtaining a separate GSTIN for every location. Rule 11 addresses the situation where the business seeks separate registration for a place of business within the same State/UT.

9. Practical Decision Framework – Should You Take a Separate GSTIN?

  1. List every location in the State/UT.
  2. Identify which locations genuinely qualify as places of business.
  3. Determine whether separate registration is commercially or operationally necessary.
  4. Assess the effect on composition eligibility.
  5. Map inter-location supplies that will occur after separate registration.
  6. Estimate the additional return, reconciliation and compliance workload.
  7. Confirm ERP capability for multiple GSTINs.
  8. Confirm who will own each GSTIN's monthly compliance.
  9. Document the management rationale for the registration structure.
Management principle: A second GSTIN may provide operational separation, but it also creates additional compliance boundaries. Take the registration only when the business benefit justifies the additional control burden.

11. Construction & Infrastructure Example

Consider a contractor headquartered in Telangana with several project offices or operational locations in the same State. Management may be tempted to obtain a separate GSTIN for every project simply because each project has a separate site.

That decision should not be made automatically. The finance team should first determine whether each location satisfies the statutory concept of a place of business and whether separate registration is actually appropriate under Section 25(2) and Rule 11.

If separate GSTINs are obtained, project-to-GSTIN mapping becomes essential. Contractor invoices, subcontractor records, ITC, e-invoice/e-way bill data where applicable, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and reconciliation should be controlled at the correct registration level.

12. Common Mistakes

  • Using old articles that still describe Rule 11 as registration for “business verticals.”
  • Assuming every branch or project site automatically requires a separate GSTIN.
  • Assuming a second GSTIN has no effect because PAN is common.
  • Ignoring the composition restriction under Rule 11.
  • Failing to issue the appropriate tax document for supplies between separately registered places.
  • Maintaining a single undifferentiated ITC reconciliation file for multiple GSTINs.
  • Mapping the wrong GSTIN to project invoices.
  • Creating separate GST registrations without assigning separate compliance responsibility.

13. Audit and Departmental Notice Relevance

During GST audit or departmental verification, officers may examine why multiple registrations were obtained, whether the registered places are genuine places of business and whether transactions between separately registered places were correctly documented.

For businesses with multiple GSTINs, maintain a registration matrix showing:

  • GSTIN;
  • State/UT;
  • principal place of business;
  • additional places;
  • effective date;
  • business/project mapped to the GSTIN;
  • composition/regular status where relevant; and
  • inter-GSTIN transaction controls.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

Can one company have multiple GSTINs in the same State?

Yes, the GST framework permits separate registrations for multiple places of business within the same State/UT subject to the conditions in Section 25(2) and Rule 11.

Is current Rule 11 about business verticals?

No. The current Rule 11 is about separate registration for multiple places of business within a State or Union territory. The earlier business-vertical wording was substituted with effect from 1 February 2019.

Can one GSTIN use composition while another GSTIN of the same person uses regular GST?

Rule 11 restricts this arrangement. The tax regime of all separately registered places must be evaluated together before choosing composition.

Do separate GSTINs require GST on supplies between them?

Supplies between separately registered places of the same person are subject to the GST framework applicable to supplies between distinct registrations. The appropriate document and tax treatment should be determined for the particular transaction.

Can every project site get a separate GSTIN?

Not automatically. First establish whether the location qualifies as a place of business and whether the statutory conditions for separate registration are satisfied.

Is a separate GSTIN mandatory for every additional place of business?

No. Rule 11 provides a mechanism for separate registration; businesses can also have additional places under an existing registration where the applicable law permits and the business chooses that structure.

How is the separate registration applied for?

Rule 11(2) provides for a separate application in FORM GST REG-01 for the relevant place of business. Rules 9 and 10 then apply to verification and grant of registration.

What is the next Rule after Rule 11?

Rule 12 deals with grant of registration to persons required to deduct tax at source or collect tax at source under the applicable GST provisions.

16. Source & Verification Note

This page follows the current Rule 11 framework in force for separate registration for multiple places of business within a State or Union territory. The rule was substituted in 2019; older versions used the expression “multiple business verticals.” The page intentionally explains the current rule rather than reproducing the superseded wording.

The practical examples and business controls are explanatory. The exact registration decision should be tested against the applicant's actual places of business, Section 25 requirements, composition eligibility and other applicable GST provisions.

17. Practical Takeaway

Rule 11 allows a business to create separate GST registration identities for eligible places of business within the same State/UT, but the decision creates real compliance consequences. Before taking another GSTIN, confirm that the location qualifies as a place of business, evaluate composition restrictions, design inter-GSTIN transaction controls and ensure the ERP and GST reconciliation process can operate registration-wise.
