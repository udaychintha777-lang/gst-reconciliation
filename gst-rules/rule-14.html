CGST Rule 14 – Registration for Overseas OIDAR & Online Money Gaming Suppliers

A practical guide to the special GST registration route for persons supplying online information and database access or retrieval services from outside India to non-taxable online recipients in India, and for persons supplying online money gaming from outside India to persons in India.

1. What Current Rule 14 Covers

Current Rule 14 is a specialised registration rule for certain suppliers located outside India. It covers two important categories:

  1. Persons supplying online information and database access or retrieval services (OIDAR) from a place outside India to a non-taxable online recipient; and
  2. Persons supplying online money gaming from a place outside India to a person in India.
Important update: Online money gaming was added to the Rule 14 heading and sub-rule (1) by Notification No. 51/2023-Central Tax, effective from 1 October 2023. Therefore, older Rule 14 pages that discuss only OIDAR are incomplete for the current framework. citeturn2search0turn2search32

Rule 14 is not the ordinary resident GST registration procedure. It exists because the supplier may be located outside India while the taxable recipient/supply is connected with India under the special IGST provisions.

2. First Category – OIDAR Services

OIDAR means online information and database access or retrieval services. The expression is defined in the IGST Act and is relevant to services supplied electronically where the recipient can receive the service through information technology networks.

Examples can include certain electronically delivered digital products and services such as access to online databases, digital content and other services falling within the statutory OIDAR definition.

Do not classify every online service as OIDAR. The statutory definition and the actual nature of the service must be examined. A foreign business should not use Rule 14 merely because its service happens to be delivered through the internet.

3. Second Category – Online Money Gaming

Rule 14 was expanded in 2023 to cover a person supplying online money gaming from a place outside India to a person in India.

This change was connected with the introduction of the special IGST framework for online money gaming under Section 14A of the IGST Act and related GST amendments.

Separate the concepts: Rule 14 provides the registration mechanism. The valuation, place-of-supply, rate, payment and other tax consequences for online money gaming are governed by the relevant GST/IGST provisions and should not be inferred from Rule 14 alone.

4. Rule 14(1) – FORM GST REG-10

Any person covered by the Rule 14 categories must electronically submit an application for registration in FORM GST REG-10.

The application is:

  • submitted electronically;
  • duly signed or verified through electronic verification code;
  • filed through the common portal; and
  • filed directly or through a Facilitation Centre notified by the Commissioner.

The current REG-10 form specifically accommodates the two categories of supply and provides for selection of the relevant type of supply, including OIDAR, online money gaming or both. citeturn2search10turn2search0

5. Rule 14(2) – Grant of Registration

The applicant under Rule 14(1) is granted registration in FORM GST REG-06, subject to the conditions and restrictions and by the officer notified by the Central Government on the recommendations of the GST Council.

This wording is important because Rule 14 does not simply say that the ordinary registration officer handles the application in exactly the same manner as a normal taxpayer. The competent officer and applicable conditions are specifically notified for these special overseas registrations.

Practical point: A foreign digital supplier should maintain a separate Rule 14 compliance file rather than assuming its ordinary overseas corporate tax documentation is sufficient.

6. Rule 14 Registration Flow

1Identify OIDAR / online money gaming scope

3File REG-10

After registration is granted, the foreign supplier must comply with the applicable GST/IGST payment, return, invoice and record requirements relevant to its category. Registration itself does not determine every subsequent tax consequence.

7. OIDAR – Who Is the Relevant Recipient?

Rule 14 specifically refers to an OIDAR supplier located outside India providing services to a non-taxable online recipient. This distinction matters because the GST treatment can differ where the recipient is already a registered person.

SituationWhy it matters
Foreign OIDAR supplier → Indian individual/non-taxable online recipientRule 14's special registration mechanism may apply, subject to the statutory conditions.
Foreign OIDAR supplier → Indian registered businessDo not automatically apply Rule 14 merely because the supplier is foreign. Examine the recipient status and the applicable IGST reverse-charge/other provisions.
Foreign supplier → recipient outside IndiaRule 14 is not triggered merely by being an overseas digital supplier; analyse place-of-supply and export/import rules separately.
Key compliance lesson: Recipient classification is not a minor customer-master field. It can change the GST mechanism applicable to an overseas digital supplier.

8. Practical OIDAR Examples

Example 1 – Foreign digital subscription

A foreign digital platform supplies a qualifying OIDAR service to an Indian individual who is not registered under GST. The supplier should examine the Rule 14 registration requirement and the associated IGST provisions before commencing the Indian-facing supply.

Example 2 – Foreign database access

A foreign business provides online access to a specialised database to Indian consumers. If the service falls within OIDAR and the recipients are non-taxable online recipients, Rule 14 may become relevant.

Example 3 – B2B customer

A foreign digital service provider supplies a qualifying service to an Indian GST-registered company. The supplier should first establish the customer's registration status and determine the correct GST/IGST mechanism. It should not automatically treat every Indian customer as a Rule 14 non-taxable online recipient.

9. Practical Online Money Gaming Examples

Example 1 – Overseas platform

A gaming platform located outside India supplies online money gaming to persons located in India. Rule 14's special registration mechanism must be examined along with Section 14A of the IGST Act and the other provisions applicable to online money gaming.

Example 2 – Platform serves both Indian and overseas users

The foreign operator should have a robust customer-location and transaction-classification system. Indian transactions must be identified using the applicable legal tests and records should be capable of supporting the GST treatment.

Example 3 – Mixed OIDAR and gaming services

The current REG-10 framework accommodates a supplier selecting OIDAR, online money gaming or both. The finance/tax team should maintain separate transaction classifications even when one foreign legal entity provides both categories.

11. Rule 14 vs Ordinary GST Registration

A foreign company should not automatically use REG-01 simply because it has Indian customers. First determine whether it falls within a special registration category such as Rule 13 or Rule 14.

QuestionWhat to analyse
Does the foreign supplier have a fixed place/business presence in India?Analyse ordinary registration and other provisions.
Is the supplier an NRTP?Consider Rule 13.
Is it supplying qualifying OIDAR from outside India to non-taxable online recipients?Consider Rule 14.
Is it supplying online money gaming from outside India to persons in India?Consider Rule 14 with Section 14A and related provisions.

13. Why Customer Location Data Is Critical

For a foreign digital business, GST compliance is not just a ledger exercise. The business may need reliable systems to determine where customers are located and whether they are taxable/registered recipients.

Depending on the applicable provisions, evidence may include customer declarations, registration details, billing information, payment information and other legally relevant indicators. The exact evidence requirements should be determined from the applicable place-of-supply and OIDAR provisions.

Management principle: Build tax determination into the platform before transactions scale. Retrospective reconstruction of millions of digital transactions is substantially harder than capturing the correct data at the time of supply.

14. Audit & Departmental Verification Relevance

Rule 14 registrations can be particularly data-intensive. During departmental verification, the supplier may need to demonstrate:

  • why it falls within the Rule 14 category;
  • where the supplier is located;
  • the nature of the digital service or gaming supply;
  • where recipients are located;
  • whether recipients are taxable or non-taxable as relevant;
  • the GSTIN and registration records;
  • taxable turnover and tax calculations; and
  • return/payment reconciliation.

A foreign business should therefore retain system-generated transaction reports and the underlying evidence used by its tax engine.

15. Common Mistakes

  • Using outdated Rule 14 content that covers only OIDAR and ignores online money gaming.
  • Assuming every internet-based service is OIDAR.
  • Failing to distinguish a registered Indian customer from a non-taxable online recipient.
  • Using ordinary REG-01 without checking whether the special Rule 14 route applies.
  • Failing to update the registration/compliance framework after the 2023 online-money-gaming amendments.
  • Mixing OIDAR, gaming and other digital supplies in one tax category.
  • Not retaining customer-location evidence.
  • Relying on billing-country data alone without analysing the applicable statutory tests.
  • Assuming Rule 14 itself determines the tax rate, valuation or place of supply.

17. Frequently Asked Questions

What is Rule 14 of the CGST Rules?

Rule 14 provides a special registration mechanism for specified overseas suppliers: qualifying OIDAR suppliers located outside India supplying to non-taxable online recipients, and persons supplying online money gaming from outside India to persons in India.

Which form is used under Rule 14?

FORM GST REG-10 is the prescribed application for registration under Rule 14.

What certificate is issued?

Registration is granted in FORM GST REG-06, subject to the prescribed conditions and restrictions.

When was online money gaming added to Rule 14?

The online money gaming wording was inserted through Notification No. 51/2023-Central Tax, with effect from 1 October 2023.

Does every foreign digital service provider need Rule 14 registration?

No. The precise service, supplier location, recipient status and applicable GST/IGST provisions must be examined. Rule 14 specifically covers the categories stated in the rule.

Does Rule 14 itself determine GST rate?

No. Rule 14 is primarily a registration provision. Rate, valuation, place of supply, payment and other consequences arise under the applicable GST/IGST provisions.

Is OIDAR the same as every online service?

No. OIDAR is a defined statutory category. The actual service should be tested against the applicable definition rather than classified solely because it is delivered online.

What is the next Rule?

Rule 15 deals with extension of the registration period for casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons.

19. Source & Verification Note

The current Rule 14 text was verified against the CGST framework and the 2023 amendment that expanded the rule to include online money gaming. The GST Council records the amendment to Rule 14 and the corresponding REG-10 changes. citeturn2search0turn2search6

The rule currently provides for REG-10 application and REG-06 grant subject to notified conditions, restrictions and competent authority. citeturn2search1turn2search9

The special registration rule should be read with the relevant IGST provisions, especially the provisions governing OIDAR and online money gaming. Rule 14 itself should not be treated as a complete statement of the tax rate, valuation or place-of-supply rules.

20. Practical Takeaway

Rule 14 is a specialised compliance gateway for overseas digital suppliers. The key question is not simply “Is the supplier foreign?” but “What is being supplied, from where, to whom, and which special GST/IGST provision applies?” For OIDAR and overseas online money gaming, accurate customer classification, REG-10 registration and strong transaction-level data are central to defensible GST compliance.
