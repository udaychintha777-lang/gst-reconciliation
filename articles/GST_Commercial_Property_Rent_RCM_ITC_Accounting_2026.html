GST on Renting of Commercial Property – RCM, Landlord & Tenant Compliance, ITC and Accounting

1. Scope and key conclusion

Renting or leasing commercial immovable property for business or commerce is generally a taxable supply of service. The usual GST rate is 18%. The person liable to pay GST depends on the supplier's registration status, the recipient's GST status and whether the recipient has opted for the composition levy.

Registered landlord → registered tenant

Normally forward charge: landlord charges GST on rent and reports the supply.

Unregistered landlord → regular registered tenant

Generally RCM under Entry 5AB of Notification 13/2017-CTR, effective 10 October 2024.

Unregistered landlord → composition taxpayer

Composition taxpayers are excluded from Entry 5AB from 16 January 2025.

Unregistered landlord → unregistered tenant

Entry 5AB does not apply because the recipient is not a registered person.

Important distinction RCM is not a general rule that all commercial rent is payable by the tenant. It applies to the specific supplier-recipient combination notified in Entry 5AB.

2. What is renting of immovable property?

Schedule II to the CGST Act treats renting of immovable property as a supply of service. The expression covers arrangements under which a person permits another person to use or occupy property for consideration, whether described as rent, lease rent, licence fee or a similar payment.

Property / arrangementTypical GST analysis
Office premisesCommercial renting service, generally taxable at 18%.
Retail shop / showroomCommercial renting service, generally taxable at 18%.
Warehouse / godownNon-residential property rental, generally taxable at 18%.
Factory shed / industrial buildingCommercial or industrial property rental, generally taxable at 18%.
Commercial land / open yardReview lease rights and classification; generally taxable, subject to specific exemptions.
Parking / storage spaceExamine the actual right supplied and service classification.
Serviced office / co-working spaceMay include renting plus facility/support services; analyse composite or multiple supplies.

The substance of the agreement matters more than its label. A leave-and-licence agreement, lease deed, occupancy agreement or facility agreement may all need review to identify the actual supply.

3. GST rate and SAC classification

Non-residential real estate services are generally classified under Heading 9972. SAC 997212 is commonly used for rental or leasing services involving own or leased non-residential property. Under the general rate entry for real estate services, the standard aggregate GST rate is 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST for an intra-State supply, or 18% IGST for an inter-State supply).

ParticularsRate / treatment
Typical SAC997212 – rental or leasing services involving own or leased non-residential property

Rate caution: 18% is the general position for taxable commercial renting. Check the exact transaction for specific exemptions or special entries, particularly government leases, long-term industrial land leases and composite real-estate arrangements.

5. RCM on rent paid to an unregistered landlord

Notification No. 09/2024-Central Tax (Rate), dated 8 October 2024, inserted Entry 5AB in Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate). It became effective from 10 October 2024. The entry covers renting of any immovable property other than a residential dwelling by an unregistered person to a registered person. The corresponding Integrated Tax Rate notification inserted Entry 6AB.

Core rule Where an unregistered landlord supplies commercial property rental service to a registered recipient other than a person who has opted for composition levy, the registered recipient is liable to pay GST under RCM, subject to the precise wording of the applicable notification.

Example

A registered company takes an office on rent from an individual landlord who is not registered under GST. Monthly rent is ₹1,00,000. The company is a regular GST taxpayer.

ParticularsAmount
Monthly rent₹1,00,000
GST under RCM @ 18%₹18,000
Payment to landlord (assuming rent only)₹1,00,000
GST paid separately by tenant to Government₹18,000

The landlord does not collect GST where the landlord is unregistered. The tenant calculates and pays the RCM liability, reports it in the relevant return and evaluates ITC separately.

Does landlord turnover below the registration threshold remove RCM?

No. The landlord's reason for being unregistered does not by itself remove the recipient's RCM obligation under Entry 5AB where the recipient is a regular registered person.

What if landlord becomes registered during the lease?

Review supplies before and after the effective date separately. Earlier qualifying supplies may fall under RCM; supplies after registration generally move to forward charge. Avoid duplicate payment under both mechanisms.

6. Composition taxpayer exception

Notification No. 07/2025-Central Tax (Rate), dated 16 January 2025, amended Entry 5AB by excluding a registered person who has opted to pay tax under the composition levy. Accordingly, Entry 5AB does not apply to a composition taxpayer receiving the covered commercial renting service from an unregistered landlord from 16 January 2025.

PeriodPosition
10 October 2024 to 15 January 2025Entry 5AB initially covered registered recipients. CBIC Circular No. 245/02/2025-GST regularised the intervening period for composition taxpayers on an “as is where is” basis.
From 16 January 2025Composition taxpayers are expressly excluded from Entry 5AB.
Do not confuse composition with small turnover. The exclusion is for a person who has actually opted for the composition levy. A regular taxpayer with turnover below a threshold is not covered by this exclusion if registered under the regular scheme.

7. Landlord GST registration – practical treatment

A landlord supplying taxable commercial renting services must evaluate aggregate turnover under Section 22 and the applicable registration threshold. For an unregistered landlord renting to a registered regular tenant, Entry 5AB can shift payment of tax to the recipient. It does not automatically mean that every landlord must obtain GST registration solely because the property is rented.

Landlord situationPractical GST point
Registered regular landlordCharge GST under forward charge and comply with invoicing and return requirements.
Unregistered landlord below thresholdMay remain unregistered if no compulsory registration provision applies; registered tenant may pay RCM under Entry 5AB.
Turnover crosses thresholdEvaluate registration liability and effective date. Do not continue treating the landlord as unregistered after registration becomes effective.
Multiple properties / tenantsAggregate turnover is generally evaluated on a PAN-India basis, subject to statutory rules; do not assess each property in isolation.
Co-owned propertyEstablish who is supplier under the agreement, rent entitlement, ownership shares, PAN and invoicing arrangement.

Consider all taxable supplies, multiple properties and other business activities when determining registration liability.

8. Residential dwelling vs commercial property

Entry 5AB specifically excludes residential dwellings. Residential dwelling rentals have a separate exemption and RCM framework under the relevant rate notifications. A property should not be classified solely by the name used in the rent agreement.

SituationBroad GST treatment
Residential dwelling rented for use as residence to an unregistered personGenerally exempt under the relevant exemption entry, subject to conditions.
Residential dwelling rented to a registered personSeparate RCM provisions generally apply, subject to the specific proprietor's personal-residence exception and other notified conditions.
Non-residential office / shop / warehouseGenerally taxable at 18%; Entry 5AB may apply if landlord is unregistered and tenant is a regular registered person.
Residential dwelling used as an officeRequires factual analysis of property, contractual use and applicable exemption/RCM wording.
Separate legal analysis required: Do not use this commercial-property article as the sole basis for residential rent. Residential dwelling RCM and exemption conditions must be separately verified.

9. Government, local authority and public-sector landlords

Renting by Central Government, State Government, Union Territory or a local authority to a business entity may be covered by separate reverse-charge provisions in Notification No. 13/2017-Central Tax (Rate), subject to the nature of the property, identity of the authority and applicable exemptions. Do not apply Entry 5AB mechanically to a government landlord: government services have their own entries and exceptions.

Examine whether the authority qualifies as a “Governmental Authority”, “Government Entity” or “local authority” for the particular exemption or RCM entry. These are defined legal concepts and are not interchangeable with “government-owned”.

Before posting government rent: Obtain the allotment / lease document, identify the legal entity issuing the demand, classify the authority under the CGST Act and check the current notification entry and exemption conditions.

10. Rent, security deposit, CAM and other recoveries

10.1 Monthly rent and escalation

Rent, periodic lease charges and contractual escalation are ordinarily part of taxable value. Update tax calculation from the effective date of escalation and reconcile the revised amount to invoice or RCM working.

10.2 Refundable security deposit

A refundable security deposit is not automatically consideration when it is merely held as security. The CGST Act excludes a deposit from consideration unless the supplier applies it as consideration for the supply. If later appropriated towards unpaid rent, damages or another charge, examine the nature of the appropriation and its GST consequences.

10.3 Non-refundable deposit / advance rent

A non-refundable amount or amount adjusted against rent may form part of consideration. Review the agreement and payment trail. For RCM, determine time of supply under the specific reverse-charge rules; do not blindly apply forward-charge advance rules.

10.4 CAM and facility charges

Common area maintenance, security, housekeeping, lift maintenance, common electricity, facility management, parking and generator charges may be separate services or form part of a composite supply with renting. Review who provides the service, whether it is separately contracted and whether the charge is a recovery or consideration for a service.

10.5 Electricity and utility recoveries

Electricity supply itself is generally treated under the electricity exemption framework, but bundled facility charges, power backup, generator services and recoveries require separate analysis. A landlord's recovery is not automatically outside GST merely because the underlying electricity supply is exempt. Apply Rule 33 pure-agent treatment only where its conditions are genuinely satisfied and documented.

10.6 Repairs, fit-outs and restoration

Tenant-funded fit-outs, landlord reimbursements, repair recoveries, restoration charges and deductions from deposits should be separately classified. A deduction described as “damage recovery” is not automatically outside GST; analyse whether it is compensation, consideration for a supply or an adjustment to the rental contract.

11. ITC eligibility for tenant

GST paid on commercial rent or RCM can be eligible for ITC where the recipient satisfies Section 16 and the credit is not blocked or restricted under Section 17. The premises should be used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business, and the recipient must hold the prescribed tax document and meet the other statutory conditions.

Use of premisesITC review
Office for taxable business operationsGenerally eligible, subject to Section 16 and other restrictions.
Warehouse for taxable outward suppliesGenerally eligible, subject to conditions.
Factory / project officeGenerally eligible, subject to business nexus and restrictions.
Premises exclusively for exempt suppliesITC restricted; apply Section 17(2) and Rules 42/43 as relevant.
Taxable and exempt business useCommon-credit apportionment and reversal may be required.
Personal / non-business useITC not available to the extent of non-business use.
Residential accommodation for personal useExamine blocked-credit provisions, personal consumption and exact use; do not assume office-rent treatment.
Rent ITC vs construction ITC: Section 17(5)(c) and 17(5)(d) deal with specified works contract and construction-related credits. GST on renting an already completed commercial office or warehouse is not automatically blocked merely because the building is immovable property. Analyse rental service separately from construction of the property.

ITC on RCM

The tenant must first discharge RCM in cash through the electronic cash ledger. Eligible RCM tax may then be claimed as ITC, subject to Sections 16 and 17, documentation and return conditions. RCM liability cannot be discharged using existing ITC balance.

12. Place of supply and correct GSTIN

For services directly related to immovable property, Section 12(3) of the IGST Act generally fixes place of supply at the location of the immovable property. This matters where a tenant has registrations in multiple States or the property is located in a State different from the head office.

  • Record complete property address and State in the lease master.
  • Identify the GST registration that receives and uses the rental service.
  • Determine intra-State or inter-State nature by applying supplier-location and place-of-supply provisions.
  • Do not simply book every rent invoice against the head-office GSTIN.
  • For property in another State, assess whether registration, tax reporting or allocation is required there.
Multi-State control: Maintain a property-wise GST register with landlord PAN/GSTIN, tenant GSTIN, property State, tax mechanism, rate and ITC eligibility.

13. Time of supply and RCM payment

For services liable under RCM, Section 13(3) of the CGST Act generally determines time of supply by reference to the earlier of the date of payment recorded in the recipient's books / bank debit and the date immediately following 60 days from the supplier's invoice or document. Where the recipient is required to issue the invoice, the amended provision also specifically covers the date of issue of that recipient invoice.

For supplies received from an unregistered supplier where the recipient is liable under RCM, review Section 31(3)(f) and the amendments effective from 1 November 2024 concerning recipient-issued invoices. Read the timing of the self-invoice together with Section 13(3).

EventCompliance review
Monthly rent becomes dueRecord service period, agreement rent and invoice / document date.
Payment made before invoiceCheck whether payment date triggers time of supply under Section 13(3).
Invoice from unregistered landlordIssue recipient invoice where required under Section 31(3)(f); track its date.
RCM tax paymentDischarge in cash in the applicable tax period.
Do not use the 60-day rule as a substitute for monthly close. Identify the actual time-of-supply trigger from invoice, payment and self-invoice facts and report in the correct return period.

14. Invoice and documentation

Registered landlord – forward charge

  • Landlord issues a tax invoice with supplier GSTIN, tenant details, property description, SAC, taxable value, rate and tax amount.
  • Invoice should identify the correct recipient GSTIN and place of supply where applicable.
  • Landlord reports outward supply in GSTR-1 and pays tax through GSTR-3B.

Unregistered landlord – tenant under RCM

  • Obtain lease agreement, rent demand / debit note or other supporting document.
  • Verify landlord's unregistered status and tenant's regular GST status for the relevant period.
  • Prepare recipient-issued invoice under Section 31(3)(f) where required.
  • Maintain monthly RCM working with property, rent period, taxable value, tax rate, time of supply, tax head and payment reference.
  • Keep evidence of cash payment and subsequent ITC claim.

Lease master should capture

Landlord PAN, legal name and GSTIN status
Tenant GSTIN and composition / regular status
Property address, State and usage
Rent, escalation, deposit and CAM terms
Tax mechanism, SAC, rate and ITC decision

15. GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and reconciliation

Registered landlord under forward charge

The landlord reports taxable commercial rent in the appropriate outward-supply tables of GSTR-1 and discharges output tax in GSTR-3B. The tenant reconciles the landlord's invoice with GSTR-2B and its rent ledger.

Tenant paying RCM

The tenant reports inward supplies liable to reverse charge in the applicable GSTR-3B inward RCM table and pays tax in cash. Eligible ITC is reported separately only after statutory conditions are met. The tenant should not expect an unregistered landlord's rent invoice to appear in GSTR-2B as a normal supplier-reported B2B invoice.

ControlTenant review
Rent ledger vs lease scheduleConfirm monthly rent, escalation and rent-free periods.
Forward-charge invoice vs GSTR-2BMatch supplier GSTIN, invoice number, date and tax value.
Unregistered landlord rentIdentify RCM and prepare self-invoice / RCM register.
RCM liability vs GSTR-3BReconcile taxable value and tax to return and cash ledger.
RCM tax vs ITC claimedConfirm cash payment and eligibility before ITC claim.
Credit notes / rent concessionsLink adjustments to original rental period and tax treatment.

Do not book RCM as an ordinary forward-charge invoice from the landlord. Keep a separate RCM tax code and distinguish RCM expense / liability from eligible ITC.

17. Worked practical examples

Example 1 – Registered landlord, regular tenant

Monthly rent ₹2,00,000. Landlord is registered under regular scheme. GST @18% = ₹36,000. Landlord invoices ₹2,36,000 and pays output tax. Tenant may claim eligible ITC subject to Sections 16 and 17.

Example 2 – Unregistered landlord, regular registered tenant

Monthly rent ₹75,000. RCM @18% = ₹13,500. Tenant pays rent to landlord and discharges ₹13,500 GST to Government in cash, then evaluates ITC.

Example 3 – Unregistered landlord, composition tenant

Monthly rent ₹50,000. From 16 January 2025, Entry 5AB excludes this recipient. No RCM under Entry 5AB. Check other independent provisions only if facts warrant it.

Example 4 – Commercial office in another State

Head office is registered in State A, but company rents a warehouse in State B. Section 12(3) points to the property location. Determine correct recipient registration, tax head and reporting before processing the invoice.

Example 5 – Refundable security deposit

Tenant pays ₹3,00,000 as a refundable deposit. If genuinely held as security and not applied as consideration, it is not automatically included in rental value. If later adjusted against rent, reassess GST on the facts.

Example 6 – Rent plus CAM

Rent is ₹1,00,000 and CAM is ₹15,000. If both form one bundled rental/facility arrangement, examine composite supply and valuation. If CAM is an independent service from a separate provider, analyse that invoice separately.

Example 7 – Landlord registers mid-year

Landlord is unregistered up to 30 September and registration becomes effective 1 October. Reconcile periods before and after the effective date separately; earlier qualifying supplies may fall under RCM, later supplies generally under forward charge.

Example 8 – Tenant makes exempt supplies

Tenant pays RCM of ₹18,000 but uses the office for taxable and exempt supplies. Tax must be paid under RCM; ITC is subject to Section 17(2) and applicable apportionment rules.

18. Monthly audit and compliance checklist

  1. Maintain a complete property and lease register.
  2. Verify landlord GST registration status for every relevant period.
  3. Verify tenant GSTIN and regular / composition status.
  4. Classify premises as residential or non-residential based on facts and contract.
  5. Apply Entry 5AB only where supplier and recipient conditions are met.
  6. Check whether a government / local authority RCM entry or exemption applies.
  7. Confirm SAC, taxable value and rate.
  8. Review rent escalation, rent-free periods, deposits, CAM, utilities and other recoveries.
  9. For RCM, determine time of supply and issue recipient invoice where required.
  10. Discharge RCM liability in cash and reconcile to GSTR-3B.
  11. Claim ITC only after payment and satisfaction of statutory conditions.
  12. Reconcile forward-charge invoices with GSTR-2B.
  13. Review exempt and non-business use for ITC reversals.
  14. Check whether property is in a different State from head-office GSTIN.
  15. Retain lease deed, invoices, payment proofs, RCM calculations and ITC working.
Recommended ERP / Tally control Create separate ledgers for Rent – Commercial Property, Rent – Residential Property, CAM / Facility Charges, Security Deposit, RCM CGST, RCM SGST / IGST, and eligible / ineligible RCM ITC. Maintain a property-wise RCM register rather than relying only on the rent expense ledger.

19. Practical FAQs

1. Is GST applicable on commercial shop rent?

Generally yes, at 18%, subject to applicable exemptions and charge mechanism.

2. If landlord is unregistered, who pays GST?

If the property is non-residential and tenant is a registered regular taxpayer, tenant generally pays GST under RCM Entry 5AB.

3. Does landlord turnover below ₹20 lakh remove RCM?

No. The landlord's threshold position does not by itself remove RCM under Entry 5AB where the landlord is unregistered and recipient is a regular registered person.

4. Is RCM applicable to a composition taxpayer?

From 16 January 2025, a person who opted for composition levy is excluded from Entry 5AB. The intervening period was regularised as clarified by CBIC.

5. Can tenant claim ITC on RCM rent?

Yes, where eligible under Section 16 and not restricted by Section 17. RCM tax must first be paid in cash.

6. Is ITC on rent blocked because building is immovable property?

No blanket block applies merely because the rented building is immovable property. Construction-related blocked-credit provisions must be distinguished from rental-service ITC.

7. Is refundable security deposit taxable?

Not automatically. A deposit is excluded from consideration unless applied as consideration for the supply.

8. Is CAM always taxed separately?

No. Determine whether CAM is part of a composite supply with rent or an independent service.

9. What if property is located in another State?

Section 12(3) generally fixes place of supply at the property's location. Assess the correct GSTIN, tax head and reporting before booking.

10. Does residential rent follow Entry 5AB?

No. Entry 5AB excludes residential dwellings. Residential renting has separate exemption and RCM provisions.

11. Does rent paid to a government authority attract RCM?

It may fall under a separate government-service RCM entry, subject to identity of authority, nature of service and exemptions.

12. Can RCM be paid using ITC balance?

No. RCM liability must be discharged through the electronic cash ledger. Eligible ITC can be evaluated after tax payment.

Practical takeaway

For every commercial rental contract, determine property type, landlord registration, tenant tax scheme, property State, charge mechanism and ITC eligibility before the first rent payment. A property-wise GST register can prevent missed RCM, duplicate tax, incorrect GSTIN reporting and ineligible ITC claims.
