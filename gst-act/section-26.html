GST ACT — SECTION 26

Deemed Registration

Section 26 creates a coordination mechanism between the Central GST registration system and the corresponding State GST or Union Territory GST law. Its purpose is to avoid a taxpayer having to obtain two separate substantive registration decisions for the same application, while preserving the corresponding law's power to reject the application within the prescribed period.

Key takeaways

1. What Section 26 does

Section 26 is a short provision, but it is important because GST registration operates through corresponding Central and State/Union Territory GST laws.

The CGST Act provides the central registration framework. The corresponding SGST/UTGST law contains a parallel registration framework. Section 26 prevents the same registration application from requiring two independent substantive outcomes in ordinary circumstances.

Core idea: If registration or a Unique Identity Number is granted under the CGST Act, Section 26 treats that grant as a grant under the corresponding State GST or Union Territory GST law, subject to the statutory condition regarding rejection. Conversely, a rejection under the CGST Act is deemed to be a rejection under the corresponding law.

India Code lists Section 26 as “Deemed registration” and records it among the provisions brought into force on 22 June 2017. citeturn0search0

3. Why does deemed registration exist?

GST is a dual tax system. For an intra-State supply, Central GST and State/Union Territory GST operate together, but their legislation is enacted through corresponding laws.

Without a coordination rule, a taxpayer could face an unnecessary situation where the Central authority grants registration while the State authority treats the same application as a completely separate process. Section 26 establishes a statutory bridge between the two registration decisions.

Central law
CGST registration application and decision.
Corresponding law
SGST/UTGST registration consequence.
Section 26
Connects the grant/rejection outcomes.
Section 25
Provides the underlying application and verification framework.
Section 26 therefore should not be read as creating a separate route to registration. It is a coordination provision operating after the registration application and statutory decision process.

4. Section 26(1) — deemed grant

Section 26(1) provides that the grant of registration or a Unique Identity Number under the CGST Act is deemed to be a grant under the corresponding State GST or Union Territory GST law, subject to the condition that the application has not been rejected under that corresponding law within the time specified in Section 25(10).

The condition is important. The phrase does not mean that a State/UT law has no role whatsoever. The corresponding law retains its statutory rejection mechanism within the prescribed period.

StageResult
Application submittedRegistration application enters the prescribed verification process.
CGST grantGrant is deemed to operate under the corresponding SGST/UTGST law, subject to the statutory condition.
No corresponding-law rejection within prescribed periodSection 26's deemed-grant mechanism operates.
Corresponding-law rejection within the permitted periodThe statutory condition is not satisfied; the rejection must be dealt with under the applicable law.

5. Section 26(2) — deemed rejection

Section 26(2) provides the reverse coordination rule. A rejection of an application for registration or a Unique Identity Number under the CGST Act is deemed to be a rejection of the application under the corresponding State GST or Union Territory GST law.

Practical consequence: A taxpayer cannot normally treat a CGST rejection as irrelevant and rely on an independent corresponding-law registration outcome for the same application.

This creates symmetry:

Grant under CGST
→ deemed grant under corresponding law, subject to Section 26(1).
Rejection under CGST
→ deemed rejection under corresponding law.

6. Unique Identity Number is also covered

Section 26 expressly refers to the grant or rejection of a Unique Identity Number in addition to ordinary registration.

This matters for entities such as specified international organisations, embassies, consulates and other notified persons that use the UIN mechanism for specified GST purposes.

Section 25(9) provides the statutory basis for UIN. Section 26 then coordinates the grant/rejection outcome with the corresponding GST law.

Do not confuse UIN with GSTIN: UIN is a special identification mechanism for specified entities, whereas ordinary GST registration identifies a taxable person under the GST registration framework.

7. CGST, SGST and UTGST relationship

Section 26 is designed around the corresponding-law structure. For a person operating in a State, the relevant counterpart is the State GST law. For a Union Territory with its own GST law, the corresponding UTGST framework applies.

CGSTSGST / applicable State GST lawGrant/rejection is coordinated.
CGSTUTGST / applicable UT GST frameworkGrant/rejection is coordinated.
UIN under CGSTCorresponding GST lawSame deemed grant/rejection principle.

The practical objective is uniformity in the registration decision for the same taxpayer and place of business.

8. Relationship with Section 25

Section 25 contains the substantive procedure for applying for registration. It specifies when the application must be made, where it must be made and how registration is granted, verified or rejected.

Section 26 then links the outcome under the CGST Act to the corresponding GST law.

Think of the sequence as:
Section 22/24 → liability → Section 25 application → verification/decision → Section 26 corresponding-law consequence.

Section 26 therefore does not replace the Section 25 application. It operates on the grant/rejection of that application.

9. Verification, deficiency and rejection

The deemed-registration mechanism should not be confused with automatic approval on the day an application is filed. The registration application remains subject to the verification and deficiency procedure prescribed under Section 25 and the Registration Rules.

Where the proper officer communicates a deficiency and the applicant fails to satisfactorily respond, the application can be rejected under the prescribed process. The corresponding-law consequences then follow Section 26.

Professional point: Maintain the ARN, clarification notices, replies, supporting documents and final order/certificate. Section 26 concerns the legal effect of the decision; it does not eliminate the underlying verification process.

10. Section 26 deemed registration vs Section 25 deemed grant

There are two related but different concepts that are often confused.

ConceptProvisionMeaning
Deemed grant under Section 25(12)CGST Act Section 25(12)Where the prescribed period expires without deficiency being communicated, registration/UIN is deemed granted subject to the statutory provision.
Deemed registration under Section 26CGST Act Section 26A grant/rejection under one GST law is given corresponding effect under the other GST law.
Do not merge these concepts: Section 25(12) deals with the effect of the officer's failure to communicate deficiency within the prescribed period. Section 26 deals with coordination between corresponding GST laws after grant/rejection.

11. Effective date and certificate

Section 26 does not independently establish a new effective date for the taxpayer's registration. The effective-date rules arise from Section 25 and the Registration Rules.

Accordingly, a professional review should distinguish:

  • date on which registration liability arose;
  • date on which the application was filed;
  • date on which registration was granted or deemed granted;
  • effective date stated/derived under the registration rules;
  • date on which the corresponding SGST/UTGST consequence arises through Section 26.
Example: If a person became liable to registration on 10 September and files within the prescribed period, the effective date can relate to the liability date under the applicable registration rule. Section 26 then coordinates the Central and corresponding State/UT registration consequence; it does not reset that effective date.

13. GST Council / legislative background

The dual-GST architecture required a coordination mechanism so that Central and State/UT registration systems could work together. Section 26 is part of that original registration architecture.

Dual GST structure
CGST and corresponding SGST/UTGST laws operate together.
Single registration application outcome
The legal system coordinates grant/rejection.
Administrative consistency
Prevents contradictory registration outcomes for the same application.
Procedural separation
Section 25 supplies the verification mechanism; Section 26 supplies the deemed legal consequence.

The official India Code record confirms that Sections 22–30, including Section 26, were part of the core registration chapter brought into force on 22 June 2017. citeturn0search0

14. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — ordinary State registration

A company applies for GST registration in Telangana. The CGST registration is granted and the corresponding State-law condition is satisfied. Section 26 gives the grant corresponding effect under the State GST law.

Example 2 — CGST rejection

An application is rejected under the CGST Act after the prescribed verification process. Section 26(2) deems the application rejected under the corresponding State/UT GST law as well.

Example 3 — UIN

A qualifying international organisation applies for a UIN. Section 26 applies the same grant/rejection coordination principle to the UIN.

Example 4 — deficiency notice

The officer issues a clarification/deficiency notice. The taxpayer responds. Section 26 does not bypass this process; the final grant or rejection determines the corresponding-law consequence.

Example 5 — deemed grant under Section 25(12)

No deficiency is communicated within the prescribed Section 25 period and the statutory deemed-grant condition is satisfied. Section 25(12) governs the deemed grant; Section 26 then determines its corresponding-law effect.

Example 6 — voluntary registration

A person voluntarily registers under Section 25(3). Once the registration is granted, Section 26 coordinates the corresponding State/UT registration consequence.

Example 7 — multiple State registrations

A company has registrations in Telangana and Karnataka. Each State registration is separately applied for and processed; Section 26 operates within the corresponding Central/State legal framework for each registration.

Example 8 — SEZ registration

An SEZ unit obtains its separate GST registration under Section 25. Section 26 coordinates the corresponding Central and State/UT registration outcome.

Example 9 — rejection under corresponding law

If the corresponding State/UT law rejects the application within the period contemplated by Section 26(1), the statutory condition for deemed grant under that subsection is not satisfied.

Example 10 — wrong assumption

A taxpayer assumes that Section 26 means registration is automatically granted immediately upon filing. That is incorrect: Section 25 verification and the Registration Rules continue to apply.

18. Common mistakes

  • Thinking Section 26 means automatic registration immediately after filing.
  • Confusing Section 25(12) deemed grant with Section 26 deemed registration.
  • Ignoring the corresponding-law rejection condition in Section 26(1).
  • Assuming Section 26 creates a separate application route.
  • Ignoring deficiency notices because “CGST and SGST are automatically linked”.
  • Confusing GSTIN with UIN.
  • Failing to preserve the application and verification trail.
  • Assuming Section 26 determines the effective date independently of Section 25 and the Rules.

20. Key takeaways

  • Section 26 is a coordination provision between CGST registration and the corresponding State/UT GST registration law.
  • Grant of registration under CGST is deemed to be grant under the corresponding law, subject to the Section 26(1) condition.
  • Rejection under CGST is deemed to be rejection under the corresponding GST law.
  • The mechanism also covers Unique Identity Numbers.
  • Section 26 does not replace Section 25 verification or the Registration Rules.
  • Section 25(12) deemed grant and Section 26 deemed registration are two different legal concepts.
  • The effective date comes from the registration framework; Section 26 does not independently create a new effective date.
  • Maintain the complete ARN, deficiency, response, grant/rejection and certificate trail.
