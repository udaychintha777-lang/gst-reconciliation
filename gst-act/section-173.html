CGST Act Section 173 – Amendment of Act 32 of 1994

Section 173 is a key transition provision that omits Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994, subject to the exceptions provided elsewhere in the CGST Act. It should be read together with Section 174 when dealing with historical service-tax matters.

3. Statutory Text of Section 173

173. Amendment of Act 32 of 1994.— Save as otherwise provided in this Act, Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 shall be omitted.
Key wording: “Save as otherwise provided in this Act” is important. Section 173 cannot be read independently of the other transition provisions in the CGST Act.

4. Section 173 in Simple Language

Before GST, services were subject to the central service-tax framework contained in Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994. With the introduction of GST, the CGST Act established a new tax framework for supplies of goods and services.

Section 173 forms part of that legislative transition. It states that, except where the CGST Act provides otherwise, Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 shall be omitted.

The important point is that omission of the old charging framework does not automatically erase historical liabilities and proceedings. Those consequences are addressed by the separate saving provisions in Section 174.

Practical rule: For any old service-tax issue, do not stop after reading Section 173. Read Section 174 and the specific transitional/saving provisions relevant to the transaction period.

5. What Was Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994?

Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 contained the central statutory service-tax framework that applied before the GST regime. Section 173 deals with the omission of that Chapter as part of the transition to GST.

6. How Section 173 Operates

Step 1Identify Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 as the affected legacy service-tax chapter.
Step 2Apply the omission stated in Section 173 from the appointed transition date.
Step 3Check whether the CGST Act provides otherwise for a particular historical matter.
Step 4Read Section 174 for rights, liabilities, proceedings, recovery and other savings.

Section 173 itself is only one sentence. Its practical importance comes from its interaction with the transition and saving provisions surrounding it.

7. Understanding “Save as Otherwise Provided in This Act”

The opening words of Section 173 qualify the omission: “Save as otherwise provided in this Act”.

This means the provision cannot be treated as an isolated command that every part of the historical service-tax framework instantly becomes irrelevant for every purpose. The CGST Act contains specific transition and saving provisions that must be considered.

Is the matter a new GST-era supply?GST provisions generally become the starting point, subject to the relevant transitional rules.

9. Section 173 and Section 174 – Read Together

Section 173 and Section 174 perform different but connected functions.

ProvisionMain function
Section 173Provides for omission of Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994, subject to the CGST Act.
Section 174Contains repeal-and-saving provisions, including preservation of previous operation, rights, privileges, obligations, liabilities, taxes, interest, penalties, investigations, proceedings, recovery and remedies subject to its terms.

Section 174 expressly states that the amendment of the Finance Act, 1994 to the extent mentioned in Section 173 does not, among other things, affect the previous operation of the amended Act or duly done acts, or specified rights, obligations and liabilities. It also addresses investigations, assessment proceedings, adjudication, legal proceedings and recovery of arrears.

Practical conclusion: Section 173 explains the omission; Section 174 explains much of what survives that transition for historical matters.

10. Historical Service-Tax Matters After GST

Section 173 is particularly relevant when a company is dealing with a historical service-tax notice, audit, refund, appeal, adjudication or recovery proceeding.

Records that should normally be located

  • Original service-tax registration and registration amendments.
  • Relevant service-tax returns and payment records.
  • Invoices and contracts for the disputed period.
  • Service-tax notices, audit observations and orders.
  • Correspondence with the department.
  • Appeal papers and court orders, where applicable.
  • Relevant Finance Act provisions and service-tax rules.
  • Section 173 and Section 174 analysis.
  • Transitional notifications and other applicable instruments.
  • Proof of tax, interest, penalty or other amounts already paid.
Do not assume: The fact that Chapter V was omitted does not by itself dispose of an old service-tax dispute. The saving provisions can preserve specified historical matters.

11. Practical Examples

Example 1 – What Section 173 does

Section 173 is a short transition provision. It provides that, except where the CGST Act otherwise provides, Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 shall be omitted.

Example 2 – Why Chapter V matters

Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 was the statutory framework containing the central service-tax law. Section 173 formed part of the transition from the service-tax regime into GST.

Example 3 – Effective date

Section 173 commenced on 1 July 2017. India Code records that Sections 165 to 174, including Section 173, commenced on that date.

Example 4 – Omission is not the same as erasing history

Section 173's omission of Chapter V does not mean that every past service-tax liability, investigation or proceeding disappeared. Section 174 contains the separate repeal-and-saving framework and must be read with Section 173.

Example 5 – Existing service-tax matter

A company facing a service-tax dispute relating to a pre-GST period cannot decide the case by reading Section 173 alone. Section 174 and the applicable provisions of the amended Finance Act must also be examined.

Example 6 – Service-tax refund

A historical refund matter may continue to require the old service-tax provisions because the saving framework preserves specified rights, liabilities, proceedings and remedies. The exact provision and period must be checked.

Example 7 – Service-tax audit

An audit relating to the pre-GST service-tax period should be analysed using the law applicable to that period together with the repeal-and-saving provisions.

Example 8 – Recovery of old dues

Section 174 expressly addresses duties, taxes, interest, penalties and recovery of arrears under the earlier law. Therefore, Section 173 should not be read in isolation as cancelling all old dues.

Example 9 – Investigation already started

Where an investigation or inquiry relates to the earlier service-tax law, the saving provisions in Section 174 may permit it to continue subject to their terms.

Example 10 – New service after GST

A service supplied after the GST regime commenced is generally analysed under GST rather than the omitted Chapter V, subject to the applicable transitional provisions and the exact nature and date of the transaction.

Example 11 – 'Save as otherwise provided'

The opening words are important. Section 173 itself contains an exception for cases where the CGST Act provides otherwise. The transition therefore must be read with the rest of the CGST Act.

Example 12 – Section 173 versus Section 174

Section 173 performs the amendment/omission of Chapter V. Section 174 contains the repeal-and-saving consequences. They operate together in understanding the transition from service tax to GST.

Example 13 – Finance team legal file

For historical service-tax matters, a finance team can maintain the original service-tax law, the CGST Act, Section 173, Section 174, relevant notifications and the underlying period-wise records in one legal file.

Example 14 – Contract review

A contract spanning 30 June 2017 and 1 July 2017 may require period-wise tax analysis because the applicable indirect-tax regime changed at the appointed date.

Example 15 – Invoice date alone may not answer every issue

For a historical service-tax dispute, the relevant taxable event, period, point of taxation and transitional provisions may need examination. Section 173 alone does not answer those questions.

Example 16 – Historical legal research

When researching a pre-GST service-tax issue today, start with the transaction period, identify the Finance Act provisions that applied, then read Sections 173 and 174 and the relevant transitional instruments.

Example 17 – Court proceeding after GST

A court or departmental proceeding concerning an earlier service-tax liability may continue even though Chapter V was omitted, where the saving provisions preserve the relevant proceeding or liability.

Example 18 – No automatic fresh service tax under omitted chapter

The omission of Chapter V is part of the legislative transition to GST. It should not be interpreted as creating a new service-tax charge for post-GST supplies.

12. Historical Service-Tax Matter Workflow

  1. Fix the date: Identify the exact period of the service, liability, payment or dispute.
  2. Identify the old provision: Determine the relevant Finance Act, 1994 section/rule/notification.
  3. Check transition: Read Section 173 and identify the effect of the omission.
  4. Read Section 174: Determine which rights, liabilities, proceedings or remedies are saved.
  5. Check transitional instruments: Search applicable notifications, rules and orders.
  6. Check amendments: Identify changes made to the old law before the relevant date.
  7. Build the evidence file: Match legal conclusions to invoices, returns, payments and notices.
  8. Review litigation status: Identify pending appeal, adjudication, recovery or court proceedings.
  9. Document the conclusion: Record the legal provisions and dates relied upon.

14. Common Mistakes

  • Reading Section 173 without Section 174.
  • Assuming omission of Chapter V automatically extinguished all historical service-tax liabilities.
  • Applying GST law to a pre-GST transaction without checking the transitional framework.
  • Ignoring the exact transaction date and applicable service-tax provisions.
  • Failing to preserve old service-tax returns, notices and payment records.
  • Confusing Section 173 with a general repeal of every indirect-tax law.
  • Assuming Section 173 itself contains all saving rules.
  • Ignoring a pending appeal, adjudication or recovery proceeding.
  • Using a current GST rule to answer a historical service-tax question without checking the law applicable to the relevant period.
  • Failing to distinguish Section 173's omission function from Section 174's saving function.

16. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 173 of the CGST Act?

Section 173 is titled 'Amendment of Act 32 of 1994'. It provides that, except as otherwise provided in the CGST Act, Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 shall be omitted.

2. What is Act 32 of 1994?

Act 32 of 1994 is the Finance Act, 1994. Chapter V of that Act contained the central service-tax framework that preceded GST.

3. When did Section 173 come into force?

Section 173 commenced on 1 July 2017. India Code records the commencement of Sections 165 to 174 on that date.

4. Does Section 173 abolish every old service-tax liability?

No. Section 173 must be read with Section 174, which contains detailed saving provisions for previous operation, rights, obligations, liabilities, taxes, interest, penalties, investigations, proceedings, recovery and remedies.

5. Why does Section 174 matter with Section 173?

Section 173 omits Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994, while Section 174 sets out the repeal-and-saving consequences. The two provisions form part of the transition from the old indirect-tax laws to GST.

6. Does Section 173 apply to service-tax transactions before 1 July 2017?

Section 173 is part of the transition and commenced on 1 July 2017. Historical service-tax transactions must be analysed under the law applicable to the relevant period together with the saving provisions.

7. Can an old service-tax investigation continue after GST?

Section 174 contains provisions preserving specified investigations, inquiries, verification, assessment proceedings, adjudication and other legal proceedings relating to earlier liabilities. The exact facts and statutory conditions should be checked.

8. Can old service-tax arrears still be recovered?

Section 174 expressly preserves recovery of arrears and certain related rights and liabilities, subject to its terms.

9. Does Section 173 itself contain detailed saving provisions?

No. Section 173 is a short omission provision. The detailed saving rules are principally in Section 174.

10. What does 'Save as otherwise provided in this Act' mean?

It indicates that the omission is subject to provisions elsewhere in the CGST Act that expressly provide otherwise.

11. Is Section 173 the same as repeal?

Section 173 is described as an amendment of the Finance Act, 1994 and provides for omission of Chapter V. Section 174 separately contains repeal-and-saving provisions for specified earlier laws and the amended Finance Act.

12. Does Section 173 affect State VAT or State service-tax laws?

Section 173 specifically concerns Chapter V of the Central Finance Act, 1994. Other legacy indirect-tax enactments have their own treatment under the CGST transition framework and relevant State/UT legislation.

13. Does Section 173 affect customs law?

No. Section 173 specifically addresses Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994. Customs law is outside the scope of this provision.

14. Does Section 173 create GST liability?

No. It is a transition/omission provision and does not itself levy GST.

15. Can a post-1 July 2017 service be charged under old service tax merely because the contract began earlier?

The answer depends on the applicable transitional and tax-point provisions and facts. Section 173 alone does not determine the tax treatment of a contract spanning the transition date.

16. What records should be preserved for old service-tax disputes?

Preserve contracts, invoices, returns, tax payments, ST-3 returns where applicable, notices, orders, correspondence, accounting records and the relevant statutory/notification framework.

17. What is the relationship between Section 173 and Section 174?

Section 173 omits Chapter V of the Finance Act, 1994 subject to the CGST Act; Section 174 provides the detailed repeal-and-saving consequences. Both should be read together for historical service-tax matters.

18. Is this article legal advice?

No. This is an educational explanation. Historical service-tax matters can depend on the exact transaction period, point of taxation, saving provision, notifications and judicial decisions, so the applicable law should be verified.

17. Quick Revision – Section 173

  • Chapter concerned: Chapter V — Service Tax.
  • Commencement: 1 July 2017.

  • Important phrase: “Save as otherwise provided in this Act”.
  • Key distinction: Section 173 handles omission; Section 174 handles detailed repeal and savings.
  • Compliance lesson: Historical service-tax disputes must be analysed period-wise with the applicable old law and transitional provisions.
