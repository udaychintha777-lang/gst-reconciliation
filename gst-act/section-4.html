CGST ACT • CHAPTER II — ADMINISTRATION

Section 4 — Appointment of Officers

Section 4 provides the second layer of the Central Tax administrative structure. While Section 3 identifies the classes of officers that the Government appoints by notification, Section 4 gives the Board power to appoint additional persons as officers and, through an authorised officer, to appoint officers below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.

Section 4 at a Glance

ProvisionSection 4
ChapterChapter II — Administration
SubjectAppointment of Officers

Section 4 is primarily an administrative enabling provision. It allows the Board to supplement the classes of officers notified under Section 3 and creates a mechanism for appointment of Central Tax officers below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.

In simple terms: Section 3 creates the notified officer classes; Section 4 provides an additional appointment mechanism; Section 5 deals with how powers and duties may be exercised and delegated.

1. What Does Section 4 Do?

The administration of GST requires more than a list of senior designations. A large tax administration needs officers at different operational levels and needs flexibility to place the required officers in the field.

Section 4 addresses this requirement in two ways. First, the Board can appoint persons as officers in addition to those notified by the Government under Section 3. Second, the Board can authorise an officer covered by Section 3(a) to (h) to appoint Central Tax officers below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.

This creates an administrative chain from the statutory officer structure to the actual working organisation used for GST administration.

2. Section 4(1) — Board's Power to Appoint Additional Officers

Section 4(1) provides that the Board may, in addition to the officers notified by the Government under Section 3, appoint such persons as it considers fit to be officers under the CGST Act.

The significance of the words “in addition to” is important. Section 4 does not replace Section 3. The Government's notification under Section 3 and the Board's appointment power under Section 4 operate as complementary parts of the administrative structure.

The expression “such persons as it may think fit” provides administrative flexibility. It allows the Board to appoint officers needed for actual administration without having to amend the CGST Act every time the working officer structure changes.

Practical example: The Government has notified the statutory classes of Central Tax officers under Section 3. The Board may then appoint persons to serve as officers under the Act in accordance with Section 4, so that the administrative machinery can function at the required operational level.

3. Section 4(2) — Appointment of Officers Below Assistant Commissioner

Section 4(2) provides an additional mechanism. The Board may, by order, authorise any officer referred to in clauses (a) to (h) of Section 3 to appoint Central Tax officers below the rank of Assistant Commissioner for administration of the Act.

This is effectively a controlled delegation of the appointment function. The Board does not give an unrestricted appointment power to every officer. The statutory provision identifies the senior officer classes that may be authorised and limits the resulting appointments to officers below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.

Important distinction: Section 4(2) is about the authority to appoint officers. It should not be confused with Section 5, which deals with exercise of statutory powers and discharge of duties.

4. Section 3 vs Section 4 vs Section 5

ProvisionMain purposeKey question
Section 3Identifies classes of officers appointed by Government through notification.What classes of Central Tax officers are recognised under the Act?
Section 4Provides additional appointment powers to the Board and authorised senior officers.Who can be appointed as an officer under the Act?
Section 5Deals with powers, duties and delegation.Who can exercise a particular statutory power or perform a particular duty?
Section 6Deals with authorisation of State/UT tax officers as proper officers in specified circumstances.When can State/UT officers act as proper officers under the CGST Act?

This distinction is particularly important in litigation. An officer's appointment status and the officer's authority to exercise a particular statutory function are related questions, but they are not identical questions.

5. When Did Section 4 Come Into Force?

Section 4 was brought into force from 22 June 2017 through Notification No. 01/2017-Central Tax dated 19 June 2017.

The same notification brought Sections 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 22 to 30, 139, 146 and 164 into force from 22 June 2017. The remaining major groups of CGST provisions were subsequently brought into force from 1 July 2017 through Notification No. 09/2017-Central Tax dated 28 June 2017.

Section 4 therefore formed part of the early administrative foundation of the GST system and was operational before the principal charging and compliance provisions commenced on 1 July 2017.

6. Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax — Why It Matters

Section 4 should be read with the initial officer and jurisdiction framework notified in 2017. Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax dated 19 June 2017 was issued to notify the jurisdiction of Central Tax officers.

The notification was subsequently amended on multiple occasions as the Central Tax administrative structure developed. These changes are important because the officer structure and jurisdiction applicable today cannot automatically be assumed to be identical to the position in 2017.

19 June 2017
Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax — initial jurisdiction framework for Central Tax officers.
1 July 2017
Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax — assigned jurisdiction and powers to officers of various directorates.
2018–2022 and later
Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax and related jurisdiction arrangements were amended from time to time to reflect administrative changes, specialised formations and revised jurisdiction.
Research point: Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax is a jurisdiction instrument. It should not be described simply as an amendment to Section 4. The Act provides the appointment power; the notification/order gives effect to the administrative structure and jurisdiction.

7. Amendments to Section 4 — What Changed?

A review of the current statutory text against the original text shows that the operative wording of Section 4 remains the same in its two sub-sections. In other words, there is no major substantive rewrite of Section 4 comparable to the amendments seen in several other GST provisions.

The important developments around Section 4 have instead been through the appointment, jurisdiction and function-assignment framework under notifications and orders.

Important legal-research conclusion: Do not create an artificial amendment history for Section 4 merely because Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax or subsequent jurisdiction notifications were amended. Those are administrative instruments operating under the statutory framework.

Some secondary sources describe Section 4 as having been retrospectively amended by the Finance Act, 2023. The enacted Finance Act provisions and the current India Code text do not establish a corresponding substantive change to the wording of Section 4. Accordingly, this article does not present that unsupported claim as an amendment to Section 4.

8. GST Council Background

The GST Council considered the need to notify the administrative provisions at the beginning of the GST regime. The 15th GST Council meeting agenda specifically recorded that Sections 3, 4 and 5 of the CGST Act needed to be notified so that officers could be appointed under the CGST Act and the Government could reorganise its field formations before the appointed date.

The agenda proposed notification of Sections 1, 3, 4 and 5 from 19 June 2017. This illustrates why Section 4 was brought into the administrative framework before the main GST provisions became effective from 1 July 2017.

The Council's role here should be distinguished from the legal instrument that actually brought the provision into force. The Council's recommendation/agenda is part of the policy and legislative process; the notification issued under the Act creates the operative legal effect.

9. Circulars and Function Assignment — Why They Matter

Circular No. 3/3/2017-GST dated 5 July 2017

This circular assigned specified functions as proper officer to designated Central Tax officers under various provisions of the CGST Act. It is useful for understanding the practical relationship between the officer hierarchy and the statutory functions to be performed.

For example, a taxpayer receiving a notice should not examine only whether the issuing officer is a Central Tax officer. The taxpayer should also identify the statutory provision invoked and whether the particular function has been assigned to that officer.

Later Proper Officer Circulars

CBIC has issued and amended circulars over the years assigning or revising proper-officer functions. For example, Circular No. 31/05/2018-GST dealt with proper officers for Sections 73 and 74, and later Circular No. 239/33/2024-GST amended that framework. Such circulars are relevant to the exercise of functions, but they should not be confused with an amendment to Section 4 itself.

10. Appointment Power vs Delegation of Statutory Powers

A recurring source of confusion is the difference between appointing an officer and delegating a statutory power.

  • Section 4: deals with appointment of persons as officers.
  • Section 5: deals with exercise of powers and discharge of duties.
  • Proper-officer assignment: identifies the officer responsible for a particular statutory function.
  • Jurisdiction notification: determines the territorial or specialised administrative area in which officers operate.

Therefore, a taxpayer challenging an order should analyse all four dimensions instead of making a general objection that “the officer had no authority”.

11. Practical Examples

Example 1 — Assistant Commissioner issues a notice

A taxpayer receives a notice from an Assistant Commissioner. The fact that Assistant Commissioner is a recognised class under Section 3 does not by itself prove that the officer is the proper officer for the particular notice. The relevant statutory provision and function assignment must also be checked.

Example 2 — Superintendent performs an administrative function

A Superintendent of Central Tax is below the rank of Assistant Commissioner. If the officer has been appointed through the mechanism authorised under Section 4(2), and the particular function has been properly assigned, the officer may validly perform the relevant function. The analysis should therefore cover both appointment and function assignment.

Example 3 — Change in GST jurisdiction

A registered company moves its principal place of business from one Commissionerate to another. For a later proceeding, the finance team should check the jurisdiction notification applicable to the relevant date rather than relying on the officer structure that existed in 2017.

Example 4 — DGGI investigation

A taxpayer receives communication from an officer working in a specialised directorate. The correct analysis requires checking the notification assigning jurisdiction and powers to officers of that directorate, along with the relevant statutory provisions.

Example 5 — Historical proceeding from 2017

If a company is defending a proceeding relating to 2017, the legal team should establish the appointment and jurisdiction framework applicable on the relevant date. A current designation or present Commissionerate structure should not automatically be applied retrospectively.

Example 6 — Appointment vs power

Suppose an officer was validly appointed under Section 4 but later exercises a power that was not assigned to that officer. Valid appointment alone does not answer whether that particular statutory action was authorised. Section 5 and the applicable assignment framework become relevant.

12. Practical Checklist for Management, Tax Team and Auditors

  1. Identify the designation of the officer.
  2. Check the officer's appointment under the relevant statutory framework.
  3. Identify the taxpayer's Central Tax jurisdiction for the relevant date.
  4. Identify the exact section under which the officer is acting.
  5. Check whether the officer is the designated proper officer for that function.
  6. Check any delegation/order relied upon.
  7. For historical matters, verify the notification/order applicable on the relevant date.
  8. Retain copies of relevant notifications and circulars in the litigation/audit file.

13. Statutory Text of Section 4

The provision is reproduced below for ease of reference:

4. Appointment of officers.—

(1) The Board may, in addition to the officers as may be notified by the Government under
section 3, appoint such persons as it may think fit to be the officers under this Act.

(2) Without prejudice to the provisions of sub-section (1), the Board may, by order, authorise
any officer referred to in clauses (a) to (h) of section 3 to appoint officers of central tax
below the rank of Assistant Commissioner of central tax for the administration of this Act.

The statutory text should be read with the applicable appointment, jurisdiction, delegation and proper-officer notifications/orders for the relevant period.

14. Key Takeaways

  • Section 4 provides an additional mechanism for appointment of officers under the CGST Act.
  • The Board may appoint persons in addition to officers notified under Section 3.
  • The Board may authorise specified senior officers to appoint Central Tax officers below the rank of Assistant Commissioner.
  • Section 4 is about appointment; Section 5 is about powers and duties.
  • Notification No. 01/2017-Central Tax brought Section 4 into force from 22 June 2017.
  • Notification No. 02/2017-Central Tax established the initial Central Tax jurisdiction framework.
  • Notification No. 14/2017-Central Tax assigned jurisdiction and powers to officers of various directorates.
  • Proper-officer circulars are important for identifying who may perform particular statutory functions.
  • The operative wording of Section 4 has remained substantially unchanged; administrative instruments around it have evolved.
  • For a historical dispute, the appointment and jurisdiction framework applicable on the relevant date should be verified.
