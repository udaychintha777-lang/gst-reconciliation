1. Executive summary – education is not automatically GST-exempt

GST exemption depends on the legal definition of an “educational institution”, the nature of the course, the recipient and the exact service supplied. The fact that a business teaches students, issues a certificate or operates an online learning platform does not by itself make the service exempt.

Generally exempt

Services supplied by a qualifying educational institution to its students, faculty and staff under Entry 66(a) of Notification 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate).

Generally taxable

Commercial coaching, private tuition, examination preparation and non-qualifying online courses are generally taxable at 18%, subject to the exact facts and any specific exemption.

Hostel accommodation

A separate exemption may apply where accommodation is not more than ₹20,000 per person per month and is supplied for a minimum continuous period of 90 days.

ITC

Exempt supplies generally restrict ITC attributable to them. Taxable coaching businesses may claim eligible ITC subject to Sections 16 and 17.

Key compliance principle Analyse each revenue stream separately: tuition, admission, examination, coaching, study material, hostel, food, transport, sponsorship, affiliation and online subscriptions may have different GST treatment.

2. Who is an “educational institution” under GST?

The definition is in paragraph 2(y) of Notification No. 12/2017-Central Tax (Rate), dated 28 June 2017. It covers an institution providing education through any of the following routes:

CategoryCoveragePractical examples
Clause (i)Pre-school education and education up to higher secondary school or equivalent.Pre-schools, primary schools, secondary schools and higher secondary schools.
Clause (ii)Education as part of a curriculum for obtaining a qualification recognised by any law for the time being in force.Recognised degree, diploma or other legally recognised qualification programmes where the institution delivers the prescribed curriculum.
Clause (iii)Education as part of an approved vocational education course.Specified ITI/ITC and approved vocational courses satisfying the notification definition.

Approved vocational education course – do not assume every skill course qualifies

The notification definition is specific. It includes courses run by an industrial training institute or industrial training centre affiliated to the National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET) or a State Council for Vocational Training (SCVT), offering designated trades notified under the Apprentices Act, 1961. It also covers specified Modular Employable Skill Courses approved by NCVET and run by a person registered with the Directorate General of Training, Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship.

Common mistake: A private institute does not become an educational institution merely because its course prepares students for CA, CMA, ACCA, CPA, CS, JEE, NEET, banking or other examinations. Determine who conducts the course, who awards the qualification, whether the curriculum is recognised by law and whether the institute itself satisfies clause 2(y).

Educational Board clarification

Central and State Educational Boards are treated as educational institutions for the limited purpose of providing services by way of conduct of examinations to students. This limited deeming provision should not be extended to every activity of a board or council.

3. Entry 66 of Notification 12/2017 – what exactly is exempt?

Serial No. 66 is the principal GST exemption for specified educational services. It is not a blanket exemption for all suppliers operating in the education sector.

EntryNature of serviceCore condition
66(a)Services provided by an educational institution to its students, faculty and staff.Supplier must qualify as an educational institution under paragraph 2(y).
66(aa)Conduct of entrance examination by an educational institution against entrance fee.Must be an entrance examination conducted by the qualifying institution.
66(b)(i)Transportation of students, faculty and staff to an educational institution.Limited to qualifying institutions providing pre-school education and education up to higher secondary or equivalent.
66(b)(ii)Catering, including specified government-sponsored mid-day meal schemes.Subject to the institution category and the exact outsourced/direct supply arrangement.
66(b)(iii)Security, cleaning or housekeeping services performed in the educational institution.Subject to the institution category specified in the proviso.
66(b)(iv)Services relating to admission to, or conduct of examination by, the institution.Recipient must be a qualifying educational institution and the service must be directly related to admission or examination.
66(b)(v)Supply of online educational journals or periodicals to an educational institution.Subject to the specific proviso and institution/course category in the notification.
Important reading rule: The service codes in the exemption notification are indicative; the description of the exempt service and the conditions in the notification control. Do not rely on SAC alone to claim exemption.

Entry 66(a) is broad as to services supplied by a qualifying institution to its own students, faculty and staff. However, a separate supply by a third-party vendor to the institution must be tested under the specific clauses of Entry 66(b), not automatically treated as exempt.

5. GST on coaching centres, tuition classes and competitive examination institutes

Private coaching and commercial training are generally taxable at 18% under the applicable rate entry for commercial training or coaching services, commonly classified under SAC 999293. The exemption is not available merely because students are enrolled in recognised schools or the coaching is aligned to a school syllabus.

ActivityIndicative treatmentReason / review point
Private tuition for school studentsGenerally 18% if supplied as commercial coaching by a non-qualifying institution.Coaching provider is not automatically the school or educational institution.
JEE / NEET / UPSC / banking coachingGenerally 18%.Examination preparation is not by itself a recognised qualification curriculum supplied by a qualifying institution.
CA / CMA / CS / ACCA / CPA coachingGenerally 18%, subject to course structure and the exact legal status of the provider.Preparation for an examination administered by a professional body does not automatically make the coaching institute an educational institution.
Corporate training / employee upskillingGenerally taxable at 18% unless a specific exemption applies.Review whether a specific approved vocational or government skill programme exemption is available.
Recognised college conducting its own degree curriculumGenerally exempt under Entry 66(a).Institution and curriculum must satisfy paragraph 2(y).
Practical conclusion A student may attend a GST-exempt school and separately pay a taxable coaching centre. The tax treatment of the school fee does not flow through to the coaching centre.

6. Online courses, recorded lectures, e-learning and OIDAR

Online delivery does not automatically make an educational service exempt, nor does it automatically make the service an OIDAR service. First identify the nature of the underlying supply and the supplier's legal status.

OIDAR test under Section 2(17) of the IGST Act

OIDAR services are services whose delivery is mediated by information technology over the internet or an electronic network and whose nature makes their supply essentially automated, involving minimal human intervention and impossible without information technology. The statutory definition and its application must be tested against the actual service model.

Business modelGST analysis
Automated access to a digital library, database, downloadable digital content or self-service platformMay fall within OIDAR depending on the exact features, automation and statutory definition.
Live teacher-led online classes with interaction, doubt solving and mentoringMay be commercial training/coaching rather than OIDAR; examine substance and facts.
Pre-recorded lectures bundled with live sessions, mentoring, mock tests and assessmentAnalyse the composite supply and principal supply; do not classify solely by the video format.
Online degree course supplied by a qualifying educational institutionMay remain exempt under Entry 66(a) if the institution and curriculum satisfy the definition.
Subscription to online educational journals by an institutionCheck the specific Entry 66(b)(v) conditions and provisos.
Cross-border caution: Where a foreign supplier provides OIDAR services to a non-taxable online recipient in India, the special provisions under Section 14 of the IGST Act may apply. Do not apply the domestic coaching analysis to a foreign digital supplier without checking the cross-border rules.

7. Important 2026 advance rulings – coaching and online education

7.1 Friends Classes – academic coaching to school students

In the Gujarat AAR matter concerning Sanjaykumar Ishwerlal Sadadiwala, trading as Friends Classes, reported in 2026, the authority considered supplementary academic coaching supplied to students of Standards 5 to 12. The coaching provider was held not to qualify as an educational institution merely because its students were enrolled in schools following recognised curricula. The coaching service was treated as commercial training/coaching and taxable at 18%.

7.2 Allen Career Institute Private Limited – online coaching and OIDAR

In Advance Ruling No. RAJ/AAR/2025-26/23 dated 25 March 2026, the Rajasthan AAR examined online training supplied through live and recorded digital platforms. The ruling treated the applicant's structured, teacher-led competitive examination coaching as commercial training and coaching under SAC 999293 rather than OIDAR, considering the human-led teaching, interaction, doubt resolution, assessments and other facts. It also examined place of supply for the applicant's supplies to students in other States under Section 12(5) of the IGST Act.

7.3 Learnfluence Education – professional course coaching

In the Kerala AAR matter, M/s Learnfluence Education Limited (formerly Learnfluence Education Private Limited), Order No. KER/18/2026 dated 21 August 2026, the authority examined coaching for professional courses including CA, ACCA, CMA, CS and CPA. The ruling declined to treat the private coaching provider as an educational institution under paragraph 2(y) merely because the professional qualification was recognised or awarded by an external professional body. It also considered bundled study materials, examination fees collected as agent, foreign exchange differences and training-partner receipts.

How to use AAR decisions: Under Section 103 of the CGST Act, an advance ruling binds only the applicant and the concerned or jurisdictional officer, subject to the statutory conditions. These rulings are useful indicators of departmental interpretation and risk, but are not universal judgments binding every coaching provider. Compare the facts, agreements, course approvals and legal structure before relying on them.

8. GST on hostels, student residences, PG accommodation and boarding

Hostel accommodation requires a separate analysis. It is not automatically exempt merely because the occupants are students or the hostel is operated by a school, college or charitable trust.

Specific exemption under Entry 12A

Notification No. 04/2024-Central Tax (Rate) dated 12 July 2024 inserted Entry 12A in Notification 12/2017-CT(R), effective from 15 July 2024. It exempts accommodation services having a value of supply of not more than ₹20,000 per person per month, provided the accommodation is supplied for a minimum continuous period of 90 days.

ConditionRequirement
Monthly valueNot more than ₹20,000 per person per month.
DurationMinimum continuous period of 90 days.
Effective date15 July 2024.
Type of supplyAccommodation services under Heading 9963, subject to the entry's wording.

CBIC Circular No. 228/22/2024-GST dated 15 July 2024 also regularised the specified past period on an “as is where is” basis where the value and 90-day conditions were met.

Examples

ArrangementIndicative Entry 12A result
₹8,000 per student per month for 10 monthsExempt if the continuous 90-day condition and other requirements are satisfied.
₹20,000 per student per month for 4 monthsWithin the monetary ceiling; verify continuous stay and value per person.
₹22,000 per student per month for 6 monthsDoes not satisfy the ₹20,000 ceiling; examine the applicable taxable accommodation rate.
₹12,000 for a 45-day short stayDoes not satisfy the minimum continuous 90-day condition.
Do not use the residential dwelling exemption automatically. Notification 04/2024 expressly clarifies that the residential dwelling renting exemption does not cover accommodation services for students in student residences or services provided by hostels, camps, PG accommodation and similar facilities. The separate Entry 12A conditions must be examined.

For taxable accommodation outside Entry 12A, determine the correct classification and rate under the rate notification as amended for the relevant date. Do not assume that every hostel room is taxed at one fixed rate without checking the applicable accommodation rate entry and value slab.

9. Transport, catering, canteen, security and housekeeping

Service provider / arrangementGST treatment to examine

Third-party transport operator supplies student transport to a qualifying schoolMay be exempt under Entry 66(b)(i), subject to the institution category and exact service.
School itself supplies meals to students, faculty and staffExempt under Entry 66(a); CBIC Circular 85/04/2019-GST confirms the treatment of food supplied by the institution itself.
Outsourced caterer supplies food under a contractGenerally taxable at 5% under the relevant catering entry as clarified by Circular 85/04/2019-GST; test the exact facts and exemption wording.
Security / cleaning / housekeeping supplied to a qualifying schoolMay be exempt under Entry 66(b)(iii), subject to the school-level institution restriction and statutory conditions.

Where a school or college operates a canteen through a contractor, review the contract, who supplies food to whom, who collects the student payment, whether the institution is the recipient, and whether the service is a catering contract or a separate sale of food. The name “canteen” does not decide the tax treatment.

10. Books, study material, uniforms, stationery, tablets and other goods

Entry 66 is an exemption for specified services. It does not automatically exempt goods sold by a school, college, bookshop or third-party vendor.

ItemGST approach
Printed books falling under HSN 4901Generally Nil-rated under the applicable goods rate notification, subject to correct classification.
Notebooks, exercise books and stationeryCheck the specific HSN and rate; do not treat all educational stationery as exempt.
School uniforms and footwearTaxable at the applicable goods rate based on classification and product specifications.
Computers, tablets and electronic devicesGenerally taxable at the applicable rate; educational use does not itself create an exemption.
Printed study material bundled with coachingExamine composite supply and principal supply; do not automatically split the book value from coaching consideration.

Where books or materials are supplied as part of a single coaching package, apply Sections 2(30) and 8 of the CGST Act and the relevant facts. A separate invoice description or price allocation does not by itself establish separate independent supplies.

11. Affiliation, accreditation, inspection and examination services

ServiceGST position / clarification

Board / council affiliation services to private schoolsGenerally taxable; affiliation is distinct from admission or conduct of examination.
Affiliation services by Central / State educational boards or similar bodies to government schoolsExempt under Entry 66A inserted by Notification 08/2024-CT(R), effective 10 October 2024, subject to the definition of government school.
Specified past affiliation duesCircular 234/28/2024-GST regularised payment for the period 1 July 2017 to 17 June 2021 on an “as is where is” basis for the specified services to schools.
Services directly relating to admission or conduct of examinationMay be exempt under Entry 66(b)(iv) where the recipient and nature of service satisfy the entry.

Do not confuse affiliation or accreditation (monitoring institutional standards and granting permission to operate) with examination-related services. The 2024 clarification expressly distinguishes the two.

12. Donations, grants, sponsorships and CSR funding

A genuine donation without any identifiable reciprocal benefit is generally not consideration for a supply. However, the label “donation”, “grant” or “CSR contribution” is not conclusive. Examine whether the payer receives advertising, naming rights, event promotion, access, branding, a research deliverable or another benefit in return.

ReceiptGST analysis
Voluntary donation with no benefit to donorGenerally outside the scope of supply where there is no quid pro quo.
Donation with prominent logo display or advertising commitmentsMay be consideration for sponsorship / advertising services and taxable.
Government grant with no direct supply to grantorExamine grant conditions and whether a supply is made to the Government.
Research grant against specified research deliverablesAnalyse the recipient, contractual obligations and any specific exemption entry; do not assume every grant is outside GST.
CSR funding for a project implemented by an institutionReview whether the institution supplies services to the corporate donor or merely receives a contribution without reciprocal obligation.

Maintain sanction letters, grant agreements, utilisation certificates, donor correspondence, board approvals and evidence of whether any promotional or other benefit was promised.

13. GST registration and taxable turnover

An institution making only exempt supplies is generally not required to register solely because it receives exempt education fees, subject to the statutory provisions and any other taxable activities. Where taxable coaching, accommodation, goods, renting, sponsorship or other taxable supplies are made, registration must be evaluated using aggregate turnover and the applicable threshold rules.

  • Aggregate turnover is PAN-based and includes taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports and inter-State supplies, but excludes GST taxes and inward supplies taxable under RCM.
  • For service providers, the general registration threshold is commonly ₹20 lakh, with a lower threshold in specified special category States, subject to applicable notifications and compulsory registration provisions.
  • Do not treat every receipt as exempt education turnover. Separate taxable coaching, accommodation, goods and other supplies.
  • Where the entity operates in multiple States, evaluate separate State registrations and the place of supply rules.
  • Where an institution supplies only exempt services, Section 23 may be relevant; review its exact applicability before deciding not to register.
Example: A school charges exempt tuition fees of ₹1.50 crore and separately operates a taxable commercial shop selling uniforms and stationery. The institution must analyse the taxable goods business, aggregate turnover and registration consequences; it should not assume that all revenue is exempt because its main activity is education.

14. Input tax credit – Sections 16, 17 and Rules 42 & 43

ITC treatment depends on whether inputs and input services are used for taxable supplies, exempt supplies or non-business purposes.

Input / expenseTypical ITC position
Books and teaching materials used exclusively for exempt educationITC attributable to exempt supplies is not available under Section 17(2), read with Rule 42 where applicable.
Faculty salariesSalary to employees in the course of employment is generally outside GST; no input GST credit arises on salary itself.
Rent, security, housekeeping and software used exclusively for exempt educationInput GST is generally attributable to exempt supplies and not available as ITC.
Inputs used exclusively for taxable coaching servicesPotentially eligible subject to Section 16, invoice, receipt, supplier reporting and other conditions.
Common expenses used for taxable coaching and exempt educationCommon ITC must be apportioned under Rule 42; capital goods may require Rule 43.
Construction of immovable property / civil worksReview Section 17(5), especially construction on own account and works contract restrictions. Business use alone does not remove the statutory block.
Motor vehicles, food and beverages, club / personal expensesCheck specific blocked-credit clauses and statutory exceptions; no blanket entitlement.

Practical ITC segregation

  1. Identify each GST registration and each business vertical / activity.
  2. Tag inward supplies as exclusively taxable, exclusively exempt, non-business or common.
  3. Reverse ITC attributable to exempt supplies under Rule 42 and capital goods under Rule 43, as applicable.
  4. Review blocked credits under Section 17(5) separately; do not mix them with Rule 42 reversals.
  5. Reconcile eligible credits with GSTR-2B and purchase register.
  6. Maintain a monthly ITC working and year-end true-up.
Important: Exempt education revenue can materially reduce common ITC. An institution running both exempt degree programmes and taxable coaching or commercial activities should maintain a documented Rule 42 / Rule 43 working rather than claiming all common input tax.

15. Place of supply and time of supply

Place of supply – training and performance appraisal

For training and performance appraisal services supplied to a registered person, Section 12(5)(a) of the IGST Act generally places the supply at the location of the recipient. Where supplied to an unregistered person, Section 12(5)(b) generally refers to the place where the services are actually performed. The OIDAR provisions have a separate place-of-supply framework.

The Allen Career Institute Rajasthan AAR ruling dated 25 March 2026 is a useful illustration of the interaction between classification and place of supply for online coaching. Its conclusion must be read in the context of the applicant's facts and the binding limits of an advance ruling.

Time of supply

For services, determine time of supply under Section 13 of the CGST Act, including invoice date, provision of service and receipt of payment, as applicable. For goods supplied separately, apply Section 12. Review advances, instalments, semester fees, refunds and credit notes in accordance with the statutory provisions and current notifications.

Practical control: For online and multi-State training, collect the student's registration status, billing address, State, course type, delivery method and contract details before issuing the invoice. The student location alone does not decide every place-of-supply question.

19. GST audit and internal control checklist

  1. Obtain the institution's recognition, affiliation, course approvals and statutory authorisations.
  2. Map each course to paragraph 2(y) of Notification 12/2017-CT(R).
  3. Separate qualifying education from private coaching, professional examination preparation and commercial training.
  4. Review every fee type: tuition, admission, examination, application, revaluation, migration, certificate and late fee.
  5. Review online learning platforms for automation, human intervention, live interaction and bundled services.
  6. Examine hostel occupancy, monthly per-person charges and continuous 90-day records for Entry 12A.
  7. Review transport, catering, security and housekeeping contracts against Entry 66(b) and its provisos.
  8. Verify books, uniforms, stationery and electronic goods by HSN; do not apply service exemptions to goods.
  9. Review affiliation, accreditation and inspection invoices separately from examination-related services.
  10. Classify donations, grants, sponsorships and CSR receipts based on reciprocal obligations.
  11. Evaluate aggregate turnover and GST registration State-wise.
  12. Maintain separate taxable, exempt and non-GST income ledgers.
  13. Reconcile outward supplies with GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and the trial balance.
  14. Prepare Rule 42 and Rule 43 ITC workings and retain the annual true-up.
  15. Review blocked ITC under Section 17(5), especially construction, catering and motor vehicle expenses.
  16. Check e-invoicing applicability, invoice series, credit notes and refund records.
  17. Reconcile GSTR-2B with the purchase register and vendor invoices.
  18. Retain contracts, fee circulars, student records, hostel registers, vendor agreements and tax workings.
Recommended accounting structure Maintain separate ledgers for Exempt Tuition Income, Taxable Coaching Income, Hostel Accommodation – Exempt, Hostel Accommodation – Taxable, Admission / Examination Fees, Sale of Books, Uniforms & Stationery, Catering Income, Sponsorship Income, Output GST, Input GST Eligible, Input GST Ineligible and Rule 42 / Rule 43 Reversal.

20. Frequently asked questions

1. Is GST applicable on school fees?

Services supplied by a qualifying school to its students are generally exempt under Entry 66(a) of Notification 12/2017-CT(R).

2. Are all colleges and universities GST-exempt?

No. The institution must provide education as part of a curriculum for a qualification recognised by law or satisfy another limb of the educational institution definition.

3. Is GST applicable to private tuition classes?

Generally yes, at 18%, where the tuition provider is supplying commercial coaching and does not qualify for a specific exemption.

4. Is CA coaching exempt because CA is a recognised qualification?

Not automatically. A private coaching centre that prepares students for examinations conducted by a professional body does not become an educational institution merely because the final qualification is recognised.

5. Is online coaching exempt?

Online delivery does not itself create an exemption. The supplier, course, curriculum and nature of the service must satisfy the exemption conditions.

6. Is every online course an OIDAR service?

No. Apply the statutory OIDAR definition, including the degree of automation and human intervention. Live teacher-led coaching may be commercial training rather than OIDAR.

7. Is hostel accommodation for students exempt?

It may be exempt under Entry 12A where value does not exceed ₹20,000 per person per month and the service is supplied for at least 90 continuous days.

8. Is the ₹20,000 hostel limit per room or per student?

The entry specifies value per person per month. Maintain records to identify the value attributable to each person.

9. Is hostel stay for 60 days exempt under Entry 12A?

No. The minimum continuous period is 90 days. Check the taxable accommodation treatment separately.

10. Is outsourced school catering exempt?

CBIC Circular 85/04/2019-GST clarifies that food supplied by the institution itself to students, faculty and staff is exempt, while outsourced catering under contract is generally taxable at 5%, subject to the exact entry and facts.

11. Are admission and migration certificate fees taxable?

Fees charged by a qualifying educational institution for admission, entrance application, eligibility certificate in the admission process and migration certificate are covered by the clarification in Circular 177/09/2022-TRU.

12. Is university affiliation fee taxable?

Yes, affiliation services by universities to constituent colleges are taxable at 18% as clarified in Circular 234/28/2024-GST.

13. Can an institution claim ITC on rent and software?

Only to the extent credit is attributable to taxable supplies and other Section 16 conditions are met. Common credits must be apportioned under Rule 42 / Rule 43.

14. Are school uniforms exempt because they are compulsory?

No. Goods are classified and taxed under their applicable HSN entries. The educational service exemption does not automatically cover uniforms or stationery.

15. Is a donation received by a school taxable?

A genuine voluntary donation without reciprocal benefit is generally not consideration for a supply. If the donor receives advertising, sponsorship or other benefits, examine GST separately.

16. Does an AAR ruling apply to every coaching institute?

No. Under Section 103, an advance ruling binds the applicant and the concerned or jurisdictional officer, subject to statutory conditions. It may still be persuasive for similar facts.

Practical takeaway

For every education-sector receipt, identify the supplier, recipient, course approval, legal recognition, service description and consideration. Keep exempt tuition separate from taxable coaching, accommodation, goods and sponsorship. For hostel accommodation, document the per-person monthly value and 90-day continuity. For mixed activities, prepare a defensible ITC apportionment working and reconcile it to GST returns.
