FY 2025–26: Reconciliation working papers and practical decisions

Updated 28 November 2026. Based on the November 2026 ICAI Technical Guide on GST Reconciliation Statement, with original illustrative workings. Read this supplement alongside the notified FY 2025–26 form; where historical examples differ, use the current instructions.

1. Determine applicability and filing sequence

Section 44 and Rule 80(3) require a self-certified GSTR-9C where aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore, subject to statutory exclusions. Aggregate turnover is PAN-wide, not GSTIN-wise. A business with Telangana turnover ₹4 crore and Maharashtra turnover ₹3 crore crosses the threshold; both regular registrations require reconciliation statements even though neither individually exceeds ₹5 crore. Finalize GSTR-9 before filing GSTR-9C. Normal deadline is 31 December 2026, subject to extension. Attach the required audited annual financial statements and verify portal requirements.

2. Collect the financial and GST evidence

Obtain signed audited financial statements, notes, GSTIN-wise trial balances, ledger extracts, sales and purchase registers, unbilled revenue and advance schedules, fixed asset disposal register, branch transfer and cross-charge records, credit notes, export and SEZ evidence, RCM register, ISD records, GSTR-1/1A, GSTR-3B, Table 8A, GSTR-9 workings and electronic ledgers. Capture original document date, return period, tax head, rate, accounting ledger, GSTIN and adjustment reason. Preserve a mapping from every reported figure to source records.

4. Worked turnover bridge (illustrative)

Audited GSTIN revenue ₹10 crore includes ₹30 lakh closing unbilled revenue for which GST is not yet due. Opening unbilled revenue ₹20 lakh is taxable this year and distinct-person supplies ₹10 lakh are not recorded in revenue. Adjusted turnover = ₹10 crore + ₹20 lakh − ₹30 lakh + ₹10 lakh = ₹10 crore. If GSTR-9 turnover is ₹9.95 crore, investigate ₹5 lakh difference. If this is an omitted taxable invoice at 18%, potential tax is ₹90,000 plus applicable interest. If instead it is a documented reporting-period difference, explain and map the actual return/payment evidence. Do not assume all unbilled revenue automatically follows these signs without testing time of supply.

5. Taxable turnover and rate reconciliation

Assume adjusted turnover ₹10 crore includes exempt supplies ₹50 lakh and eligible zero-rated supplies without payment ₹1 crore. Taxable turnover for the simplified example is ₹8.50 crore. If ₹2 crore is taxable at 12% and ₹6.50 crore at 18%, liability is ₹24 lakh + ₹117 lakh = ₹141 lakh. Compare by IGST, CGST, SGST and cess with GSTR-9 and payment records, not merely combined tax. If ₹140 lakh is discharged, investigate ₹1 lakh by invoice and tax head before deciding whether additional payment is due. Maintain separate pre/post 22 September 2025 rate buckets and apply time-of-supply rules for rate changes.

6. Worked ITC bridge

Assume book ITC ₹30 lakh includes ₹2 lakh relating to the current year but claimed next year. Prior-year credit ₹1 lakh was claimed this year. Adjusted book credit is ₹29 lakh (30 + 1 − 2). If comparable annual return ITC is ₹28.80 lakh, investigate ₹20,000. It may be blocked credit, a posting error, reversal, or timing difference. Align the gross/net basis and treatment of reclaims with the actual GSTR-9C instructions before drawing conclusions. A difference alone is not proof of excess credit.

7. Multi-registration controls

Prepare a GSTIN allocation matrix with one row per ledger and columns for each registration, unallocated amounts and audited total. Use actual invoice location and supply evidence for direct revenue and purchases. For common costs, document the allocation basis and separately review ISD and cross-charge obligations; allocation of accounting expense does not itself transfer ITC. Reconcile branch supplies eliminated in entity financial statements to GST records.

8. Final decisions and review

Classify each mismatch as timing, valuation, classification, exemption, tax head, omission, duplicate, ITC eligibility or payment difference. Record amount, legal basis, supporting document, corrective action, payment reference and reviewer. Where additional liability is established, determine lawful discharge and interest; do not treat GSTR-9C as a revised monthly return. Under Circular 246/03/2025-GST, late fee for incomplete annual return continues until required GSTR-9C is filed; Table 17 captures the applicable late fee. Review the notified form and portal validation before self-certification.

1. What is Form GSTR-9C?

Form GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement that compares the figures reported in the annual return in Form GSTR-9 with the corresponding figures derived from the taxpayer's annual financial statements and books of account.

It is intended to bring differences into the open: turnover that appears in the accounts but not in the returns, tax paid that differs from the tax determined, or ITC reported in the annual return that does not reconcile with the books and supporting records.

Turnover reconciliation

Explain the bridge between financial-statement turnover and turnover reported in GSTR-9, including adjustments and differences in taxable turnover.

Tax-paid reconciliation

Compare tax liability emerging from the reconciliation with liability reported and discharged through returns and ledgers.

ITC reconciliation

Compare ITC in annual return with the eligible credit supported by purchase records, expense ledgers, fixed assets and other relevant documents.

Unreconciled differences

Identify the reason, quantify the impact, decide whether a correction or payment is required, and retain evidence for the conclusion.

Key distinction: GSTR-9 is the annual return. GSTR-9C is the reconciliation statement required for eligible taxpayers above the prescribed turnover threshold. The requirement is not the same as the earlier GST audit certification regime; GSTR-9C is self-certified under the current framework.

2. Who is required to file GSTR-9C?

Under Rule 80(3) of the CGST Rules, a registered person whose aggregate turnover during a financial year exceeds ₹5 crore is generally required to furnish a self-certified reconciliation statement in Form GSTR-9C along with the annual return, subject to the statutory exclusions and any applicable notifications or special provisions.

Do not confuse the threshold with a single GSTIN's turnover. Aggregate turnover is generally computed on an all-India basis for persons having the same PAN, across taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports and inter-State supplies, while excluding specified items such as GST taxes and inward supplies liable to reverse charge, as provided in the law. The reconciliation statement, however, is prepared with the relevant GSTIN/state-wise financial information and the applicable form instructions.
QuestionPractical review
Is aggregate turnover above ₹5 crore?Compute it for the relevant financial year using PAN-level data and the statutory definition. Do not rely only on one GSTIN's sales ledger.
Is the taxpayer within an excluded category?Check the exclusions in Section 44 and Rule 80 and any applicable notification before concluding applicability.
Is GSTR-9 required for the taxpayer?Review the annual-return requirement and applicable exemptions for the specific financial year.
Has the due date been extended?Check current CBIC notifications and GST Portal advisories for the relevant financial year before setting the filing deadline.

Due date framework: Rule 80 generally sets 31 December following the end of the financial year for the annual return and required GSTR-9C. Government extensions and year-specific exemptions may apply, so confirm the current notified due date.

3. Correct sequence: reconcile first, then file

1
Close the books for the financial year.

Complete sales, credit notes, accruals, provisions, fixed assets, expense ledgers, advances, inter-branch entries and year-end adjustments. Identify entries passed after year-end that relate to the year under review.

2
Reconcile monthly GST returns to books.

Compare GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B, electronic liability ledger, cash ledger, credit ledger and the purchase/sales registers. Resolve period-level mismatches where possible.

3
Prepare GSTR-9 workings.

Build the annual-return figures from filed returns, books and the applicable instructions. Document differences relating to prior-year transactions, amendments, credit notes, ITC reversals and other adjustments.

4
Prepare the GSTR-9C reconciliation.

Bridge financial statements to annual-return figures for turnover, taxable turnover, tax paid and ITC. Investigate each material difference instead of forcing a balancing figure.

5
Assess additional liability and corrective action.

For any tax shortfall, excess credit or other discrepancy, determine the applicable legal treatment, interest, payment mechanism and disclosure. Do not assume that entering a reason in GSTR-9C by itself settles a tax liability.

6
File GSTR-9 before GSTR-9C.

Follow the GST Portal's prescribed sequence. Save the filed forms, acknowledgements, workings and approvals in the annual compliance folder.

5. Turnover reconciliation: from financial statements to GSTR-9

Do not expect the revenue line in the profit and loss account to equal the annual-return turnover automatically. The financial statements and GST returns can measure and classify transactions differently. The objective is to explain each difference with evidence and apply the relevant form instructions.

Common reasons for differences

  • GST treatment differs from accounting presentation: revenue may be shown net of discounts, while the GST return treatment depends on the nature and documentation of the discount.
  • Credit notes and debit notes: book entries and GST adjustments may occur in different periods or may not qualify for the same GST treatment.
  • Advances and unbilled revenue: accounting recognition and GST time-of-supply rules may not coincide. Analyse the specific supply and applicable provisions.
  • Export and zero-rated supplies: reconcile export invoices, shipping bills or service-export evidence, LUT/payment-of-tax treatment and return reporting.
  • Exempt, nil-rated and non-GST supplies: classify each item correctly; accounting labels alone do not determine GST classification.
  • Sale of assets, scrap or recoveries: assess the nature of the transaction and whether it constitutes a supply under the GST law.
  • Branch transfers and distinct persons: evaluate supplies between distinct persons, cross-charge and ISD treatment separately from book revenue.
  • Prior-period entries and post-year-end adjustments: identify the underlying supply date, invoice date, accounting period and GST reporting period before deciding the treatment.

6. Tax-paid reconciliation

Tax-paid reconciliation is not simply a comparison of total GST expense in the books with total cash paid. The reconciliation should distinguish tax payable, tax reported, tax discharged through the cash ledger, tax discharged through the credit ledger, and any other items required by the form.

Review these items separately

  • Tax liability declared in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, including amendments and credit/debit notes.
  • Reverse-charge liability and the corresponding payment and ITC eligibility, if any.
  • Tax discharged through cash and eligible credit, with reconciliation to the electronic ledgers.
  • Interest, late fee, penalty and other amounts, where relevant to the prescribed reconciliation.
  • Additional liability identified during annual-return preparation or the GSTR-9C review.
Do not net unlike items. For example, excess ITC in one tax head should not be silently netted against a shortfall in another tax head. Determine the correct treatment under the law and the portal's permitted payment and reporting process.

7. ITC reconciliation: annual return, books and supporting records

ITC differences need a reason-wise analysis. A purchase ledger total is not automatically equal to eligible ITC, and GSTR-2B is not a substitute for checking the statutory conditions for credit.

Use a reason code for every item, such as timing difference, supplier reporting issue, ineligible ITC, duplicate, wrong GSTIN, RCM, import, credit note, reversal/reclaim or unresolved. Keep the source invoice and the reason for the final decision linked to the working paper.

Recommended control: Require preparer and reviewer sign-off for material ITC differences. Keep the annual-return number, GSTR-2B reference, invoice number, ledger voucher, tax amount, action taken and evidence in one row wherever practicable.

9. How to handle unreconciled differences

A difference should not be treated as resolved merely because a narrative has been entered in a reconciliation statement. The underlying cause and tax consequences must be assessed.

Step A — Identify

Assign a unique difference ID and record the exact amount, GSTIN, period, return table and ledger account.

Step B — Evidence

Link the invoice, contract, credit note, ledger extract, return download or other source record.

Step C — Legal/tax assessment

Decide whether the difference affects taxable turnover, output tax, ITC eligibility, interest or another disclosure.

Step D — Closure

Record action, payment or correction reference where relevant, responsible person, reviewer approval and closure date.

Where the correct treatment is uncertain, document the uncertainty and obtain appropriate professional review before filing. Do not assume that an annual return can always be revised later or that a disclosure alone extinguishes a liability.

10. Businesses with multiple GST registrations under one PAN

For businesses registered in multiple states, do not use the consolidated PAN-level revenue figure as if it were automatically the turnover for each GSTIN. Prepare a controlled allocation/reconciliation that explains how the financial statements support the relevant state/GSTIN-level turnover, and separately evaluate supplies between distinct persons.

  • Maintain a GSTIN-wise turnover bridge linked to the trial balance and sales registers.
  • Reconcile inter-state branch transfers and supplies between distinct persons, where applicable.
  • Keep ISD credits, cross-charge entries and common-cost allocation workings separate and traceable.
  • Explain eliminations and consolidation adjustments where group or consolidated accounts are used as a starting point.
  • Confirm the applicable GSTR-9C form instructions and portal data requirements for the relevant year.
Practical control: The total of GSTIN-level working papers should be explainable against the PAN-level financial data, with documented adjustments for consolidation, inter-unit transactions and other relevant differences.

11. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using draft trial balance figures instead of the final approved books.
  • Taking turnover from the P&L without checking GST classification and the relevant form instructions.
  • Using one GSTIN's turnover to test the PAN-level aggregate-turnover threshold.
  • Copying GSTR-3B values into GSTR-9C without reconciling to GSTR-1, books and ledgers.
  • Assuming every invoice in GSTR-2B is eligible for ITC.
  • Ignoring reverse charge, credit notes, advances, export transactions or fixed-asset purchases.
  • Netting tax heads or periods without confirming the permitted treatment.
  • Entering generic explanations such as “timing difference” without a schedule or supporting documents.
  • Failing to consider interest or additional liability when a short-payment or excess-credit issue is identified.
  • Filing without retaining a final review checklist, reconciliations and portal acknowledgements.

13. Frequently asked questions

Is GSTR-9C required when turnover is exactly ₹5 crore?

The general wording is turnover that exceeds ₹5 crore. If the computed aggregate turnover is exactly ₹5 crore, the threshold condition alone is not met; verify the statutory computation, applicable year-specific notifications and exclusions before finalising applicability.

Is GSTR-9C still certified by a Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant?

Under the current general framework, GSTR-9C is a self-certified reconciliation statement. Do not apply the earlier mandatory GST audit certification process to current years without checking the applicable law for the relevant period.

Can GSTR-9C be filed before GSTR-9?

The GST Portal's prescribed workflow requires filing the annual return in Form GSTR-9 before the GSTR-9C reconciliation statement can be filed. Follow the portal instructions for the relevant financial year.

Does GSTR-9C itself create or pay additional tax?

GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement. If the review identifies additional liability, determine the correct legal disclosure, payment, interest and correction process separately. Do not assume that entering a difference alone settles it.

What if turnover in books does not match GSTR-9?

Prepare a bridge from the financial statements to the annual return, identify each difference, support it with records and assess its GST impact. Unexplained balancing entries are not a sound reconciliation method.

Should GSTR-2B equal ITC in GSTR-9C?

Not necessarily. GSTR-2B is an important source for purchase-side reconciliation, but ITC eligibility depends on statutory conditions, restrictions, reversals, timing and the records relevant to the taxpayer. Explain differences and document the conclusion.

What is the due date for GSTR-9C?

The general Rule 80 framework uses 31 December following the financial year, alongside the annual return. Always verify any extension, exemption or year-specific instruction before filing.

Can one GSTR-9C be prepared for all GSTINs under a PAN?

Do not assume that one consolidated statement replaces the GSTIN-wise filing requirement. Review the relevant GST registrations, annual-return requirements and form instructions, and prepare reconciliations that support each applicable registration.
