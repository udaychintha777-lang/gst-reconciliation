GST ACT — SECTION 68

Section 68 CGST Act — E-Way Bill, Inspection of Goods in Transit & Vehicle Verification

Section 68 is the statutory bridge between the CGST Act and the e-way bill/transport verification system. It allows the Government to require the person in charge of a conveyance carrying a consignment of goods above the specified value to carry prescribed documents and devices. This guide explains e-way bills, transport documents, vehicle interception, inspection of goods in transit, verification, detention, Rule 138A to 138E and practical compliance for businesses and transporters.

1. Why Section 68 matters

Searches such as “Section 68 CGST Act”, “Section 68 GST”, “e-way bill under Section 68”, “GST goods in transit”, “GST vehicle checking”, “e-way bill documents”, “Rule 138A”, “EWB-01”, “EWB-03”, “EWB-04” and “GST vehicle detention” relate directly to this provision.

Section 68 is important because GST does not only regulate what happens in the accounting system. It also regulates the movement of goods. When goods are transported, the Government can prescribe documents and devices that must travel with the consignment so that officers can verify the movement.

Simple idea: Section 68 gives the legal authority. Rule 138 and the connected e-way bill rules provide the detailed operational system.

2. Section 68 in very simple language

Suppose a company sends goods from Hyderabad to Bengaluru. The GST system needs a way for an officer checking the truck on the road to understand:

  • what goods are being transported;
  • who is sending them;
  • who is receiving them;
  • what document supports the movement; and
  • whether the movement matches the GST records.

Section 68 allows the Government to require the person in charge of the vehicle to carry the prescribed documents and devices. Rule 138 operationalises this through the e-way bill system. CBIC describes the e-way bill as the document required in prescribed cases for movement of goods under Section 68 read with Rule 138. citeturn0search12

Supply / other movement → E-way bill where prescribed → Documents with vehicle → Interception/verification → Inspection if required

3. What Section 68 actually says

Section 68 empowers the Government to require the person in charge of a conveyance carrying any consignment of goods of value exceeding the specified amount to carry with him prescribed documents and prescribed devices.

When the conveyance is intercepted by an officer, the person in charge must produce the required documents and devices for verification.

The detailed rules determine the documents, e-way bill requirements and verification procedure.

4. Section 68 and the e-way bill

The e-way bill is not a separate Act section replacing Section 68. It is the operational document prescribed through the Rules for specified movement of goods.

5. ₹50,000 threshold and Rule 138

Rule 138 generally requires e-way bill information before commencement of movement where the consignment value exceeds ₹50,000 in the prescribed situations.

The threshold should not be misunderstood as saying that every movement below ₹50,000 is legally identical. Rule 138 contains specific provisions, exceptions and optional generation situations.

Important: The ₹50,000 figure is primarily the Rule 138 operational threshold. Section 68 itself says “such amount as may be specified”. Therefore, the Act and Rules should be read together.

Rule 138's framework covers movement in relation to a supply, movement for reasons other than supply and specified inward supplies from unregistered persons. citeturn0search15

6. Which movements can require an e-way bill?

Movement for supply
Sale, transfer or other supply-related movement where Rule 138 applies.
Movement other than supply
Specified stock transfers, returns, job work and other non-sale movements.
Inward supply from unregistered person
Rule 138 specifically addresses prescribed movements caused by inward supply from an unregistered person.
Inter-State job work
Special Rule 138 provisions apply, including the principal's responsibility irrespective of value in the specified case.

Always check the exact Rule 138 entry and the nature of movement before deciding whether an e-way bill is required.

7. Documents to be carried

Rule 138A requires the person in charge of a conveyance to carry the prescribed transport documents.

Document/devicePurpose
InvoiceSupports taxable supply and identifies goods/value.
Bill of supplyUsed where the GST rules require a bill of supply.
Delivery challanUsed for prescribed movements other than supply or where permitted.
E-way bill / EBNProvides the electronic movement record where required.
Other prescribed document/deviceMay apply depending on the prescribed transport mechanism.

CBIC's e-way bill rules state that the person in charge should carry the invoice/bill of supply/delivery challan as applicable and a copy of the e-way bill or e-way bill number in the prescribed manner. citeturn0search0

8. Invoice, bill of supply or delivery challan

The transport document must match the actual movement.

Example — Sale of goods

A supplier sends taxable goods to a customer. The tax invoice and e-way bill should identify the relevant transaction and conveyance details.

Example — Job work

Goods sent to a job worker may move on a delivery challan in the situations permitted under GST law, together with the prescribed e-way bill where applicable.

Example — Goods moved without supply

Movement for testing, repair, exhibition or another permitted reason may require a delivery challan rather than a tax invoice, depending on the facts and applicable rules.

The document carried by the vehicle should tell the same commercial story as the actual movement. A correct e-way bill cannot cure a fundamentally incorrect invoice or ownership record.

9. Interception of conveyance

Rule 138B permits the Commissioner or an authorised officer to authorise the proper officer to intercept conveyances to verify the e-way bill or e-way bill number for inter-State and intra-State movement of goods.

Physical verification of conveyances is carried out by the authorised proper officer. Where specific information regarding tax evasion exists, physical verification of a specific conveyance may also be undertaken after the required approval.

The objective is to compare the physical movement with the documents and electronic data.

10. Inspection and verification

Rule 138C provides a reporting framework for inspection of goods in transit.

  • A summary report of inspection is recorded online in Part A of FORM GST EWB-03 within 24 hours of inspection.
  • The final report is recorded in Part B of FORM GST EWB-03 within three days of inspection.

This creates an electronic trail of transit verification.

11. No repeated physical verification

Where physical verification of goods being transported has already been carried out at one place during transit, the same conveyance should generally not be subjected to another physical verification in the same State/UT or another State/UT.

The important exception is where specific information relating to evasion of tax becomes available subsequently.

This rule is intended to prevent repeated routine physical verification of the same movement while still allowing fresh action where specific tax-evasion information emerges.

12. Vehicle detention and EWB-04

Rule 138D provides a facility for a transporter to upload information where a vehicle has been intercepted and detained for more than thirty minutes.

The prescribed form is FORM GST EWB-04.

This is particularly useful for transporters because it creates an electronic record of prolonged detention and the related movement-verification event.

13. E-way bill mismatch and consequences

A missing or incorrect e-way bill should not automatically be treated as a final tax demand. The legal consequence depends on the nature of the breach and the applicable provision.

Possible issues include:

  • no e-way bill where required;
  • expired e-way bill;
  • wrong vehicle number;
  • wrong invoice number/date;
  • incorrect consignor/consignee;
  • wrong place of delivery;
  • quantity/value mismatch;
  • goods different from the description;
  • movement without supporting invoice/delivery challan; and
  • e-way bill generated for a different transaction.

Detention and release of goods in transit in specified circumstances is separately dealt with under Section 129, while confiscation is dealt with under Section 130. These should not be confused with Section 68 itself.

14. Responsibilities of supplier, recipient and transporter

15. Construction and infrastructure examples

Material from central warehouse to project site

Maintain invoice/delivery challan, e-way bill, vehicle number and project/site details. The site receiving team should reconcile the physical quantity with the document.

Inter-state movement to a project

Confirm place of supply, GSTIN, delivery address, invoice and e-way bill before the truck leaves.

Job-work movement

Maintain job-work challans and e-way bills, particularly for inter-State movements.

Machinery movement

For machinery sent between project sites or for repair, maintain ownership, asset-register, delivery-challan and movement records.

Site-to-site transfers

Do not treat an internal transfer as “no GST relevance” without checking whether it is a supply between distinct persons and what documentation is required.

Return of unused material

Maintain the return document and e-way bill where prescribed so the reverse movement can be reconciled to the original dispatch.

16. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — ₹2 lakh sale

A ₹2 lakh taxable consignment moves by road. Rule 138 applies in the prescribed case, so the required e-way bill information should be generated before movement.

Example 2 — ₹40,000 movement

The basic ₹50,000 threshold does not automatically mean every ₹40,000 movement is free from all documentation. The nature of movement and specific Rule 138 provisions must be checked.

Example 3 — Wrong vehicle number

The e-way bill contains vehicle ABC-1234 but the goods are transported in ABC-5678. The business should correct/update the conveyance details as permitted before or during movement as applicable.

Example 4 — Delivery challan movement

Goods are sent for repair without a sale. A delivery challan may be the appropriate document where the GST Rules permit it, along with an e-way bill where applicable.

Example 5 — Job worker

A principal in one State sends goods to a job worker in another State. Rule 138 contains a specific e-way bill requirement for the principal irrespective of value in the prescribed case.

Example 6 — Vehicle intercepted

The driver produces the invoice and e-way bill number. The officer verifies the data and the physical goods.

Example 7 — Physical verification already done

If the same conveyance was already physically verified at another place during transit, another routine physical verification should generally not occur unless specific tax-evasion information subsequently becomes available.

Example 8 — Vehicle detained for 45 minutes

The transporter may upload detention information using the prescribed EWB-04 facility.

Example 9 — Quantity mismatch

The invoice says 1,000 units but the vehicle carries 1,200 units. The business should be able to explain the difference with valid supporting documents; otherwise further proceedings may arise.

Example 10 — Multiple GSTINs

A company has GST registrations in several States. The e-way bill should correctly identify the relevant consignor/consignee GSTIN and actual movement location.

17. Important exceptions and special situations

  • Rule 138 contains specified exemptions and special movement provisions.
  • Consignment value is determined under the prescribed Rule 138 method and includes specified taxes/cess in the manner stated in the Rule.
  • Specified movements by rail, air or vessel have particular Part-B/document requirements.
  • Short-distance movement to a transporter within the State/UT has a specific conveyance-detail relaxation under Rule 138.
  • Inter-State movement of specified job-work goods has a special e-way bill requirement.
  • Handicraft goods moved by specified unregistered persons have a special Rule 138 treatment.
  • Government may prescribe alternative documents instead of an e-way bill in specified circumstances.

These are rule-level details, so they should be checked against the current Rule 138 text and applicable notifications at the time of movement.

20. Section 68 vs Sections 67, 129 and 130

ProvisionMain subject
Section 67Inspection, search and seizure.
Section 68Inspection of goods in movement and documents/devices carried with the conveyance.
Section 129Detention, seizure and release of goods/conveyances in transit in specified cases.
Section 130Confiscation of goods or conveyances and related penalty in specified circumstances.
A vehicle being checked on the highway under the e-way bill framework is not automatically a Section 67 “search”. The legal provision actually invoked should be identified from the facts and documents.

23. Common mistakes

  • Generating an e-way bill after the vehicle has already started moving where it was required before movement.
  • Wrong vehicle number.
  • Wrong GSTIN or delivery address.
  • Invoice quantity not matching actual goods.
  • Expired e-way bill.
  • Using an invoice when a delivery challan is required for a non-supply movement.
  • Failing to update vehicle details after transshipment.
  • Not tracking job-work movements.
  • Assuming a low-value movement never needs an e-way bill without checking the specific Rule 138 provisions.
  • Not recording prolonged vehicle detention.

24. Section 68 GST FAQs

What is Section 68 of the CGST Act?

Section 68 allows the Government to require the person in charge of a conveyance carrying a consignment of goods above the specified value to carry prescribed documents and devices and allows verification when the conveyance is intercepted.

Is the e-way bill under Section 68?

Section 68 provides the statutory authority, while Rule 138 prescribes the e-way bill mechanism for specified movements.

What is the ₹50,000 e-way bill limit?

Rule 138 generally requires e-way bill information for consignments exceeding ₹50,000 in prescribed cases. Specific exceptions and special situations must also be checked.

Who should carry the e-way bill?

The person in charge of the conveyance must carry the prescribed documents/e-way bill information in the manner specified by Rule 138A.

Can GST officers stop a truck?

Yes. Rule 138B provides for interception of conveyances for verification of e-way bill/e-way bill number.

What is EWB-03?

EWB-03 is used for reporting inspection/verification of goods in transit, with the prescribed summary and final reporting stages.

What is EWB-04?

EWB-04 provides a facility for the transporter to upload information where the vehicle has been intercepted and detained for more than thirty minutes.

Can the same truck be checked again?

Generally, no further physical verification should be carried out after physical verification at one place during transit, unless specific information relating to tax evasion becomes available subsequently.

Does Section 68 apply only to inter-State movement?

No. Rule 138B covers verification for both inter-State and intra-State movement of goods.

Can an e-way bill be required for movement other than sale?

Yes. Rule 138 specifically covers prescribed movement for reasons other than supply, subject to its conditions.

Is a delivery challan valid instead of an invoice?

For prescribed non-supply movements and other permitted situations, a delivery challan can be the appropriate document. The exact reason for movement must be checked.

What happens if goods are detained?

The applicable detention/release provisions depend on the reason for detention. Section 129 is important for specified detention, seizure and release situations involving goods/conveyances in transit.

Is an e-way bill alone enough?

No. The e-way bill should correspond with the underlying invoice, bill of supply or delivery challan and the actual goods and movement.

26. Key takeaways

  • Section 68 provides the statutory framework for inspection of goods in movement.
  • The e-way bill is the key operational mechanism prescribed through Rule 138.
  • The basic Rule 138 threshold is ₹50,000 in prescribed cases.
  • Goods can move for reasons other than supply and still fall within prescribed e-way bill requirements.
  • The person in charge must carry the prescribed invoice/bill of supply/delivery challan and e-way bill information.
  • Rule 138B permits interception and verification of conveyances for both inter-State and intra-State movement.
  • Rule 138C creates an electronic inspection-reporting trail through EWB-03.
  • EWB-04 provides a facility to report vehicle detention exceeding thirty minutes.
  • Repeated physical verification is restricted unless specific information relating to tax evasion becomes available subsequently.
  • Section 68 should not be confused with Section 67 search/seizure or Section 129 detention/release proceedings.
  • For businesses, accurate invoice, e-way bill, vehicle, stock and receipt records are the best defence against avoidable transit disputes.
