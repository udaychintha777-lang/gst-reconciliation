GST ACT — SECTION 83

Section 83 CGST Act — Provisional Attachment of Property to Protect GST Revenue

Section 83 gives the Commissioner a powerful but temporary revenue-protection mechanism: after specified proceedings have been initiated, property including a bank account can be provisionally attached where the Commissioner forms the statutory opinion that attachment is necessary to protect Government revenue. This detailed guide explains the provision in simple language, Rule 159, DRC-22, DRC-23, objections, the one-year limit, departmental guidelines and practical safeguards.

1. Why Section 83 matters

Searches such as “Section 83 CGST Act”, “Section 83 GST”, “GST provisional attachment”, “GST bank account attachment”, “GST property attachment”, “DRC-22”, “DRC-23”, “Rule 159 GST”, “GST attachment one year”, “GST department freeze bank account” and “provisional attachment of property under GST” relate directly to this provision.

Section 83 is one of the strongest preventive tools available to the GST administration. It is designed to protect Government revenue while specified proceedings are underway, before the normal recovery stage necessarily begins.

Because an attachment can affect bank accounts, property and business operations, the provision must be understood as a temporary revenue-protection power, not as an automatic declaration that the taxpayer has finally lost the property or that the tax demand has already become final.

Simple idea: If specified GST proceedings have started and the Commissioner believes attachment is necessary to protect revenue, property including a bank account can be provisionally attached, subject to the statutory and procedural safeguards.

2. Section 83 in very simple language

Imagine a taxpayer is under a serious GST investigation or assessment proceeding. The department believes that valuable assets could be moved, withdrawn or otherwise put beyond recovery if no protective action is taken.

Section 83 allows the Commissioner, after the specified proceedings have been initiated and after forming the required opinion, to temporarily attach property to protect Government revenue.

Specified proceeding starts → Commissioner forms statutory opinion → DRC-22 attachment → temporary revenue protection → release/expiry according to law

This is why Section 83 is different from final recovery. The purpose is to preserve assets while the underlying proceedings continue.

3. What Section 83 says

Section 83(1) provides that where, after initiation of any proceeding under Chapter XII, Chapter XIV or Chapter XV, the Commissioner is of the opinion that, for protecting the interest of Government revenue, it is necessary to do so, the Commissioner may, by order in writing, provisionally attach any property, including a bank account, belonging to the taxable person or any person specified in Section 122(1A), in the prescribed manner.

Section 83(2) provides that every such provisional attachment shall cease to have effect after expiry of one year from the date of the order made under Section 83(1).

4. When can Section 83 be used?

The trigger is not simply “the taxpayer has not paid GST”. Section 83 is linked to the initiation of proceedings under specified Chapters.

The statutory language requires the proceeding to have been initiated before the provisional attachment power is exercised.

This is an important safeguard. The department must identify the proceeding that gives rise to the Section 83 power.

The precise applicability must always be tested against the current statutory text and the proceeding actually initiated.

6. Commissioner’s statutory opinion

Section 83 does not use an automatic formula such as “every investigation above ₹X must result in attachment”. The Commissioner must form the opinion that attachment is necessary for protecting Government revenue.

This makes the factual record important. A proper Section 83 decision should be connected to the proceeding and the revenue risk identified by the authority.

For a taxpayer challenging attachment, the relevant questions can include:

  • What proceeding was initiated?
  • When was it initiated?
  • What revenue risk was identified?
  • What property was selected?
  • Why was attachment considered necessary?
  • Is the attachment proportionate to the risk?

7. “Necessary to protect revenue”

This phrase is central to Section 83.

The purpose of attachment is not to punish the taxpayer. It is to protect the Government's revenue interest while the relevant proceedings are underway.

Revenue risk
Could the taxpayer's assets become unavailable for recovery?
Asset identification
What property is proposed to be attached?
Proceeding
What statutory proceeding is underway?
Proportionality
Is the protective measure connected to the identified revenue risk?

8. What property can be attached?

The statutory wording is broad: “any property, including bank account”.

Depending on the facts, the attachment mechanism can therefore affect different forms of property.

Movable propertyNature, possession and prescribed procedure matter.
Other propertyExact legal character and ownership must be established.

9. Bank account attachment

Bank-account attachment is particularly disruptive because the account may contain money needed for salaries, vendor payments, statutory dues and project expenses.

Rule 159 provides for communication of the DRC-22 attachment order to the concerned bank/post office/financial institution or other authority, as applicable. The order can restrict operation of the attached account.

Practical point: A taxpayer facing bank-account attachment should immediately identify the exact account covered, amount/risk involved, statutory proceeding, date of DRC-22 and the available objection/release route.

10. Persons under Section 122(1A)

Section 83 also refers to a person specified in Section 122(1A), rather than limiting the power in all cases to the registered taxable person.

This is important in cases involving persons who are legally brought within the specified penalty framework. The exact identity and liability of the person should be verified before concluding that Section 83 applies.

11. Why it is called provisional

“Provisional” means the attachment is a protective measure connected with pending proceedings. It is not the same as final confiscation or final sale of property.

The underlying tax liability still has to be dealt with under the applicable assessment/demand proceedings.

Provisional attachmentFinal recovery
Protects revenue while specified proceedings are underway.Enforces an amount legally recoverable under the recovery framework.
Temporary statutory mechanism.Recovery can involve separate statutory methods.
Section 83 + Rule 159.Section 79 and applicable recovery Rules.

12. One-year statutory limit

Section 83(2) is clear that every provisional attachment ceases to have effect after expiry of one year from the date of the order under Section 83(1).

This is an important protection because a provisional attachment is not intended to remain indefinitely without a time limit.

The GST Council specifically considered confusion between Section 83(2) and Rule 159(2), where field authorities sometimes continued to wait for separate written release instructions after one year. The Council recommended aligning Rule 159 and DRC-22 so that the attachment order ceases to be valid after one year.

Track the exact DRC-22 date. The one-year period is calculated from the date of the Section 83 attachment order, not simply from the date of the original investigation or notice.

13. Rule 159 procedure

Rule 159 provides the procedural framework for provisional attachment.

Key elements include:

  • Commissioner issues FORM GST DRC-22 where attachment is ordered.
  • The order identifies the property/account attached.
  • The relevant authority such as revenue, transport or financial institution is informed for placing the encumbrance/restriction.
  • The person whose property is attached receives the order.
  • An objection can be filed within the prescribed seven-day period.
  • The Commissioner considers the objection after giving an opportunity of hearing.
  • Release can be made through FORM GST DRC-23 where the property is no longer liable for attachment.

14. DRC-22

FORM GST DRC-22 is the attachment order under Rule 159.

It is the document that should be examined first when a taxpayer receives information that an account/property has been attached.

Review these details immediately

✓ Reference number
✓ Date of order
✓ Taxpayer/person name
✓ GSTIN/PAN/identification
✓ Statutory proceeding cited
✓ Property/account details
✓ Authority receiving the order
✓ Amount/revenue context
✓ One-year expiry date
✓ Objection deadline

15. DRC-23 and release

Rule 159 provides for release through FORM GST DRC-23 in the circumstances prescribed by the rule.

Rule 159(6) allows the Commissioner, on being satisfied that the property was or is no longer liable for attachment, to release the property by issuing DRC-23.

Release can become relevant where:

  • the attachment was not legally sustainable;
  • the property is no longer liable for attachment;
  • the taxpayer establishes the relevant facts;
  • the revenue risk has changed; or
  • the statutory attachment period has expired, subject to the current Rule 159/DRC-22 framework.

16. Seven-day objection

Rule 159 provides that a person whose property is attached may, within seven days of the attachment, file an objection stating that the property was or is not liable to attachment.

The Commissioner is required to provide an opportunity of hearing before deciding the objection.

Practical response: Do not treat the seven-day period as an informal suggestion. Prepare the objection immediately and support it with bank statements, ownership documents, business cash-flow evidence, the proceeding history and the legal grounds for release.

17. Perishable or hazardous property

Rule 159 contains a special mechanism for attached property that is perishable or hazardous.

Where the taxable person pays an amount equivalent to the market price of the property or the amount that is or may become payable, whichever is lower, the property can be released on proof of payment in the prescribed manner.

If the amount is not paid, the Commissioner can dispose of the perishable/hazardous property and adjust the realised amount against the amounts payable.

18. 2021 CBIC guidelines

CBIC issued detailed guidelines on 23 February 2021 for provisional attachment under Section 83. The guidelines were issued because field formations had raised questions and courts had made observations concerning the exercise of this power.

The guidelines specifically address the grounds for attachment, the type and value of property, the procedure for attachment, bank accounts, objections and release.

For professional work, the 2021 guidelines are important administrative guidance, while the Act and Rules remain the primary statutory sources.

19. Key safeguards in the guidelines

The guidelines emphasise that provisional attachment should be exercised carefully because it can affect the working capital and business operations of a taxpayer.

Important practical principles include:

Revenue protection
Attachment should have a genuine connection with protecting revenue.
Property selection
Officers should consider the nature/value of property and the amount involved.
Bank accounts
Attachment should not be treated as an unlimited power to block every account without considering the circumstances.
Release
Objections and release requests should be processed according to the prescribed framework.

The 2021 CBIC guidelines remain a useful administrative reference when analysing the exercise of Section 83 power.

22. Section 83 vs Section 79

Section 79 is the main GST recovery machinery. Section 83 is a preventive/protective measure that can operate while specified proceedings are pending.

Proceeding → Section 83 provisional protection → determination/recovery → Section 79 where legally applicable

Therefore, provisional attachment should not automatically be described as final recovery.

23. Section 67 connection

Section 67 deals with inspection, search and seizure. Section 83 can become relevant after initiation of proceedings under Chapter XIV, which includes the inspection/search framework.

However, search and attachment are different powers:

  • Search is an investigative power.
  • Seizure concerns specified goods/documents/things.
  • Provisional attachment protects revenue by restricting dealing with property.

The 40th GST Council discussed the need to amend Section 83 because courts had interpreted the earlier “during the pendency of proceedings” wording in ways that could limit attachment after a search operation. The statutory framework was subsequently recast around initiation of proceedings under specified Chapters.

24. Sections 73/74/74A connection

Section 83 can operate in the context of demand proceedings under Chapter XV. The underlying demand provision must be identified according to the relevant tax period and current law.

For older periods, Sections 73 and 74 are important. For periods to which the newer Section 74A framework applies, the current statutory framework should be checked.

Attachment under Section 83 does not itself decide the final tax liability. The demand/adjudication proceeding remains separate.

25. Business cash-flow impact

Bank-account attachment can immediately affect a business even when the underlying GST demand is still being contested or investigated.

Potential consequences include:

✓ Salary payment disruption
✓ Vendor payment delays
✓ Project cash-flow interruption
✓ Loan repayment issues
✓ Statutory payment difficulty
✓ Working-capital stress
✓ Cheque/payment failures
✓ Project mobilisation problems

This is why Section 83 response should be coordinated among tax, finance, treasury and legal teams immediately.

26. Construction and infrastructure examples

Project bank account

A contractor's operating bank account is attached while a GST investigation is pending. The company should immediately document salary, subcontractor, statutory and project obligations and seek appropriate relief through the prescribed legal route.

Fixed deposit

A company maintains FDs for bank guarantees/margin requirements. If an FD is attached, finance should establish the nature of the deposit and its connection with the business's banking arrangements.

Project land

An infrastructure company owns land and faces a major GST proceeding. The property can become subject to provisional attachment if the statutory conditions are met.

Machinery

A contractor's equipment is attached. The company should establish ownership, business use, valuation and the impact on ongoing projects.

Multiple bank accounts

A company operates separate accounts for payroll, collections, project expenses and statutory payments. The team should identify exactly which account is covered by DRC-22 rather than assuming every account is automatically frozen.

27. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — Investigation and bank account

A taxpayer is under a Chapter XIV proceeding. The Commissioner forms the required opinion that revenue needs protection and issues DRC-22 attaching a bank account. The taxpayer should review the proceeding, attachment order, account details and objection route immediately.

Example 2 — Attachment of property worth much more than exposure

A company has a disputed potential liability of ₹2 crore but an immovable property worth ₹20 crore is attached. The taxpayer can examine the statutory necessity and proportionality issues and seek appropriate relief.

Example 3 — Seven-day objection

A DRC-22 is received on Monday. The taxpayer should not wait until the end of the week to assemble the objection. The seven-day Rule 159 mechanism should be treated as an urgent deadline.

Example 4 — Attachment no longer necessary

New facts demonstrate that the property is no longer liable for attachment. The taxpayer can seek release under Rule 159 and DRC-23.

Example 5 — One-year expiry

A DRC-22 is issued on 15 September 2026. The statutory one-year period runs from the date of that attachment order. The finance/legal team should calendar the expiry and preserve evidence of the date.

Example 6 — Bank account needed for salaries

A project company depends on the attached account for payroll. The company should document salary obligations and the effect on business continuity when making its legal representation.

Example 7 — Fixed deposit

A fixed deposit is attached. Finance should establish whether it is pledged/lien-marked for another statutory/business purpose and provide supporting documents.

Example 8 — Property sold before attachment

A taxpayer sold property before DRC-22 was issued. Section 83 analysis then requires examination of ownership and the transaction facts; Section 81 may separately become relevant if the transfer was designed to defeat Government revenue.

Example 9 — Search proceeding

A search under Section 67 is initiated and the department subsequently considers attachment. The current Section 83 framework must be applied based on initiation of the specified proceeding and the Commissioner's statutory opinion.

Example 10 — Demand proceeding

A demand proceeding is initiated and significant revenue risk is identified. The Commissioner may consider provisional attachment subject to Section 83 and Rule 159 rather than waiting for final recovery.

Example 11 — Perishable goods

Perishable property is attached. Rule 159 provides a specific payment-and-release mechanism based on market value or the amount payable, whichever is lower, subject to the rule.

Example 12 — Attachment during appeal strategy

A taxpayer disputes the underlying liability. The attachment and the merits of the demand are separate issues; the taxpayer should address both the underlying proceeding and the legality/necessity of the provisional attachment.

30. Bank response and controls

When a bank receives a valid attachment communication, the bank's operations team should identify the exact account/property and follow the applicable instruction.

For the taxpayer, the bank relationship team should be asked for:

  • copy/reference of the attachment instruction;
  • date and time received;
  • account/FD/property identifier;
  • amount or restriction details;
  • current available balance; and
  • process for implementing a release order.

The taxpayer should not assume that an oral bank statement is sufficient evidence; obtain documentary confirmation.

33. Common mistakes

  • Assuming every GST notice permits provisional attachment.
  • Ignoring the requirement that a specified proceeding must have been initiated.
  • Ignoring the Commissioner's statutory opinion.
  • Not checking the exact DRC-22 date.
  • Missing the seven-day objection period.
  • Confusing provisional attachment with final recovery.
  • Assuming the attachment automatically proves the tax demand is final.
  • Failing to document business disruption from bank attachment.
  • Not tracking the one-year statutory expiry.
  • Confusing Section 83 with Section 82 first charge.
  • Confusing Section 83 with Section 81 fraudulent transfer.
  • Failing to address the underlying tax proceeding separately.

35. Judicial and administrative developments

One-year expiry and restoration

The GST Council's 52nd/53rd meeting material recorded a practical problem: Section 83(2) said the attachment ceases after one year, while Rule 159(2) referred to removal on written instructions. This created difficulties for banks and other authorities even after the statutory period expired. The Council recommended alignment so that DRC-22 itself ceases to be valid after one year.

Balaji Enterprises issue

The Council material records that the Delhi High Court had addressed the practical problem of defreezing bank accounts where the statutory attachment period had expired but authorities had not issued separate release instructions.

CBIC guidelines

The 23 February 2021 CBIC guidelines were issued specifically to bring greater uniformity to the exercise of Section 83 power and address concerns raised by field formations and courts.

36. Section 83 GST FAQs

What is Section 83 of the CGST Act?

Section 83 allows provisional attachment of property, including a bank account, to protect Government revenue after specified proceedings have been initiated and the Commissioner forms the required opinion.

Can GST department freeze a bank account?

Yes, Section 83 expressly includes bank accounts within property that can be provisionally attached, subject to the statutory conditions.

What is DRC-22?

DRC-22 is the Rule 159 form used for the provisional attachment order under Section 83.

What is DRC-23?

DRC-23 is used for release of provisionally attached property in the circumstances prescribed by Rule 159.

How long can GST provisional attachment continue?

Section 83(2) provides that provisional attachment ceases after one year from the date of the Section 83(1) order.

Can GST attachment continue beyond one year?

The statutory Section 83 attachment ceases after one year. The GST Council specifically addressed the Rule 159/DRC-22 implementation issue so that the attachment order ceases to be valid after one year.

Can I object to GST provisional attachment?

Yes. Rule 159 provides a seven-day objection mechanism for a person whose property is attached, followed by an opportunity of hearing.

What is the time limit for objection under Rule 159?

The rule provides seven days from attachment for the objection.

Can property other than a bank account be attached?

Yes. Section 83 uses “any property, including bank account”.

Can GST attach a fixed deposit?

A fixed deposit can fall within the broad property framework, subject to the statutory conditions and the actual nature of the deposit.

Does provisional attachment mean GST demand is final?

No. Provisional attachment is a protective measure. The underlying tax liability must be determined through the applicable statutory proceeding.

Is Section 83 the same as Section 79?

No. Section 79 is the principal recovery machinery; Section 83 is provisional revenue protection during specified proceedings.

Is Section 83 the same as Section 82?

No. Section 82 creates a first-charge framework, while Section 83 provides provisional attachment.

Is Section 83 the same as Section 81?

No. Section 81 concerns specified fraudulent property transfers; Section 83 concerns temporary attachment to protect revenue.

Can a taxpayer seek release of attached property?

Yes. Rule 159 contains objection and release mechanisms, including DRC-23 where the property is no longer liable for attachment.

What happens to perishable goods?

Rule 159 contains a special payment/release and disposal mechanism for perishable or hazardous property.

Does the Commissioner have unlimited attachment power?

No. Section 83 contains statutory conditions, including specified proceedings, the Commissioner's opinion that attachment is necessary to protect revenue and a one-year outer period.

What should a company do immediately after bank attachment?

Obtain DRC-22, identify the exact account, record the date, review the underlying proceeding, quantify operational impact and prepare any Rule 159 objection/release application urgently.

38. Key takeaways

  • Section 83 is a provisional revenue-protection provision, not final recovery.
  • A specified proceeding under Chapter XII, XIV or XV must have been initiated.
  • The Commissioner must form the statutory opinion that attachment is necessary to protect Government revenue.
  • Any property, including a bank account, can fall within the provision.
  • Rule 159 provides the procedural framework, including DRC-22, objections and DRC-23 release.
  • The objection period under Rule 159 is seven days.
  • Section 83(2) provides a one-year outer period from the attachment order.
  • The GST Council addressed the practical mismatch between Section 83's one-year expiry and Rule 159's earlier written-release language.
  • Bank-account attachment can seriously disrupt working capital, so finance and legal teams should respond immediately.
  • Section 83 should be clearly distinguished from Sections 79, 81 and 82.
  • The underlying tax proceeding and the provisional attachment are separate legal issues and may both need to be addressed.
