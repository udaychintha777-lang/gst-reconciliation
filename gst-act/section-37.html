1. Why Section 37 matters

Searches such as “Section 37 CGST Act”, “GSTR-1”, “GST outward supply return”, “30 November GST invoice amendment”, “GSTR-1A”, “GSTR-1 due date” and “how to report sales in GST” all point to the reporting framework created by Section 37.

Section 37 is the statutory foundation for furnishing details of outward supplies. It is not simply a procedural reference to GSTR-1. The section creates the legal obligation, while the CGST Rules prescribe the form, manner, timing and detailed reporting mechanism.

Simple way to understand it: Section 37 answers “What outward-supply information must be furnished to GST authorities and communicated through the GST system?” Rule 59 and the GST portal provide the operational mechanism.

Because outward-supply information feeds the recipient's GST records and interacts with GSTR-3B, ITC availability, e-invoicing, e-commerce reporting and annual reconciliation, Section 37 is one of the most important compliance provisions for a regular taxpayer.

3. Complete statutory structure

Section 37(1) requires every registered person, other than specified excluded categories such as an Input Service Distributor, non-resident taxable person and persons paying tax under Sections 10, 51 or 52, to furnish electronically the details of outward supplies of goods or services or both effected during a tax period, in the prescribed form and manner and subject to prescribed conditions and restrictions.

The explanation to Section 37(1) states that “details of outward supplies” includes details of invoices, debit notes, credit notes and revised invoices issued in relation to outward supplies during the relevant tax period.

Section 37(3) requires errors or omissions to be rectified in the prescribed manner, with tax and interest paid where the error caused a short payment, but prevents rectification after the statutory cut-off of 30 November following the end of the relevant financial year or furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier. citeturn1search0turn1search1

4. Who has to furnish outward-supply details?

The normal Section 37 obligation applies to registered persons, subject to the exclusions written into the section and the special return frameworks applicable to particular categories.

CategorySection 37 position
Regular registered taxpayerGenerally required to furnish outward-supply details.
Input Service DistributorExcluded from Section 37 and follows the ISD return framework.
Non-resident taxable personExcluded from Section 37 and follows its special return framework.
Composition taxpayerExcluded from Section 37; composition taxpayers follow their prescribed statement/return framework.
Specified TDS/TCS personsExcluded from Section 37 and follow the relevant statutory return mechanism.

The exclusions matter because the GSTR-1 obligation should not be applied mechanically to every GST registration.

5. What are “details of outward supplies”?

Outward supply is broader than simply “sales invoices”. Depending on the transaction, the reporting population can include:

  • tax invoices;
  • debit notes;
  • credit notes;
  • revised invoices;
  • exports;
  • supplies to registered persons;
  • supplies to unregistered persons;
  • zero-rated supplies;
  • supplies liable to reverse charge where reporting is required;
  • e-commerce-related supplies; and
  • amendments to previously furnished details.

The exact table, level of detail and reporting threshold depend on the current GSTR-1 format and Rule 59 instructions applicable to the relevant period.

6. Section 37 and GSTR-1

GSTR-1 is the principal statement used to furnish Section 37 outward-supply details.

Rule 59 provides that a registered person required to furnish Section 37 details generally furnishes them electronically in FORM GSTR-1 for the month or quarter, either directly through the common portal or through a notified facilitation centre. citeturn0search1

Therefore:

7. GSTR-1 due dates

The statutory Section 37 text historically referred to the 10th day of the succeeding month, while the operative Rule 59/GST portal framework prescribes the current filing dates.

Filing frequencyCurrent ordinary GSTR-1 due date
Monthly filer11th day of the succeeding month.
Quarterly filer under QRMP13th day of the month following the end of the quarter.
Government extensionThe date may be extended by notification for specified taxpayers/periods.

The official GST Portal GSTR-1 guidance states the 11th/13th dates and notes that the Government may extend them by notification. citeturn1search3

Important: do not confuse the wording of Section 37(1) with the presently notified Rule 59 filing dates. The Act creates the obligation; the Rules prescribe the operational date.

8. Quarterly taxpayers and IFF

Quarterly filers under the QRMP framework furnish their outward-supply statement quarterly, while the Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF) can be used for specified B2B invoice details in the first two months of a quarter.

Rule 59 provides the IFF mechanism and prevents taxpayers from unnecessarily re-reporting details already furnished through IFF in the quarterly GSTR-1, subject to the prescribed rules.

The 53rd GST Council material records the Rule 59 structure for GSTR-1 and IFF and the reporting of invoice-wise B2B details and credit/debit notes. citeturn0search1

9. GSTR-1A amendment facility

GSTR-1A is an important modern development in the Section 37 reporting process.

The GST Portal describes GSTR-1A as an optional amendment facility for the same tax period. It becomes available after GSTR-1 is filed or its due date has arrived, whichever is later, and can be used before filing GSTR-3B for the same tax period. citeturn1search3

What can GSTR-1A solve?

  • an invoice omitted from the same-period GSTR-1;
  • incorrect invoice details already reported;
  • same-period amendments before GSTR-3B; and
  • corrections that can flow into the recipient's subsequent GSTR-2B as prescribed.
Practical distinction: GSTR-1A is a same-period correction facility. It does not remove the Section 37(3) statutory year-end cut-off for rectification of earlier financial-year details.

10. Communication to recipients

Section 37(1) contains the statutory concept that furnished outward-supply details are communicated to the recipient subject to the prescribed conditions, restrictions, time and manner.

This is central to the modern GST data architecture because the supplier's outward-supply reporting becomes part of the recipient's inward-supply information and can affect ITC reconciliation.

In practical terms, an incorrect supplier invoice can therefore create consequences on both sides:

Supplier
Output tax, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and amendment consequences.
Recipient
Invoice visibility, ITC reconciliation and GSTR-2B implications.

11. Credit notes, debit notes and revised invoices

The explanation to Section 37 expressly includes invoices, debit notes, credit notes and revised invoices within “details of outward supplies”.

DocumentSection 37 relevance
Tax invoicePrimary outward-supply reporting document.
Debit noteAdditional taxable value/tax is reported as prescribed.
Credit noteReduction in value/tax is reported subject to Section 34 and the applicable reporting conditions.
Revised invoiceCorrection/replacement reporting where permitted under the Act/Rules.

Section 37 must therefore be read together with Section 34 rather than treating credit/debit-note reporting as an isolated GSTR-1 data-entry exercise.

12. Section 37(3) — correcting errors and omissions

Suppose a supplier discovers that an invoice was omitted from GSTR-1 or that the taxable value, GSTIN, invoice number, tax rate or tax amount was incorrectly reported.

Section 37(3) requires the error or omission to be rectified in the prescribed manner. Where the error resulted in short payment of tax, the taxpayer must pay the resulting tax and interest.

The important limitation is the statutory cut-off discussed below.

13. The 30 November cut-off

For current law, Section 37(3) provides that rectification of an error or omission is not allowed after:

  • 30 November following the end of the relevant financial year; or
  • the date of furnishing the relevant annual return,

whichever is earlier. citeturn1search0turn1search1

Financial yearIllustrative statutory cut-off
FY 2024-2530 November 2025, unless the relevant annual return was furnished earlier.
FY 2025-2630 November 2026, unless the relevant annual return was furnished earlier.
Do not confuse this with the GSTR-1 monthly filing due date. The 30 November date is the outer statutory rectification cut-off for the relevant financial year under Section 37(3).

14. Historical September cut-off

For the earlier GST framework, the statutory rectification cut-off was linked to the return for the month of September following the end of the financial year, or the relevant annual return, whichever was earlier.

Finance Act, 2022 changed the wording to the present 30 November formulation, brought into force from 1 October 2022 through Notification No. 18/2022-Central Tax dated 28 September 2022.

The change is significant when analysing older GST periods because the amendment date and the applicable law for the relevant reporting period must be considered. The Comptroller and Auditor General's GST audit report also noted the change from the September criterion to 30 November. citeturn1search7

15. Same-period correction vs annual cut-off

SituationPractical mechanism
Error discovered before/around GSTR-1 filingCorrect the draft before filing.
Error discovered after GSTR-1 but before GSTR-3B for same periodGSTR-1A may provide an optional same-period amendment route where available.
Error relating to a previous financial yearUse the prescribed amendment/reporting mechanism only within the applicable Section 37(3) time limit.
30 November/annual-return cut-off crossedSection 37(3) no longer permits rectification of the relevant outward-supply details through the statutory route.

Even where the Section 37(3) cut-off has passed, the correct accounting, tax-payment, disclosure and legal treatment should still be evaluated based on the facts. Passing the GSTR-1 amendment deadline does not make an underlying transaction disappear.

16. Section 37(4) — filing restrictions

Section 37(4) provides a statutory restriction against furnishing outward-supply details for a subsequent tax period where the prescribed preceding-period return/details have not been furnished, subject to the exceptions and conditions provided by the Government.

The GST portal therefore uses system controls to prevent certain later-period GSTR-1 filings where earlier compliance remains pending.

The GST Portal specifically explains that a taxpayer cannot file the current GSTR-1 where the previous GSTR-1 has not been filed and also notes the separate Rule 59(6) restriction where the preceding GSTR-3B has not been filed. citeturn1search3

17. Rule 59 — detailed mechanism

Rule 59 is the operational rule most closely connected with Section 37.

It governs:

  • GSTR-1 furnishing;
  • quarterly filing;
  • Invoice Furnishing Facility;
  • invoice-wise B2B reporting;
  • specified B2C reporting;
  • credit/debit notes;
  • GSTR-1A amendments; and
  • system restrictions on furnishing Section 37 details in specified default situations.

The 53rd GST Council material records the current Rule 59 architecture, including GSTR-1A and restrictions linked to prior GSTR-3B filing and Rule 88C intimation. citeturn0search1

18. B2B reporting

For business-to-business supplies, GSTR-1 generally requires invoice-wise details so that the recipient's GST records can be populated and reconciled.

Before filing, the supplier should verify:

  • recipient GSTIN;
  • invoice number;
  • invoice date;
  • place of supply;
  • taxable value;
  • tax rate;
  • CGST/SGST/IGST/cess;
  • reverse-charge indicator where applicable; and
  • credit/debit notes and amendments.

19. B2C reporting

Business-to-consumer reporting is structured differently from B2B reporting. The GSTR-1 tables use invoice-wise and consolidated reporting depending on the nature/value/state of the supply and the current prescribed thresholds.

The 53rd GST Council considered reducing the invoice-value threshold for invoice-wise reporting of specified inter-State B2C supplies from ₹2.5 lakh to ₹1 lakh. citeturn0search6

Always verify the current GSTR-1 table instructions and threshold. Reporting thresholds and portal tables can be changed through Rules, notifications or portal updates without rewriting the core language of Section 37.

20. Export reporting

Export invoices are part of the outward-supply reporting ecosystem. Export reporting must be consistent with the tax invoice, shipping bill/bill of export or export-service evidence, LUT/bond where applicable, place of supply and refund claim.

Rule 59 and the export-related Rules create electronic transmission and reconciliation links between GST reporting and Customs systems for export goods.

A supplier claiming zero-rated treatment should therefore reconcile its GSTR-1 export data with shipping and refund records.

21. E-commerce reporting

Supplies made through or in connection with electronic commerce operators can involve additional reporting and TCS/TDS data flows. Section 37 outward-supply reporting must therefore be read with the relevant provisions governing the supplier and the electronic commerce operator.

Rule 78 also provides matching concepts for certain e-commerce data with supplier GSTR-1 information. The GST Council's rules material reflects references to GSTR-1 as amended by GSTR-1A in that matching process. citeturn1search4

22. Impact on recipient ITC

Section 37 is important to the recipient because supplier-reported invoice details can flow into the recipient's GST system and affect ITC reconciliation.

For a recipient, a supplier invoice may therefore move through this chain:

1. Supplier invoice
Commercial transaction and tax document.
2. Supplier GSTR-1
Invoice details furnished under Section 37.
3. GST system communication
Data becomes available to the recipient subject to prescribed rules.
4. GSTR-2B/reconciliation
Recipient uses the reported data for ITC controls.

This is why a Section 37 error is not merely a supplier's internal reporting error.

23. Section 37 and annual return

The annual return is expressly relevant to the Section 37(3) correction cut-off. If the annual return for the relevant financial year is furnished before 30 November, the earlier annual-return filing date can become the effective cut-off under the “whichever is earlier” wording.

Finance teams should therefore maintain a financial-year calendar showing:

  • monthly/quarterly GSTR-1 due dates;
  • GSTR-1A availability;
  • GSTR-3B due date;
  • annual-return due date;
  • 30 November correction cut-off; and
  • actual annual-return filing date.

24. Section 37 and Section 34

Section 34 governs credit and debit notes, while Section 37 governs the furnishing of the details of those documents.

Section 34Section 37
When can a credit/debit note be issued?How are the details furnished in the outward-supply reporting system?
Tax/value adjustment framework.Electronic reporting and communication framework.
Credit-note conditions and time limit.GSTR-1/other prescribed outward-supply reporting.

Therefore, issuing a valid credit note does not by itself complete the compliance process; its details must also be reported in the prescribed manner.

25. Section 37 and GSTR-3B

GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B serve different functions.

  • GSTR-1: detailed outward-supply reporting.
  • GSTR-3B: summary return and tax-payment framework.

The figures should reconcile. If GSTR-1 shows taxable outward supplies that are not reflected appropriately in GSTR-3B, the difference can create a tax-reconciliation issue.

Rule 59 also restricts certain Section 37 filings where preceding GSTR-3B compliance is incomplete. citeturn0search1

26. Three-year filing restriction

Section 37 must also be understood alongside the wider GST return-filing restriction introduced through Finance Act, 2023.

From 1 October 2023, Section 39(11) restricts furnishing certain returns after three years from their due date, and corresponding statutory/rule controls apply across the GST return ecosystem. The Maharashtra GST Department's 2025 advisory explains that the three-year restriction covers, among others, GSTR-1/GSTR-1A and GSTR-3B and that the portal implementation was applied from the September 2025 tax period. citeturn1search6turn1search2

This is distinct from the 30 November correction cut-off under Section 37(3). The two rules address different compliance questions.

27. Major 2024 developments

One of the most important developments in the outward-supply reporting process was the introduction of GSTR-1A, an optional amendment facility for the same tax period before the relevant GSTR-3B.

The GST Council's 53rd meeting material incorporated GSTR-1A into Rule 59 and related provisions, including references to amended GSTR-1 data in downstream GST processes. citeturn1search4

The GST Portal subsequently described GSTR-1A as an optional facility available after GSTR-1 filing or its due date, whichever is later, and before GSTR-3B for the same tax period. citeturn1search3

28. Current portal compliance controls

Modern Section 37 compliance is increasingly system-controlled rather than dependent solely on manual interpretation.

Rule 59 includes restrictions where:

  • the preceding GSTR-3B has not been furnished;
  • quarterly taxpayers have not furnished the required preceding return;
  • certain Rule 88C/88D mismatch intimations remain unresolved; or
  • other prescribed conditions are not satisfied.

The 53rd GST Council material records these controls, including Rule 88C-related restrictions. citeturn0search1

This means the finance team should not wait until the GSTR-1 filing screen to discover compliance blockers.

29. Detailed practical examples

Example 1 — omitted B2B invoice

A ₹5 lakh B2B invoice issued in September is omitted from September GSTR-1. If discovered before the applicable correction mechanism closes, it should be reported using the prescribed route so the recipient's records can be populated.

Example 2 — wrong GSTIN

An invoice is reported against the wrong recipient GSTIN. The supplier should correct it through the prescribed amendment mechanism, subject to the applicable period and cut-off.

Example 3 — wrong tax amount

Taxable value is correct but IGST was reported as ₹90,000 instead of ₹99,000. The short payment must be corrected and the resulting tax/interest implications addressed.

Example 4 — GSTR-1A

A supplier files GSTR-1 and discovers an omission before filing the same-period GSTR-3B. Where GSTR-1A is available, the supplier can use the optional facility for the same-period correction.

Example 5 — previous financial year invoice

An April 2025 invoice relating to FY 2025-26 is discovered as omitted after the year ends. The supplier should track the 30 November 2026 Section 37(3) cut-off and the annual-return filing date.

Example 6 — credit note

A post-supply price reduction qualifies for a GST credit note under Section 34. The credit note must be issued within the applicable Section 34 framework and reported under Section 37/Rule 59.

Example 7 — debit note

Additional taxable value is discovered after the original invoice. A debit note is issued and its details are furnished through the prescribed outward-supply reporting mechanism.

Example 8 — QRMP taxpayer

A quarterly taxpayer uses IFF for eligible B2B invoices in the first month of the quarter. Those details are handled according to Rule 59 and are not simply duplicated in the quarterly GSTR-1.

Example 9 — GSTR-1 filed but GSTR-3B pending

A taxpayer has furnished GSTR-1 but has not furnished the relevant GSTR-3B. Rule 59 restrictions may prevent furnishing subsequent Section 37 details until the prescribed condition is satisfied.

Example 10 — old error after 30 November

A company discovers an FY 2024-25 invoice error in December 2026, after the Section 37(3) cut-off. The taxpayer should not simply alter old GSTR-1 data outside the statutory mechanism; the accounting, tax, disclosure and legal consequences must be evaluated separately.

36. Common mistakes

  • Treating GSTR-1 as merely a sales register upload instead of a statutory outward-supply statement.
  • Using the old September cut-off instead of the current 30 November rule.
  • Confusing the monthly GSTR-1 due date with the annual Section 37(3) correction cut-off.
  • Ignoring GSTR-1A when same-period corrections are required.
  • Reporting a credit note in books but failing to report it through the prescribed outward-supply mechanism.
  • Incorrect customer GSTIN or invoice number.
  • Incorrect place of supply or tax head.
  • Not reconciling GSTR-1 with GSTR-3B.
  • Not monitoring the three-year filing restriction for old pending returns.
  • Assuming that passing the Section 37 amendment cut-off eliminates the underlying tax/accounting obligation.
  • Ignoring recipient-side ITC impact of supplier reporting errors.

37. Section 37 GST FAQs

What is Section 37 of the CGST Act?

Section 37 deals with furnishing details of outward supplies of goods or services, including the reporting of invoices, debit notes, credit notes and revised invoices in the prescribed manner.

Is Section 37 the GSTR-1 section?

Yes. GSTR-1 is the principal statement through which the Section 37 outward-supply details are furnished under Rule 59.

What is the GSTR-1 due date?

Under the current ordinary framework, monthly GSTR-1 is generally due on the 11th of the succeeding month and quarterly GSTR-1 on the 13th of the month following the quarter, subject to applicable extensions.

What is the Section 37 30 November rule?

Section 37(3) generally does not allow rectification of an outward-supply error or omission after 30 November following the end of the relevant financial year or furnishing of the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier.

Can GSTR-1 be amended after filing?

Yes, subject to the prescribed amendment mechanism and applicable time limits. GSTR-1A provides an optional same-period amendment facility before the relevant GSTR-3B where available.

What is GSTR-1A?

GSTR-1A is an optional amendment facility for the same tax period. It can be used after GSTR-1 is filed or its due date has arrived, whichever is later, and before filing GSTR-3B for that period.

What happens if an invoice is missed in GSTR-1?

The taxpayer should report it through the applicable prescribed correction/reporting mechanism. For previous financial-year details, the Section 37(3) 30 November/annual-return cut-off must be checked.

Can I amend an old GST invoice after 30 November?

Section 37(3) does not permit rectification of the relevant outward-supply details after its statutory cut-off. The underlying accounting and tax implications should still be evaluated separately.

Does Section 37 cover credit notes?

Yes. The explanation to Section 37 expressly includes credit notes and debit notes among the details of outward supplies.

Does GSTR-1 affect the customer's ITC?

Yes. Supplier-reported outward-supply data is communicated through the GST system under prescribed rules and can affect the recipient's invoice visibility and ITC reconciliation.

What is Rule 59 GST?

Rule 59 prescribes the form and manner for furnishing Section 37 outward-supply details, including GSTR-1, IFF and related reporting mechanisms.

Can GSTR-1 be filed if previous GSTR-3B is pending?

Rule 59 contains restrictions that can prevent furnishing subsequent Section 37 details where the prescribed preceding return has not been furnished.

Is the 30 November date the GSTR-1 filing due date?

No. Monthly/quarterly GSTR-1 has its own due date. 30 November is the Section 37(3) outer correction cut-off for the previous financial year, subject to the “whichever is earlier” annual-return condition.

What is the difference between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

GSTR-1 provides detailed outward-supply information, while GSTR-3B is the summary return through which tax liability is reported and discharged.

39. Key takeaways

  • Section 37 is the statutory foundation for reporting outward supplies under GST.
  • GSTR-1 is the principal operational statement for Section 37 details.
  • Invoices, debit notes, credit notes and revised invoices form part of the outward-supply reporting framework.
  • The current Section 37(3) correction cut-off is 30 November following the relevant financial year or the relevant annual-return filing date, whichever is earlier.
  • The old September-based correction rule was replaced from 1 October 2022.
  • GSTR-1A provides an optional same-period correction facility before the relevant GSTR-3B where available.
  • Rule 59 contains the detailed operational GSTR-1/IFF/reporting framework.
  • Section 37 reporting can directly affect recipient-side ITC reconciliation.
  • GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B should be reconciled before filing.
  • The three-year filing restriction is a separate issue from the 30 November Section 37 correction cut-off.
  • Construction and infrastructure businesses should reconcile RA bills, advances, credit/debit notes and customer GSTINs before GSTR-1 filing.
