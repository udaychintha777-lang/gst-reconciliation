CGST Act Section 167 – Delegation of Powers

Section 167 provides the statutory mechanism by which the Commissioner may, by notification and subject to specified conditions, allow another specified authority or officer to exercise a power available to an authority or officer under the CGST Act.

1. Section 167 at a Glance

Who can direct delegation?The Commissioner.
How?By notification.
What can be delegated?Any power exercisable by an authority or officer under the CGST Act, to the extent and subject to the conditions specified in the notification.
In simple language: Section 167 is about who is legally authorised to exercise a GST power. It allows the Commissioner to specify, through a notification, another authority or officer who may also exercise a power under the CGST Act, with conditions if the notification specifies them.

3. Statutory Text of Section 167

167. Delegation of powers.— The Commissioner may, by notification, direct that subject to such conditions, if any, as may be specified in the notification, any power exercisable by any authority or officer under this Act may be exercisable also by another authority or officer as may be specified in such notification.
Core point: The section itself does not list every delegation. It provides the legal mechanism; the actual delegated authority, officer, power and conditions are identified in the relevant notification.

4. Meaning in Simple Language

GST legislation gives different powers to specified authorities and officers. In a large tax administration, it may be necessary for another officer or authority to exercise a power that the Act assigns to a particular authority or officer. Section 167 provides a statutory route for that arrangement.

The Commissioner may issue a notification stating that a particular power, otherwise exercisable by an authority or officer under the CGST Act, may also be exercised by another authority or officer specified in the notification.

Example in simple terms: If Power X is exercisable by Authority A under the Act, a valid Section 167 notification can specify that Authority B may also exercise Power X, subject to any conditions stated in that notification.

This makes the delegation notification important whenever the authority of an officer is material to a GST proceeding.

6. What Can Be Delegated?

The statutory wording is broad: it refers to any power exercisable by any authority or officer under the CGST Act. However, the practical scope of a particular delegation is determined by the notification through which the Commissioner exercises the Section 167 power.

PowerIdentify the exact statutory power being exercised.
Officer / authorityIdentify the person or class of authority specified by the notification.
ConditionsRead all conditions attached to the delegation.

A taxpayer should therefore avoid the assumption that a general designation such as “GST officer” automatically means every statutory power is available to that officer. The exact delegation framework must be checked.

7. Why the Delegation Notification Matters

Section 167 operates through a notification. For that reason, a notice, order, assessment, demand, adjudication action or other proceeding can sometimes require examination of the officer's source of authority.

8. Conditions and Limits

The words “subject to such conditions, if any, as may be specified in the notification” are important. The Commissioner can attach conditions to the delegation.

  • The delegation may identify a particular power or category of power.
  • It may identify a particular authority or officer.
  • It may contain conditions that govern exercise of the delegated power.
  • The relevant notification may have a specific scope or effective date.
  • The delegation should be read together with the statutory provision creating the underlying power.
Key distinction: Section 167 concerns authority to exercise an existing statutory power. It is not itself a charging provision and should not be read as independently creating a tax, interest, penalty or substantive liability.

9. Why Delegation Matters in GST Proceedings

GST proceedings involve many statutory powers exercised by different levels of the tax administration. Where a particular power is relevant, the legal authority of the officer exercising it can be material.

Questions to ask when reviewing an order or notice

  1. What section of the CGST Act gives the underlying power?
  2. Which authority or officer is named in that provision?
  3. Is there a Section 167 delegation notification?
  4. Does the notification cover the exact power involved?
  5. Does it cover the particular officer or authority?
  6. Were the notification's conditions satisfied?
  7. Was the delegation in force on the relevant date?
  8. Has a later notification modified or replaced it?
Important: A question about officer authority is fact-specific. The existence and wording of the relevant delegation notification should be verified before drawing a legal conclusion.

10. Practical Examples

Example 1 – Specified officer receives delegated power

A Commissioner issues a notification specifying that another officer may also exercise a particular statutory power. The officer's authority is then examined with reference to that notification and its conditions.

Example 2 – Officer designation alone is insufficient

An officer's job title does not by itself establish that every power under the Act is available to that officer. The relevant statutory provision and delegation notification should be checked.

Example 3 – Condition in notification

If a delegation notification attaches a condition, the officer's exercise of the power should be tested against that condition.

Example 4 – Historical proceeding

An order passed in an earlier year should be tested against the delegation framework applicable on the date of the relevant action, not merely today's delegation arrangements.

Example 5 – Later notification

If a later notification changes the delegation framework, the company should determine which notification governed the action under review.

Example 6 – Notice response

When a legal notice raises an officer-authority issue, the taxpayer can ask for the statutory source and applicable delegation notification rather than relying on assumptions about hierarchy.

Example 7 – Multiple GSTINs

A company operating multiple registrations should record the relevant jurisdiction and delegation framework for each material proceeding.

Example 8 – ERP legal register

A tax-management system can store delegation notifications alongside jurisdiction and officer-authority references to support audit and litigation teams.

Example 9 – Delegation is not a new tax

A Section 167 notification concerns exercise of statutory power. It does not by itself create a new charging provision.

Example 10 – Circular confusion

A circular may explain administrative practice, but it should not automatically be treated as the Section 167 delegation notification.

Example 11 – Litigation file

Where officer jurisdiction is relevant to litigation, retain the Act provision, delegation notification, subsequent amendments and the order/notice being challenged or defended.

Example 12 – Internal review

A tax manager can include “authority verified?” as a checklist item before accepting a significant GST order for further appeal analysis.

Example 13 – Delegated power versus administrative task

Assigning a clerk or team member to prepare a file is different from legally authorising an officer to exercise a statutory power. The two should not be conflated.

Example 14 – Current versus historical law

For an old tax period, archive the delegation notification applicable to the date of action so that later changes do not distort the historical analysis.

11. Finance & Litigation Workflow

  1. Identify the proceeding: notice, order, audit action, investigation or other statutory action.
  2. Identify the power: note the exact CGST Act section relied upon.
  3. Identify the officer: record designation, jurisdiction and date of action.
  4. Check delegation: locate the applicable Section 167 notification, if relevant.
  5. Read conditions: verify every condition or limitation in the notification.
  6. Check effective date: ensure the notification was applicable when the power was exercised.
  7. Check amendments: search for later or prior delegation notifications.
  8. Preserve evidence: keep copies in the litigation/tax compliance file.
  9. Escalate material issues: obtain professional legal review where officer authority is a significant ground of dispute.

14. Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every GST officer automatically has every power under the Act.
  • Ignoring the actual Section 167 notification.
  • Checking only the officer's designation and not the delegated power.
  • Ignoring conditions specified in the notification.
  • Using a current delegation notification for an old proceeding without checking its historical applicability.
  • Confusing statutory delegation with ordinary administrative allocation of work.
  • Calling a circular or advisory a delegation notification without checking its legal source.
  • Failing to retain delegation documents in a litigation file.
  • Assuming Section 167 itself creates a tax or penalty liability.
  • Ignoring subsequent notifications that may change the delegation framework.

16. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is Section 167 of the CGST Act?

Section 167 allows the Commissioner, by notification and subject to specified conditions, to direct that a power exercisable by an authority or officer under the CGST Act may also be exercised by another specified authority or officer.

2. Who can delegate powers under Section 167?

The statutory text gives this power to the Commissioner.

3. Is delegation automatic?

No. Section 167 contemplates a direction by notification and allows conditions to be specified in that notification.

4. Can any GST officer exercise any power after Section 167 delegation?

Not merely because the officer is a GST officer. The relevant notification must specify the delegated power, the authority or officer and any applicable conditions.

5. Does Section 167 transfer the original statutory power permanently?

The section is framed as allowing another specified authority or officer also to exercise a power. The exact effect depends on the notification and its conditions.

6. Can conditions be attached to a delegation?

Yes. Section 167 expressly permits the notification to specify conditions, if any.

7. Does Section 167 apply to powers under the CGST Act?

Yes. The text refers to any power exercisable by any authority or officer under this Act.

8. What is the difference between delegation and assignment of work?

Delegation under Section 167 is a statutory mechanism for another specified authority or officer to exercise a power. An internal allocation of administrative work is not automatically a Section 167 delegation.

9. Does a Section 167 notification matter in a GST notice or order?

It can be important where the authority of the issuing officer is relevant. The applicable delegation notification and its conditions should then be checked.

10. Is Section 167 the same as Section 168?

No. Section 167 concerns delegation of powers by the Commissioner. Section 168 concerns the Board's power to issue instructions or directions for specified purposes.

11. Should companies keep delegation notifications?

Yes. For material litigation, notices and internal legal controls, retaining the relevant delegation notification can help verify the authority of the officer who acted.

12. What should be checked in a delegation notification?

Check the issuing authority, date, delegated power, specified officer/authority, conditions, jurisdiction or scope if stated, and effective date.

13. Can delegation notifications be changed?

The applicable notification history should be checked. A later notification may modify, replace or otherwise affect an earlier delegation.

14. Does delegation change the taxpayer's substantive liability?

Section 167 is about who may exercise a statutory power. It should not be treated by itself as creating a new tax liability or changing the substantive charging provision.

15. Can a delegation be challenged?

A specific challenge depends on the notification, statutory power, facts and applicable law. The question is ordinarily one of legal authority and compliance with the statutory framework.

16. Does Section 167 itself specify a list of delegated powers?

No. The section provides the enabling mechanism; the actual delegated powers and officers are identified through notifications.

17. Is a circular automatically a delegation notification?

No. The legal instrument should be identified from its text, issuing authority and statutory source.

18. Is this article legal advice?

No. It is an educational explanation. For a particular notice, order or litigation issue, verify the current and historical Act, rules, delegation notification and applicable judicial interpretation.

17. Quick Revision – Section 167

  • Subject: Delegation of powers.
  • Authority: Commissioner.
  • Method: Notification.
  • What may be delegated: Any power exercisable by an authority or officer under the CGST Act.
  • Who may receive it: Another authority or officer specified in the notification.
  • Conditions: The notification may specify conditions.
  • Key compliance point: Verify the exact statutory power, officer, notification, conditions and effective date.
  • Related section: Section 168 separately deals with Board instructions/directions.
