Rule 6 – Validity of Composition Levy

Understand when the composition option continues, when it automatically stops, what happens from the date eligibility is lost, and how to manage withdrawal and transition to the regular GST scheme.

At a glance

What Rule 6 does

Rule 6 is the continuation-and-exit rule for the composition levy. The composition option does not remain valid merely because it was once selected. It remains valid only while the taxpayer continues to satisfy the conditions in Section 10 and the composition chapter of the CGST Rules.

Core principle: Composition status is conditional. If a taxpayer ceases to satisfy an applicable condition, the taxpayer must move to the regular GST mechanism from the legally relevant date and complete the prescribed withdrawal and compliance steps.

This makes Rule 6 especially important for finance teams. A composition taxpayer should continuously monitor turnover, nature of supplies, registration status, business locations and other eligibility conditions rather than reviewing eligibility only once a year.

1. Rule 6 — statutory framework

Rule 6(1): The option exercised by a registered person to pay tax under Section 10 remains valid so long as the person satisfies all the conditions mentioned in Section 10 and under the relevant rules.

Rule 6(2): When the person ceases to satisfy any applicable condition in Section 10 or the composition chapter, the person becomes liable to pay tax under Section 9(1) from the day of such cessation, must issue tax invoices for taxable supplies made thereafter, and must furnish the prescribed CMP-04 intimation within seven days of the occurrence of the event.

Rule 6(3): A registered person who intends to withdraw from the composition scheme must, before the intended withdrawal date, electronically file an application in Form GST CMP-04, duly signed or verified as prescribed.

Important distinction: There are two different situations: voluntary withdrawal, where the taxpayer decides to leave composition, and cessation of eligibility, where an event causes the taxpayer to no longer satisfy the legal conditions. The compliance timeline and tax consequences should be analysed accordingly.

2. What Rule 6 means in simple language

Think of composition levy as a status that has to be continuously maintained.

At the time of opting
The taxpayer meets the eligibility requirements and chooses composition treatment.
During the year
The taxpayer must continue complying with the conditions. A later change in business can affect eligibility.
If eligibility is lost
Composition treatment cannot simply continue until the next financial year. Regular GST consequences arise from the legally relevant date.
If withdrawal is planned
The taxpayer should file CMP-04 before the intended withdrawal date and plan the accounting and invoicing transition.

Practical message: The biggest risk is not the original composition application. It is failing to identify the date on which the business stopped satisfying the conditions.

3. Events that can put composition validity at risk

Rule 6 refers back to the conditions in Section 10 and the composition chapter. Therefore, the finance team should monitor changes that can affect eligibility, including:

  • Crossing the applicable statutory turnover threshold.
  • Starting a category of supply that is not permitted under the chosen composition route.
  • Making supplies or undertaking activities inconsistent with the conditions applicable to the taxpayer.
  • Changes in the nature or structure of the business.
  • Changes affecting the taxpayer's registration or places of business.
  • Changes that cause the taxpayer to fall outside the applicable composition provisions.
  • Any other event that causes a condition under Section 10 or the relevant rules to cease being satisfied.
Do not wait for the GST portal to tell you that eligibility has ended. The legal consequence can arise from the underlying business event itself. A monthly compliance review is therefore safer than relying only on return filing.

4. Why the date of loss of eligibility matters

The date is one of the most important parts of Rule 6. Under Rule 6(2), when the taxpayer ceases to satisfy an applicable condition, regular GST liability under Section 9(1) applies from the day of cessation.

5. Practical business examples

Example 1 — Eligibility is lost during the year

A composition taxpayer is eligible at the beginning of the year. During the year, an event occurs that makes the taxpayer cease to satisfy a condition of Section 10. The taxpayer should not continue treating all later supplies as composition supplies merely because the composition option was originally selected.

The finance team should identify the date of the event, determine the regular GST consequences from that date, issue appropriate tax invoices for taxable supplies thereafter, and complete the prescribed CMP-04 compliance.

Example 2 — Business expansion

A small trader under composition opens a new business activity and starts making a category of supply that is not compatible with its composition eligibility. The correct question is not “Can we continue because our old GST registration is still active?” The correct question is “Do we still satisfy every applicable condition of Section 10 and the composition rules?”

Example 3 — Planned voluntary withdrawal

A taxpayer expects its customer profile and business model to change and decides that the regular GST scheme will be commercially preferable from a future date. Instead of waiting for a compliance failure, the taxpayer can plan an orderly withdrawal using CMP-04 and align the effective date, invoicing, tax masters and return reporting.

Management practice: Whenever a business changes its product mix, service mix, customer base, locations or turnover profile, include a “composition eligibility check” in the approval process.

6. Form GST CMP-04 — why it matters

CMP-04 is the prescribed form used for withdrawal from the composition levy. Rule 6 makes it relevant both to planned withdrawal and to circumstances where the taxpayer ceases to satisfy the composition conditions.

7. Transition from composition to regular GST

Exit from composition is not just a portal activity. It is an accounting and operational transition.

  1. Identify the effective date.
  2. Freeze the old composition tax configuration from the applicable date.
  3. Activate regular GST tax codes for taxable supplies.
  4. Switch from bill of supply to tax invoice wherever required.
  5. Review input tax credit eligibility under the provisions applicable on transition.
  6. Reconcile sales from the transition date separately so that composition and regular-scheme transactions are not mixed.
  7. Update return workings and tax ledgers consistently.
ERP risk: If the tax status is changed in the GST portal but the billing software remains configured as composition, invoices, tax ledgers and GST returns can become inconsistent.

8. Audit and GST notice relevance

Rule 6 can become important during departmental verification because the department may examine not only whether CMP-04 was filed, but also whether the taxpayer correctly identified the date from which composition eligibility ceased.

Documents to maintain

  • Original composition option and registration records.
  • Monthly turnover and eligibility workings.
  • Business-change approvals and supporting agreements where relevant.
  • Sales register around the date of the event.
  • Stock and inventory records where transition affects ITC or tax treatment.
  • CMP-04 filing acknowledgement.
  • Invoices issued immediately before and after the transition.
  • GST return reconciliation for the transition period.
Common notice issue: A delayed exit can create a mismatch between the date the taxpayer claims to have ceased composition and the date the underlying event actually occurred. Maintain evidence supporting the precise date.

10. Common mistakes under Rule 6

  1. Assuming composition continues automatically for the whole financial year. Validity depends on continuing eligibility.
  2. Identifying the exit date only when the return is prepared. The underlying event may have occurred earlier.
  3. Filing CMP-04 but continuing to issue bills of supply. Operational systems must change with the tax status.
  4. Changing the GST portal status without changing ERP tax masters.
  5. Not separately reconciling transactions around the transition date.
  6. Ignoring customer communication. Customers may need proper tax invoices once regular GST treatment begins.
  7. Not documenting why the business remained eligible. A simple monthly eligibility working can be valuable during an audit.

11. Management decision framework

For a composition taxpayer, management should ask these questions every month:

1. Are we still eligible?
Review Section 10 and the relevant composition conditions.
2. Has the business changed?
Review new products, services, customers, locations and contracts.
3. Has any trigger occurred?
Identify the exact date of any event affecting eligibility.
4. Has the ERP changed?
Ensure billing, tax masters and ledgers match the legal status.

13. Frequently asked questions

What is GST Rule 6?

Rule 6 of the CGST Rules deals with the validity of the composition levy. The composition option remains valid while the taxpayer continues to satisfy the conditions in Section 10 and the relevant rules.

Does composition levy remain valid for the entire financial year?

Not necessarily. Its validity is conditional. If the taxpayer ceases to satisfy an applicable condition, Rule 6 provides for regular GST treatment from the relevant date of cessation.

What happens when a composition taxpayer becomes ineligible?

The taxpayer becomes liable to pay tax under Section 9(1) from the day the applicable condition ceases to be satisfied, must issue tax invoices for taxable supplies made thereafter, and must complete the prescribed CMP-04 compliance.

What is CMP-04 in GST?

Form GST CMP-04 is the prescribed form for withdrawal from the composition levy. Rule 6 specifies its use in voluntary withdrawal and in the circumstances covered by cessation of eligibility.

How quickly must CMP-04 be filed after loss of eligibility?

Rule 6(2) specifies an intimation in Form GST CMP-04 within seven days of the occurrence of the event causing cessation of eligibility.

Can a composition taxpayer voluntarily withdraw?

Yes. Rule 6(3) provides for a registered person who intends to withdraw to file CMP-04 before the date of withdrawal.

Why is the exact date of withdrawal important?

The date controls when the taxpayer moves from composition treatment to regular GST treatment and therefore affects invoicing, tax calculation, accounting, return reporting and transition controls.

14. Practical Rule 6 compliance checklist

  • Review composition eligibility every month.
  • Monitor turnover and all statutory conditions.
  • Review new business activities before launch.
  • Document any event that may affect eligibility.
  • Determine the exact date of cessation, if applicable.
  • File CMP-04 within the applicable timeline.
  • Switch to regular GST invoicing from the relevant date.
  • Update ERP and accounting tax masters.
  • Reconcile pre- and post-exit transactions separately.
  • Retain evidence supporting the eligibility and transition decision.

15. Practical takeaway

Rule 6 establishes a simple but very important principle: composition is valid only while the taxpayer remains eligible. The compliance team should therefore treat composition status as a monitored condition, not as a permanent registration setting.

Best control sequence: Monitor eligibility → identify event → determine exact date → file CMP-04 → switch invoicing → update ERP → reconcile transition → preserve evidence.

This approach reduces the risk of continuing composition treatment after eligibility has ended and gives management a clear audit trail for the transition to the regular GST scheme.
